## US-0801. Reassign a case whose compartment the new assignee is not in

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-HRS-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Open, assign and resolve an HR service request
- **Dimension**: `HRS` HR service delivery and cases
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `hr-service`, `compartment-membership`, `explicit-grant`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Case HR-6010 sits in a matter compartment with four members.
- It is reassigned 2026-08-24 to a partner outside the compartment.
- The reassignment is approved by the case owner.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the compartment membership to be granted explicitly rather than implied by the assignment, so that membership is a decision rather than a consequence

**Scenario**

Assigning a case to someone outside its compartment either fails or silently grants them membership. Silent grant means the compartment grows by assignment, which is how walls erode. The assignment requires an explicit membership grant with its own scope, and the case owner's approval covers both acts rather than the membership following from the assignment.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a case is assigned outside its compartment
   **When** the assignment is evaluated
   **Then** it requires an explicit membership grant rather than granting membership implicitly
2. **Given** the membership is granted
   **When** it is recorded
   **Then** the membership carries its own scope and date and defaults to forward-only, so the compartment's BusinessState is unchanged by the addition
3. **Given** the assignment is later moved on
   **When** the membership is evaluated
   **Then** it does not lapse automatically and is reviewed, because a member who read the matter has read it

**Data read**

- `case`: the case, its compartment and the membership
- `people`: the proposed assignee

**Data written**

- `case`: the membership grant and the assignment

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The new assignee receives FULL disclosure within their granted scope.
- The compartment's prior content outside their scope is DENIED by name with its existence visible, so they can request it.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- membership follows from the assignment -> the compartment grows by assignment and the wall erodes
- membership lapses automatically when the assignment moves -> the record understates who has read the matter

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The membership grant with its scope.
- The assignment and the approval covering both.
- The membership's persistence after reassignment.

## US-0802. Handle an approval whose approver has changed role since queueing

- **Persona**: manager
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Resolve an approval requirement and bind the decision
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `authority-at-decision`, `re-routing`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. An approval was routed to a manager on 2026-05-11.
- They moved to a role without that authority on 2026-05-18.
- They open the item on 2026-05-20.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want the authority to be evaluated at the decision rather than at routing, so that somebody who no longer holds the authority does not exercise it

**Scenario**

The item was routed to them when they held the authority and they no longer do. Evaluating the authority at routing would let a queue outlive the authority that filled it. The approval is refused with the authority change named, the item re-routes to the current holder of the role, and the earlier routing remains in the item's history so the delay is explicable.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the approver's authority ended after routing
   **When** they submit a decision
   **Then** it is refused and the item's RequestState stays PENDING_APPROVAL, because the authority is evaluated at the decision rather than at routing
2. **Given** the item re-routes
   **When** the routing runs
   **Then** it goes to the current holder of the required role and the earlier routing remains in the history
3. **Given** the approver still holds the authority
   **When** they submit a decision
   **Then** it succeeds and the authority snapshot records the authority as it stood at the decision

**Data read**

- `identity`: the approver's authority intervals
- `workflow`: the item, its routing history and the approval requirement

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the refusal and the re-routing

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The original approver receives FULL disclosure of the refusal and the authority change.
- The reason for their role change is DENIED to them in this context; the authority interval is disclosed.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- authority is evaluated at routing -> a queue outlives the authority that filled it
- the earlier routing is removed on re-routing -> the delay has no explanation in the record

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The authority intervals and the decision time.
- The refusal and the re-routing.
- The retained routing history.

## US-0804. Split a shared services cost across the entities it serves

- **Persona**: finance partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-BIL-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Attribute platform cost to a tenant and a capability
- **Dimension**: `BIL` Billing and commercial
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US and United Kingdom
- **Tags**: `billing`, `cost-allocation`, `transfer-pricing`, `cross-border`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A shared HR service costs 840,000.00 USD in 2026 and serves three entities.
- Headcount shares are 62, 28 and 10 percent.
- Transfer pricing rules require an arm's length allocation.

**Story**

> As a finance partner, I want the allocation basis to be defensible under transfer pricing rather than merely proportionate, so that the allocation survives a tax authority's question

**Scenario**

A headcount split is proportionate and is not automatically arm's length. The allocation basis is documented against the service actually provided: headcount is used where the driver is headcount and a different driver is used where it is not, and the whole basis is recorded with the reasoning. An allocation nobody documented is one the entity that paid most cannot defend when asked why.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a shared cost is allocated across entities
   **When** the basis is chosen
   **Then** it is documented against the service actually provided rather than defaulting to headcount
2. **Given** different components have different drivers
   **When** the allocation runs
   **Then** each component uses its own driver and the components sum to the total
3. **Given** a tax authority asks for the basis
   **When** the documentation is produced
   **Then** it states the driver, its rationale and the computation for each component
4. **Given** the drivers change during the year
   **When** the allocation runs
   **Then** it is computed per period under the drivers in force rather than annually under the year-end drivers

**Data read**

- `billing`: the shared cost, its components and the allocation drivers
- `finance`: the transfer pricing documentation requirements
- `people`: the headcount by entity

**Data written**

- `billing`: the per-component allocation and its documentation

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Each entity receives FULL disclosure of its own allocation and the drivers behind it.
- Other entities' allocations are disclosed within the group and are DENIED to any external recipient other than an authority entitled to them.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- headcount is used for every component -> the allocation is proportionate and not defensible for components headcount does not drive
- the basis is not documented -> the entity that paid most cannot answer why when asked

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The components and their drivers.
- The per-component computation.
- The documentation as produced.

## US-0806. Reconcile a benefits invoice against enrollment at the invoice date

- **Persona**: benefits administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-BEN-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Reconcile a carrier file against local enrollment
- **Dimension**: `BEN` Benefits
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `benefits`, `invoice-reconciliation`, `coverage-period`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A carrier invoices 2026-08-04 for August coverage of 1,412 subscribers.
- Local enrollment at 2026-08-04 shows 1,418.
- Six enrollments took effect 2026-08-01 and were transmitted 2026-08-03.

**Story**

> As a benefits administrator, I want the comparison to be made at the coverage period rather than at the invoice date, so that six enrollments in flight are not six discrepancies

**Scenario**

An invoice for August coverage compared against enrollment on the day it was issued mixes two time coordinates. The comparison is made on coverage for the period the invoice covers: all 1,418 are covered in August and six were transmitted the day before the invoice was cut. The carrier's next invoice will include them. The reconciliation reports six as timing rather than as discrepancies, and a difference that persists into the following month is what gets investigated.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an invoice covers a period
   **When** the reconciliation runs
   **Then** the comparison is made on coverage for that period rather than on enrollment at the invoice date
2. **Given** enrollments were transmitted close to the invoice cut
   **When** the difference is classified
   **Then** it is classified as timing with the transmission dates shown
3. **Given** a timing difference persists into the following month
   **When** the reconciliation runs
   **Then** it becomes a discrepancy and is investigated
4. **Given** the carrier's own cut date is unknown
   **When** the reconciliation runs
   **Then** it is requested and recorded, because the classification depends on it

**Data read**

- `benefits`: the enrollments, their effective dates and their transmission dates
- `finance`: the carrier invoice and its coverage period
- `integration`: the transmission log and the carrier's cut date

**Data written**

- `benefits`: the reconciliation with its timing and discrepancy classification

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The benefits function receives FULL disclosure of both sides and the classification.
- Subscribers are notified only of genuine discrepancies affecting their own coverage; timing differences are WITHHELD from them because nothing is wrong.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the comparison uses enrollment at the invoice date -> six in-flight enrollments are investigated as discrepancies every month
- timing differences are never re-examined -> a real discrepancy hides behind a timing classification indefinitely

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The coverage period and the enrollment at it.
- The transmission dates and the carrier cut date.
- The classification and the following month's check.

**Story**

> As a payroll administrator, I want the correction to append to the locked period rather than being added to the current one, so that the hours are attributed to the week I worked them

**Scenario**

The hours were worked in the week ending 2026-06-15 and the period is locked. Adding them to the current period would pay them and would attribute them to the wrong week, which matters because overtime is computed weekly and the two weeks have different hours. The correction appends to the locked period, the overtime for that week is recomputed, and the payment rides the next run with the corrected period clearly identified.

   **Then** the weekly overtime for that week is recomputed rather than the hours being paid at straight time
3. **Given** the payment is made
   **When** the statement renders
   **Then** it identifies the corrected period rather than presenting the hours as current-period earnings
4. **Given** the correction would change a filed figure
   **When** the reporting is evaluated
   **Then** the amendment path is followed rather than the difference being absorbed into the current period

**Data read**

- `payroll`: the paid period and the overtime computation
- `time`: the locked period, its entries and the dispute

**Data written**

- `payroll`: the recomputed overtime and the payment
- `time`: the appended correction to the locked period

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The manager's own agreement to the hours is disclosed to the worker and the manager's other timecard decisions are DENIED to them.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the hours are added to the current period -> the weekly overtime is computed on the wrong week and the figure is wrong in both weeks
- the correction is paid at straight time -> the premium the hours earned in their own week is never paid

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The locked period and the appended correction.

## US-0808. Handle a merger where two tenants hold the same person

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PEO-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Merge two person records that describe the same human

- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `people`, `tenant-merger`, `person-matching`, `acquisition`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme acquires a company whose tenant borealis holds 340 workers on 2027-02-01.
- Eleven people exist in both tenants, having worked for both companies.
- The two tenants' person records were never linked.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the eleven to be identified and merged deliberately rather than existing twice after the migration, so that each person has one record with two employment histories

**Scenario**

Eleven people worked for both companies and exist twice. Migrating without matching produces eleven duplicate persons with split histories, and every later merge is harder than doing it now. The candidates are identified by the ordinary matching rules and resolved individually before the migration, each producing one person with two employment intervals. A match that cannot be resolved with confidence is migrated as a separate person with the candidate link recorded rather than being merged on a guess.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the same human exists in both tenants
   **When** the migration is planned
   **Then** candidates are identified by the matching rules and resolved before the migration rather than after
2. **Given** a match is confirmed
   **When** the merge runs
   **Then** one person survives with two employment intervals and the non-surviving identifier redirects
3. **Given** a match cannot be resolved with confidence
   **When** the migration runs
   **Then** the person is migrated separately with the candidate link recorded rather than being merged on a guess
4. **Given** a merged person has conflicting facts between the two tenants
   **When** the merge runs
   **Then** each fact keeps its source and its interval rather than one tenant's version overwriting the other's
5. **Given** the acquired tenant's records are under a legal hold
   **When** the migration is planned
   **Then** the hold moves with the records and the merge does not release it

**Data read**

- `people`: both tenants' person records and their employment histories
- `tenant`: the acquisition, the migration scope and any holds

**Data written**

- `people`: the merged persons with their redirects and the recorded candidate links

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The merged workers receive FULL disclosure of the merge and both employment histories.
- Each source tenant's other workers are WITHHELD from the other tenant until the migration itself moves them.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the tenants are migrated without matching -> eleven duplicate persons exist with split histories and every later merge is harder
- an unconfident match is merged -> two people become one and unpicking it is far worse than leaving them separate

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The match candidates and their scores.
- The confirmed merges with their redirects.
- The unresolved candidates and their recorded links.

## US-0809. Apply a mass layoff notice requirement across multiple sites

- Tenant acme plans reductions affecting 84 workers across three sites on 2027-03-16.

**Story**

**Scenario**

One site crosses on its own count and two do not. The rule also aggregates in defined circumstances, and evaluating only per site would miss that.    **When** the obligation is created
   **Then** the obligation carries the notice period and the required recipients, and the reductions stay in RequestState PENDING until ObligationState reaches SATISFIED
4. **Given** the population changes before the notice date
   **When** the thresholds are re-evaluated
   **Then** a reduction that falls below a threshold does not retroactively discharge a notice already owed
5. **Given** state requirements differ from the federal one
   **When** the evaluation runs
   **Then** each is evaluated separately and the more demanding one governs where they overlap

**Data written**

- `operations`: the notice obligations with their periods and recipients
- `people`: the reductions blocked until the periods elapse

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The affected workers and the required public recipients receive FULL disclosure as the notice requires.
- The internal deliberation about which workers were selected is DENIED to the recipients; the notice's required content is disclosed.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- only the per-site test runs -> an aggregation rule goes unnoticed because no single site crosses
- a later reduction in scope discharges a notice already owed -> an obligation that arose is treated as never having arisen

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both threshold evaluations and their inputs.
- The notice obligations with their periods and recipients.
- The state evaluations and the governing requirement.

## US-0810. Restore service after a cell failure with partially replicated state

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-REP-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Run a recovery test and prove the restore works
- **Dimension**: `REP` Reconciliation, repair and operations
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `repair`, `failover`, `replication-window`, `unknown-outcome`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme's cell fails at 2026-11-08T03:14 with replication lagging 90 seconds.
- Failover to the standby completes at 03:22.
- Intents committed in the final 90 seconds may not have replicated.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the possibly-lost window to be enumerated rather than assumed empty, so that we know which intents to ask about rather than hoping there were none

**Scenario**

The standby is 90 seconds behind and the failover took eight minutes. Between the last replicated position and the failure there may be committed intents that the standby does not hold. Assuming the window was empty is the cheapest and least defensible option. The window is enumerated from the primary's own durable log where it survives and from the submitting clients' receipts where it does not, each candidate intent is resolved as replicated, lost or unknown, and the lost ones are resubmitted with their original idempotency keys.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a failover occurs with replication lag
   **When** the recovery runs
   **Then** the window between the last replicated position and the failure is enumerated rather than assumed empty
2. **Given** an intent in the window is resolved
   **When** the resolution runs
   **Then** it is classified replicated, lost or unknown, and unknown is a distinct outcome rather than being folded into either
3. **Given** a lost intent is resubmitted
   **When** the resubmission runs
   **Then** it carries its original idempotency key so a replicated-but-unobserved intent is not applied twice
4. **Given** an intent's outcome cannot be determined
   **When** the recovery concludes
   **Then** it is recorded as unknown with its submitting client identified, so the question can be asked rather than closed
5. **Given** the primary's durable log survives
   **When** the enumeration runs
   **Then** it is used in preference to client receipts, and which source was used is recorded per intent

**Data read**

- `operations`: the intents in the window
- `provenance`: the durable log and the client receipts
- `tenant`: the cell state, the replication position and the failover timeline

**Data written**

- `operations`: the enumerated window, the per-intent classification and the resubmissions

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The customer receives FULL disclosure of the window, the counts by classification and any unknown intents.
- Other tenants on the same cell are WITHHELD from this recovery's scope and are not named in the customer's report.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the window is assumed empty -> committed work is lost silently and is discovered by the people it affected
- unknown intents are classified as lost and resubmitted -> a replicated intent is applied twice unless the idempotency key holds, and the key is the only thing preventing it

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The replication position and the failure time.
- The enumerated window and its per-intent classification.
- The resubmissions with their original keys.

## US-0811. An operator changes a rule pack to alter a computation already under dispute

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TAX-005`](../workflows/catalog.md) Apply a regulatory change when a rule pack version updates
- **Dimension**: `TAX` Tax and regulatory
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `tax`, `adversarial`, `immutable-version`, `dispute-tampering`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A worker disputes a 2026-04 withholding computation on 2026-09-14.
- On 2026-09-16 an operator edits the rule pack version that governed April.
- The pack version is referenced by 41,000 computations.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want a published pack version to be immutable so a dispute cannot be answered by changing the rule, so that the rule that governed April is the rule that governed April

**Scenario**

The edit would change what 41,000 computations were computed under, retroactively and invisibly, and would make the disputed figure reproducible against a rule that did not exist in April. Published pack versions are immutable: the edit is refused and a new version is offered, which takes effect from its own date and does not touch the computations bound to the earlier one. The attempt is recorded and surfaced, because an edit to a version under active dispute is worth distinguishing from an ordinary configuration change.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a published rule pack version is edited
   **When** the edit is submitted
   **Then** the edit is refused, the published version's BusinessState is immutable, and a new version is offered instead
2. **Given** the version is referenced by existing computations
   **When** the refusal is issued
   **Then** the reference count is stated so the operator sees the blast radius the edit would have had
3. **Given** the version is under active dispute
   **When** the attempt is recorded
   **Then** it is surfaced to compliance as an edit attempt on a disputed version rather than logged as an ordinary refusal
4. **Given** a new version is published
   **When** the disputed computation is re-read
   **Then** it still resolves to the original version and the new one does not apply to it
5. **Given** the operator has legitimate grounds to change the rule going forward
   **When** the new version is published
   **Then** it applies from its own effective date and the earlier computations are unaffected

**Data read**

- `case`: the open dispute
- `payroll`: the computations bound to the disputed version
- `tax`: the pack versions, their immutability and their reference counts

**Data written**

- `tax`: the refused edit and the recorded attempt
