## US-0001. Create a person for a US hire with a clean identity match

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PEO-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Create a person from verified identity claims
- **Dimension**: `PEO` People and core HR
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, Texas
- **Tags**: `people`, `identity`, `happy-path`, `government-identifier`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme, organization scope acme/workforce.
- Priya Raghavan, no existing Person record, claims: legal name, date of birth 1994-03-11, SSN last four 4417.
- Identity match policy v3 pinned, uncertainty band 0.62 to 0.88.
- Processing purpose HIRE_ONBOARDING is active for the tenant.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want to create a Person record from an incoming hire's identity claims, so that the rest of onboarding has a subject to attach to

**Scenario**

The HR business partner submits the three claims. Identity resolution returns a top score of 0.11 against the Person index, well below the uncertainty band, so the route is NO_MATCH and no human resolution task is created. The proposal is built with the normalized claim set and its provenance. Because SSN is a government identifier, the compliance approval requirement fires and Dana Whitfield approves it against the proposal digest. The Person is committed with recorded-at 2026-02-09T14:22:11Z. The observe step confirms the Person is retrievable at that watermark and the intent closes with RequestState COMPLETED and ObligationState PENDING, because the identity-proof retention obligation runs for the life of the employment.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** no Person in tenant acme matches the claim set above a score of 0.20
   **When** the create_person intent is submitted with all three claims
   **Then** the match decision records NO_MATCH with the top score 0.11 and the policy version v3, and no human resolution task is created
2. **Given** the claim set includes an SSN
   **When** the proposal reaches the governance step
   **Then** a compliance approval requirement is raised and the intent cannot commit until Dana Whitfield's decision is bound to the proposal digest
3. **Given** the Person has been committed
   **When** the intent reaches its terminal
   **Then** RequestState is COMPLETED, BusinessState is ACTIVE, and ObligationState is PENDING with the identity-proof retention obligation named
4. **Given** the caller requests the result
   **When** the response is rendered
   **Then** the SSN is not echoed back in any field, and the claim is represented by its class and its last four digits only

**Data read**

- `people`: Person index and existing identity claims
- `privacy`: processing purpose HIRE_ONBOARDING and the classification floor for government identifiers

**Data written**

- `people`: Person aggregate P-88213 and three identity-claim revisions
- `privacy`: processing activity entry naming HIRE_ONBOARDING and the claim classes used

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The HR business partner sees the match score and the policy version.
- The full SSN is never returned in any response; requesting it returns DENIED by name.
- A recruiter with no HIRE_ONBOARDING purpose gets WITHHELD for this Person until the employment exists.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the same claim set is submitted twice within the idempotency window -> REJECT on the idempotency scope, returning the first intent id
- HIRE_ONBOARDING is not active for the tenant -> FAIL_CLOSED with a typed purpose refusal that does not echo the identifier

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Canonical request digest over the normalized claim set.
- Match evidence: candidate count, top score 0.11, policy version v3.
- Ledger fact for the Person creation with effective-at 2026-02-09 and known-at 2026-02-09T14:22:11Z.

## US-0002. Identity match lands inside the uncertainty band and waits for a human

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PEO-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Create a person from verified identity claims
- **Dimension**: `PEO` People and core HR
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, California
- **Tags**: `people`, `identity`, `uncertainty`, `human-in-the-loop`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Candidate Marcus Bell, claimed date of birth 1988-07-22.
- An existing Person P-40127 has the same surname, a date of birth of 1988-07-02, and an address two blocks away.
- Match policy v3, uncertainty band 0.62 to 0.88. The computed score is 0.74.
- The human resolution task has a 48-hour deadline.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the system to stop and ask rather than guess when two records might be the same person, so that a wrong merge does not propagate into payroll and benefits

**Scenario**

The match returns 0.74, inside the band, so the route is POSSIBLE_MATCH and a human resolution task is created for the HR business partner with a 48-hour deadline. The task shows which claims were compared and their individual similarities: surname exact, date of birth two days apart, address within 0.4 miles. It does not show P-40127's SSN, because the resolver's purpose does not extend to it. The resolver has 48 hours; nobody acts. At the deadline the intent moves to BLOCKED with ObligationState PENDING and escalates to the HR operations queue. It does not auto-create and it does not auto-merge.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the computed match score is 0.74 and the band is 0.62 to 0.88
   **When** the create_person intent is evaluated
   **Then** the route is POSSIBLE_MATCH, a human resolution task is created, and no Person is committed
2. **Given** the resolver opens the task
   **When** the comparison is displayed
   **Then** the compared claims and their per-claim similarities are shown, and P-40127's SSN is DENIED by name rather than omitted
3. **Given** 48 hours pass with no resolver decision
   **When** the task deadline fires
   **Then** the intent is BLOCKED with ObligationState PENDING and the escalation is recorded; no Person is created and no merge occurs
4. **Given** the resolver decides that the two are different people
   **When** the decision is recorded
   **Then** the new Person is created and the decision, with its comparison basis, is retained as match evidence

**Data read**

- `identity`: match policy v3 and its uncertainty band
- `people`: Person index, P-40127's comparable claims, prior match decisions

**Data written**

- `people`: human resolution task; a Person only after the resolver decides

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The resolver's view of P-40127 is PARTIAL: the compared claims are disclosed and its government identifiers are DENIED by name.
- A recruiter querying the candidate gets no signal that a possible match exists.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the resolver attempts to auto-approve a merge from inside this task -> REJECT; a merge is WF-PEO-002 with its own dual approval, and this task can only decide same or different
- the deadline passes with no decision -> BLOCKED with ObligationState PENDING; the system never auto-merges above the uncertainty threshold

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Match evidence including the score, the band, the policy version and the compared claim list.
- Task creation, deadline and escalation records.
- The absence of any Person write before the resolver decides, provable from the ledger.

## US-0003. Merge two person records with conflicting year-to-date payroll

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PEO-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Merge two person records that describe the same human
- **Dimension**: `PEO` People and core HR
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, New York
- **Tags**: `people`, `identity`, `merge`, `payroll-interaction`, `separation-of-duties`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Person P-11004 and Person P-33871 are the same human, Elena Sokolova.
- P-11004 has 2026 year-to-date gross of 42,300.00 USD under EIN A; P-33871 has 9,150.00 USD under EIN B.
- Neither Person is under a legal hold. A separation plan template exists.
- Two independent HR business partners are available as approvers; the proposer is Ruth Kelmann.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want to merge two duplicate person records without corrupting payroll history, so that the worker has one record and the tax filings still reconcile

**Scenario**

Ruth builds the merge with P-11004 as survivor. The reference enumeration finds 41 referencing aggregates across nine domains. The simulation reports a disagreement set that includes the year-to-date accumulators, which routes the proposal to dual review. Both accumulators are real: they belong to two different EINs and two Forms W-2 have already been issued. The workflow refuses to commit. The correct sequence is to merge the Person identity while leaving the two payroll enrollments intact and separately reportable, or to run a payroll correction intent first. Ruth chooses the first: the proposal is replanned to exclude the payroll accumulator streams, both approvers approve the narrowed digest, and the merge commits with redirects for the 39 non-payroll references.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the two persons have conflicting year-to-date accumulators under two EINs
   **When** the merge proposal is simulated
   **Then** the disagreement set names the accumulators and the proposal is REJECTED before commit rather than netting them
2. **Given** Ruth is the proposer
   **When** the approval requirement resolves
   **Then** Ruth is excluded from the approver set and two other HR business partners are required, each bound to the same proposal digest
3. **Given** the proposal is narrowed to exclude the payroll streams
   **When** both approvals are recorded and the merge commits
   **Then** 39 references are redirected, each with its own ledger fact, and neither payroll enrollment is deleted or merged
4. **Given** a downstream domain rejects its redirect after the local commit
   **When** reconciliation runs
   **Then** ConsistencyState is DRIFT and a bounded repair plan names exactly the references that did not move; the merge is not rolled back
5. **Given** a legal hold is applied to P-33871 while the proposal waits for the second approval
   **When** the second approver decides
   **Then** the decision is invalidated and the intent routes to REPLAN_AND_REAPPROVE, because the retention constraint changed after the first approver saw the proposal

**Data read**

- `benefits`: enrollments and dependants on both records
- `identity`: entitlements derived from either record
- `payroll`: year-to-date accumulators and issued statements on both records
- `people`: both Person aggregates, all identity claims, both worker and employment chains

**Data written**

- `dataops`: reference-repair batch covering the 39 redirected references
- `people`: survivor P-11004, redirect records for P-33871, merge decision record

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Both approvers see the full disagreement set including the accumulator figures.
- A manager of Elena sees only the survivor record after commit and cannot enumerate the loser's history.
- The pre-merge snapshot is retained under the records schedule and is DENIED to anyone without a records purpose.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- an approver is also the proposer -> FAIL_CLOSED on separation of duties
- the merge is attempted with the payroll streams included -> REJECT before commit with the conflicting accumulators named
- the loser Person is deleted rather than redirected -> REJECT; the merge has no delete path

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Full pre-merge snapshot of both records sufficient to reconstruct them.
- Two approval certificates bound to the identical narrowed proposal digest.
- One ledger fact per redirected reference, not a single aggregate fact.
- The exclusion decision recording why the payroll streams were not merged.

## US-0005. A manager change is proposed by someone with a stale delegation

- **Persona**: manager

- **Dimension**: `PEO` People and core HR
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, Illinois
- **Tags**: `people`, `authorization`, `delegation`, `refusal`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-100882 Tanya Osei.
- Manager Greg Ivory delegated his authority to Nina Hart for 2026-05-01 to 2026-05-14 while on leave.
- Nina submits a manager change on 2026-05-18, four days after the delegation expired.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want the system to refuse an action taken under an expired delegation, so that authority cannot be exercised after it has lapsed

**Scenario**

Nina opens the change on 2026-05-18 and the surface still shows it as available, because the delegation record has not been swept. The capability resolves the delegation chain at request time rather than at session time and finds that the delegation interval ended on 2026-05-14. The intent is refused with a typed unauthorized-initiator refusal naming stale delegation. The refusal does not disclose Tanya's current manager or any other fact about her; Nina learns only that she is not authorized. The attempt is recorded against Nina and against the expired delegation, so a pattern of post-expiry use is visible.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** Nina's delegation interval ended 2026-05-14
   **When** Nina submits a manager change on 2026-05-18
   **Then** the intent is REJECTED with a typed unauthorized-initiator refusal citing stale delegation, and no proposal is created
2. **Given** the refusal is rendered
   **When** Nina reads it
   **Then** the message names the delegation expiry and Nina's own authority, and discloses nothing about Tanya's record
3. **Given** the attempt is refused
   **When** the ledger is inspected
   **Then** the attempt is recorded with the delegation reference and its expiry, so repeated post-expiry attempts are detectable
4. **Given** Greg returns and submits the same change himself
   **When** the authority is resolved
   **Then** the intent proceeds normally, proving the refusal was about authority rather than about the change

**Data read**

- `organization`: Greg's own authority scope
- `people`: Tanya's worker record, resolved only after authority passes
- `security`: the delegation record and its interval

**Data written**

- `security`: the refused attempt, recorded

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Nina's read of Tanya is WITHHELD in its entirety; the refusal does not distinguish an unauthorized subject from a nonexistent one.
- Nina sees only that she lacks authority; the target's manager and organization are not disclosed in the refusal.
- Greg is notified that an attempt was made under his expired delegation.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- Nina retries immediately -> the same typed refusal, with no additional disclosure and no rate-limit bypass
- Nina asks for the delegation to be extended retroactively to cover 2026-05-18 -> REJECT; a delegation is effective-dated and a retroactive extension to legitimize a past act is refused

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The refused attempt with its typed code and the delegation reference.
- No proposal, no read of Tanya's protected fields, provable from the absence of a data access manifest evaluation.

## US-0006. Correct a misrecorded hire date after two pay periods

- **Persona**: payroll administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PEO-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Correct an employment fact retroactively

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-101533 Sam Ortega, employment start recorded as 2026-01-19.
- The true start date was 2026-01-12; Sam worked and was not paid for that week.
- Two semi-monthly periods have closed and been paid. Form 941 for Q1 has not yet been filed.
- Field owner for employment start is the People domain; the proposer is Marta Quinn.

**Story**

> As a payroll administrator, I want to correct a wrong hire date and have every downstream figure follow, so that the worker is paid for the week they worked and the quarterly return is right the first time

**Scenario**

Marta reconstructs the employment state as known at 2026-01-19 and as known now. The corrective revision appends effective-at 2026-01-12 with known-at 2026-03-02, superseding rather than overwriting. The simulation reports the downstream impact: 40 unpaid hours at 34.00 USD, a 1,360.00 USD gross retro, a benefits eligibility date that moves one week earlier, and one week of additional PTO accrual. Because a closed pay period is affected, a payroll retro child intent is bound. Marta and the payroll administrator both approve; separation of duties is satisfied because Marta is not the payroll approver. The correction and the retro commit together. The provider accepts the retro. Q1's Form 941 has not yet been filed, so no amended return is needed, but the obligation record notes that the figures moved after two statements were issued and California Labor Code 226 wage statements for those periods are now inaccurate.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the true start date is 2026-01-12 and the recorded date is 2026-01-19
   **When** the correction is committed
   **Then** a new revision appends with effective-at 2026-01-12 and known-at 2026-03-02, and the original revision remains retrievable
2. **Given** the corrected interval overlaps two closed pay periods
   **When** the simulation runs
   **Then** a payroll retro child intent is bound to the parent with 1,360.00 USD gross and its period attribution, and the parent cannot commit without it
3. **Given** Marta proposed the correction
   **When** the approval requirement resolves
   **Then** Marta is excluded from the payroll approval and a separate payroll administrator must approve
4. **Given** the retro is dispatched and the provider accepts
   **When** the intent reaches its terminal
   **Then** BusinessState is CLOSED while ObligationState stays PENDING for the wage statement correction obligation
5. **Given** a projection rebuild is running over January when the correction is submitted
   **When** the commit is attempted
   **Then** REPLAN until the rebuild watermark passes, so the corrected fact is not overwritten by a stale replay

**Data read**

- `benefits`: eligibility date derived from the start date
- `leave`: accrual computed from the start date
- `payroll`: the two closed periods, their accumulators and their issued statements
- `people`: employment revision chain with both time axes
- `regulatory`: Q1 filing state

**Data written**

- `benefits`: eligibility recalculation
- `leave`: accrual adjustment
- `payroll`: retro adjustment of 1,360.00 USD attributed to the 2026-01-12 to 2026-01-19 interval
- `people`: corrective employment revision superseding the erroneous one

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Sam sees the corrected start date and the retro line on the next statement.
- Sam's manager sees the corrected start date but the retro amount is DENIED to them.
- An auditor can retrieve both the original and the corrected view.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the correction would change a figure on an already-filed return -> the local commit proceeds and ObligationState stays PENDING until the amended filing is submitted
- only one approver is available and it is the proposer -> FAIL_CLOSED on separation of duties
- the retro is applied to the wrong period by the provider -> ConsistencyState DRIFT and a bounded repair scoped to the affected period

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Bitemporal trace showing both the original and the corrected view.
- Explanation artifact naming each downstream figure that moved and by how much.
- Obligation record for the inaccurate wage statements with its California basis.

## US-0008. Convert a long-tenured contractor in California and fail the ABC test

- **Persona**: hiring manager

- **Dimension**: `PEO` People and core HR
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, California
- **Tags**: `people`, `classification`, `california`, `contingent`, `reservation-expiry`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme, Acme US Inc. Contingent worker C-7741 Devika Nair, engaged 19 months through Northgate Staffing.
- Devika performs the same work as three Acme employees on the same team, in California.
- A funded position ENG-224 exists. The vendor contract has a conversion fee of 18,000 USD and a no-hire clause expiring 2026-08-30.
- Conversion date proposed as 2026-07-01.

**Story**

> As a hiring manager, I want to convert a contractor into an employee cleanly, so that the work continues without a classification or contract problem

**Scenario**

The hiring manager proposes the conversion for 2026-07-01. The classification determination runs against California's ABC test and returns EMPLOYEE, failing prong B because Devika performs work inside Acme's usual course of business. That is the correct result for a conversion and does not block it, but it also establishes that the prior 19 months were probably misclassified, which the determination records as a separate compliance finding rather than silently ignoring. The no-hire clause has not expired, so the workflow blocks pending legal review rather than treating the clause as a warning. Legal negotiates a waiver against the 18,000 USD fee. The position and budget reservations are taken, five approvals are collected, and the conversion is held to 2026-07-01. At the checkpoint the budget reservation is found to have expired on 2026-06-28, so the proposal is replanned with a fresh reservation and reapproved before the exit and start effects commit as one bound graph.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** Devika performs work within Acme's usual course of business in California
   **When** the classification determination runs
   **Then** the ABC test returns EMPLOYEE with prong B named as the failing prong, and the determination cites the rule pack version
2. **Given** the determination establishes probable prior misclassification
   **When** the conversion proceeds
   **Then** a separate compliance finding is recorded for the prior 19 months with its own owner, and it is not closed by the conversion
3. **Given** the vendor no-hire clause expires 2026-08-30 and the conversion date is 2026-07-01
   **When** the proposal reaches governance
   **Then** the intent is BLOCKED pending legal review with the clause named, rather than proceeding with a warning
4. **Given** the budget reservation expires on 2026-06-28
   **When** the 2026-07-01 checkpoint runs
   **Then** REPLAN with a fresh reservation and reapproval; the lapsed reservation is never silently renewed
5. **Given** the conversion commits
   **When** the engagement end and the employment start are compared
   **Then** they form one contiguous interval with no gap and no overlap, and both effects are bound in one effect graph

**Data read**

- `billing`: the Northgate contract terms
- `budget`: available compensation budget in the destination cost centre
- `people`: contingent engagement, tenure, vendor relationship
- `position`: ENG-224 capacity and reservation state
- `regulatory`: California AB 5 rule pack and its version

**Data written**

- `benefits`: eligibility evaluation triggered by the new employment
- `compensation`: initial package
- `people`: engagement end 2026-06-30, new Employment and Assignment from 2026-07-01
- `regulatory`: misclassification compliance finding

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Devika's disclosure of her own conversion terms is FULL; the classification finding about her prior engagement is DENIED to her and to the hiring manager.
- The hiring manager sees the classification result and the blocking clause, not the negotiated fee terms.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the determination returns CONTRACTOR for the destination jurisdiction -> FAIL_CLOSED; a conversion on an inconsistent determination is refused and the finding is recorded
- the no-hire clause has not been waived -> BLOCKED pending legal review

## US-0098. Approve a queued proposal that changed while it waited

- **Persona**: manager
- **Workflow**: [`WF-MSS-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Act on a pending approval from the manager queue
- **Dimension**: `MSS` Manager self-service
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `manager-self-service`, `approval`, `stale-proposal`, `digest-binding`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A pay change proposal for W-123770 sat in the manager's queue from 2026-04-02.
- On 2026-04-05 the proposer amended the amount from 94,000 to 96,500 USD.
- The manager opens the item on 2026-04-07.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want the queue to show me the current proposal rather than the one that arrived, so that I approve what is actually going to happen

**Scenario**

The queue item was created against the original proposal digest. When the manager opens it on 2026-04-07 the item is refreshed against the current proposal, and the changed amount is highlighted with its previous value. Approving binds the current digest. Had the item served the stale summary, the manager would have approved 94,000 while 96,500 committed.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the proposal changed after the item was queued
   **When** the manager opens the item
   **Then** it is refreshed against the current proposal and the changed field is shown with its previous value
2. **Given** the manager approves
   **When** the decision is bound
   **Then** it binds the current proposal digest and RequestState moves to APPROVED against that digest, so a subsequent change returns it to PENDING_APPROVAL rather than leaving an approval standing
3. **Given** the manager's authority lapsed since the item was queued
   **When** the decision is attempted
   **Then** authority is revalidated at decision time and the decision is refused with the reason; the queue is not authority

**Data read**

- `people`: the manager's authority and delegations
- `workflow`: pending items, their proposals and digests

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the approval decision with its authority snapshot

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The manager receives FULL disclosure of the exact proposal content, its digest and its amendment history.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the queue serves a cached summary -> REJECT that design; approving a stale summary is the failure this refresh prevents
- the manager bulk-approves twenty items -> permitted where the policy allows, and recorded as a bulk action so its scrutiny level is visible

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The digest shown to the approver, matching what they decided on.
- Authority revalidation at decision time.
- The amendment history.

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Manager Ines Duarte may initiate pay changes but the tenant hides absolute pay from managers below director level.
- She proposes a 4 percent increase for W-124000 effective 2026-08-01.
- The band and the increase percentage are visible to her; the resulting absolute figure is not.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want to propose a raise as a percentage when I may not see the absolute salary, so that I can do my job without the tenant having to widen my access

**Scenario**

The manager enters a 4 percent increase. The simulation returns the band position and the approval chain, but the resulting absolute figure is DENIED to her by name so she knows it exists and that she cannot see it. The proposal is submitted with the delta as the material input. Approvers above her who may see absolute pay see the full figures. The tenant's choice to hide absolute pay is a policy decision the surface supports rather than works around.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the manager may not see absolute pay
   **When** she proposes a percentage increase
   **Then** the proposal is expressed as a delta and the resulting absolute figure is DENIED to her by name
2. **Given** the simulation runs
   **When** the results are displayed
   **Then** band position and the approval chain are shown, and the denied figure is named rather than omitted
3. **Given** an approver who may see absolute pay opens the proposal
   **When** it renders for them
   **Then** the full figures are shown, so the same proposal discloses differently to different readers

- `compensation`: band and budget, subject to the manager's authorization
- `compliance`: the tenant's pay visibility policy
- `people`: the report's current state

**Data written**

- The manager's disclosure is PARTIAL: the delta, the band position and the approval chain are disclosed and the resulting absolute figure is DENIED by name.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the manager infers the absolute figure from the band position and the percentage -> a real limitation of hiding pay while showing band position; the tenant's policy is what it is and the system implements it rather than pretending it is airtight
- the simulation reports a band breach -> submission is blocked pending the exception path, so the manager learns before the approvers do

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Simulation output as the manager saw it, including the denied field.
- The approval chain as displayed.
- The manager's authorization to initiate this change type.

## US-0100. Plan coverage when a return date is genuinely unknown

- **Persona**: manager
- **Workflow**: [`WF-MSS-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Plan team coverage around absences without seeing their reasons
- **Dimension**: `MSS` Manager self-service
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `manager-self-service`, `unknown-return`, `no-estimation`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A report has been on medical leave since 2026-03-02 with no expected return date.
- The certification is open-ended pending further evaluation.
- The manager needs to decide whether to backfill.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want an unknown return date to be shown as unknown rather than estimated, so that I make a coverage decision on the truth instead of a fabricated date

**Scenario**

The team view shows the absence start and an expected return of UNKNOWN. It does not estimate, average or default to the entitlement exhaustion date, any of which would look like information and would be wrong. The manager can see the leave is open-ended and route a backfill request through the ordinary temporary staffing path, which does not require knowing the reason.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the certification has no expected return date
   **When** the team view renders
   **Then** the return date shows as UNKNOWN rather than being estimated from the entitlement or from an average
2. **Given** the manager requests more detail
   **When** the request is evaluated
   **Then** DENIED; the reason and the medical basis stay with HR and the manager gets dates only
3. **Given** the manager requests a temporary backfill
   **When** the request routes
   **Then** it proceeds through the ordinary staffing path without requiring the absence reason

**Data read**

- `leave`: absence interval with no expected return
- `privacy`: absence reason classification
- `time`: schedule and coverage requirements

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The manager's disclosure is PARTIAL: the start date and the explicit UNKNOWN return are disclosed and the reason is DENIED.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the return date is defaulted to the entitlement exhaustion date -> REJECT; it looks like information, it is not, and a manager will plan around it
- the manager contacts the worker to ask when they will return -> outside the system, but the surface should not create the pressure by presenting an obvious gap with no explanation

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Per-absence disclosure result with the reason denied.
- The explicit UNKNOWN rather than an estimate.
- Zero-effect receipt.
