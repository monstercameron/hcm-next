## US-0901. Handle a whistleblower report that names the compliance officer

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-ERL-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Receive a concern report and open an investigation
- **Dimension**: `ERL` Employee relations and investigations
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `employee-relations`, `whistleblower`, `subject-is-the-function`, `alternative-channel`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A report received 2026-11-09 names the compliance officer as the subject.
- The compliance function owns the intake process.
- The report arrives through the standard hotline.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the intake to route around the named subject without depending on their own restraint, so that the process does not require the person it is about to recuse themselves

**Scenario**

The standard intake routes to a function the report is about. Depending on the officer to recuse themselves works when they are honest and fails exactly when it matters. The intake evaluates the named subjects against the routing target's membership before delivery and, where they overlap, routes to the pre-declared alternative channel, which for this class is the audit committee. The officer is not notified that a report naming them exists, and the routing decision is recorded in the alternative channel rather than in the compliance function's own records.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a report names the routing target's own function
   **When** the intake runs
   **Then** it routes to the pre-declared alternative channel before delivery
2. **Given** the alternative channel is pre-declared
   **When** the routing runs
   **Then** it is used as declared rather than being chosen at the moment by whoever notices
3. **Given** the named subject holds administrative access to the case system
   **When** the compartment is created
   **Then** their access to it is excluded and the exclusion is enforced by the compartment rather than by policy
4. **Given** the routing decision is recorded
   **When** the record is written
   **Then** it lives in the alternative channel's own records rather than in the function the report is about
5. **Given** no alternative channel is declared
   **When** the intake runs
   **Then** the absence is a blocking finding and the report is held rather than being delivered to its own subject

**Data read**

- `case`: the report, its named subjects and the routing rules
- `identity`: the named subject's administrative access
- `people`: the compliance function's membership and the alternative channel

**Data written**

- `case`: the report in the alternative channel's compartment and the recorded routing

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The alternative channel receives FULL disclosure of the report and the routing decision.
- The named subject is WITHHELD from the existence of the report entirely, including from any administrative view, queue count or notification they would ordinarily receive.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the intake depends on the subject's own recusal -> the process works when they are honest and fails exactly when it matters
- the routing is recorded in the compliance function's records -> the subject can read the record of a report about themselves

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The named subject evaluation before delivery.
- The pre-declared alternative channel and the routing.
- The enforced access exclusion and the record's location.

## US-0902. A principal replays an approval by resubmitting an unchanged proposal

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Resolve an approval requirement and bind the decision
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `adversarial`, `proposal-identity`, `replay`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A proposal was approved and committed on 2026-06-01.
- On 2026-06-08 the same proposal payload is submitted again unchanged.
- Its digest is identical and the approval certificate verifies.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the second submission to be recognised as a resubmission of an executed proposal rather than a new one, so that an approved change is not applied twice by resubmitting it

**Scenario**

The payload is identical, so its digest is identical and the approval that bound that digest verifies against it. A system that treats digest plus valid approval as sufficient will execute the change a second time. The proposal identity is what carries execution state: the digest identifies what was approved and the proposal identifies whether it ran. The resubmission resolves to the executed proposal, the second execution is refused, and the caller receives the original outcome so a genuine retry after a lost response also works.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an identical payload is resubmitted
   **When** it is evaluated
   **Then** it resolves to the existing proposal rather than creating a new one
2. **Given** the existing proposal has executed
   **When** the second execution is attempted
   **Then** the second execution is refused because the proposal's ExecutionState is already COMPLETED, and the original outcome is returned
3. **Given** the caller lost the original response
   **When** the resubmission is served
   **Then** it receives the original outcome, so a genuine retry works and a replay does not execute
4. **Given** the payload differs in a non-material field
   **When** the digest is computed
   **Then** it is identical under the canonicalization profile and the resubmission resolves to the same proposal
5. **Given** the payload differs in a material field
   **When** the digest is computed
   **Then** it differs, a new proposal is created and the earlier approval does not bind it

**Data read**

- `compliance`: the canonicalization profile and the approval binding
- `workflow`: the proposal, its digest and its execution state

**Data written**

- `security`: the refused second execution and the recorded resubmission

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The caller receives FULL disclosure of the original outcome.
- Whether the submission was recognised as a replay is WITHHELD from the caller, whose response is identical to a first-time success.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- digest plus valid approval is treated as sufficient -> the change executes a second time and the approval was only ever for one
- the resubmission returns an error -> a genuine retry after a lost response is indistinguishable from a replay and legitimate callers break

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The proposal identity and its execution state.
- The digest resolution across both submissions.
- The refused second execution and the returned original outcome.

## US-0903. An agent is asked to summarise a case it may read into a channel it may write

- **Persona**: AI agent
- **Workflow**: [`WF-AIA-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Let an agent propose a change a human then approves
- **Dimension**: `AIA` Semantic and agent intelligence
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `agent`, `adversarial`, `capability-composition`, `audience-boundary`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. An agent holds read access to a case compartment and write access to a team channel.
- A compartment member asks it to post a summary of the case to the channel.
- The channel's membership is wider than the compartment's.

**Story**

> As an AI agent, I want the agent to refuse the transfer rather than treating two grants as permission to move data between them, so that holding two capabilities is not a licence to bridge them

**Scenario**

The agent may read the compartment and may write the channel, and doing both in sequence moves compartmented content to a wider audience. Two grants do not compose into a third. The agent evaluates the destination's audience against the source's compartment, refuses the transfer with the audience difference named, and offers to post to a destination whose audience is within the compartment. The request is recorded and surfaced to the case owner, because a member asking for a summary to be posted wider is worth knowing about whether or not they meant it.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a read grant and a write grant would move data across a boundary
   **When** the action is evaluated
   **Then** it is refused and no posting RequestState is created, because two grants do not compose into permission to bridge them
2. **Given** the refusal is issued
   **When** it renders
   **Then** it names the audience difference and offers a destination within the compartment
3. **Given** the requester is a compartment member
   **When** the refusal is issued
   **Then** their own membership does not authorize the destination's audience, and the refusal says so
4. **Given** the request is recorded
   **When** the case owner is notified
   **Then** they are told a member asked for compartment content to be posted to a wider audience
5. **Given** the destination's audience is within the compartment
   **When** the action is evaluated
   **Then** it proceeds, because the boundary is the audience rather than the channel

**Data read**

- `agent`: the agent's grants and the requested action
- `case`: the compartment and its membership
- `experience`: the channel and its audience

**Data written**

- `agent`: the refusal and the recorded request
- `case`: the notification to the case owner

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The requester receives FULL disclosure of the refusal and the audience difference.
- The case content is WITHHELD from the channel absolutely, and the notification to the case owner is WITHHELD from the requester.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- two grants are treated as composing -> compartmented content reaches a wider audience through a sequence nobody authorized
- the requester's own membership authorizes the destination -> a member can widen a compartment by choosing where to ask for a summary

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The two grants and the audience comparison.
- The refusal and its named difference.
- The recorded request and the case owner notification.

## US-0904. A tenant's own administrator requests an export of another tenant's shared benchmark

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator

- Tenant acme participates in a benchmarking programme with aggregated cross-tenant metrics.

- The programme publishes aggregates only.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the contributions to be refused even though acme's own contribution is among them, so that participating in an aggregate is not access to its inputs

**Scenario**

The administrator's own tenant contributed to the aggregate, which makes some of the underlying data theirs. The rest is not, and an export of the contributions would disclose other participants' figures to a participant. Acme's own contribution is disclosed because it is theirs; the others are refused, and the refusal does not state how many participants there are, because a small participant count would let the administrator narrow the aggregate toward individual figures.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a participant requests the aggregate's inputs
   **When** the request is evaluated
   **Then** their own contribution is disclosed and the others are refused
2. **Given** the refusal is issued
   **When** it renders
   **Then** it does not state the participant count, because a small count narrows the aggregate toward individual figures
3. **Given** the requester asks for the aggregate at a finer cut
   **When** the request is evaluated
   **Then** it is refused where the cut's participant count falls below the programme's threshold, and the query creates no RequestState because it never runs
4. **Given** the requester combines several published cuts
   **When** the pattern is evaluated
   **Then** the differencing risk across cuts is assessed and the programme's published set is constrained rather than each cut being assessed alone
5. **Given** the requester holds a platform role as well as a tenant role
   **When** the request is evaluated
   **Then** the platform role does not widen the programme's disclosure, which is governed by the programme's own terms

**Data read**

- `analytics`: the benchmark programme, its aggregates and its thresholds
- `identity`: the requester's tenant and roles
- `tenant`: acme's own contribution

**Data written**

- `analytics`: the disclosed own contribution and the refusal

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The administrator receives FULL disclosure of their own tenant's contribution.
- Other participants' contributions are WITHHELD absolutely, and the participant count and the programme's membership are WITHHELD so the aggregate cannot be narrowed.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the contributions are exported because the requester contributed to them -> a participant reads every other participant's figures
- each cut is assessed alone -> several published cuts differenced together reconstruct what no single cut disclosed

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The own-contribution disclosure.
- The refusal without the participant count.
- The differencing assessment across the published cut set.

- **Workflow**: [`WF-PRV-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Fulfil a data subject access request
- **Dimension**: `PRV` Privacy and data governance
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `privacy`, `adversarial`, `request-volume`, `right-not-refused`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-172590 makes 47 subject access requests over 2026-11.
- Each is narrowly scoped and individually valid.
- Together they cover most of the tenant's holdings about them and their team's shared records.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the pattern to be assessed without any individual request being refused for being one of many, so that a right exercised often is still a right

**Scenario**

Each request is valid and the pattern is unusual. Refusing one because there have been forty-six others would be refusing a right on the basis of its exercise. The requests are answered, the pattern is assessed separately for whether any individual request is manifestly excessive on its own terms, and the third-party content in the shared records is assessed each time rather than becoming easier to disclose through repetition. The pattern is surfaced to compliance as a fact, not as a ground for refusal.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** many requests are made in a short period
   **When** each is assessed
   **Then** it is assessed on its own terms and is not refused for being one of many
2. **Given** a request is manifestly excessive on its own terms
   **When** it is assessed
   **Then** the statutory test is applied to that request rather than to the series
3. **Given** the requests cumulatively reach third-party content
   **When** each is assessed
   **Then** the third-party balancing runs each time and does not weaken through repetition
4. **Given** the pattern is surfaced
   **When** it is recorded
   **Then** it is recorded as a fact for compliance rather than as a ground for refusing the next request
5. **Given** the requester's purpose is asked about
   **When** the question is evaluated
   **Then** purpose is not a condition of the right and asking is recorded rather than the answer conditioning the response

**Data read**

- `people`: the requester and the shared records' other subjects
- `privacy`: the requests, their scopes and the third-party assessments

**Data written**

- `privacy`: the per-request assessments and the surfaced pattern

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The requester receives FULL disclosure of their own data as each request's assessment supports.
- Third-party content is DENIED with the ground stated on each request, and the surfaced pattern is WITHHELD from the requester.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- a request is refused for being one of many -> a right is refused on the basis of its exercise
- the third-party balancing weakens through repetition -> content refused on request one is disclosed on request forty because the assessor is tired

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The per-request assessments and their grounds.
- The third-party balancing on each.
- The surfaced pattern recorded as a fact.

## US-0995. A configuration change is timed to land between a preview and an execution

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-CFG-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Validate a configuration change against live data
- **Dimension**: `CFG` Configuration, rules and policies
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `configuration`, `adversarial`, `digest-binding`, `timing-window`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. An eligibility rule is previewed at 2026-12-08T09:00 showing 40 affected workers.
- The rule's underlying decision table is edited at 09:14.
- The rule is executed at 09:20 affecting 1,140.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the execution to bind the configuration the preview evaluated rather than the current one, so that the preview and the execution describe the same rule

**Scenario**

The preview and the execution are six minutes apart and a table edit sits between them. Whatever the intent, a preview that does not bind its configuration is a preview of something else. The execution binds the configuration digest the preview evaluated, refuses when it differs, and names both digests. The edit is legitimate on its own terms and is not reversed; what is refused is executing under a configuration nobody previewed.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a configuration changes between preview and execution
   **When** the execution runs
   **Then** it is refused on the configuration digest mismatch and both digests are named
2. **Given** the preview is re-run
   **When** the new preview binds the current configuration
   **Then** the execution proceeds against it and the affected count is whatever the current configuration produces
3. **Given** the edit is examined
   **When** it is evaluated
   **Then** it is legitimate on its own terms and is not reversed; only the execution under it is refused
4. **Given** the attempt is recorded
   **When** the log is inspected
   **Then** it records both digests, the edit's actor and the execution's actor as separate facts
5. **Given** the edit and the execution share an actor
   **When** the pattern is evaluated
   **Then** it is surfaced to compliance, because the same principal previewing, editing and executing within eleven minutes is worth distinguishing from two people's timing

**Data read**

- `configuration`: the decision table, its versions and their digests
- `identity`: the editing and executing principals
- `population`: the preview result and the execution's evaluation

**Data written**

- `configuration`: the refused execution and the recorded attempt

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The executor receives FULL disclosure of the refusal and both configuration digests.
- The 1,100 additional workers the current configuration would reach are WITHHELD from the refusal, which names the count rather than the membership.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the execution binds the rule rather than the configuration -> a preview describes one thing and an execution does another
- the edit is reversed as a remediation -> a legitimate change is undone to fix an execution that should simply be refused

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both configuration digests and the preview's binding.
- The refused execution.
- The recorded attempt with both actors.

## US-0996. A departing administrator schedules a job to run after their access ends

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-EVT-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Schedule a recurring job and prove it ran
- **Dimension**: `EVT` Events, scheduling and automation
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `events`, `adversarial`, `scheduled-after-departure`, `authority-at-execution`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. An administrator's employment ends 2026-12-19.
- On 2026-12-17 they schedule a job to run 2027-01-05 under a service principal.
- The job deletes a set of audit records.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the scheduled job's authority to be evaluated at execution rather than at scheduling, so that a job does not outlive the authority that created it

**Scenario**

The job is scheduled under a service principal and its creator will be gone when it runs. A job that carries its creator's authority forward executes on behalf of somebody who no longer holds it. The execution evaluates the scheduling principal's authority at run time as well as the service principal's, the job is refused because its creator's authority has ended, and jobs scheduled by a departing principal are surfaced during offboarding rather than being discovered when they run. The job's own effect, deleting audit records, is separately a finding.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a job is scheduled by a principal whose authority later ends
   **When** the job runs
   **Then** it is refused, because the scheduling principal's authority is evaluated at execution
2. **Given** a principal departs
   **When** offboarding runs
   **Then** jobs they scheduled with future run dates are surfaced rather than being discovered when they run
3. **Given** the job's effect is examined
   **When** it is recorded
   **Then** a job deleting audit records scheduled by a departing administrator is a finding independent of whether it ran
4. **Given** a legitimate job was scheduled by a departing principal
   **When** it is surfaced
   **Then** it is reassigned to a current principal with its own authority rather than being cancelled by default
5. **Given** the service principal's own authority would permit the deletion
   **When** the execution is evaluated
   **Then** it is still refused, because the scheduling principal's authority is a separate condition rather than being substituted by the service principal's

**Data read**

- `events`: the scheduled job, its schedule and its scheduling principal
- `identity`: both principals' authority intervals
- `provenance`: the audit records the job would delete

**Data written**

- `events`: the refused execution
- `security`: the recorded finding and the surfaced jobs

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of the refusal, both principals and the job's effect.
- The departing administrator receives nothing, and whether the job was surfaced is WITHHELD from them.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the job carries its creator's authority forward -> it executes on behalf of somebody who no longer holds it
- the service principal's authority substitutes -> any departing principal can schedule anything a service principal may do

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The job, its schedule and its scheduling principal.
- Both authority intervals at execution.
- The surfaced jobs at offboarding and the recorded finding.

## US-0997. A tenant's own data export is redirected by a changed destination

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TEN-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Execute a tenant exit and hand back the data
- **Dimension**: `TEN` Tenant and platform administration
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `tenant`, `adversarial`, `destination-binding`, `export-redirection`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme requests a full data export on 2027-01-12.
- The export's delivery destination is changed at 2027-01-13T02:00 to an address outside the tenant's declared set.
- The change is made by a principal with export configuration authority.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the destination to be bound to the export's own authorization rather than being a mutable configuration, so that an approved export goes where it was approved to go

**Scenario**

The export was authorized to a declared destination and the destination is now different. If the destination is mutable configuration, an approved export becomes a delivery to wherever the configuration points at the moment it runs. The destination is bound into the export's authorization: a change invalidates it and the export is refused rather than delivered. The attempt is recorded with both destinations, and because the new one is outside the tenant's declared set it is surfaced to security rather than treated as a configuration correction.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an export's destination is changed after authorization
   **When** the export runs
   **Then** it is refused, because the destination is bound into the authorization
2. **Given** the new destination is outside the tenant's declared set
   **When** the attempt is recorded
   **Then** it is surfaced to security rather than treated as a configuration correction
3. **Given** the tenant genuinely wants a different destination
   **When** a new authorization is obtained
   **Then** the export is re-authorized to the new destination rather than the existing one being redirected
4. **Given** the export had already begun delivering
   **When** the change is attempted
   **Then** it is refused and the in-flight delivery continues to the authorized destination
5. **Given** the changing principal holds export configuration authority
   **When** the change is evaluated
   **Then** their authority does not extend to redirecting an authorized export, and the refusal says so

**Data read**

- `integration`: the delivery state
- `security`: the tenant's declared destination set and the changing principal
- `tenant`: the export, its authorization and its bound destination

**Data written**

- `security`: the refused export and the recorded attempt with both destinations

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The tenant receives FULL disclosure of the refusal, both destinations and the changing principal.
- The new destination's own identity beyond its address is WITHHELD pending the security investigation.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the destination is mutable configuration -> an approved export delivers to wherever the configuration points when it runs
- export configuration authority extends to redirecting an authorized export -> the authorization is decided by whoever configures rather than by whoever approved

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The authorization with its bound destination.
- The attempted change and both destinations.
- The refusal and the surfaced attempt.

## US-0998. Record a worker's consent to be photographed at a company event

- **Dimension**: `PRV` Privacy and data governance
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: EU, France
- **Tags**: `privacy`, `granular-consent`, `image-use`, `france`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme, Acme France SAS. Photographs are taken at an event on 2026-06-18.
- Worker W-172600 consents to internal use and not to external publication.
- The two uses have different consequences.

**Story**

> As an employee, I want the two uses to be separately consented rather than bundled, so that internal use is not the same as being on a website

**Scenario**

Bundling internal and external use into one consent means a worker who is comfortable with an intranet photograph is asked to accept a public one. The two are separate consents with separate records, the external use is checked against its own consent before any publication, and a withdrawal of the external consent is honoured by removing the photograph rather than by stopping future use alone.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** two uses have different consequences
   **When** the consent is captured
   **Then** each use is separately consented with its own record
2. **Given** an external publication is prepared
   **When** the consent is checked
   **Then** the external consent is checked specifically rather than the internal one being relied on
3. **Given** the external consent is withdrawn
   **When** the withdrawal is honoured
   **Then** the published photograph is removed rather than only future use stopping

**Data read**

- `documents`: the photographs and their publication records
- `privacy`: the consents, their uses and their scopes

**Data written**

- `privacy`: the per-use consents and any withdrawal

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of what they consented to for each use and where each is used.
- Other attendees' consent states are DENIED to the worker by name.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the two uses share one consent -> a worker comfortable with an intranet photograph is asked to accept a public one
- a withdrawal stops future use only -> the published photograph stays up and the withdrawal achieves nothing the worker wanted

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The per-use consents with their scopes.
- The publication check against the external consent.
- The removal on withdrawal.

## US-0999. Handle a request to correct a performance rating the worker disputes

- **Persona**: employee
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TAL-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Run a performance review cycle to final ratings
- **Dimension**: `TAL` Talent and performance
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: EU, Ireland
- **Tags**: `talent`, `rectification`, `fact-versus-opinion`, `ireland`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme, Acme Ireland Ltd. Worker W-172610 disputes their 2026 rating on 2027-02-16.
- They assert it is inaccurate rather than merely unfair.
- The rating is an opinion informed by recorded facts.

**Story**

> As an employee, I want the accuracy of the underlying facts to be assessed separately from the opinion drawn from them, so that a wrong fact is corrected even where an opinion stands

**Scenario**

A rating is an opinion and the facts it rests on are not. A rectification right reaches inaccurate facts and does not convert an opinion into one the subject prefers. The underlying facts are assessed for accuracy and corrected where wrong, the rating is then reconsidered in the light of any correction, and where the facts stand the opinion stands with the worker's own statement of disagreement recorded alongside it.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a rating is disputed as inaccurate
   **When** the assessment runs
   **Then** the underlying facts are assessed for accuracy separately from the opinion
2. **Given** an underlying fact is wrong
   **When** it is corrected
   **Then** the rating is reconsidered in the light of the correction rather than standing on a corrected fact, and the reconsidered rating is a new revision rather than an edit of the original
3. **Given** the facts stand
   **When** the outcome is recorded
   **Then** the opinion stands and the worker's statement of disagreement is recorded alongside it
4. **Given** the worker asks for the rating to be deleted
   **When** the request is assessed
   **Then** it is assessed against the retention basis rather than being treated as a rectification

**Data read**

- `privacy`: the rectification request and its assessment
- `talent`: the rating, its recorded basis and the underlying facts

**Data written**

- `talent`: any corrected facts, the reconsidered rating and the recorded disagreement

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the underlying facts, the assessment and the outcome.
- The calibration discussion comparing them to colleagues is DENIED to them by name, with the third-party ground stated.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the rating is treated as a fact -> a rectification right is used to change an opinion, which it does not reach
- the facts are not assessed -> a wrong fact stands because the opinion it informs is not rectifiable

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The underlying facts and their accuracy assessment.
- Any correction and the reconsidered rating.
- The recorded statement of disagreement.

## US-1000. Close a tenant's account with obligations that outlive the relationship

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TEN-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Execute a tenant exit and hand back the data
- **Dimension**: `TEN` Tenant and platform administration
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `tenant`, `exit`, `surviving-obligations`, `retention-reconciliation`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme's contract ends 2027-06-30 after five years.
- Fourteen obligations remain open, including two statutory record retentions running to 2030.
- The tenant's data is scheduled for deletion 90 days after exit.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the obligations that outlive the contract to be identified and assigned before the account closes, so that an obligation running to 2030 has an owner after the relationship ends

**Scenario**

The contract ends and fourteen obligations do not. Two of them run for three more years and belong to the tenant rather than to the platform, and two belong to the platform under its own processor obligations. Closing the account without assigning them leaves obligations with no owner and, in two cases, records scheduled for deletion that a retention requires. Each obligation is identified, its owner after the exit is determined, the deletion schedule is reconciled against the retentions, and the exit does not complete until every one of the fourteen has an owner and a route.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** obligations outlive the contract
   **When** the exit is planned
   **Then** each is identified with its own ObligationState and its post-exit owner is determined before the account closes
2. **Given** an obligation requires retention past the deletion date
   **When** the schedules are reconciled
   **Then** the deletion is deferred for the covered records and the deferral is recorded with the obligation as its basis
3. **Given** an obligation belongs to the platform as processor
   **When** the assignment is made
   **Then** it stays with the platform and its discharge is arranged independently of the tenant's account
4. **Given** an obligation belongs to the tenant
   **When** the assignment is made
   **Then** it transfers with the exported data and the tenant is told what they now own
5. **Given** an obligation has no determinable owner
   **When** the exit is evaluated
   **Then** it does not complete, because an obligation with no owner after the exit is the outcome the process exists to prevent

**Data read**

- `documents`: the records the retentions cover
- `operations`: the fourteen open obligations and their bases
- `tenant`: the exit, the contract end and the deletion schedule

**Data written**

- `operations`: the assigned obligations and the deferred deletions
- `tenant`: the exit plan with its obligation assignments

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The exiting customer receives FULL disclosure of every obligation, its owner after the exit and any deferred deletion.
- The platform's own processor obligations are disclosed to the customer as facts about their data, and the platform's internal arrangements for discharging them are WITHHELD.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the account closes with obligations unassigned -> fourteen obligations have no owner and two of them run for three more years
- the deletion runs on schedule -> records a retention requires are destroyed because a contract ended

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The fourteen obligations with their bases and deadlines.
- The post-exit owner determined for each.
- The deletion schedule reconciled against the retentions.
