## US-0101. Request leave for my own serious health condition

- **Persona**: employee
- **Workflow**: [`WF-LVE-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Request, certify and start a medical leave
- **Dimension**: `LVE` Leave and absence
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, Texas
- **Tags**: `leave`, `fmla`, `certification`, `texas`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-124330 has 3 years of service and 1,940 hours in the prior 12 months.
- The worksite has 210 employees within 75 miles.
- Leave requested 2026-06-15 to 2026-07-27 for surgery and recovery.

**Story**

> As an employee, I want to request medical leave by category without disclosing my diagnosis, so that my manager learns my dates and not my condition

**Scenario**

The request is submitted with a reason category rather than a diagnosis. Eligibility passes on service, hours and worksite size. The eligibility and rights notice issues within five business days and the certification request carries a fifteen-calendar-day deadline. The manager is told the dates and the expected return only.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** service, hours and worksite tests all pass
   **When** eligibility is evaluated
   **Then** the worker is eligible and the notice issues within five business days with its date recorded
2. **Given** the certification is requested
   **When** the deadline is computed
   **Then** it is fifteen calendar days from the request, the deadline is recorded rather than assumed, and ObligationState is PENDING until the certification is received
3. **Given** the manager opens the absence
   **When** the record renders
   **Then** dates and expected return are visible and the reason category is DENIED

**Data read**

- `leave`: programmes, entitlements, prior usage in the leave year
- `people`: tenure, hours of service, worksite headcount within 75 miles

**Data written**

- `leave`: leave case and its entitlement consumption

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker's disclosure of their own case is FULL; the manager's is PARTIAL, with the reason category DENIED and the certification WITHHELD.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the certification is incomplete -> a cure notice issues with its statutory period; a denial without a cure period is itself a violation
- the worker is ineligible federally but eligible under a state programme -> the leave proceeds under the state programme and the notice states which applies

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Eligibility and rights notice with its issue date.
- Certification deadline computation.
- The compartment access log.

## US-0102. A leave certification is incomplete and a cure period opens

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-LVE-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Request, certify and start a medical leave
- **Dimension**: `LVE` Leave and absence
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, Illinois
- **Tags**: `leave`, `certification`, `cure-period`, `illinois`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Certification for W-124660 returned 2026-04-20 with the frequency and duration fields blank.
- The plan requires a seven-calendar-day cure period.
- The requested leave starts 2026-05-04.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want an incomplete certification to trigger a cure notice rather than a denial, so that the worker gets a chance to fix it before anything is refused

**Scenario**

The certification arrives missing the frequency and duration fields, which are exactly the fields an intermittent leave depends on. The workflow issues a cure notice naming the specific deficient fields and giving seven calendar days. It does not deny. The corrected certification arrives on 2026-04-27 and the leave is approved. Had the cure notice been skipped, the denial would have been an interference claim regardless of the certification's state.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** specific fields are blank
   **When** the certification is reviewed
   **Then** a cure notice issues naming those exact fields, not a general request for a better certification
2. **Given** the cure period is seven calendar days
   **When** the deadline is set
   **Then** it runs from the notice and the notice date is recorded
3. **Given** the corrected certification arrives inside the period
   **When** the leave is approved
   **Then** the approval cites the corrected certification and the original remains in the compartment
4. **Given** the cure period lapses with nothing received
   **When** the decision is made
   **Then** the leave may be denied, and the denial cites the cure notice and its lapse rather than the original deficiency alone

**Data read**

- `documents`: both certifications in a protected compartment
- `leave`: the certification, its deficiencies, the cure policy

**Data written**

- `leave`: the cure notice, the corrected certification and the approval

**Authorization and disclosure**

- HR receives FULL disclosure of both certifications; the manager's view of either is WITHHELD.
- The worker sees the cure notice and which fields were deficient.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the leave is denied without a cure notice -> an interference claim; the cure period exists precisely for this
- HR contacts the healthcare provider directly -> restricted; direct contact has strict conditions and the worker must generally be given the chance to cure first

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The cure notice with its date, its named deficiencies and its deadline.
- Both certifications in the compartment with access logged.
- The approval citing the corrected certification.

## US-0103. Return to work with a lifting restriction

- **Dimension**: `LVE` Leave and absence

- **Tags**: `leave`, `accommodation`, `interactive-process`, `california`

- Tenant acme. Worker W-124990 returns 2026-08-03 with a 15-pound lifting restriction for 90 days.
- The role's documented essential functions include occasional lifting up to 40 pounds.
- California FEHA applies alongside the ADA.

**Story**

> As an employee, I want the restriction to be mapped against my actual essential functions, so that an accommodation is considered instead of my leave being extended by default

**Scenario**

The restriction arrives in the protected compartment as a functional statement, not a diagnosis. It is mapped against the documented essential functions and touches one: occasional lifting to 40 pounds. That routes to the interactive process. Three options are considered and recorded with reasons: a lifting aid, a temporary reassignment of the lifting task, and a schedule change. The lifting aid is accepted. The manager is told the functional limit only.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the restriction touches an essential function
   **When** the mapping runs
   **Then** the interactive process is opened rather than the leave being extended by default
2. **Given** three accommodation options are considered
   **When** the process is recorded
   **Then** each option is recorded with the reason it was accepted or rejected, because an undocumented process is the weakest employer position
3. **Given** the accommodation is approved
   **When** the manager is notified
   **Then** the functional restriction is communicated and the medical basis is DENIED
4. **Given** the accommodation has a 90-day horizon
   **When** it is committed
   **Then** a review date is registered; an accommodation with no review becomes permanent by inattention

**Data read**

- `documents`: the restriction in a protected compartment
- `leave`: the case and its entitlement
- `people`: the job profile and its essential functions

**Data written**

- `leave`: work restriction, accommodation decision and return date
- `people`: assignment adjustments the accommodation requires

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the interactive process record; the manager's view is PARTIAL, with the medical basis DENIED.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- no accommodation is possible without undue hardship -> the hardship analysis is recorded with its cost and operational basis; an undocumented hardship claim is untenable
- the worker refuses the offered accommodation -> the refusal is recorded and the process continues; a refusal does not automatically end the obligation

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Each option considered with its accept or reject reason.
- The functional restriction as told to the manager.
- The accommodation review date.

   **When** the reversal runs
   **Then** the point is reversed automatically and the incident is logged
4. **Given** the recertification is returned with a wider frequency
   **When** the certification is replaced
   **Then** the new frequency governs from its own date and the earlier increments keep the frequency they were tested against

**Data written**

**Authorization and disclosure**

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the entitlement exhausts mid absence -> the remainder is unprotected and the workflow says so explicitly, so the manager applies the attendance policy knowingly

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

**Story**

> As a payroll administrator, I want the state benefit and the employer top-up to be coordinated without exceeding my regular wage, so that my claim is not disqualified by an overpayment

The employer top-up is computed as the difference between the benefit and the regular wage, capped so the total never exceeds it, because exceeding the wage can disqualify the state claim. **Acceptance criteria**

2. **Given** the tenant policy would require accrued leave to be used first
   **When** the configuration is evaluated
   **Then** REJECT with the Washington statute cited; the requirement is unlawful there
3. **Given** the state denies the claim
   **When** the leave is evaluated
   **Then** job protection continues on its own statute and the workflow does not end the protected leave because the money stopped
4. **Given** the employer verification response is late
   **When** the deadline is evaluated
   **Then** the miss is recorded with its consequence for the worker's benefit start date
5. **Given** the benefit period ends before the protected leave does
   **When** the two clocks are compared
   **Then** the protected leave continues on its own statute and the payroll treatment changes to unpaid without the leave closing

**Data read**

- `leave`: the case and its protected interval
- `payroll`: wage history for the benefit calculation and accrued balances

**Data written**

- `leave`: the benefit determination recorded against the case
- `payroll`: top-up lines with the cap applied

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the benefit, the top-up and the resulting net; the manager sees the absence dates only.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the combined payment exceeds the regular wage -> the cap applies and the excess is not paid; an overpayment can disqualify the state claim
- job protection is ended when the benefit period ends -> REJECT; the protection may outlast the benefit and the two clocks are separate

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

## US-0106. Leave entitlement exhausts with no return date

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-LVE-005`](../workflows/catalog.md) Handle a leave that exhausts entitlement without a return

- The New York State Paid Family Leave entitlement is separately exhausted.
- No return date exists. The tenant has a 26-week maximum leave policy.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the maximum leave policy not to trigger an automatic separation, so that each case is assessed individually rather than by a clock

**Scenario**

The exhaustion date is confirmed across every applicable programme, not just the federal one. The worker is notified in writing that protection has ended and what options remain. The interactive process runs to consider additional leave as a reasonable accommodation. The tenant's 26-week maximum is not a defence: the EEOC's position is that inflexible maximum-leave policies violate the ADA, so the policy informs the analysis without deciding it.    **Then** each programme's exhaustion is computed separately and the latest governs; using only the federal date would be wrong
2. **Given** the tenant has a 26-week maximum leave policy
   **When** the separation decision is considered
   **Then** FAIL_CLOSED on an automatic separation; the policy informs the individualized analysis and does not decide it
3. **Given** additional leave is granted as an accommodation
   **When** the grant is committed
   **Then** it carries a defined review date; an indefinite leave is not a reasonable accommodation
4. **Given** a separation is eventually proposed
   **When** the approval is resolved
   **Then** HR, compliance and legal all approve with the accommodation analysis attached
5. **Given** the worker qualifies for a state programme the employer overlooked
   **When** the exhaustion is recomputed
   **Then** any separation in flight is REPLANNED against the corrected date

**Data read**

- `benefits`: coverage and its continuation trigger
- `leave`: entitlements consumed across programmes and their certifications
- `people`: employment status and tenure

**Data written**

- `leave`: exhaustion record and the accommodation analysis
- `people`: any separation, through the termination workflow

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the written notice and the accommodation decision; the accommodation analysis is DENIED to the manager.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- an automatic termination fires at exhaustion -> FAIL_CLOSED; this is the single most litigated ADA failure and the workflow refuses to encode it
- additional leave is granted with no end date -> BLOCKED; a defined review date is required

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Exhaustion computation per programme with its dates.
- The individualized accommodation analysis including any undue hardship reasoning.

## US-0107. Accrued vacation is forfeited at year end in a state that forbids it

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-LVE-006`](../workflows/catalog.md) Accrue, carry over and pay out a leave balance
- **Dimension**: `LVE` Leave and absence
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, Colorado
- **Tags**: `leave`, `vacation`, `forfeiture`, `colorado`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-126110 has 84 hours of accrued vacation at 2026-12-31.
- The tenant policy forfeits anything above a 40-hour carryover.
- Colorado treats accrued vacation as earned wages that cannot be forfeited.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want a forfeiture to be refused where the jurisdiction treats the balance as earned wages, so that we do not take back pay the worker has already earned

**Scenario**

The year-end accrual processing applies the tenant's 40-hour carryover cap. For Colorado workers the forfeiture is refused: Colorado treats accrued vacation as earned wages and forfeiture is void. The 44 hours above the cap carry over rather than being forfeited, and the refusal cites the rule. A reasonable accrual cap that stops further accrual is permitted and is a different mechanism, which the workflow distinguishes.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the policy would forfeit 44 hours at year end
   **When** the jurisdiction rule is applied
   **Then** the forfeiture is REJECTED for Colorado workers with the rule cited, and the hours carry over
2. **Given** the same policy runs for a Texas worker
   **When** the rule is applied
   **Then** the forfeiture proceeds, because Texas leaves the question to policy, and the different outcomes sit side by side
3. **Given** an accrual cap is proposed instead
   **When** it is evaluated
   **Then** a cap that stops further accrual is permitted and is distinguished from a forfeiture of accrued time
4. **Given** the worker is notified before the boundary
   **When** the notice issues
   **Then** it issues before the forfeiture date rather than after, so the worker can use the time

**Data read**

- `leave`: accrual rules, prior balance, usage
- `regulatory`: forfeiture and payout rules by jurisdiction

**Data written**

- `leave`: balance revision, carryover or forfeiture record with its basis

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of their balance and the carryover outcome; the differing treatment by state is FULL to HR.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- a national forfeiture is applied uniformly -> REJECT; it is unlawful in Colorado, California, Massachusetts and several other states
- the forfeiture boundary crosses a time zone -> the tenant's declared zone governs and the decision records it, because an hour can forfeit a day

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Accrual computation with its rule version.
- The carryover or forfeiture decision with the jurisdiction rule cited.
- Advance notice to the worker before the boundary.

## US-0108. Military leave with an oral notice and an escalator return

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-LVE-007`](../workflows/catalog.md) Administer military leave and reemployment rights
- **Dimension**: `LVE` Leave and absence
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `leave`, `userra`, `escalator`, `oral-notice`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-126440 tells his manager verbally on 2026-02-10 that he is deploying for 14 months.
- He has used 11 months of cumulative service under USERRA previously.
- During his absence his peer group receives a level promotion and a 4 percent pay progression.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want an oral notice to be sufficient and an escalator return to be computed, so that I return to the job I would have had, not the one I left

**Scenario**

The oral notice is accepted; requiring written notice as a condition would itself be unlawful. Cumulative service is computed at 11 months used, so the 14-month deployment brings the total to 25 months, well inside the five-year limit. Health coverage continues on the statutory terms and pension service is recorded for crediting on return. The reemployment application deadline scales with the service length. On return the escalator position is computed: he is reemployed at the promoted level with the pay progression his peer group received, not at the level he left.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** notice is given orally
   **When** the leave is recorded
   **Then** it is accepted and the absence of a written notice does not affect the rights
2. **Given** cumulative service reaches 25 months
   **When** the five-year limit is evaluated
   **Then** the leave is within the limit and the cumulative computation is retained
3. **Given** his peer group is promoted during the absence
   **When** the return is computed
   **Then** the escalator position is determined and he is reemployed at the position he would have attained, with the pay progression applied
4. **Given** pension service is restored
   **When** the return commits
   **Then** the service is credited as though the absence had not occurred
5. **Given** a dismissal without cause is proposed 60 days after reemployment
   **When** the discharge protection is evaluated
   **Then** FAIL_CLOSED; the protection period runs from 180 days to one year depending on service length

**Data read**

- `benefits`: health coverage continuation and pension crediting
- `compensation`: the pay progression his peer group received
- `people`: service history, prior military leave, position progression

**Data written**

- `benefits`: restored pension service
- `compensation`: restored pay progression
- `leave`: military leave record and cumulative service
- `people`: reemployment into the escalator position

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the escalator determination and its basis; the manager sees the reemployment date and level.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- he is reemployed into the position he left -> REJECT; the escalator principle is the core of the statute
- a written notice is demanded as a condition -> REJECT; imposing that condition is itself unlawful

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Notice record including that an oral notice was accepted.

## US-0199. An agent proposes a bulk change and a human approves a subset

- **Persona**: AI agent
- **Workflow**: [`WF-AIA-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Let an agent propose a change a human then approves
- **Dimension**: `AIA` Semantic and agent intelligence
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `agent`, `bulk-proposal`, `exception-surfacing`, `human-approval`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. An HR business partner asks the assistant to correct cost centre attribution for a team of 22.
- Two of the 22 have a pending transfer that would make the correction wrong.
- The assistant's authority ceiling is the requesting partner's own.

**Story**

> As an AI agent, I want the agent to surface the two exceptions rather than proposing all 22, so that the human approves something that is actually correct

**Scenario**

The assistant reads the 22 workers within the partner's authority and finds two with pending transfers whose destination cost centre differs from the proposed correction. Rather than proposing all 22 and letting the approver find the problem, it proposes 20 and names the two with the reason. The partner reviews, seeing exactly what the assistant saw, and approves the 20. The two are handled separately after the transfers land. The agent is recorded as proposer and the partner as approver.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** two workers have pending transfers that conflict with the correction
   **When** the agent assembles the proposal
   **Then** it proposes 20 and names the two with the reason rather than proposing all 22
2. **Given** the partner reviews
   **When** the proposal renders
   **Then** they see what the agent saw, including the two exceptions and their basis
3. **Given** the agent's tool call would read a worker outside the partner's scope
   **When** the gateway evaluates it
   **Then** DENY; agent initiation never increases authority
4. **Given** the partner approves
   **When** the intents proceed
   **Then** the agent is recorded as proposer and the partner as approver, and the tool trace is retained
5. **Given** the agent's output is treated as the decision
   **When** the execution is attempted
   **Then** FAIL_CLOSED; the agent proposes and a human decides

**Data read**

- `agent`: agent definition, tool permissions and memory scope
- `compliance`: the authority ceiling for agent-initiated intents
- `people`: the 22 workers and their pending changes

**Data written**

- `agent`: the agent execution trace and its tool invocations
- `workflow`: the proposal for 20 and the named exceptions

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The partner receives FULL disclosure of everything the agent saw, including the two named exceptions; the agent trace is FULL to compliance.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the agent proposes all 22 to keep the interaction simple -> the approver is asked to catch a problem the agent already found, which is the wrong division of labour
- the agent's proposal cites a fact the approver cannot see -> the proposal is refused rather than shown with holes

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Complete tool invocation trace with inputs and outputs.
- The authority ceiling evaluated per call.
- The human approval bound to the proposal digest.

## US-0200. An assistant refuses a question no published article covers

- **Persona**: AI agent
- **Workflow**: [`WF-AIA-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Ground an agent answer in cited knowledge and refuse otherwise
- **Dimension**: `AIA` Semantic and agent intelligence
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal, Utah
- **Tags**: `agent`, `knowledge-grounding`, `jurisdiction-filter`, `refusal`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A Utah worker asks whether the company offers paid parental leave beyond statutory entitlements.
- No published knowledge article covers Utah parental leave policy.
- Articles exist for California and New York.

**Story**

> As an AI agent, I want the assistant to refuse rather than answer from a different state's article, so that I am not given California's answer in Utah

**Scenario**

The retrieval filters by the worker's jurisdiction before scoring. The California and New York articles are excluded by the filter rather than ranked lower, so they cannot leak into the answer. Nothing in scope remains. The assistant refuses to answer and routes to a human, stating that no published guidance covers the question for the worker's location. A plausible answer composed without a citation is exactly the failure this design prevents.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** no in-scope article exists
   **When** the assistant composes
   **Then** it refuses and routes to a human rather than answering from an out-of-scope article
2. **Given** out-of-scope articles exist
   **When** retrieval runs
   **Then** they are excluded by the filter before scoring, so they cannot influence the answer
3. **Given** an answer is produced for another question
   **When** it renders
   **Then** every assertion carries a citation to the article version behind it

**Data read**

- `knowledge`: published articles, their versions and audience scopes
- `people`: the worker's jurisdiction and scope

**Data written**

- `agent`: the answer record with its refusal and retrieval set

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the refusal and the route to a human; the retrieval set is FULL to compliance.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the assistant answers plausibly with no citation -> the answer is suppressed; an uncited policy answer is the specific failure mode
- the cited article is later corrected -> the answer record keeps the version it cited, so the reliance interval is identifiable

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The retrieval set and the jurisdiction filter applied.
- The refusal record with its reason.
- The routing to a human.
