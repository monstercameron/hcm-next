## US-0301. Check whether my equipment is recorded as returned

- **Persona**: employee
- **Workflow**: [`WF-ONB-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Assign and later recover equipment
- **Dimension**: `ONB` Onboarding and offboarding
- **Complexity tier**: 1
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `onboarding`, `asset`, `custody-view`, `tier-1`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-153330 returned a monitor to the depot on 2026-05-04.
- The depot recorded an inspected return receipt on 2026-05-05.
- A laptop remains in custody.

**Story**

> As an employee, I want to see which items are closed and which are still mine, so that I am not surprised by a recovery notice after I leave

**Scenario**

The custody view shows the monitor closed with its inspected return receipt and date, and the laptop still in custody with its handoff date. The distinction is visible to the worker rather than only to IT, which is what lets a dispute be settled before it becomes one.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the monitor has an inspected return receipt
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** ObligationState shows SATISFIED with the receipt date and the receiving party, rather than showing satisfied on dispatch
2. **Given** the laptop has no return receipt
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** it shows as in custody with its handoff date
3. **Given** the worker reported returning the laptop informally
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** the self-report does not appear as a closure; only a receipt closes custody

**Data read**

- `asset`: custody history, receipts and condition

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of their own custody records.
- Other workers' custody records are WITHHELD from this view.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- a return receipt exists with no inspection -> the item shows as received pending inspection rather than as closed
- the asset was written off -> the write-off and its approval are shown rather than the item disappearing

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Handoff and return receipts with their dates.
- The custody state per item.
- Zero-effect receipt.

## US-0302. Escalate an unclaimed work item before its deadline

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Claim, complete and hand off a work item
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `work-item`, `escalation`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Work item WI-5512 requires an identity verification decision within 48 hours.
- It sits unclaimed in the queue for 36 hours.
- The escalation threshold is 75 percent of the deadline.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want an unclaimed item to escalate before its deadline rather than after, so that the deadline is met rather than explained

**Scenario**

The item enters the queue with a 48-hour deadline. At 36 hours, 75 percent elapsed, it is still unclaimed. The escalation fires to the queue owner rather than waiting for the breach, because an escalation after the deadline is a report rather than a remedy. The queue owner assigns it directly, which is a different act from a claim and is recorded as such.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** 75 percent of the deadline elapses unclaimed
   **When** the escalation fires
   **Then** it goes to the queue owner before the breach rather than after it
2. **Given** the queue owner assigns it directly
   **When** the assignment is recorded
   **Then** a direct assignment is recorded distinctly from a claim, and the item's RequestState records the supervisor as its actor rather than the assignee
3. **Given** the item is completed within the deadline
   **When** the clock closes
   **Then** the escalation and the direct assignment both remain in the item's history

**Data read**

- `people`: the queue owner
- `workflow`: the queue, the item, its deadline and its eligibility pool

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the escalation, the direct assignment and the completion

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The queue owner receives FULL disclosure of the item and its deadline.
- The item's subject data remains bounded by its own data access manifest, so the queue owner's disclosure is PARTIAL.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the escalation fires only after the breach -> REJECT that design; an escalation after the deadline is a report rather than a remedy
- the direct assignment is recorded as a claim -> the eligibility bypass becomes invisible, which is the thing worth seeing

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The deadline and the escalation threshold.
- The escalation and its recipient.
- The direct assignment, distinguished from a claim.

2. **Given** the graph exceeds the declared depth
   **When** compilation runs

**Data read**

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the failed compilation record

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The publisher receives FULL disclosure of both breaches.
- Running instances are unaffected and their state is WITHHELD from this compilation.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the limits are raised silently to make the definition compile -> the diff makes it visible; limits raised without review defeat their purpose
- the definition compiles at the limit boundary -> it compiles, and the proximity to the limit is worth noting rather than blocking

## US-0304. Migrate a running instance to a new definition version

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Compile and publish a workflow definition
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `migration`, `in-flight`, `defect-scoping`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. 14 instances are running on definition version 3, which has a defect in a decision route.
- Version 4 corrects the route. The migration policy is pinned by default.
- Six of the 14 have already passed the affected node.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want a migration to distinguish instances that have passed the defect from those that have not, so that we do not re-run a node that already produced a decision

**Scenario**

The 14 instances are examined against the defective node. Six have already passed it and produced decisions under version 3; migrating them would not undo those decisions and re-running the node would produce a second decision on the same instance. Eight have not reached it. The migration is scoped to the eight. The six are left on version 3 with a recorded reason and their decisions are reviewed separately, because a defective route may have routed them wrongly.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** six instances have passed the affected node
   **When** the migration is scoped
   **Then** they are excluded and their decisions are routed for separate review rather than being migrated
2. **Given** eight instances have not reached the node
   **When** they are migrated
   **Then** they continue on version 4 with their ExecutionState carried across the migration rather than reset, and the migration is recorded per instance
3. **Given** the default migration policy is pinned
   **When** the migration is proposed
   **Then** it is an explicit override of the default with its own approval rather than an automatic repin
4. **Given** a migrated instance's variables do not map to version 4's schema
   **When** the migration is attempted
   **Then** that instance is excluded and quarantined rather than migrated with a partial mapping
5. **Given** the six excluded decisions are reviewed
   **When** a wrong route is found
   **Then** it becomes a correction with its own intent rather than being fixed by re-running the instance

**Data read**

- `compliance`: the migration policy and its default
- `workflow`: instance state, node history and both definition versions

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the migration records, the exclusions and the quarantined instances

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of the per-instance disposition.
- The subjects of the instances receive no disclosure of the migration; their outcomes are unchanged by it.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- all 14 are migrated uniformly -> six instances re-enter a node that already produced a decision, which produces a second decision on one instance
- the six are left on version 3 and forgotten -> the defective decisions stand unreviewed, which is the actual harm the defect caused

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The per-instance node history behind the scoping.
- The migration override with its approval.
- The excluded set and their separate review.

## US-0305. Handle a signal that arrives before the instance waits for it

- **Persona**: integration system
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TRG-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Process an inbound trigger from an external system safely
- **Dimension**: `TRG` System and trigger-driven
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `signal`, `early-arrival`, `buffer`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A carrier confirmation signal for instance WI-6640 arrives at 2026-04-02T11:14.
- The instance does not reach its WAIT node until 11:22.
- The signal correlation key matches the instance.

**Story**

> As an integration system, I want an early signal to be buffered rather than dropped, so that a fast counterparty does not cause a stuck instance

**Scenario**

The signal arrives eight minutes before the instance reaches the node that waits for it. Dropping it would leave the instance waiting forever for a signal that already came, which is a stuck instance nobody can diagnose. The signal is buffered against the correlation key. When the instance reaches the WAIT node it consumes the buffered signal immediately and proceeds. The buffer has a bounded lifetime so a signal for an instance that never arrives does not accumulate.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the signal arrives before the WAIT node is reached
   **When** it is processed
   **Then** it is buffered against the correlation key rather than dropped
2. **Given** the instance reaches the WAIT node
   **When** the buffer is consulted
   **Then** the buffered signal is consumed immediately and the wait does not begin
3. **Given** no instance ever consumes a buffered signal
   **When** the buffer lifetime elapses
   **Then** the signal is discarded with a recorded reason rather than accumulating indefinitely
4. **Given** two signals arrive for the same correlation key
   **When** the buffer is written
   **Then** the duplicate is suppressed on the signal's own idempotency scope

**Data read**

- `integration`: the inbound signal and its correlation key
- `workflow`: instance state, the WAIT node and the signal buffer

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the buffered signal and its consumption

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of the buffer and its contents.
- The signal payload's subject data is bounded by the instance's own manifest, so operations' disclosure of it is PARTIAL.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the early signal is dropped -> the instance waits forever for something that already happened, which is the hardest class of stuck instance to diagnose
- the buffer has no lifetime -> signals for instances that never start accumulate without bound

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The signal receipt time and the node arrival time.
- The buffered signal and its consumption.
- The buffer lifetime and any discard.

## US-0306. Approve a proposal where the resolver returns the proposer

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A proposal's approval requirement resolves to the two most senior people in the unit.
- The proposer is one of the two.
- The quorum is two.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the proposer to be excluded and the quorum recomputed over who remains, so that self-approval does not happen because the resolver was naive

**Scenario**

The resolver returns two eligible approvers, one of whom is the proposer. The proposer is excluded, leaving one eligible approver against a quorum of two. Rather than silently reducing the quorum, the requirement escalates to the next level to find a second approver. Reducing the quorum because the pool shrank would make separation of duties depend on how many senior people happen to be in a unit.

**Acceptance criteria**

   **When** the exclusion runs
   **Then** the proposer is excluded from their own approval pool
2. **Given** one eligible approver remains against a quorum of two
   **When** the requirement is evaluated
   **Then** it escalates to find a second approver rather than reducing the quorum
3. **Given** no second approver can be found at any level
   **When** the requirement resolves
   **Then** FAIL_CLOSED; an unsatisfiable approval requirement is a configuration defect rather than a licence to proceed

**Data read**

- `organization`: the escalation path
- `people`: candidate approvers and their authority
- `workflow`: the approval requirement, its resolver expression and its quorum

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the exclusion, the escalation and the eventual decisions

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The proposer receives FULL disclosure that they were excluded and why.
- The identity of the escalated approver is FULL to the proposer, since they will see the decision.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the quorum is reduced because the pool shrank -> separation of duties becomes a function of unit size, which is not a control
- the proposer approves under a delegation they hold -> the exclusion applies to the person rather than to the role, so the delegation does not cure it

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The resolver output before and after the exclusion.
- The escalation and its basis.
- Each decision bound to the digest with its authority snapshot.

## US-0307. An approver forges an approval certificate

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Resolve an approval requirement and bind the decision
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `adversarial`, `forged-evidence`, `signature-verification`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A submitted approval decision carries a certificate whose signature does not verify against any registered key.
- The claimed approver is a real principal with the right authority.
- The proposal digest in the certificate matches a real proposal.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want a certificate whose signature does not verify to be rejected outright, so that an approval cannot be manufactured from a plausible-looking payload

**Scenario**

The certificate names a real approver, cites a real proposal digest and would satisfy the requirement if it were genuine. Its signature does not verify against any key in the registry. The decision is rejected before it is recorded, because a certificate that cannot be verified is not evidence of a decision regardless of how plausible its contents are. The attempt is recorded as a forged evidence event with the claimed approver, the real approver's key set and the submitting principal, which are three different things.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the certificate's signature does not verify
   **When** the decision is submitted
   **Then** it is REJECTED before being recorded, and no approval is registered against the proposal
2. **Given** the certificate names a real approver with the right authority
   **When** the refusal is returned to the submitter
   **Then** it is byte-identical to the refusal for a certificate naming a principal that does not exist, so the attempt does not confirm who holds the authority
3. **Given** the attempt is refused
   **When** the security log is inspected
   **Then** a forged evidence event records the claimed approver, the submitting principal and the proposal digest as three separate facts
4. **Given** the real approver later approves genuinely
   **When** the decision is recorded
   **Then** it verifies and is recorded, and the earlier forged attempt remains in the log beside it
5. **Given** the submitting principal repeats the attempt with a different digest
   **When** the pattern is evaluated
   **Then** the repetition escalates to a security incident; each individual refusal remains identical

**Data read**

- `security`: the key registry and the submitting principal
- `workflow`: the proposal, its digest and the approval requirement

**Data written**

- `security`: the forged evidence event

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The submitting principal receives only the typed verification refusal; whether the named approver exists is WITHHELD.
- The real approver receives FULL disclosure that an approval was claimed in their name.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the certificate verifies against a retired key -> REJECT; a retired key's signatures are not accepted for new decisions, only for verifying historical ones
- the certificate is accepted because its contents are plausible -> REJECT that behaviour outright; verification is the control and plausibility is what an attacker supplies

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The verification failure with its key registry lookup.
- The forged evidence event with all three principals distinguished.
- The absence of any recorded approval against the proposal.

## US-0308. A caller replays an approved proposal digest against a new proposal

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Resolve an approval requirement and bind the decision
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `adversarial`, `digest-replay`, `approval-binding`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Proposal P-4410 for a 3 percent increase was approved on 2026-05-02 and executed.
- A new proposal P-4471 for a 19 percent increase is submitted on 2026-05-09.
- The submitter supplies P-4410's approval certificate against P-4471.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want an approval bound to one digest not to satisfy another, so that an approval cannot be moved from a small change to a large one

**Scenario**

The submitter presents a genuine, verifiable approval certificate signed by a real approver with the right authority. Its bound digest is P-4410's, the 3 percent increase approved a week earlier. The proposal it is offered against is P-4471, the 19 percent increase, whose canonical digest differs because the amount is a material field under the published canonicalization profile. The binding check compares the two digests and refuses. A genuine certificate is not a general-purpose approval token: it approves one immutable proposal and nothing else, which is the entire reason approvals bind digests rather than intent ids. The attempt is recorded as a replay of an approval across proposals, which is a different event from an ordinary approval failure.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the certificate is genuine and verifies
   **When** the binding is checked
   **Then** its bound digest does not match P-4471's and the approval is REJECTED
2. **Given** the two proposals differ only in the amount
   **When** the digests are computed
   **Then** the canonicalization profile includes the amount as a material field, so the digests differ
3. **Given** the attempt is refused
   **When** the log is inspected
   **Then** it is recorded as an approval replay across proposals, and P-4471's RequestState stays PENDING_APPROVAL rather than moving to APPROVED
4. **Given** the submitter alters P-4471 until its material fields equal P-4410's
   **When** the digests are recomputed
   **Then** the altered proposal is a new immutable proposal with its own id, and the approval applies to it only because the digest it binds is the one it has always bound
5. **Given** a non-material field differs between the two
   **When** the digests are computed
   **Then** they match under the canonicalization profile and the approval applies, because the material change is identical

**Data read**

- `compliance`: the canonicalization profile and its material field set
- `workflow`: both proposals, their digests and the approval certificate

**Data written**

- `security`: the approval replay event

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The submitter receives the typed binding refusal; the contents of P-4410 are WITHHELD from them unless they were party to it.
- The original approver receives FULL disclosure that their certificate was presented against a different proposal.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the certificate is accepted because it is genuine -> a genuine certificate is not a general-purpose token; the binding is what makes it an approval of something
- the canonicalization profile omits the amount -> the profile is defective and the two proposals would be indistinguishable, which is a platform bug of the first order

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both proposal digests and their comparison.
- The approval replay event.
- The certificate's original binding, unaltered.

## US-0310. A worker submits a forged medical certification

- The named provider has no record of the patient when contacted under the permitted authentication route.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want a suspected forgery to be handled through the permitted verification route rather than by denial, so that an accusation is not a substitute for a process

**Scenario**

The document itself is inconsistent: the provider identifier printed on it belongs to a different practice in another state. That is a defect in the artefact, established without asking anything about the worker's condition. HR then uses the permitted authentication route, which allows a healthcare provider to be contacted to confirm that a certification was issued but does not permit questions about the underlying condition. The named provider does not confirm issuance. Two independent signals now point at a fabricated document, and the employer's next step is still to give the worker the opportunity to cure with a sufficient certification rather than to deny the leave and make a finding of fraud in the same act. The document defect, the authentication attempt and its result are recorded in the compartment.

**Acceptance criteria**

   **When** the document is examined
   **Then** the inconsistency is recorded as a defect in the artefact, established without any inquiry into the worker's condition
2. **Given** the provider does not confirm issuance
   **When** the result is recorded
   **Then** it is recorded as an authentication result rather than as a finding of fabrication
3. **Given** the employer contacts the provider
   **When** the contact is made
   **Then** it is limited to authentication of issuance; questions about the condition are refused by the workflow
4. **Given** the certification is suspected to be forged
   **When** the next step is taken
   **Then** the worker is given the opportunity to cure or clarify before any denial
5. **Given** the worker cures with a verifiable certification
   **When** the leave is approved
   **Then** the earlier authentication result stays in the compartment and does not affect the approval
6. **Given** the worker cannot cure and the leave is denied
   **When** the denial is issued
   **Then** it cites the failure to provide a sufficient certification rather than making a finding of fraud, which is a separate matter with its own process

**Data read**

- `documents`: the certification in a protected compartment
- `leave`: the case, its entitlement and the certification requirement
- `regulatory`: the permitted authentication route and its limits

**Data written**

- `case`: any consequent conduct matter, opened separately
- `leave`: the authentication result and the cure opportunity

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The authentication result is WITHHELD from the worker's manager entirely.
- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the cure request; the provider contact detail is DENIED to them.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the employer asks the provider about the condition -> REJECT; the authentication route permits confirming issuance and nothing more
- the leave is denied and fraud is alleged in the same act -> REJECT; the leave decision and a conduct finding are separate matters with separate processes and separate evidence standards
- the manager is told the certification is suspect -> WITHHELD; the manager receives dates and a return date, and the existence of a certification question is not among the facts they are told exist

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The authentication attempt, its route and its result, in the compartment.
- The cure request and its deadline.
- The separation between the leave decision and any conduct matter.

- **Workflow**: [`WF-REC-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Screen an application and record a decision
- **Dimension**: `REC` Recruiting and ATS
- **Complexity tier**: 1
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `recruiting`, `self-identification`, `separation`, `tier-1`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A hiring manager opens application A-5512 on 2026-06-19.
- The candidate supplied voluntary self-identification at application.
- The tenant separates self-identification from the selection record.

**Story**

> As a hiring manager, I want the self-identification not to appear in the record I decide from, so that my decision is not influenced by data I should not have

**Scenario**

The application shows the candidate's experience, qualifications and their responses to the declared screening criteria. The voluntary self-identification is stored separately and does not appear in the selection record at all: not hidden behind a control the manager could open, but absent from the record they read. That separation is what makes the compliance data usable without contaminating the decision.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** self-identification was supplied
   **When** the selection record renders
   **Then** it is absent from the record rather than hidden behind a control
2. **Given** the manager requests it
   **When** the request is evaluated
   **Then** DENIED; the separation is the compliance programme's foundation
3. **Given** the application renders
   **When** its contents are inspected
   **Then** experience, qualifications and criteria responses are present and complete

**Data read**

- `privacy`: the self-identification, held separately
- `recruiting`: the application and the declared screening criteria

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The hiring manager receives PARTIAL disclosure: the selection record is FULL and the self-identification is DENIED.
- The self-identification is FULL only to the compliance function.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the self-identification is hidden rather than absent -> a control that can be opened will be opened, and the separation must be structural
- the manager infers it from other fields -> outside the system's control, which is why the declared criteria and the recorded reasons matter

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The selection record's field set.
- The absence of self-identification from it.
- Zero-effect receipt.
