## US-0701. See the cost centre my time is charged to

- **Persona**: employee
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TIM-005`](../workflows/catalog.md) Allocate labor cost across projects and cost centres
- **Dimension**: `TIM` Time, attendance and scheduling
- **Complexity tier**: 1
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `time`, `cost-attribution`, `self-service`, `tier-1`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-171250's position charges to cost centre CC-4410.
- They logged hours to two projects in the period ending 2026-05-29.
- One project charges to a different centre.

**Story**

> As an employee, I want the default and the project-driven centre to be distinguishable, so that I can see when my hours leave my own team's budget

**Scenario**

The view shows the position's default centre and, per project, the centre the hours actually charged to. One project routes cost to another team, which is a fact the worker's own manager will eventually ask about and which the worker can otherwise only guess at.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** hours charge to more than one centre
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** each project's charged centre is shown alongside the position's default
2. **Given** a project overrides the default
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** the override is visible as an override rather than as the default
3. **Given** no project is specified for some hours
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** those hours show the default centre explicitly rather than showing no centre

**Data read**

- `finance`: the project cost centre mapping
- `position`: the default cost attribution
- `time`: the logged hours and their project allocation

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of their own hours and the centres they charged to.
- The centres' balances and other charges are DENIED to the worker in this view by name.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- only one centre is shown -> hours leaving the team's budget are invisible to the person logging them
- unallocated hours show no centre -> the default is applied silently and nobody sees where the cost went

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The hours with their projects.
- The charged centre per project and the default.
- Zero-effect receipt.

## US-0702. Check whether a policy applies to my location

- **Persona**: employee
- **Workflow**: [`WF-HRO-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Configure a tenant policy and publish it
- **Dimension**: `HRO` HR operations and administration console
- **Complexity tier**: 1
- **Jurisdiction**: US state variation
- **Tags**: `hr-operations`, `policy-applicability`, `location-scope`, `tier-1`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A parental leave policy applies to workers in five US states.
- Worker W-171260 works in Arizona, which is not among them.
- A federal baseline applies everywhere.

**Story**

> As an employee, I want the policy to state whether it applies to me rather than leaving me to check a list, so that I do not read a policy that does not govern my situation

**Scenario**

The policy opens with a clear statement that it does not apply at the worker's location, and names the federal baseline that does. A worker who reads a policy that does not apply to them plans around terms they do not have, and a list of states at the bottom of a document is not how most people read.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the policy's scope excludes the worker's location
   **When** the policy opens
   **Then** it states that it does not apply and names what does
2. **Given** the policy's scope includes the worker's location
   **When** the policy opens
   **Then** it states that it applies, so the applicability is explicit in both directions
3. **Given** the worker's location changes
   **When** the policy is re-read
   **Then** the applicability is re-derived from the current location

**Data read**

- `compliance`: the policy, its jurisdiction scope and the baseline
- `people`: the worker's work location

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the policy text and its applicability to them.
- Other locations' terms are shown as scope rather than as entitlements, so the policy does not imply the worker may claim them.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- applicability is a list at the bottom -> the worker plans around terms they do not have
- applicability is stated only when the policy applies -> silence is ambiguous and the worker cannot tell whether it was checked

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The policy scope and the worker's location.
- The applicability determination.
- Zero-effect receipt.

## US-0703. Read the audit trail of a configuration change

- **Persona**: auditor
- **Workflow**: [`WF-CFG-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Roll back a configuration change safely
- **Dimension**: `CFG` Configuration, rules and policies
- **Complexity tier**: 1
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `configuration`, `rollback`, `audit-trail`, `tier-1`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A decision table was changed 2026-03-11 and rolled back 2026-03-13.
- Both acts had different actors.
- The rollback restored the prior version rather than editing the new one.

**Story**

> As an auditor, I want the rollback to be a distinct act rather than appearing as a third edit, so that I can tell a reversal from a change that happens to restore an earlier state

**Scenario**

The trail shows a change, then a rollback identified as a rollback with the version it restored. A rollback rendered as an ordinary edit that happens to produce the earlier content is indistinguishable from a coincidence, and the difference matters when an auditor asks whether anyone reviewed the reversal.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a change is rolled back
   **When** the trail renders
   **Then** the rollback is identified as such with the version it restored
2. **Given** both acts have different actors
   **When** the trail renders
   **Then** each act shows its own actor and timestamp
3. **Given** the rollback restored a prior version
   **When** the versions are compared
   **Then** the restored version is the prior one rather than a new version with identical content

**Data read**

- `configuration`: the decision table versions, the change and the rollback

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The auditor receives FULL disclosure of both acts and their actors.
- The actors' reasons, where not attached to the acts, are DENIED to the trail by name.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the rollback renders as an ordinary edit -> a reversal and a coincidence are indistinguishable
- the rollback creates a new version with identical content -> the version history grows and the restoration is not visible as one

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both acts with their actors and timestamps.
- The rollback and the version it restored.
- Zero-effect receipt.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the membership to show when each worker was added rather than a flat list, so that I can tell whether a record predating an addition was preserved

**Scenario**

The hold shows 34 members with the date each was added. Two were added seven weeks in, which means their records were subject to ordinary retention until that date. A flat list implies the hold covered everyone from the start, and a preservation question about those two would be answered wrongly.

- `people`: the members' employment states

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The compliance officer receives FULL disclosure of the membership and its dates.
- The matter's substance is WITHHELD from every reader outside its compartment, whose views carry no indication that a matter exists; the membership view carries the hold and never the matter.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- a flat list is shown -> a preservation question about a late addition is answered wrongly
- terminated members are dropped -> the hold appears to have shrunk when it has not

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The membership with per-member addition dates.
- The employment states of the members.
- Zero-effect receipt.

- Tenant acme. An announcement is drafted 2026-07-13 to a rule-defined audience.

- The send is scheduled for 2026-07-15.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the audience to be listable at draft time rather than counted, so that I check the list rather than trust a rule I wrote

**Scenario**

The draft shows the audience as a list of 214 with their units. A count is not checkable: a rule with a wrong operator often produces a plausible number. Listing the members is what lets the author notice that a whole division is included that should not be, which is the only review that catches the error before it sends.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an audience is defined by rule
   **When** the draft renders
   **Then** the resolved membership is listable rather than only counted
2. **Given** the rule is edited
   **When** the audience re-resolves
   **Then** the change in membership is shown as a difference rather than only a new count
3. **Given** the send is scheduled
   **When** the audience is noted
   **Then** the draft states that the audience re-resolves at send and that the listed members may differ

**Data read**

- `experience`: the announcement, its audience rule and the resolved membership
- `people`: the workers matching the rule

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The author receives FULL disclosure of the resolved membership within their own authority.
- Workers outside the author's authority who match the rule are DENIED by name and counted, so the author knows the audience is larger than they can see.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- only a count is shown -> a rule with a wrong operator produces a plausible number and passes review
- out-of-authority matches are omitted entirely -> the author sends to more people than they knew about

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The audience rule and its resolution.
- The listed membership and any denied count.
- Zero-effect receipt.

## US-0707. Correct a hire date that changes benefits eligibility

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PEO-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Correct an employment fact retroactively
- **Dimension**: `PEO` People and core HR
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `people`, `hire-date-correction`, `eligibility-derivation`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-171270's hire date was recorded as 2026-02-16; it was 2026-01-19.
- The correction is made 2026-04-06.
- The plan's eligibility waiting period is 60 days from hire.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the correction to re-derive eligibility rather than only changing the date, so that coverage starts when it should have started

**Scenario**

The corrected hire date moves eligibility from 2026-04-17 to 2026-03-20, which has already passed. Correcting the date alone leaves the worker uncovered for a period they were entitled to. The eligibility is re-derived, the enrollment window is reopened with its original relative timing, and any claims incurred in the gap are addressed with the carrier as retroactive coverage rather than as denials.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the hire date is corrected
   **When** eligibility is re-derived
   **Then** the waiting period runs from the corrected date
2. **Given** the derived eligibility date has passed
   **When** the correction runs
   **Then** the enrollment window is reopened with its original relative timing rather than being treated as missed
3. **Given** claims were incurred in the uncovered gap
   **When** the correction runs
   **Then** retroactive coverage is sought with the carrier rather than the claims standing as denials

**Data read**

- `benefits`: the plan's waiting period and the enrollment window
- `payroll`: the periods affected by the corrected date
- `people`: the hire date revisions

**Data written**

- `benefits`: the re-derived eligibility and the reopened window
- `people`: the corrected hire date

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the correction, the new eligibility date and the reopened window.
- The cause of the original error is DENIED to the worker where it names another person's action.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- only the date is corrected -> the worker is uncovered for a period they were entitled to and nobody notices
- the enrollment window is treated as missed -> the employer's error costs the worker a plan year of coverage

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The hire date revisions with their intervals.
- The re-derived eligibility computation.
- The reopened window and any retroactive coverage.

## US-0708. Handle an offer accepted after its expiry

- **Persona**: recruiter
- **Workflow**: [`WF-REC-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Extend, negotiate and sign an offer
- **Dimension**: `REC` Recruiting and ATS
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `recruiting`, `expired-offer`, `late-acceptance`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Offer O-4610 expired 2026-05-15 with no response.
- The candidate accepts on 2026-05-18.
- The requisition is still open.

**Story**

> As a recruiter, I want the late acceptance to require a re-issued offer rather than being treated as valid, so that both sides know which terms bind

**Scenario**

The offer expired and the candidate's acceptance is therefore an offer from them rather than an acceptance of ours. Treating it as valid leaves both sides believing an agreement exists on terms whose expiry passed, and the compensation approval behind it may also have lapsed. The offer is re-issued with a new expiry and, where the approval has lapsed, a fresh approval, and the candidate is told plainly rather than being left to assume.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an acceptance arrives after expiry
   **When** it is evaluated
   **Then** it is not treated as an acceptance and a re-issued offer is required
2. **Given** the compensation approval behind the offer has lapsed
   **When** the re-issue is prepared
   **Then** a fresh approval is obtained rather than the lapsed one being reused
3. **Given** the candidate is told
   **When** the message is composed
   **Then** it states plainly that the offer expired and a new one is being issued rather than proceeding as if nothing happened

**Data read**

- `compensation`: the approval behind the offer and its validity
- `recruiting`: the offer, its expiry and the acceptance

**Data written**

- `recruiting`: the re-issued offer and its new expiry

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The candidate receives FULL disclosure of the expiry, the late acceptance and the re-issued terms.
- The internal approval history is DENIED to them; the terms and their validity period are disclosed.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the late acceptance is treated as valid -> both sides believe an agreement exists on terms whose expiry passed
- the offer is re-issued on a lapsed approval -> the terms rest on an approval that no longer stands

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The offer, its expiry and the acceptance timestamp.
- The approval's validity at re-issue.
- The re-issued offer with its new expiry.

## US-0709. Refuse to attach a document to a case the uploader cannot read

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-HRS-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Open, assign and resolve an HR service request
- **Dimension**: `HRS` HR service delivery and cases
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `hr-service`, `compartment`, `non-disclosing-upload`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A worker uploads a document referencing case HR-5810 on 2026-06-22.
- They are not in the case's compartment.
- The document is relevant to the case.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the upload to be accepted without confirming that the case exists, so that an upload is not a way to test whether a case is real

**Scenario**

The document is relevant and the uploader cannot see the case. Attaching it directly would confirm the case exists through the success of the attachment. The document is accepted into an intake area with the claimed reference retained, the compartment owner is notified and decides whether to attach it, and the uploader receives an acknowledgement identical to the one they would receive for a reference to no case at all.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an uploader references a case they are not in
   **When** the upload is processed
   **Then** the document is accepted into intake and is not attached directly
2. **Given** the acknowledgement is returned
   **When** it renders
   **Then** it is identical to the acknowledgement for a reference to a nonexistent case
3. **Given** the compartment owner reviews
   **When** they decide
   **Then** attaching is their decision, and the uploader is not told the outcome unless the owner chooses to tell them

**Data read**

- `case`: the compartment membership and the claimed reference
- `documents`: the uploaded document and the intake area

**Data written**

- `case`: the notification to the compartment owner
- `documents`: the intake record with the claimed reference

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The uploader receives PARTIAL disclosure: receipt is acknowledged and the case reference is WITHHELD, with the acknowledgement identical either way.
- The compartment owner receives FULL disclosure of the document and the claimed reference.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the document is attached directly -> the attachment's success confirms the case exists to someone outside its compartment
- the upload is refused with a reason -> the reason confirms or denies the case, which is the fact being probed

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The intake record with the claimed reference.
- The uniform acknowledgement.
- The compartment owner's decision.

## US-0711. Refuse an equipment order for a worker who has not started

   **Then** the latest release date that still meets the start is computed and shown, so the hold does not cause a late delivery
3. **Given** the contingency clears
   **When** the order releases
   **Then** it releases automatically and the release is recorded with the clearing event

**Data read**

- `asset`: the equipment order and its lead time
- `recruiting`: the offer, its contingencies and the start date

**Data written**

- `asset`: the held order and its release condition

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The hiring manager receives FULL disclosure of the hold, its reason and the latest release date.
- The contingency's own content, where it is a background check, is DENIED to the manager and the IT function by name.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the order is placed on the start date alone -> equipment ships to someone whose offer may not complete and recovery is far harder than delay
- the hold has no computed release date -> the contingency clears late and the equipment misses the start

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The outstanding contingency.
- The computed latest release date.

**Data written**

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the category is derived from free text -> three rules depend on a manager's phrasing and two managers produce different outcomes
- the category is corrected without re-evaluating -> the rules keep the outcomes the wrong category produced

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

## US-0713. Refuse a report definition that reads a field the requester cannot see

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A report definition is authored 2026-04-20 including a compensation field.
- The author's authority does not cover compensation.
- The report is intended for a finance audience who can see it.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the definition to be authored without the author reading the field, so that authoring a report is not a way to read a field

The author cannot read compensation and the intended audience can. Refusing the definition entirely would prevent a legitimate report; running it for the author would disclose the field to them.
