## US-0401. Look up a connector's current health state

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-CON-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Monitor connector health and act before a failure lands
- **Dimension**: `CON` Connectivity and integration hub
- **Complexity tier**: 1
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `connectivity`, `health-view`, `credential-expiry`, `tier-1`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Connector CN-0440 to a benefits carrier is healthy on 2026-03-02.
- Its last successful operation was at 09:14 and its error rate over 24 hours is 0.3 percent.
- Its credential expires 2026-06-30.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the credential expiry to be part of the health view, so that an expiry is a failure I can see coming

**Scenario**

The health view shows the state, the last successful operation, the 24-hour error rate and the credential expiry of 2026-06-30. The expiry belongs here because it is a scheduled failure rather than an unpredictable one, and an operator looking at a healthy connector should be able to see that it will stop working in four months unless something happens.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the connector is healthy
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** the state, the last successful operation time and the 24-hour error rate are all shown
2. **Given** the credential expires in four months
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** the expiry date is shown alongside the health state
3. **Given** the error rate is below the degraded threshold
   **When** the view renders
   **Then** the threshold is shown alongside the rate so the margin is visible

**Data read**

- `integration`: the connector, its operation history and its credential lease

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The administrator receives FULL disclosure of the connector's health and credential expiry.
- The credential value itself is DENIED to every reader.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the credential expires with no rotation -> the connector fails at a predictable moment, which the expiry display exists to prevent
- the error rate is computed over too short a window -> a single transient failure moves the state, so the window is part of the configuration

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The health state with its thresholds.
- The last successful operation and the error rate window.
- Zero-effect receipt.

## US-0402. Retry a connector operation that failed transiently

- **Persona**: integration system
- **Workflow**: [`WF-INT-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Redrive a failed connector operation batch
- **Dimension**: `INT` Integration and external systems
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `connectivity`, `retry`, `idempotency`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A carrier enrollment operation fails with a gateway timeout on 2026-04-11T02:14.
- The operation carries an idempotency key and the target honours it.
- The retry policy allows three attempts with backoff.

**Story**

> As an integration system, I want a transient failure to be retried with the same idempotency key, so that the retry does not create a second enrollment

**Scenario**

The operation times out at the gateway, which means the request may or may not have reached the target. The retry uses the same idempotency key, so if the first attempt did reach the target the retry is suppressed there and returns the original result rather than creating a second enrollment. Three attempts are made with backoff; the second succeeds and reports that the operation had already been applied.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the first attempt times out
   **When** the retry runs
   **Then** it uses the same idempotency key rather than a new one
2. **Given** the target had already applied the first attempt
   **When** the retry is processed there
   **Then** it is suppressed and the original result is returned rather than a second enrollment being created
3. **Given** three attempts are exhausted
   **When** the operation is evaluated
   **Then** it moves to UNKNOWN with an investigation rather than a fourth blind attempt

**Data read**

- `integration`: the operation, its idempotency key and the retry policy

**Data written**

- `integration`: the retry attempts and the eventual outcome

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of every attempt and its result.
- The subscriber's enrollment data follows its own disclosure rules and is DENIED to operations beyond what the operation carries.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the retry uses a new idempotency key -> a second enrollment is created and the duplicate is found at reconciliation
- the target does not honour idempotency keys -> the retry is refused for effectful operations and the state is determined by reading back instead

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Every attempt with its key and its response.
- The suppression at the target, distinguished from a fresh success.
- The retry policy applied.

## US-0403. Migrate a connector to a new authentication method

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-SEC-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Rotate a credential lease before expiry
- **Dimension**: `SEC` Authorization, security and identity
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `connectivity`, `auth-migration`, `overlap`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A payroll provider moves from basic authentication to a token exchange on 2026-08-01.
- Both methods are accepted during a 30-day overlap.
- Operations run continuously through the window.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the new method to be proven before the old one is abandoned, so that the cutover is not a leap

**Scenario**

The provider accepts both methods for thirty days. The new token exchange is configured and proven on live operations during the overlap rather than in a test alone, because a token exchange that works in test and fails on the provider's production rate limits is a common outcome. Operations run on the new method for a week with the old one still available as a fallback, then the old credential is retired.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** both methods are accepted during the overlap
   **When** the new method is configured
   **Then** it is proven on live operations rather than in test alone
2. **Given** the new method runs for a week
   **When** the old credential is retired
   **Then** the retirement follows sustained success rather than the first successful call
3. **Given** the new method fails during the overlap
   **When** the fallback runs
   **Then** operations continue on the old method and the failure is diagnosed without an outage
4. **Given** the overlap ends with the new method unproven
   **When** the deadline is evaluated
   **Then** the obligation's deadline is the overlap's end, so it escalates while ObligationState is still PENDING rather than at the moment operations stop

**Data read**

- `integration`: the connector, both credential configurations and the operation history
- `security`: the credential leases and their rotation policy

**Data written**

- `integration`: the operations run on each method
- `security`: the new lease and the retired one

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of both configurations and the cutover state.
- Both credential values are DENIED to every reader.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the old credential is retired at the first success on the new method -> a rate limit or an intermittent failure appears after the fallback is gone
- the overlap ends unnoticed -> operations stop at a moment nobody chose

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both credential leases and their intervals.
- The live operations proving the new method.
- The retirement following sustained success.

## US-0404. Configure a connector's rate limit to match the partner's

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-CON-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Monitor connector health and act before a failure lands
- **Dimension**: `CON` Connectivity and integration hub
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `connectivity`, `rate-limit`, `partner-published`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A learning provider publishes a limit of 60 requests per minute.
- The connector is configured at 100 per minute.
- A nightly sync of 4,000 records runs at that rate.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the connector's rate to respect the partner's published limit, so that we are not throttled into a failure we caused

**Scenario**

The connector sends at 100 requests per minute against a published limit of 60. The partner throttles, which appears as intermittent failures whose cause is our own configuration. The connector's rate is set to 55, below the limit with margin, and the sync takes longer and completes. The published limit is recorded as a configuration input rather than discovered from failures.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the connector rate exceeds the partner's published limit
   **When** the sync runs
   **Then** throttling appears as intermittent failures and the cause is the connector's own configuration
2. **Given** the rate is set below the limit
   **When** the sync runs
   **Then** it completes without throttling and takes longer, which is the correct trade
3. **Given** the partner changes its published limit
   **When** the configuration is reviewed
   **Then** the limit is a recorded configuration input rather than a value discovered from failures

**Data read**

- `integration`: the connector, its rate configuration and the partner's published limit

**Data written**

- `integration`: the rate configuration and the sync outcomes

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of the rate configuration and the partner's limit.
- The synced records' contents follow their own disclosure rules.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the failures are treated as partner instability -> the cause is ours and the diagnosis goes to the wrong party
- the rate is set exactly at the limit -> burst behaviour crosses it; the margin is what makes the configuration reliable

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The published limit as a configuration input.
- The configured rate and its margin.
- The sync outcomes before and after.

## US-0405. Detect that a partner silently changed a field's meaning

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-CON-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Handle a breaking schema change from an external partner
- **Dimension**: `CON` Connectivity and integration hub
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `connectivity`, `semantic-drift`, `distribution-check`, `no-schema-change`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A benefits carrier's coverage tier field changes meaning on 2026-05-01: value 2 moves from employee-plus-spouse to employee-plus-one.
- The schema is unchanged; only the semantics moved.
- The change is not announced.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want a semantic change with no schema change to be detectable, so that we do not enroll people in the wrong tier for months

**Scenario**

The field's type, name and permitted values are unchanged, so schema validation passes and the connector continues. The meaning of value 2 has changed, which no schema check catches. It is detected by a distribution check: the proportion of subscribers at tier 2 shifts sharply against its historical baseline, which is the signal a semantic change produces. The connector pauses on the anomaly rather than continuing to enroll people in a tier that no longer means what the mapping assumes.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the schema is unchanged and the meaning has moved
   **When** schema validation runs
   **Then** it passes, which is why the distribution check exists alongside it
2. **Given** the tier distribution shifts sharply against its baseline
   **When** the anomaly detection runs
   **Then** the connector pauses and the anomaly is raised rather than the sync continuing
3. **Given** the partner confirms the semantic change
   **When** the mapping is updated
   **Then** the update is a new mapping version proven on fixtures for both meanings
4. **Given** the already-synced records used the old meaning
   **When** the remediation runs
   **Then** the affected records are identified by their sync date and corrected rather than the whole population being resynced
5. **Given** the distribution shift had a legitimate business cause
   **When** the anomaly is investigated
   **Then** it is cleared with the cause recorded and the baseline is updated

**Data read**

- `benefits`: the enrolled tiers and their history
- `integration`: the mapping, the schema and the historical distribution baseline

**Data written**

- `benefits`: the corrected enrollments
- `integration`: the anomaly, the pause and the new mapping version

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of the anomaly and the distribution comparison.
- Individual subscribers' tiers are DENIED to operations beyond the aggregate distribution.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- only schema validation is run -> a semantic change passes and enrollments are wrong for as long as it takes someone to complain
- the whole population is resynced -> records that were correct are rewritten, which creates churn and new errors

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The distribution baseline and the observed shift.
- The pause and the partner confirmation.
- The affected record identification by sync date.

## US-0406. Publish a data quality rule as advisory before making it blocking

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-DQG-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Define and evaluate a data quality invariant
- **Dimension**: `DQG` Data quality and lineage governance
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `data-quality`, `advisory-first`, `severity`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A rule requires every worker to have a recorded work location.
- 412 workers currently violate it.
- The proposed final severity is blocking.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the rule to run advisory first so the backlog can be cleared before it blocks, so that we do not block writes on a rule 412 records already fail

**Scenario**

The rule is published as advisory. It produces findings without refusing writes, which lets the 412-record backlog be worked while new violations are visible. When the backlog reaches zero the severity is raised to blocking in a second, separate publication with its own approval. Publishing as blocking on day one would have blocked routine edits to 412 records for reasons unrelated to those edits.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** 412 records violate the rule
   **When** it is published as advisory
   **Then** it produces findings and does not refuse writes
2. **Given** the backlog is cleared
   **When** the severity is raised
   **Then** it is a second publication with its own approval rather than an edit of the first
3. **Given** a new violation is created while the rule is advisory
   **When** the write runs
   **Then** it succeeds and produces a finding, so the trend is visible

**Data read**

- `dataops`: the rule, its severity and the current violations
- `people`: the records in scope

**Data written**

- `dataops`: the advisory rule version and, later, the blocking one

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The data authority receives FULL disclosure of the violation count and the trend.
- The individual records' contents follow their own disclosure rules.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the rule is published as blocking with a backlog -> routine edits to 412 records fail for reasons unrelated to those edits
- the severity is edited rather than republished -> the version that decisions cited no longer says what it said

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The advisory version and its findings.
- The backlog trend to zero.
- The blocking version with its own approval.

## US-0407. Trace a figure whose lineage crosses a manual step

- **Persona**: auditor
- **Workflow**: [`WF-DQG-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Trace the lineage of a derived figure
- **Dimension**: `DQG` Data quality and lineage governance
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `data-quality`, `lineage`, `manual-node`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A published headcount figure includes an adjustment entered manually on 2026-06-14.
- The adjustment has an actor and a reason but no upstream computation.
- The rest of the lineage is automated.

**Story**

> As an auditor, I want the manual step to be visible as manual rather than looking like a computation, so that I can tell which part of the figure somebody typed

**Scenario**

The lineage walks back through automated nodes to a manual adjustment. The adjustment has an actor, a timestamp and a reason but no inputs, because a person entered it. Rendering it like the automated nodes would make it look computed. It is marked as a manual entry with its actor and reason, which is what tells an auditor which part of the figure rests on a person's judgement.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a lineage node is a manual entry
   **When** the trace renders
   **Then** it is marked as manual with its actor and reason rather than appearing as a computation
2. **Given** the manual node has no inputs
   **When** the trace renders
   **Then** the absence of inputs is shown as expected for a manual entry rather than as a gap
3. **Given** the reason is free text
   **When** the trace renders
   **Then** it is shown verbatim, because it is the only explanation the node has
4. **Given** the adjustment is later superseded
   **When** the trace is re-read
   **Then** the superseded adjustment remains in the lineage with its interval

**Data read**

- `analytics`: the published figure and its computation
- `provenance`: the lineage nodes, their kinds and their actors

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The auditor receives FULL disclosure of the lineage including the manual node's actor and reason.
- Nodes outside the auditor's purpose are DENIED by name with their position visible.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the manual node renders like a computation -> the auditor cannot tell which part of the figure was typed
- the manual node has no reason recorded -> the node is unexplainable and the figure it feeds is weakened

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The node kinds distinguishing manual from computed.
- The actor, timestamp and reason for the manual entry.
- Zero-effect receipt.

## US-0409. Roll back a committed import batch

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-DOP-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Stage, validate and commit a bulk data import
- **Dimension**: `DOP` DataOps and administration
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `dataops`, `rollback`, `corrective-batch`, `dispatched-effects`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A 620-row import committed on 2026-07-14 used a wrong effective date for every row.
- The error is found on 2026-07-16 after downstream effects dispatched for 118 rows.
- The tenant asks to roll the batch back.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want a rollback to be a corrective batch rather than an undo, so that the 118 dispatched effects are handled rather than pretended away

**Scenario**

The batch committed 620 changes with a wrong effective date. There is no undo: 118 of them have dispatched downstream effects that the outside world has acted on. The rollback is a corrective batch of 620 corrections, each appending a corrected revision. The 118 with dispatched effects additionally produce repair intents for their downstream consequences. Calling it a rollback would suggest the 118 can be made not to have happened.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the batch committed 620 changes
   **When** a rollback is requested
   **Then** a corrective batch of 620 corrections is produced rather than an undo
2. **Given** 118 rows dispatched downstream effects
   **When** the correction runs
   **Then** those 118 additionally produce repair intents for their downstream consequences
3. **Given** the corrections commit
   **When** the record is read
   **Then** the original 620 revisions remain in the chain alongside their corrections
4. **Given** a corrected row fails
   **When** the batch completes
   **Then** per-row outcomes are reported and the failed correction is independently addressable
5. **Given** the original batch's approval is examined
   **When** the correction is approved
   **Then** the correction has its own approval rather than inheriting the original's

**Data read**

- `dataops`: the committed batch and its per-row outcomes
- `integration`: the 118 dispatched effects and their observations
- `people`: the 620 changed records

**Data written**

- `operations`: repair intents for the 118 dispatched effects
- `people`: 620 corrective revisions

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The affected workers receive FULL disclosure of the correction affecting them.
- The batch-level error is DENIED to individual workers and FULL to the data authority.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the batch is deleted -> the 620 original revisions disappear and the 118 downstream effects have no explanation
- the corrections are applied without their own approval -> a second bulk write runs on the authority of the first, which was the one that was wrong

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The original batch and its per-row outcomes.
- The corrective batch with its own approval.
- The repair intents for the dispatched effects.

## US-0411. Explain why a projection disagrees with the authoritative stream

- Tenant acme. A people projection reports 1,798 active workers; the authoritative stream reports 1,802.

**Story**

The projection reports four fewer active workers than the authoritative stream. The projection watermark is forty minutes behind, and four activations occurred in that window. The difference is fully explained by the lag. That is not a defect: a projection is eventually consistent by design.    **Then** the explanation can be checked rather than taken on trust

   **When** the comparison runs
   **Then** it is reported separately as unexplained and is the one worth investigating
4. **Given** the projection catches up
   **When** the comparison is repeated
   **Then** the difference resolves, which confirms the attribution

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the lag is not considered and an incident is opened -> effort goes into investigating a projection working as designed
- an unexplained difference is absorbed into the lag -> the real defect is hidden by a plausible explanation

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both watermarks and the lag.
- The intents in the lag window, named.
