## US-0601. Apply a court order that arrives after the worker has left

- **Persona**: payroll administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PAY-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Set up and apply a wage garnishment
- **Dimension**: `PAY` Payroll
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US state variation
- **Tags**: `payroll`, `post-termination-order`, `residual-payment`, `court-answer`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A garnishment order for former worker W-170910 arrives 2026-07-08.
- They terminated 2026-06-19 and final pay settled 2026-06-19.
- A residual commission of 3,400.00 USD is scheduled for 2026-08-14.

**Story**

> As a payroll administrator, I want the order to attach to the scheduled residual rather than being returned as unemployable, so that the employer answers the order accurately rather than conveniently

**Scenario**

The worker has left and a payment is still owed. Returning the order as inapplicable would be convenient and wrong: the employer holds funds payable to the debtor and the order reaches them. The order is applied to the 2026-08-14 residual with the state's disposable earnings computation, the court is answered stating the employment ended and a residual payment is scheduled, and the order is closed after that payment.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the order arrives after termination
   **When** it is evaluated
   **Then** it is applied to any scheduled payment rather than returned as inapplicable, and its ObligationState stays PENDING between the answer and the payment
2. **Given** the answer to the court is composed
   **When** it is sent
   **Then** it states the termination date and the scheduled residual rather than only that employment ended
3. **Given** the residual is paid
   **When** the deduction is computed
   **Then** it uses the state's disposable earnings computation on that payment
4. **Given** no further payment is scheduled
   **When** the order is answered
   **Then** the answer states that no funds are held and the order is returned with that basis
5. **Given** a further payment is scheduled after the order is closed
   **When** it is evaluated
   **Then** the order's closure is revisited rather than the payment being made free of it

**Data read**

- `documents`: the order in a restricted compartment
- `payroll`: the final pay, the scheduled residual and the disposable earnings basis
- `people`: the termination date

**Data written**

- `documents`: the answer to the court
- `payroll`: the deduction on the residual

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The former worker receives FULL disclosure of the deduction and its basis on their statement.
- The order's full contents are DENIED to their former manager and to anyone outside payroll.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the order is returned as inapplicable -> the employer holds funds payable to the debtor and answers the court inaccurately
- the residual is paid without the deduction -> the employer may become liable for the amount that should have been withheld

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The order and its receipt date.
- The scheduled residual and the deduction computation.
- The answer to the court and its statements.

## US-0602. Migrate a benefits plan to a new carrier mid plan year

- **Persona**: benefits administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-BEN-013`](../workflows/catalog.md) Migrate a plan to a new carrier and transfer its accumulators
- **Dimension**: `BEN` Benefits
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `benefits`, `carrier-migration`, `accumulator-transfer`, `mid-year`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. The dental carrier changes effective 2026-08-01, mid plan year.
- 1,420 subscribers and 2,180 dependants are enrolled.
- Deductibles accrued year to date must carry over under the new contract.

**Story**

> As a benefits administrator, I want the accrued deductibles to transfer rather than resetting at the carrier change, so that a family that met its deductible in March does not meet it again in August

**Scenario**

A carrier change mid year resets nothing from the subscriber's point of view: the plan year is the same and the deductible they met in March was met. The migration transfers year-to-date accumulators per subscriber, the new carrier confirms receipt per record rather than in aggregate, and any record the new carrier cannot match is held rather than being enrolled with a zero accumulator.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the carrier changes mid plan year
   **When** the migration runs
   **Then** year-to-date accumulators transfer per subscriber rather than resetting
2. **Given** the new carrier confirms receipt
   **When** the confirmation is processed
   **Then** it is confirmed per record rather than as an aggregate count
3. **Given** a record cannot be matched by the new carrier
   **When** it is handled
   **Then** it is held with ExecutionState SUSPENDED rather than enrolled with a zero accumulator
4. **Given** a claim is submitted for a date before the change
   **When** it is routed
   **Then** it goes to the prior carrier, whose runout period is recorded and communicated
5. **Given** a subscriber's accumulator differs between the two carriers' records
   **When** the discrepancy is found
   **Then** it is resolved against the tenant's own claims data rather than by accepting either carrier's figure

**Data read**

- `benefits`: the enrollments, the accumulators and both contracts
- `integration`: both carrier connectors and their confirmations

**Data written**

- `benefits`: the transferred accumulators and the held records
- `integration`: the per-record confirmations

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Subscribers receive FULL disclosure of their transferred accumulators and the runout arrangements.
- Other subscribers' accumulators are WITHHELD, and the new carrier receives only the records within its contract's scope.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- accumulators reset at the change -> families meet their deductible twice in one plan year
- receipt is confirmed in aggregate -> a missing record is invisible until a claim is denied

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The accumulators before and after the transfer.
- The per-record confirmations and the held records.
- The prior carrier's runout period.

## US-0603. An integration replays a termination event to trigger a second final pay

- **Persona**: integration system
- **Workflow**: [`WF-INT-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Receive and verify an inbound webhook event
- **Dimension**: `INT` Integration and external systems
- **Complexity tier**: 5
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `integration`, `adversarial`, `business-key-idempotency`, `replay`, `tier-5`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A termination event for W-170920 was delivered and processed on 2026-05-11.
- The same signed event is delivered again on 2026-05-13 with a fresh timestamp and signature.
- The event's business key identifies the same termination.

**Story**

> As an integration system, I want the second delivery to be suppressed on its business key rather than passing the freshness check, so that a re-signed event does not produce a second final pay

**Scenario**

The second delivery is not a stale replay: it carries a current timestamp and a valid signature, so the replay window does not catch it. What identifies it is the business key, which names a termination that has already been processed. The event is suppressed on that key, the suppression is recorded as a duplicate rather than as an error, and the sender receives the original outcome so a genuine retry after a lost response also works.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an event's business key names an already-processed transition
   **When** it is delivered
   **Then** it is suppressed on the key, no second RequestState is created, and the original intent's own state is unchanged by the delivery
2. **Given** the event carries a fresh timestamp and a valid signature
   **When** the checks run
   **Then** the freshness and signature checks pass and the business key check is what refuses it
3. **Given** the sender lost the original response
   **When** the retry is delivered
   **Then** it receives the original outcome rather than an error, so a genuine retry is served
4. **Given** the suppression is recorded
   **When** the log is inspected
   **Then** it is recorded as a duplicate with both delivery times rather than as a processing failure
5. **Given** a second genuine termination for the same worker is later delivered
   **When** it is evaluated
   **Then** its business key differs because the employment interval differs, so it is processed rather than suppressed

**Data read**

- `integration`: the inbox, the business keys and the prior processing outcome
- `payroll`: the final pay already produced
- `people`: the termination and its employment interval

**Data written**

- `integration`: the suppression record with both delivery times

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The sender receives FULL disclosure of the original outcome, which is what a legitimate retry needs.
- Whether the suppression was a duplicate or a fresh refusal is WITHHELD from the sender, whose response is identical either way.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the freshness check is relied on alone -> a re-signed event with a current timestamp passes and produces a second final pay
- the duplicate returns an error -> a genuine retry after a lost response is indistinguishable from an attack and legitimate integrations break

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both delivery records with their timestamps and signatures.
- The business key and the prior processing outcome.
- The suppression recorded as a duplicate.

- The plan line remains reserved against it.

**Story**

> As a hiring manager, I want the decision to release the plan line rather than leaving it reserved, so that the capacity goes back to the plan rather than sitting idle

**Scenario**

The decision not to fill is a decision, and leaving it unrecorded holds a plan line against a position nobody intends to fill. Recording it releases the reservation, returns the line to the plan and leaves the position in a deliberate vacant state with a review date rather than an indefinite one.

**Acceptance criteria**

2. **Given** the position remains open
   **When** it is read
   **Then** it shows as deliberately unfilled with a review date rather than as an ordinary vacancy
3. **Given** the decision is reversed
   **When** a new reservation is requested
   **Then** it competes for plan capacity on its merits rather than reclaiming a line that was held

**Data read**

- `planning`: the plan line and its reservation
- `position`: the position and its vacancy

**Data written**

- `planning`: the released line
- `position`: the deliberate vacancy with its review date

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The finance partner receives FULL disclosure of the release and the restored capacity.
- The hiring manager's reasoning is DENIED to other units competing for the line; the release itself is disclosed.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the decision is not recorded -> a line is reserved indefinitely against a position nobody intends to fill
- the vacancy has no review date -> a deliberate decision becomes an indefinite state nobody revisits

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The decision with its date and review date.
- The reservation release receipt.
- The restored plan capacity.

The increase existed because of the promotion.    **When** the differencing check runs

3. **Given** the suppression is applied
   **When** the report renders
   **Then** the threshold and the number of suppressed cells are stated

**Data read**

- `analytics`: the report definition and the reporting threshold
- `people`: the population and the grouping attributes

**Data written**

- `analytics`: the report with its suppressions

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The auditor receives FULL disclosure of the thirty-three cells above threshold.
- The suppressed cells are WITHHELD and the differencing check ensures the totals do not reveal them.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the whole report is refused -> thirty-three usable cells are lost for eleven that cannot be shown
- only the small cells are suppressed -> subtraction from the totals recovers them and the suppression achieves nothing

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The threshold and the cell counts.

**Data written**

- `case`: the membership with its explicit scope

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The new member receives FULL disclosure within their granted scope.
- Notes outside the scope are DENIED by name with their existence visible, so the member can request the history rather than being unable to see that it exists.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- membership grants the full history by default -> an investigator brought in for one interview reads every prior complainant interview
- out-of-scope notes are concealed entirely -> the member cannot tell there is a history to request and the case owner is never asked

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**
