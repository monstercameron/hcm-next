## US-0501. Resolve an approval requirement whose only approver is the requester

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Resolve an approval requirement and bind the decision
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `self-approval`, `escalation`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A cost centre change requires approval by the cost centre owner.
- The requester is the cost centre owner for CC-2240.
- No alternate owner is configured.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the requirement to escalate rather than resolve to the requester, so that nobody approves their own request because they happen to hold the role

**Scenario**

The requirement names the cost centre owner and the requester is that owner. Resolving it to them would let the role coincidence stand in for a second judgement. The requester is excluded from their own approval pool, which empties it, and the requirement escalates to the next authority in the cost centre hierarchy. The escalation is recorded with its reason, so the approval reads as an escalated one rather than as an ordinary owner approval.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the requester is the only approver in the pool
   **When** the requirement is resolved
   **Then** they are excluded and the pool is empty
2. **Given** the pool is empty after exclusion
   **When** the escalation runs
   **Then** it goes to the next authority in the hierarchy rather than the requirement being waived
3. **Given** the escalated approval is recorded
   **When** it is read
   **Then** it names the escalation and its reason rather than appearing as an ordinary owner approval
4. **Given** no next authority exists
   **When** the escalation runs
   **Then** the request is blocked with RequestState PENDING_APPROVAL and the gap in the hierarchy is raised as its own configuration finding

**Data read**

- `budget`: the cost centre ownership and the hierarchy
- `identity`: the requester's roles
- `workflow`: the approval requirement and its resolution rules

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the exclusion, the escalation and the resulting approval

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The requester receives FULL disclosure that their own approval was excluded and where the requirement escalated to.
- The escalated approver's deliberation is DENIED to the requester; the decision and its basis are disclosed.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the requirement resolves to the requester -> a role coincidence replaces a second judgement, which is the failure the separation exists to prevent
- the requirement is waived when the pool empties -> the control disappears exactly when it is hardest to satisfy

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The pool before and after the exclusion.
- The escalation with its reason.
- The approval recorded as escalated.

## US-0502. Hand off a claimed work item to someone who has not accepted it

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Claim, complete and hand off a work item
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `handoff`, `continuous-ownership`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Work item WI-7710 is claimed by an HR partner on 2026-04-06.
- They hand it to a colleague on 2026-04-09 before going on leave.
- The colleague does not open it until 2026-04-14.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the handoff to leave the item owned rather than unowned between the two, so that an item does not sit unclaimed while its clock runs

**Scenario**

The handoff is offered on the ninth and accepted on the fourteenth. Between those dates the item must be owned by someone. Releasing it on offer would leave it unclaimed for five days with its service clock running. The original holder remains the owner until acceptance, and the pending handoff is visible on the item so a supervisor can see it is in transition rather than stalled.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a handoff is offered
   **When** the item's ownership is read
   **Then** the original holder remains the owner until the handoff is accepted
2. **Given** the handoff is pending
   **When** the item renders in a supervisor view
   **Then** the pending transition is visible rather than the item appearing simply claimed
3. **Given** the handoff is not accepted within the configured window
   **When** the window elapses
   **Then** it escalates to the queue supervisor rather than the item being released to an unclaimed pool

**Data read**

- `case`: the service clock running on the item
- `workflow`: the item, its claim and the handoff offer

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the handoff offer, its acceptance and the ownership interval

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The supervisor receives FULL disclosure of the ownership interval and the pending handoff.
- The item's case content is DENIED to the offered recipient until they accept, so an unaccepted offer discloses nothing.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the item is released on offer -> it sits unclaimed with its clock running and nobody responsible
- the recipient can read the case before accepting -> an offer becomes a way to read a case without taking responsibility for it

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The claim, the offer and the acceptance with their times.
- The continuous ownership interval.
- The escalation if the offer is not accepted.

## US-0503. Recover a workflow instance whose external call outcome is unknown

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-WRK-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Recover a workflow instance after a runtime failure
- **Dimension**: `WRK` Workflow and human work
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `workflow`, `recovery`, `unknown-outcome`, `read-back`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Instance WI-8020 dispatched a carrier enrollment at 2026-07-21T14:02 and the process crashed at 14:03.
- The carrier's response was never recorded.
- The carrier supports a read-back by enrollment reference.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the recovery to determine the outcome by reading back rather than by retrying, so that we do not create a second enrollment to find out whether the first one landed

**Scenario**

The instance dispatched and crashed before recording a response. Retrying is the obvious move and the wrong one: if the first call landed, the retry creates a duplicate enrollment that the subscriber will discover as a double deduction. The recovery reads back by the enrollment reference, determines that the enrollment exists, and records the outcome as observed rather than as returned. The instance resumes from the step after the dispatch.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the outcome of a dispatched effectful call is unknown
   **When** recovery runs
   **Then** it reads back by reference rather than retrying
2. **Given** the read-back finds the enrollment
   **When** the outcome is recorded
   **Then** ExecutionState reaches COMPLETED on the read-back rather than on a returned response, so the evidence says how the outcome was learned
3. **Given** the read-back finds nothing
   **When** the outcome is recorded
   **Then** the call is retried, because absence at the target is the only safe basis for a retry
4. **Given** the target supports no read-back
   **When** recovery runs
   **Then** the instance is held for human determination rather than retried blind, and the held state names the reason
5. **Given** the read-back itself fails
   **When** recovery runs
   **Then** the instance stays held and the read-back is retried, because a failed read is not an absence

**Data read**

- `benefits`: the enrollment reference
- `integration`: the carrier connector and the read-back capability
- `workflow`: the instance, its checkpoint and the dispatch record

**Data written**

- `workflow`: the observed outcome and the resumed instance

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of the dispatch, the read-back and the determined outcome.
- The subscriber's enrollment detail is DENIED to operations beyond the reference and the existence answer.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the call is retried blind -> a duplicate enrollment is created and the subscriber sees a double deduction
- a failed read-back is treated as an absence -> the retry runs on a read that never answered, which is the same blind retry with extra steps

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The dispatch record and the crash point.
- The read-back and its result.
- The outcome recorded as observed rather than returned.

- The next rotation is due 2026-04-18.

**Story**

> As an integration system, I want the lease to report its last rotation and its next due date, so that a rotation that silently stopped is visible

**Scenario**

The lease reports its last rotation date, its interval and its next due date. A rotation schedule that quietly stops produces a lease that keeps working until it does not, and the only warning is the date. Showing the next due date makes a stalled rotation visible before the expiry rather than at it.

   **Then** the last rotation date, the interval and the next due date are all returned
2. **Given** the next due date has passed
   **When** the lease is read

3. **Given** the read runs
   **When** the ledger is inspected

**Data read**

- `security`: the credential lease, its rotation history and its interval

**Data written**

- none; this story must leave no domain write

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Operations receives FULL disclosure of the rotation metadata.
- The credential value is DENIED to every reader including the connector's own operators, who use it by reference rather than by value.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- only the last rotation date is shown -> an overdue rotation looks the same as a recent one
- the rotation history is not retained -> a rotation that stopped six months ago cannot be distinguished from one that never started

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The rotation history with its dates.
- The interval and the computed next due date.
- Zero-effect receipt.

## US-0505. Contain a credential compromise without stopping the business

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-SEC-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Investigate and contain a suspected credential compromise
- **Dimension**: `SEC` Authorization, security and identity
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `security`, `compromise-containment`, `ordering`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A payroll connector credential is suspected compromised at 2026-09-24T11:20.
- A payroll run is mid settlement using that connector.
- The settlement covers 1,802 workers.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the containment to issue a new lease before revoking the old one, so that we do not stop a settlement to contain a suspicion

**Scenario**

Revoking first is the instinct and it would abort a settlement covering 1,802 workers mid flight, which turns a suspected compromise into a certain payroll failure. A new lease is issued, the in-flight settlement is moved to it, and the old lease is then revoked. The window between issuance and revocation is minutes rather than the hours a settlement takes, and it is recorded so the containment timeline is auditable.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a credential is suspected compromised during an in-flight settlement
   **When** containment runs
   **Then** a new lease is issued and the settlement is moved to it before the old lease's BusinessState becomes REVOKED, so the settlement's own ExecutionState is never interrupted
2. **Given** the settlement is moved
   **When** the old lease is revoked
   **Then** the revocation follows the move rather than preceding it, and the interval between them is recorded
3. **Given** no settlement is in flight
   **When** containment runs
   **Then** the old lease is revoked immediately, because the reason for the ordering is absent
4. **Given** the move to the new lease fails
   **When** containment is evaluated
   **Then** the old lease is revoked anyway and the settlement is aborted, because a possible compromise outranks a recoverable settlement

**Data read**

- `integration`: the connector and its active operations
- `payroll`: the in-flight settlement and its coverage
- `security`: the lease, its usage and the compromise indicators

**Data written**

- `security`: the new lease, the revocation and the containment timeline

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The security function receives FULL disclosure of the indicators and the containment timeline.
- The compromise suspicion is WITHHELD from the connector's counterparty unless the investigation establishes exposure of their data.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the old lease is revoked first -> a settlement covering 1,802 workers aborts mid flight to contain a suspicion
- the ordering is used to defer revocation indefinitely -> the window is bounded by the move rather than by convenience, and an unmoved settlement forces the revocation

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The indicators and their timestamps.
- The new lease, the move and the revocation with their intervals.
- The settlement's continuity across the change.

## US-0506. Refuse to activate a principal whose sponsor has left

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-SEC-004`](../workflows/catalog.md) Register a principal and establish its authentication
- **Dimension**: `SEC` Authorization, security and identity
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `security`, `principal-sponsorship`, `offboarding`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A service principal is registered on 2026-03-02 with a named human sponsor.
- The sponsor's employment ends 2026-05-30.
- The principal is still active.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want a principal whose sponsor has left to be surfaced rather than continuing unowned, so that every non-human principal has a person who answers for it

**Scenario**

The principal keeps working after its sponsor leaves, because a service principal has no reason to stop. That is exactly the problem: an active principal with no sponsor is an entitlement nobody owns. The sponsor's offboarding surfaces every principal they sponsor and requires a new sponsor before the last day. Without one the principal is suspended rather than left running, and suspension is reversible in a way that a year of unowned access is not.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a sponsor's employment ends
   **When** offboarding runs
   **Then** every principal they sponsor is surfaced and requires a new sponsor before the last day
2. **Given** no new sponsor is named by the last day
   **When** the deadline passes
   **Then** the principal is suspended rather than continuing unowned
3. **Given** a new sponsor accepts
   **When** the sponsorship is recorded
   **Then** it is an acceptance by the new sponsor with its own RequestState rather than an assignment made on their behalf

**Data read**

- `people`: the sponsor's employment end date
- `security`: the principal, its sponsor and its entitlements

**Data written**

- `security`: the re-sponsorship or the suspension

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The new sponsor receives FULL disclosure of the principal's entitlements before accepting.
- The principal's credential value is DENIED to the sponsor, who owns the accountability rather than the secret.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the principal keeps running unowned -> an entitlement set nobody answers for persists until an access review finds it
- a new sponsor is assigned without accepting -> the accountability is nominal and the next review finds the same gap

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The sponsorship record and its history.
- The offboarding surfacing of sponsored principals.
- The acceptance or the suspension.

## US-0507. Approve a timecard that contains an unresolved exception

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-170420's timecard for the period ending 2026-05-15 has one unresolved missing punch.
- The manager approves the timecard on 2026-05-17.
- The payroll cutoff is 2026-05-18.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want the approval to be refused while an exception is unresolved rather than approving around it, so that an approved timecard means the hours were reviewed

**Scenario**

The timecard carries an unresolved missing punch. Approving it would produce an approved timecard containing hours nobody determined, and the approval is the artifact a later wage claim is tested against. The approval is refused with the exception named. The manager resolves the punch, which is a separate recorded act, and then approves. Two acts rather than one, because they are two decisions.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** an exception is unresolved
   **When** the approval is submitted
   **Then** the approval is refused, the timecard's BusinessState stays UNAPPROVED, and the exception and its date are named
2. **Given** the exception is resolved
   **When** the approval is resubmitted
   **Then** it succeeds, and the resolution and the approval are two separate recorded acts
3. **Given** the cutoff arrives with the exception unresolved
   **When** the run is prepared
   **Then** the timecard is excluded from the run with the reason recorded, rather than being included unapproved

**Data read**

- `payroll`: the run cutoff
- `time`: the timecard, its punches and the exception

**Data written**

- `time`: the exception resolution and the approval

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of the exception, its resolution and the approval.
- The manager's queue of other workers' exceptions is DENIED to the worker.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the approval covers an unresolved exception -> an approved timecard contains hours nobody determined
- the timecard is included in the run unapproved -> the run pays hours that were never reviewed, which is the harder error to unwind

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The exception and its resolution.
- The approval, separate from the resolution.
- The exclusion if the cutoff passes unresolved.

## US-0509. Publish a schedule that breaches a predictive scheduling rule

- **Persona**: manager
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TIM-003`](../workflows/catalog.md) Publish a schedule and handle a shift swap
- **Dimension**: `TIM` Time, attendance and scheduling
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US state variation
- **Tags**: `time`, `predictive-scheduling`, `premium`, `oregon`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A retail schedule for the week of 2026-07-06 is published on 2026-07-01 in Oregon.
- Oregon requires 14 days advance notice of the written schedule.
- Two workers are affected by a shift added five days out.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want the late change to compute its premium rather than being published as an ordinary change, so that the workers are paid what the rule requires without anyone remembering to

**Scenario**

The schedule is published five days out against a 14-day requirement. The publication is not blocked, because a business sometimes has to change a schedule and the statute prices that rather than forbidding it. The premium is computed per affected worker and per changed shift, attached to the change, and flows to payroll. A worker who declines a shift added with less than the required rest or notice is recorded as declining without consequence.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a schedule change falls inside the notice period
   **When** it is published
   **Then** the premium is computed per affected worker and per changed shift and attached to the change
2. **Given** the premium is computed
   **When** payroll runs
   **Then** it flows as a distinct earning code rather than being folded into base hours
3. **Given** a worker declines a shift added inside the notice period
   **When** the decline is recorded
   **Then** it is recorded as a permitted decline with no consequence attached, and the premium's ObligationState is unaffected by the decline
4. **Given** the worker accepts the shift
   **When** the acceptance is recorded
   **Then** the premium is still owed, because acceptance does not waive it

**Data read**

- `people`: the affected workers and their locations
- `regulatory`: the state predictive scheduling rule and its premium
- `time`: the schedule, its publication history and the changed shifts

**Data written**

- `payroll`: the premium earnings
- `time`: the published change and the recorded declines

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The affected workers receive FULL disclosure of the premium owed and the basis for it.
- Other workers' schedules and premiums are DENIED to them beyond the shifts they are assigned to.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the change is published as ordinary -> the premium is never computed and surfaces as a claim
- acceptance is treated as a waiver -> the premium is not paid on the shifts most likely to be accepted

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The publication times against the notice requirement.
- The per-worker premium computation.
- The declines recorded as permitted.

## US-0512. Collect multi-rater feedback where one rater is the subject's report

- **Persona**: manager
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TAL-006`](../workflows/catalog.md) Collect continuous and multi-rater feedback outside the review cycle
- **Dimension**: `TAL` Talent and performance
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `talent`, `multi-rater`, `group-threshold`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A 360 collection for manager W-170450 runs 2026-04-06 to 2026-04-24.
- Seven raters respond: three peers, three direct reports and one skip-level manager.
- The reporting threshold for a rater group is three.

**Story**

> As a manager, I want the direct report group to be reported only in aggregate, so that a report can answer honestly about their own manager

Three direct reports respond, which meets the group threshold of three. - **Workflow**: [`WF-PAY-006`](../workflows/catalog.md) Reconcile the payroll ledger to the general ledger
- **Dimension**: `PAY` Payroll
- **Complexity tier**: 2
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `payroll`, `accrual`, `period-attribution`, `tier-2`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. The 2026-06 accounting period closes 2026-07-05.
- The 2026-06-30 payroll run settles 2026-07-02.
- The accrual must land in June.

**Story**

> As a finance partner, I want the accrual to post to the period the work belongs to rather than the period it is computed in, so that June's cost is in June

**Scenario**

The work was performed in June and the settlement happens in July. Posting on the settlement date would move a month of labour cost into July and leave June understated. The accrual posts to June with a reversal in July when the actual settles, and the two entries reference each other so the pair is visible rather than looking like a duplicate.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the work belongs to June and settles in July
   **When** the accrual posts
   **Then** it posts to the June period with a July reversal, and the accrual's ObligationState stays PENDING until the reversal posts against the actual
2. **Given** the actual settles
   **When** the reversal posts
   **Then** the accrual and its reversal reference each other so the pair reads as one transaction
3. **Given** the June period closes before the actual is known
   **When** the close runs
   **Then** it closes with the accrual and the reversal falls in July rather than the close being held

**Data read**

- `finance`: the accounting periods and their close dates
- `payroll`: the run, its period and its settlement date

**Data written**

- `finance`: the accrual entry and its reversal

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Finance receives FULL disclosure of the accrual, the reversal and the variance between them.
- Individual workers' pay is DENIED to the general ledger view, which carries aggregates by cost centre.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the accrual posts on the settlement date -> a month of labour cost moves into the following period
- the reversal is omitted -> July carries both the accrual and the actual and is overstated

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The accrual with its period and basis.
- The reversal referencing it.
- The variance between accrual and actual.

- **Workflow**: [`WF-PAY-006`](../workflows/catalog.md) Reconcile the payroll ledger to the general ledger
- **Dimension**: `PAY` Payroll
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `payroll`, `ledger-reconciliation`, `duplicate-source`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A 214,800.00 USD labour cost for 2026-06 appears in the ledger twice.
- One posting came from the payroll bridge and one from a legacy accrual job.
- The legacy job was supposed to be retired in April.

**Story**

> As a finance partner, I want the duplicate to be traced to its source rather than netted out, so that the legacy job is stopped rather than compensated for every month

**Scenario**

The duplicate can be corrected with a reversing entry every month, which is cheap and permanent. Tracing it shows a legacy accrual job that was supposed to be retired in April and was not. The reversal is made for June, the job is stopped, and the prior months are examined because the job did not start duplicating in June.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** a posting appears twice from two sources
   **When** the reconciliation runs
   **Then** each posting is traced to its originating system rather than the pair being netted
2. **Given** the source is a job that should have been retired
   **When** the finding is recorded
   **Then** the job is stopped and the retirement failure is its own finding
3. **Given** the job was running before June
   **When** the prior periods are examined
   **Then** the duplicate is checked for April and May rather than assumed to have started in June
4. **Given** a prior period is closed
   **When** a duplicate is found there
   **Then** it is corrected through the amendment path rather than being adjusted into the open period

**Data read**

- `finance`: the ledger postings and their originating systems
- `operations`: the legacy job's schedule and retirement record
- `payroll`: the bridge postings and their references

**Data written**

- `finance`: the reversal and the prior period examinations
- `operations`: the job stop and the retirement failure finding

**Authorization and disclosure**

- Finance receives FULL disclosure of both postings and their sources.
- Individual workers' pay is DENIED to the ledger reconciliation, which operates on aggregates by cost centre.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the duplicate is netted out monthly -> the cause runs indefinitely and the correction becomes a routine
- only June is examined -> April and May carry the same duplicate and nobody looks

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both postings with their source references.
- The job's schedule and its failed retirement.
- The prior period examinations and any corrections.

## US-0598. Handle a data subject request from someone who may not be the subject

- **Persona**: compliance officer
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PRV-001`](../workflows/catalog.md) Fulfil a data subject access request
- **Dimension**: `PRV` Privacy and data governance
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: EU, Ireland
- **Tags**: `privacy`, `identity-verification`, `clock-suspension`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme, Acme Ireland Ltd. An access request arrives 2026-04-14 from an external email address.
- It claims to be from former worker W-170900 and supplies their employee number.
- The employee number appears on payslips and internal directories.

**Story**

> As a compliance officer, I want the identity to be verified by something an insider could not supply, so that an access request is not a way to obtain someone else's file

**Scenario**

The employee number proves only that the requester saw a payslip or a directory, both of which many people have. Verification uses a factor tied to the subject rather than to their employment: a code to the personal contact on file, or a documentary check. The one-month clock is suspended for the verification period rather than running while the requester is unidentified, and the suspension and its length are recorded so a later complaint can see it.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the request supplies only widely available identifiers
   **When** verification runs
   **Then** it requires a factor tied to the subject rather than to their employment
2. **Given** verification is outstanding
   **When** the clock is evaluated
   **Then** it is suspended for the verification period and the suspension is recorded with its start
3. **Given** verification succeeds
   **When** the clock resumes
   **Then** the remaining period is computed from the suspension rather than restarting
4. **Given** verification fails
   **When** the request is closed
   **Then** it is closed as unverified with the attempts recorded, and no data is disclosed

**Data read**

- `people`: the subject's personal contact on file, used only to verify
- `privacy`: the request, the verification policy and the clock

**Data written**

- `privacy`: the verification attempts, the clock suspension and the outcome

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The requester receives FULL disclosure of the verification requirement and the suspended clock.
- Whether the employee number matches a real record is WITHHELD from an unverified requester, so the verification step does not itself confirm the subject exists.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the employee number is accepted as proof -> anyone who has seen a payslip can obtain the file
- the clock runs during verification -> the controller is pressed toward disclosing to an unverified requester by its own deadline

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The verification requirement and the attempts.
- The clock suspension with its interval.
- The outcome, disclosing nothing if unverified.

## US-0599. Restore a sandbox from a backup that predates a masking rule

- **Persona**: IT or access administrator
- **Workflow**: [`WF-TEN-002`](../workflows/catalog.md) Reset a sandbox from production with masking
- **Dimension**: `TEN` Tenant and platform administration
- **Complexity tier**: 3
- **Jurisdiction**: US federal
- **Tags**: `tenant`, `sandbox-restore`, `masking-policy-version`, `tier-3`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. A sandbox is restored on 2026-08-19 from a 2026-05-02 backup.
- A masking rule for a new field was added 2026-06-14.
- The backup predates the rule.

**Story**

> As an IT or access administrator, I want the restore to apply the current masking policy rather than the one in force at backup, so that a restore does not reintroduce unmasked data

**Scenario**

The backup was taken before the masking rule existed, so restoring it verbatim puts the unmasked field back into the sandbox. The restore applies the current masking policy to the restored data rather than the policy in force when the backup was taken, and verification samples the restored data for the field before the sandbox is opened to its users.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** the backup predates a masking rule
   **When** the restore runs
   **Then** the current masking policy is applied to the restored data
2. **Given** the restore completes
   **When** verification runs
   **Then** the restored data is sampled for the newly masked field before the sandbox is opened
3. **Given** verification finds unmasked values
   **When** the sandbox is evaluated
   **Then** it stays closed and the restore is treated as failed rather than being opened with a follow-up task
4. **Given** the masking policy has since been relaxed for a field
   **When** the restore runs
   **Then** the current policy applies, and the relaxation is a decision with its own record rather than a restore artifact

**Data read**

- `privacy`: the masking policy versions and their intervals
- `tenant`: the backup, its date and the sandbox

**Data written**

- `tenant`: the restore with its applied policy version and the verification result

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The sandbox users receive FULL disclosure of masked data only.
- The unmasked source values are WITHHELD from the sandbox entirely, including from the verification report, which asserts absence rather than showing what was masked.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the backup's own policy is applied -> an unmasked field is reintroduced and the rule that removed it is undone by a restore
- the sandbox opens with verification pending -> users read data that has not been verified as masked

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- The backup date and the policy versions.
- The applied policy version on the restore.
- The verification sampling and its result.

## US-0600. Terminate a worker in two entities where only one relationship ends

- **Persona**: HR business partner
- **Workflow**: [`WF-PEO-007`](../workflows/catalog.md) Add a second concurrent employment for one worker
- **Dimension**: `PEO` People and core HR
- **Complexity tier**: 4
- **Jurisdiction**: US and Canada
- **Tags**: `people`, `concurrent-employment`, `scoped-termination`, `cross-border`

**Preconditions**

- Tenant acme. Worker W-170780 holds employments in Acme US and Acme Canada.
- The Canadian employment ends 2026-11-30; the US one continues.
- Canada requires statutory notice or pay in lieu.

**Story**

> As an HR business partner, I want the ending employment's obligations to be computed without touching the continuing one, so that one termination does not deprovision a working employee

**Scenario**

One of two employments ends. The termination's obligations are computed against the Canadian employment alone: its notice entitlement, its final pay, its record of employment. Access, benefits and payroll for the US employment are untouched, which requires the deprovisioning to be scoped to entitlements whose granting basis is the ending employment rather than to the person. A person-scoped termination would lock out a working employee.

**Acceptance criteria**

1. **Given** one of two employments ends
   **When** the obligations are computed
   **Then** they are computed against the ending employment alone
2. **Given** deprovisioning runs
   **When** the scope is resolved
   **Then** only entitlements whose granting basis is the ending employment are revoked
3. **Given** the continuing employment shares an entitlement with the ending one
   **When** the revocation is evaluated
   **Then** it is retained, because a basis for it survives, and the retention is recorded with the surviving basis
4. **Given** the Canadian statutory notice is computed
   **When** the service is measured
   **Then** it is measured on the Canadian employment's service rather than on the person's total service across entities
5. **Given** the worker asks about their remaining benefits
   **When** the answer is composed
   **Then** it distinguishes coverage under each employment rather than presenting one combined position

**Data read**

- `benefits`: the coverage under each employment
- `identity`: the entitlements and their granting bases
- `people`: both employments and their service
- `regulatory`: the Canadian statutory notice rules

**Data written**

- `identity`: the scoped revocations and the retained entitlements
- `operations`: the Canadian notice and final pay obligations
- `people`: the ended Canadian employment

**Authorization and disclosure**

- The worker receives FULL disclosure of what ends and what continues, per employment.
- The US entity's ongoing terms are DENIED to the Canadian entity's administrators by name.

**Failure modes and expected refusals**

- the termination is scoped to the person -> a working employee is deprovisioned and loses access to a job they still hold
- service is measured across both employments -> the Canadian notice entitlement is computed on service the Canadian employer did not have

**Evidence and ledger facts expected**

- Both employments and the one that ended.
- The granting-basis scoping of the revocations.
- The Canadian notice computed on its own service.
