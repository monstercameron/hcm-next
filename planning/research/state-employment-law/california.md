# California Employment Law Research

**State:** California | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **Minimum wage complexity**: Three-tier system (general § 1182.12: $16.90/2026; fast-food § 1474: $20/hour; healthcare § 1182.14: $21–$25/hour by facility type); plus 20+ local ordinances with higher floors.
- **Wage notice timing**: Labor Code § 2810.5 requires written notice at hiring (not on change of role/pay), with 7-day notice for pay or employment term changes; no federal equivalent applies.
- **Pay transparency mandatory**: § 432.3 bans salary history questions, requires pay scale disclosure in job postings (15+ employees), mandates 3-year wage/job title record retention, and Gov. Code § 12999 requires pay-data reporting for 100+ employers.
- **Final pay penalties**: §§ 201–203 impose continuing wage penalties (daily wage × 30 days) for delayed final pay; § 203 good-faith defense requires factual/legal wage-entitlement dispute.
- **Personnel file access**: § 1198.5 gives employees 30-day right to inspect performance/training/grievance records; $750 penalty per violation.
- **AB 5 ABC test presumes employee**: All three prongs (A: no control, B: outside usual business, C: independent trade) must be satisfied to classify as contractor; failure on any = employee.
- **Non-compete void**: Business & Professions Code §§ 16600/16600.1 voids all non-compete agreements except sale/dissolution of business entity; AB 1076 (2024) required void-clause notice by Feb 14, 2024.
- **CCPA/CPRA applies to employee data**: Jan 1, 2023 exemption expiration means employee personal information is now a consumer right; employers must honor access/deletion/opt-out requests and maintain reasonable security.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Required Notices

California is at-will employment (no state restriction on at-will doctrine). However, Labor Code § 2810.5 (Wage Theft Prevention Act of 2011) mandates written notice **at the time of hiring** containing rate(s) of pay, basis (hourly/salary/piece/commission), allowances toward minimum wage, regular payday, employer name and business aliases, main office and mailing address, phone, workers' comp carrier name, paid sick leave rights, and anti-retaliation statement. Notice must be in the language used for employment communication.

On **any change** to pay rate, pay basis, payday, or allowances, employer must notify employee in writing **within 7 calendar days**. No specific notice required for role change per state law; federal FMLA notice applies if employer has 50+ employees.

Exempt employees are excluded from § 2810.5 wage notice requirement. No separate written contract requirement at hire unless employer chooses to create one.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions, and Pay Statements

**Minimum wage—general:** California Labor Code § 1182.12 establishes tiered minimum wage indexed annually by CPI-W (up to 3.5% increase, rounded to nearest dime). As of Jan 1, 2025: $16.50/hour; Jan 1, 2026: $16.90/hour. No tip credit allowed. Meals/lodging may offset minimum wage only by voluntary written agreement.

**Minimum wage—fast-food:** Labor Code § 1474 (effective April 1, 2024) requires $20.00/hour for fast-food restaurant employees (all employers, all employee counts).

**Minimum wage—healthcare:** Labor Code § 1182.14 (effective Oct 16, 2024) tiered by employer size and mission: Large hospital systems (10,000+ FTE): $23–$25/hour; community clinics/rural: $21/hour; high-government-payor/rural hospitals: $18–$25/hour (escalating). Employees must earn $300+ and have paid SDI in prior 18 months.

**Local ordinances:** San Francisco, Los Angeles, San Diego, Santa Clara County, and 20+ other cities/counties impose higher minimums (verify: current rates as of 2026; sample 2025 rates include SF $18.50–$20.65 depending on size/tipped status, LA $15.96, Santa Clara $17.20; Human Capital Management Suite must apply highest applicable rate if employee works in multiple jurisdictions). Some jurisdictions index annually; track effective dates and escalations.

**Overtime:** Labor Code § 510 requires 1.5x regular rate for hours over 8/day or 40/week; 2x rate for hours over 12/day or 8 hours on 7th consecutive day.

**Meal and rest breaks:** § 512 requires unpaid 30-minute meal breaks when work exceeds 5 hours (and another 30 minutes if over 12 hours); § 226.7 mandates paid 10-minute rest for every 4 hours worked. Employer must provide, not employee request only.

**Pay frequency:** § 204 requires wages at least semimonthly on designated paydays; exceptions exist for certain professions. § 226 requires itemized wage statement each pay period showing: (1) gross/net wages, (2) hours worked, (3) rates, (4) deductions itemized, (5) pay-period dates, (6) employee name and last 4 SSN digits, (7) employer legal name and address, (8) piece-rate units, (9) employee's choice of payment method (for independent contractors). Wage statements may be provided electronically if employee consents.

**Final pay upon termination:** § 201 mandates all earned wages (including accrued vacation) due immediately upon discharge or mass layoff. § 202 permits 72-hour notice to resign and receive final pay (or immediate pay). § 203 imposes continuing wage penalty (employee's daily wage × 30 days, or daily rate × days between due date and final payment, whichever is smaller) if employer willfully fails to pay wages on time, unless employer has good-faith defense (factual/legal dispute over wage entitlement, documented).

**Deductions:** § 200 prohibits deductions except those legally required (taxes, garnishment) or employee-authorized in writing; § 226 requires aggregated deductions shown as one item if employee-written-authorized.

**Pay statement penalties:** § 226 imposes $250/employee per violation (initial citation), $1,000/employee for subsequent violations, for failure to provide itemized statement or maintain 3-year records.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity: Salary Range, History Ban, Pay Data Reporting, Equal Pay

**Salary history ban:** Labor Code § 432.3 (amended by SB 1162, effective Jan 1, 2023) prohibits employers from orally or in writing seeking salary history of applicants, unless applicant voluntarily discloses. Employer may not rely on prior salary to set compensation. Employer may accept voluntary salary information if candidate offers it freely.

**Pay scale disclosure:** § 432.3 requires employers to provide pay scale for position upon reasonable request from applicant or current employee. Employers with 15+ employees must **include pay scale in all job postings** (internal and external). Third-party recruiters posting on behalf of 15+ employee employers must also include scale. Violations subject to $100–$10,000 per violation civil penalty; first-time pay-scale disclosure violations may be waived if promptly corrected.

**Wage and job title history records:** § 432.3 requires employers to maintain job title and wage rate history for each employee for duration of employment plus 3 years, open to Labor Commissioner inspection.

**Equal pay:** Labor Code § 1197.5 (California Equal Pay Act) prohibits paying employees at lower wage rates based on sex for substantially similar work (skill, effort, responsibility, working conditions). Defenses: bona fide factors (seniority, merit system, quality/quantity output, collective bargaining) not based on sex. Violations: liable for back wages, interest, plus equal liquidated damages.

**Pay data reporting:** Government Code § 12999 (effective May 2023 for 2022 data onward) requires private employers with 100+ employees to submit annual pay-data reports to Civil Rights Department by May 15, covering prior calendar year (job title, race/ethnicity, sex, hours, wages). Employers using labor contractors must separately report contractor-sourced employee data. Penalties: $100/employee (first failure); $200–$1,000/employee (subsequent failures).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, CFRA, Paid Family Leave, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave (PSL):** Labor Code § 246 requires 40 hours (5 days) of paid sick leave per year for employees employed 30+ days in a year, effective Jan 1, 2024. Employee can use PSL on 90th day of employment. Accrued PSL does not reset on role change or demotion (no forfeiture of accrual). Carry-over to next year permitted unless employer front-loads or uses "use it or lose it" with 2-year carry-over. PTO can satisfy PSL if it meets accrual and use thresholds.

**CFRA (California Family Rights Act):** Government Code § 12945 et seq. requires 12 weeks unpaid job-protected leave per calendar year for employees with 12+ months tenure and 1,250 hours in preceding 12 months, for own serious health condition, family member care, or bonding with new child. CFRA runs concurrently with FMLA (if employer has 50+ employees within 75 miles).

**Paid Family Leave (PFL):** Unemployment Insurance Code § 3300 provides up to 8 weeks paid benefits (via State Disability Insurance) for wage-loss due to caring for ill family member, bonding with new child, or qualifying military deployment. Employee must have earned $300+ and paid SDI in prior 18 months. Wage replacement: 70% of prior income (2025 rate; 2026+ subject to annual adjustment). PFL is **not** job-protected (employer can terminate while on PFL); however, retaliation for taking PFL is unlawful.

**Pregnancy disability leave:** § 12945.2 provides up to 4 months unpaid leave for pregnancy-related disability, separate from CFRA. Employer must maintain health insurance during leave.

**Domestic violence leave:** Labor Code §§ 230–230.1 provide reasonable unpaid leave (and reasonable paid leave if employer provides it) for employee or employee's family member who is victim of domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking. Employer cannot require employee to exhaust sick/PTO before accessing leave; no retaliation allowed.

**Lactation:** § 1030 requires reasonable paid break time for lactation (not "just" unpaid restroom). Employer must provide private, non-bathroom space if feasible.

**Whistleblower protection:** Labor Code § 1102.5 protects employees from retaliation for reporting (to internal compliance, labor agencies, or law enforcement) workplace violations of law or regulations. Covers safety, wage/hour, discrimination, harassment, and other protected disclosures. Cannot be waived. Violations: liability for damages, attorney's fees, injunctive relief.

**Arrest records:** § 432.7 prohibits employer from asking about, considering, or disclosing arrest/detention records (unless pending charges directly relate to job). Exception: law enforcement and certain security clearances. Violation: misdemeanor and $2,500 fine per violation.

**Off-duty conduct:** Labor Code § 96(k) prohibits discipline/discharge for off-duty lawful conduct outside work (including legal cannabis use per AB 2188 above). Off-duty political activity and social-media expression have limited protections but receive heightened scrutiny under California law (verify: specific statutory provisions for political/social-media speech).

**Workplace violence prevention:** Labor Code § 6401.9 (effective Jan 1, 2024) requires employers to implement comprehensive written workplace violence prevention plan (training, assessment, emergency procedures) if they employ 50+ employees. Document incidents, provide support to affected employees. Failure to comply: citations and penalties under Cal/OSHA.

**Retaliation for safety complaints:** § 6310 et seq. prohibit retaliation against employees who report occupational safety/health violations (to employer or Cal/OSHA). Broad protection covers almost any safety-related report. Violations: reinstatement, back pay, damages.

**Non-solicitation of salary history (antitrust):** Labor Code § 432.6 (AB 51, 2015) was enjoined on First Amendment grounds in federal court (2017) and deemed unenforceable; effectively superseded by broader § 432.3 salary-history ban and pay-scale disclosure rules (2023).

**Victims of violence leave:** AB 2499 (effective Jan 1, 2025, enforced July 1, 2025) expands leave for employees or family members who are victims of qualifying acts of violence; leave runs concurrent with CFRA/FMLA if applicable; employer must inform employees of rights.

**Sick leave interaction with pay change:** Accrued PSL cannot be forfeited or reduced on demotion; employee retains PSL balance at new role. No state rule requiring PSL payout on termination except as severance agreement.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Format, Employee Inspection Rights

**Personnel file access:** Labor Code § 1198.5 grants current and former employees right to inspect personnel records (performance, education, training, grievances) at reasonable times within **30 calendar days** of written request (extendable to 35 days by mutual written agreement). For current employees, inspection at regular workplace or mutually agreed location; for former employees, at record storage location unless parties agree otherwise. Employer must provide copies within same 30–35 day window at reproduction cost. Failure to comply: $750 penalty per violation; employee may sue for injunctive relief and recover attorney's fees.

**Payroll record retention:** § 226 requires 3-year retention of itemized wage statements and deduction records at workplace or California central location. § 432.3 requires job title and wage rate history for duration of employment plus 3 years. § 204 requires payroll records (including time records, rate sheets, etc.) be retained per IWC wage orders (typically 3+ years).

**Personnel file retention:** No specific statutory retention period stated in § 1198.5; however, § 432.3 wage history must be retained 3 years post-separation. Recommend retaining all personnel records (discipline, reviews, grievances) per statute of limitations for wage claims (generally 3–4 years depending on claim type).

**Record format:** No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; § 226 wage statements may be detachable from check or separate if wages paid by cash/check. Electronic storage acceptable if original paper copy retained or scannable. No specified digital storage requirement.

## 7. Privacy and Data: CCPA/CPRA Employee Coverage, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**CCPA/CPRA employee coverage:** California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA, Civil Code § 1798.100 et seq.) originally exempted employment-related personal information and B2B transactions. However, effective **Jan 1, 2023**, exemptions for employment data expired. California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA, Prop 24, approved Nov 2020, effective Jan 1, 2023) amended CCPA to extend protections to employee personal information, including job applicants and independent contractors.

**Employee consumer rights under CPRA (effective Jan 1, 2023):**

- Right to know what personal information is collected and how used/shared
- Right to delete personal information (with statutory exceptions: legal/business necessity)
- Right to opt out of sale or sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising
- Right to correct inaccurate information
- Right to limit use/disclosure of sensitive personal information (health, race, sex, precise location, etc.)

**Business obligations:** Covered employers must provide privacy notice at or before collection, honor access/deletion/opt-out/correction requests within 45 days (or 90 days if unverifiable request; verify: CPPA guidance on verification threshold), and maintain reasonable security. Civil Code § 1798.150 creates private right of action for data breaches (unencrypted/unredacted personal information loss) with minimum statutory damages of $100–$750 per consumer per incident.

**Biometric information:** Civil Code § 1798.100 et seq. does not expressly restrict biometric collection, but employers using facial recognition or fingerprint/iris scanning for time-tracking, access control, or surveillance should disclose in CCPA notice, obtain explicit consent, and delete data upon request (verify: case law on biometric consent requirement scope).

**Breach notification:** Civil Code § 1798.82 requires notification without unreasonable delay (typically 30–60 days) for breaches of unencrypted/unredacted California residents' personal information. No employer exemption; applies to HCM systems storing employee data.

**Data residency:** CPRA does not mandate data residency in California, but employers must comply with CPRA requirements if they process California residents' data, regardless of server location.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify, Discrimination, FEHA

**Discrimination and FEHA (Government Code §§ 12900–12999):** California Fair Employment and Housing Act (FEHA) prohibits employment discrimination based on protected classes (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age 40+, disability, genetic information, marital status, military status, gender identity, sexual orientation, and others) by employers with 5+ employees. Covers hiring, firing, compensation, benefits, training, and terms/conditions of employment. Department of Civil Rights enforces; private right of action available. Employer liability: damages, attorney's fees, injunctive relief.

**Fair Chance Act (Government Code § 12952):** Effective Jan 1, 2018, employers with 5+ employees cannot ask about or consider criminal history before conditional job offer. Law prohibits conviction history questions in job ads, applications, or interviews before conditional offer. After conditional offer, employer may conduct background check and consider convictions only if they have direct/harmful relationship to job duties (nexus test). Employer must provide copy of background report and opportunity for applicant to dispute accuracy.

**Background check limits:** No separate state law limiting scope beyond Fair Chance Act; federal FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act) applies if third-party vendor used. Employers must obtain written consent and provide notice before/after purchase of report.

**Drug testing:** Labor Code § 11001 et seq. does not prohibit pre-employment drug testing; employer testing is permitted if employer has written drug-free workplace policy. Testing must be reasonable. Post-offer testing preferred to avoid ADA issues.

**Cannabis discrimination:** AB 2188 (effective Jan 1, 2024, under Labor Code § 12954) makes it unlawful to discriminate against an applicant or employee based on off-duty use of cannabis (with exceptions for positions requiring federal background clearance, CDL drivers, collective bargaining agreements). Cannot test for cannabis as condition of employment (absent federal funding/licensing requirement).

**E-Verify mandate:** No state mandate for E-Verify use; federal I-9 rules apply. California does not require employers to participate in E-Verify.

## 9. Separation: Cal-WARN, Severance, Notice, Non-Compete and Non-Solicit

**Cal-WARN notice (Labor Code §§ 1400–1408):** California Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act requires **60-day written notice** of mass layoff (50+ employees in 30 days), plant closure, or relocation to:

- All affected employees (method ensuring receipt: written, email, or in person)
- Employment Development Department (EDD)
- Local area agency (workforce development)
- Chief elected official of each affected city/county

Applies to **covered establishments** (75+ employees) ordering mass layoffs. Exceptions: physical calamity, act of war (verify: unforeseeable circumstances defense scope). § 1402.5 permits director exemptions if employer meets specific conditions.

**Cal-WARN penalties:** § 1411 imposes liability to each affected employee for back pay and benefits coverage, capped at **60 days or one-half the period of employment**, whichever is less. No offset for wages paid during notice period.

**Severance:** No state mandate for severance; common law requires only accrued and earned wages per §§ 201–203. Employer can offer severance as part of separation agreement but is not required. Severance agreements must not waive statutory claims (Labor Code § 206.5 voids non-waivable rights).

**Non-compete agreements:** Business and Professions Code § 16600 voids all contracts restraining lawful profession/business. Non-compete clauses in employment contracts are void unless exception applies (sale/dissolution of business entity with assets or equity sale). AB 1076 (effective Feb 14, 2024) requires employers to notify **current and certain former employees** (those with non-compete agreements issued on/after Jan 1, 2024 or who have worked within prior 2 years) that non-compete clauses are void.

**Non-solicit agreements:** § 16600 also applies to non-solicit (non-solicitation of customers/employees) unless narrowly tailored to sale/dissolution context. Broad non-solicits of general customer base are unenforceable; customer-specific non-solicits in sale context are permitted.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: ABC Test, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**AB 5 ABC test:** Labor Code § 2750.5 (effective Jan 1, 2020) codifies ABC test for independent contractor classification. Worker is employee unless hiring entity proves **all three** conditions:

- **(A)** Worker is free from control/direction of hiring entity in performance and in fact
- **(B)** Worker performs work outside usual course of hiring entity's business
- **(C)** Worker is customarily engaged in independently established trade/occupation/business of same nature

Failure to satisfy any single prong = worker is employee. Burden on hiring entity to prove all three. Common test of "independent judgment" is subsumed; ABC test is narrower and favors employee classification.

**Exceptions to AB 5:** Certain professions exempt (licensed professionals, health care providers, certain sales professionals, insurance agents, musicians, etc.); each exemption requires fact-specific analysis.

**Remote workers:** No special remote-work classification rule. Remote California employees are subject to same minimum wage, overtime, meal break, and leave rules as on-site employees. Employer with employees in multiple states must apply most-stringent state rule (California rules apply if any employee is in California, even part-time).

**Multi-state reciprocity:** California does not defer to another state's wage/hour laws if employee works in California (even part-time or temporarily). Human Capital Management Suite must apply California rules to any California employee regardless of employer headquarters or other-state employment.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: System Design and Obligations for Employment Changes

**Priority rules for tiered compliance:** California's multi-layer wage/leave/privacy rules require Human Capital Management Suite to apply a waterfall logic:

- Always apply the most-stringent rule (state > local minimum wage; CFRA/PFL concurrency over FMLA).
- Track effective dates of statutory changes (annual wage adjustments on Jan 1; AB 2499 enforcement July 1, 2025; SB 399 TBD).
- Preserve employee accruals (PSL, CFRA eligibility, PFL balance) across pay/role changes; never forfeit without express statutory exception.
- Honor CCPA/CPRA consumer rights even for employees who do not opt in; expiration of employment exemption (Jan 1, 2023) is mandatory.

**Must-model obligations for pay and role changes:**

1. **Minimum-wage tier selection (§§ 1182.12, 1474, 1182.14):** System must identify employee job classification (general, fast-food, healthcare facility type), look up current rate, check local ordinance override, and apply highest applicable minimum. Flag when rate changes annually (typically Jan 1, but some local ordinances on different schedules) or when statutory law changes. For healthcare, confirm facility FTE count and mission (hospital vs. clinic vs. rural) to apply correct schedule (verify: 2026 rates for each facility category).

2. **Wage notice (§ 2810.5):** Upon hire, system must generate/display/send notice. On any pay/payday/basis change, system must flag 7-day notification requirement and track completion.

3. **Pay scale disclosure (§ 432.3):** System must store current pay scale per position, surface in job posting export, and provide on employee request within 30 days or face liability.

4. **Pay-data reporting (Gov. Code § 12999):** For employers with 100+ employees, system must collect and export annual pay-data report (job title, race/ethnicity, sex, hours, wages) by May 15 deadline. Track whether employer uses labor contractors (separate report required).

5. **Final pay timing (§§ 201–203):** On termination/resignation, system must calculate final pay (regular + accrued vacation + accrued PSL + any penalties), alert employer of 203-penalty risk if payment delayed beyond 72 hours on resignation or immediately on discharge, and track payment date to flag 30-day maximum penalty exposure.

6. **Pay statement accuracy (§ 226):** System must generate itemized statements with all 9 required fields, retain 3-year copies, and track deduction authorization (written employee consent). Note: wage statements may be electronic if employee consents.

7. **Personnel file access (§ 1198.5):** On employee request, system must flag 30-day deadline (extendable to 35 with agreement) to provide inspection/copies or $750 penalty risk.

8. **CFRA/PFL/PSL interaction:** System must track concurrent CFRA (12 weeks), PFL (up to 8 weeks paid), and PSL (40 hours/year), prevent double-benefit, maintain job protection marker, and flag when PSL cannot be forfeited on role change.

9. **Non-compete voidance:** System should flag § 16600 voidance and AB 1076 required notice for any employee with non-compete clause issued after Jan 1, 2024 or working within prior 2 years; enforce void status in separation workflows.

10. **ABC test classification review:** On contractor classification, system should prompt for all three ABC prongs and warn if any fails (presumption = employee).

11. **CCPA/CPRA data access (Civ. Code § 1798.100 et seq., effective Jan 1, 2023):** System must support employee requests to access, delete, correct, or opt out of personal data per CPRA; implement mechanism to honor requests within 45 days (or 90 days if unverifiable; verify: CPPA threshold guidance). Maintain audit log of requests and responses.

12. **Cannabis/domestic violence/victim leave:** System must flag eligibility for AB 2188 cannabis use non-discrimination (no testing unless federal-funding job or CDL), AB 2499 victim-of-violence leave (runs concurrent with CFRA/FMLA, enforced July 1, 2025), and § 230–230.1 domestic violence leave (unpaid or employer-provided paid, cannot force exhaustion of other leave first) on hire and on employee request.

13. **Workplace violence prevention (§ 6401.9):** For 50+ employers, system should flag requirement to maintain written plan, document incidents, provide employee support resources, and log trainings. Non-compliance: Cal/OSHA citations.

14. **FEHA discrimination compliance (Gov. Code § 12900+):** System should track protected classes, prevent discriminatory questions at hire/promotion/termination, and maintain audit logs of adverse decisions with business reasons documented. Private lawsuits available; Civil Rights Department also investigates complaints.

**Open questions:**

- Does 7-day notice for pay change (§ 2810.5) apply retroactively (notice before or after effective date of change)?
- What constitutes reasonable verification for CPRA employee-data access requests, and when can request be rejected as unverifiable (90-day threshold)?
- Does § 203 good-faith dispute defense require employer to articulate the dispute in writing to employee or can it be raised after complaint?
- Which local minimum-wage ordinances have indexed increases, and how should Human Capital Management Suite track them?
- SB 399 captive-audience meeting rule: does it apply to all employers or only certain sizes/industries (verify current status; bill appeared in 2024 session)?
