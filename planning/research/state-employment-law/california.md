# California Employment Law Research

**State:** California | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Wage notice timing**: Labor Code § 2810.5 requires written notice at hiring (not on change of role/pay), with 7-day notice for pay or employment term changes; no federal equivalent applies.
- **Pay transparency mandatory**: § 432.3 bans salary history questions, requires pay scale disclosure in job postings (15+ employees), and mandates 3-year wage/job title record retention.
- **Final pay penalties**: §§ 201–203 impose continuing wage penalties (up to 30 days) for delayed final pay; no good-faith defense unless employer disputes wage entitlement on law/fact grounds.
- **Paid leave interacts with pay changes**: CFRA (12 weeks unpaid) and PFL (up to 8 weeks paid) run concurrently; paid sick leave (40 hours/5 days annually) accrues but doesn't restore on demotion or pay cut.
- **Personnel file access**: § 1198.5 gives employees 30-day right to inspect performance/training/grievance records; $750 penalty per violation if employer refuses.
- **AB 5 ABC test presumes employee**: All three prongs (A: no control, B: outside usual business, C: independent trade) must be satisfied to classify as contractor; failure on any prong = employee.
- **Non-compete void**: Business & Professions Code § 16600 voids all non-compete agreements except sale/dissolution of business entity; AB 1076 (2024) required void-clause notice by Feb 14, 2024.
- **CCPA/CPRA applies to employee data**: Jan 1, 2023 exemption expiration means employee personal information is now a consumer right; employers must honor access/deletion/opt-out requests.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Required Notices

California is at-will employment (no state restriction on at-will doctrine). However, Labor Code § 2810.5 (Wage Theft Protection Act of 2011) mandates written notice **at the time of hiring** containing rate(s) of pay, basis (hourly/salary/piece/commission), allowances toward minimum wage, regular payday, employer name and business aliases, main office and mailing address, phone, workers' comp carrier name, paid sick leave rights, and anti-retaliation statement. Notice must be in the language used for employment communication.

On **any change** to pay rate, pay basis, payday, or allowances, employer must notify employee in writing **within 7 calendar days**. No specific notice required for role change per state law; federal FMLA notice applies if employer has 50+ employees.

Exempt employees are excluded from § 2810.5 wage notice requirement. No separate written contract requirement at hire unless employer chooses to create one.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions, and Pay Statements

**Minimum wage:** California § 526 requires $16.90/hour effective Jan 1, 2026 (verify: increases annually; $17.40 effective Jan 1, 2027 per DIR announcement). No tip credit allowed. Meals/lodging may offset minimum wage only by voluntary written agreement.

**Overtime:** Labor Code § 510 requires 1.5x regular rate for hours over 8/day or 40/week; 2x rate for hours over 12/day or 8 hours on 7th consecutive day.

**Meal and rest breaks:** § 512 requires unpaid 30-minute meal breaks when work exceeds 5 hours (and another 30 minutes if over 12 hours); § 226.7 mandates paid 10-minute rest for every 4 hours worked. Employer must provide, not employee request only.

**Pay frequency:** § 200 requires wages at least semimonthly on designated paydays; § 226 requires itemized wage statement each pay period showing gross/net wages, hours worked, rates, deductions, period dates, employee name and last 4 SSN digits, employer legal name and address, and piece-rate units if applicable.

**Final pay upon termination:** § 201 mandates all earned wages (including accrued vacation) due immediately upon discharge or mass layoff. § 202 permits 72-hour notice to resign and receive final pay (or immediate pay). § 203 imposes continuing wage penalty (employee's daily wage × 30 days, or daily rate × days between due date and final payment, whichever is smaller) if employer willfully fails to pay wages on time, unless employer has good-faith defense (factual/legal dispute over wage entitlement).

**Deductions:** § 200 prohibits deductions except those legally required or employee-authorized in writing; § 226 requires aggregated deductions shown as one item if employee-written-authorized.

**Pay statement penalties:** § 226 imposes $250/employee per violation (initial citation), $1,000/employee for subsequent violations, for failure to provide itemized statement or maintain 3-year records.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity: Salary Range, History Ban, Pay Data Reporting, Equal Pay

**Salary history ban:** Labor Code § 432.3 (amended by SB 1162, effective Jan 1, 2023) prohibits employers from orally or in writing seeking salary history of applicants, unless applicant voluntarily discloses. Employer may not rely on prior salary to set compensation. Employer may accept voluntary salary information if candidate offers it freely.

**Pay scale disclosure:** § 432.3 requires employers to provide pay scale for position upon reasonable request from applicant or current employee. Employers with 15+ employees must **include pay scale in all job postings**. Third-party recruiters posting on behalf of 15+ employee employers must also include scale. Violations subject to $100–$10,000 per violation civil penalty; first-time pay-scale disclosure violations may be waived if promptly corrected.

**Wage and job title history records:** § 432.3 requires employers to maintain job title and wage rate history for each employee for duration of employment plus 3 years, open to Labor Commissioner inspection. (verify: confirm if this overlaps with § 226 retention or extends it)

**Equal pay:** No separate California equal pay statute beyond federal EPAA; Civil Rights Act § 11601 etc. exist but are enforcement statutes, not substantive pay rules. State courts apply federal EPAA principles.

**Pay data reporting:** No mandatory state pay equity audit or EEO-6 equivalent state form; federal EEO-1 filing applies if employer has 100+ employees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, CFRA, Paid Family Leave, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave (PSL):** Labor Code § 246 requires 40 hours (5 days) of paid sick leave per year for employees employed 30+ days in a year, effective Jan 1, 2024. Employee can use PSL on 90th day of employment. Accrued PSL does not reset on role change or demotion (no forfeiture of accrual). Carry-over to next year permitted unless employer front-loads or uses "use it or lose it" with 2-year carry-over. PTO can satisfy PSL if it meets accrual and use thresholds.

**CFRA (California Family Rights Act):** § 12945 et seq. requires 12 weeks unpaid job-protected leave per calendar year for employees with 12+ months tenure and 1,250 hours in preceding 12 months, for own serious health condition, family member care, or bonding with new child. CFRA runs concurrently with FMLA (if employer has 50+ employees within 75 miles).

**Paid Family Leave (PFL):** Unemployment Insurance Code § 3300 provides up to 8 weeks paid benefits (via SDI) for wage-loss due to caring for ill family member, bonding with new child, or qualifying military deployment. Employee must have earned $300+ and paid SDI in prior 18 months. PFL is **not** job-protected (employer can terminate while on PFL); employer contribution required for some in-home care workers.

**Pregnancy disability leave:** § 12945 provides up to 4 months unpaid leave for pregnancy-related disability, separate from CFRA. Employer must maintain health insurance during leave.

**Sick leave interaction with pay change:** Accrued PSL cannot be forfeited or reduced on demotion; employee retains PSL balance at new role. No state rule requiring PSL payout on termination except as severance agreement.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Format, Employee Inspection Rights

**Personnel file access:** Labor Code § 1198.5 grants current and former employees right to inspect personnel records (performance, education, training, grievances) at reasonable times within **30 calendar days** of written request (extendable to 35 days by mutual written agreement). For current employees, inspection at regular workplace or mutually agreed location; for former employees, at record storage location unless parties agree otherwise. Employer must provide copies within same 30-35 day window at reproduction cost.

**Payroll record retention:** § 226 requires 3-year retention of itemized wage statements and deduction records at workplace or California central location. § 432 (as amended by § 432.3) requires job title and wage rate history for duration of employment plus 3 years.

**Personnel file retention:** No specific statutory retention period stated in § 1198.5; however, § 432.3 wage history must be retained 3 years post-separation. Recommend retaining all personnel records (discipline, reviews, grievances) per statute of limitations for wage claims (generally 3–4 years depending on claim type).

**Penalties for non-compliance:** § 1198.5 imposes $750 penalty per violation if employer fails to permit inspection or copy within 30–35 days; employee may sue for injunctive relief and recover attorney's fees.

**Record format:** No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; § 226 wage statements may be detachable from check or separate if wages paid by cash/check. No specified digital storage requirement.

## 7. Privacy and Data: CCPA/CPRA Employee Coverage, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**CCPA/CPRA employee coverage:** California Consumer Privacy Act (CCPA, Civil Code § 1798.100 et seq.) originally exempted employment-related personal information and B2B transactions. However, effective **Jan 1, 2023**, exemptions for employment data expired. California Privacy Rights Act (CPRA, Prop 24, approved Nov 2020, effective Jan 1, 2023) amended CCPA to extend protections to employee personal information, including job applicants and independent contractors.

**Employee consumer rights under CPRA (effective Jan 1, 2023):**

- Right to know what personal information is collected and how used/shared
- Right to delete personal information (with exceptions)
- Right to opt out of sale or sharing for cross-context behavioral advertising
- Right to correct inaccurate information
- Right to limit use/disclosure of sensitive personal information

**Business obligations:** Covered employers must provide privacy notice, honor access/deletion/opt-out requests, and maintain reasonable security. Civil Code § 1798.150 creates private right of action for data breaches (unencrypted/unredacted personal information loss).

**Biometric information:** Civil Code § 1798.100 et seq. does not expressly restrict biometric collection but § 22577 governs collection of fingerprints/DNA in criminal context. Employers using facial recognition or fingerprint clocks for time-tracking should disclose in CCPA notice and obtain consent (verify: limited case law on scope of biometric consent requirement for time-tracking).

**Breach notification:** Civil Code § 1798.82 requires notification without unreasonable delay (typically 30–60 days) for breaches of unencrypted/unredacted California residents' personal information. No employer exemption; applies to HCM systems storing employee data.

**Data residency:** CPRA does not mandate data residency in California, but employers must comply with CPRA requirements if they process California residents' data, regardless of server location.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Fair Chance Act (Government Code § 12952):** Effective Jan 1, 2018, employers with 5+ employees cannot ask about or consider criminal history before conditional job offer. Law prohibits:

- Conviction history questions in job ads, applications, or interviews before conditional offer
- "Ban the Box" language (e.g., "No Felons")
- Requesting criminal history orally or in writing pre-offer

After conditional offer, employer may conduct background check and consider convictions only if they have direct/harmful relationship to job duties. Employer must provide copy of background report and opportunity for applicant to dispute accuracy.

**Background check limits:** No separate state law limiting scope beyond Fair Chance Act; federal FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act) applies if third-party vendor used. Employers must obtain written consent and provide notice.

**Drug testing:** Labor Code § 11001 et seq. does not prohibit pre-employment drug testing; employer testing is permitted if employer has written drug-free workplace policy. Testing must be reasonable. Post-offer testing preferred to avoid ADA issues.

**E-Verify mandate:** No state mandate for E-Verify use; federal I-9 rules apply. California does not require employers to participate in E-Verify.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN (Cal-WARN), Severance, Notice, Non-Compete and Non-Solicit

**Cal-WARN notice:** Labor Code § 1400 et seq. requires 60-day notice of mass layoff, plant closure, or relocation to:

- All affected employees (method ensuring receipt)
- California Employment Development Department (EDD)
- Local area agency
- Chief elected official of each affected city/county

**Who must comply:** Applies to employers with 75+ employees if 50+ terminations in 30-day period (or 500+ in 90 days for multi-site closure). Exceptions for strikes, military closures, unforeseeable events (verify: verify "unforeseeable" defense scope).

**Penalties for non-compliance:** § 1411 imposes liability to each affected employee for back pay (up to 60 days or half the period of employment, whichever is less) plus cost of benefits.

**Severance:** No state mandate for severance; common law requires only accrued and earned wages per §§ 201–203. Employer can offer severance as part of separation agreement but is not required.

**Non-compete agreements:** Business and Professions Code § 16600 voids all contracts restraining lawful profession/business. Non-compete clauses in employment contracts are void unless exception applies (sale/dissolution of business entity with assets or equity sale). AB 1076 (effective Feb 14, 2024) requires employers to notify current and certain former employees that non-compete clauses are void.

**Non-solicit agreements:** § 16600 also applies to non-solicit (non-solicitation of customers/employees) unless narrowly tailored to sale/dissolution context. Broad non-solicits of general customer base are unenforceable; customer-specific non-solicits in sale context are permitted.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: ABC Test, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**AB 5 ABC test:** Labor Code § 2750.1 (effective Jan 1, 2020) codifies Dynamex _abc_ test for independent contractor classification. Worker is employee unless hiring entity proves **all three** conditions:

- **(A)** Worker is free from control/direction of hiring entity in performance and in fact
- **(B)** Worker performs work outside usual course of hiring entity's business
- **(C)** Worker is customarily engaged in independently established trade/occupation/business of same nature

Failure to satisfy any single prong = worker is employee. Burden on hiring entity to prove all three. Common test of "independent judgment" is subsumed; ABC test is narrower and favors employee classification.

**Exceptions to AB 5 § 2750.1:** Certain professions exempt (licensed professions, health care providers, certain sales professionals, insurance agents, musicians, etc.); each exemption requires fact-specific analysis.

**Remote workers:** No special remote-work classification rule. Remote California employees are subject to same minimum wage, overtime, meal break, and leave rules as on-site employees. Employer with employees in multiple states must apply most-stringent state rule (California rules here apply if any employee is in California).

**Multi-state reciprocity:** California does not defer to another state's wage/hour laws if employee works in California (even part-time or temporarily). HCM Next must apply California rules to any California employee regardless of employer headquarters or other-state employment.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

**Must-model obligations for pay and role changes:**

1. **Wage notice (§ 2810.5):** Upon hire, system must generate/display/send notice. On any pay/payday/basis change, system must flag 7-day notification requirement and track completion.
2. **Pay scale disclosure (§ 432.3):** System must store current pay scale per position, surface in job posting export, and provide on employee request.
3. **Final pay timing (§§ 201–203):** On termination/resignation, system must calculate final pay (regular + accrued vacation + any penalties), alert employer of 203-penalty risk if payment delayed, and track payment date to flag 30-day maximum penalty exposure.
4. **Pay statement accuracy (§ 226):** System must generate itemized statements with all 9 required fields, retain 3-year copies, and track deduction authorization (written employee consent).
5. **Personnel file access (§ 1198.5):** On employee request, system must flag 30-day deadline to provide inspection/copies or $750 penalty risk.
6. **CFRA/PFL interaction:** System must track concurrent CFRA (12 weeks) and PFL (8 weeks) eligibility, prevent double-benefit, maintain job protection marker.
7. **Leave accrual on demotion:** On pay/role downgrade, system must preserve accrued PSL, CFRA eligibility, and PFL status; no forfeiture allowed.
8. **Non-compete voidance:** System should flag § 16600 voidance and AB 1076 required notice for any employee with non-compete clause; enforce void status in separation workflows.
9. **ABC test classification review:** On contractor classification, system should prompt for all three ABC prongs and warn if any fails (presumption = employee).
10. **CCPA/CPRA data access:** System must support employee requests to access, delete, or opt out of personal data per CPRA (effective Jan 1, 2023); implement mechanism to honor requests within statutory timeframe (verify: 45 days standard, 90 days if unverifiable request).

**Open questions:**

- Does 7-day notice for pay change (§ 2810.5) apply retroactively (notice before or after effective date of change)?
- Does § 432.3 wage history retention (duration + 3 years) extend beyond § 226 pay-statement retention, creating separate retention obligation?
- Does § 203 good-faith dispute defense require employer to articulate the dispute in writing to employee or can it be raised after complaint?
- Does CPRA employee-data access request require proof of identity, and what constitutes reasonable verification (verify: Attorney General guidance on "unverifiable" request threshold)?
