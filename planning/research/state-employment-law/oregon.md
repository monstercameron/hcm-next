# Oregon Employment Law Research

**State:** Oregon | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **Three-tier minimum wage system**: Portland metro $16.80/hr, standard $15.55/hr, nonurban $14.55/hr (effective July 1, 2026; indexed annually by CPI).
- **Final pay on discharge**: All wages due end of next business day after discharge; resignation with 48 hours' notice due immediately; without notice due within 5 days or next payday (ORS 652.140).
- **Salary history ban**: Cannot inquire about or consider prior compensation in hiring; unsolicited voluntary disclosure not actionable (ORS 659A.357).
- **Equal pay (ORS 652.220)**: Unlawful to pay employees of protected classes lower rates for "work of comparable character"; permissible differences based on seniority, merit, production, location, travel, education, training, experience.
- **Oregon Sick Time (ORS 653.601-661)**: 40 hours per year; accrual 1 hour per 30 hours worked; unpaid employers ≤5 employees, paid employers 6+; 90-day delay before use permitted; employers 10+ employees must provide paid leave.
- **Paid Leave Oregon (ORS 657B)**: 12 weeks' state-funded job-protected leave for birth/adoption, employee/family illness, or domestic violence; employee/employer payroll contributions since 2023.
- **OFLA redefined by SB 1515 (effective July 1, 2024)**: Now covers only bereavement, pregnancy disability, sick-child leave; child bonding and serious health conditions moved to PLO; no concurrent use.
- **Non-compete threshold (ORS 653.295)**: Unenforceable unless employee earned >$116,427/year (indexed annually; $116,427 effective Feb. 2025), written notice 2 weeks before hire, 12-month max, garden leave 50%+ base salary, signed copy within 30 days of termination.
- **Ban-the-box (ORS 659A.360)**: Cannot inquire about criminal record until after initial interview; Portland ordinance stricter (conditional offer threshold); cannot exclude applicant solely for past conviction.
- **Workplace Fairness Act (ORS 659A.370–375)**: Void non-disparagement/NDA clauses covering discrimination/sexual assault except when employee requests with 7-day revocation, or employer found employee engaged in prohibited conduct; whistleblower retaliation if applied to good-faith reports.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Oregon recognizes employment-at-will; employers and employees may terminate without cause except by statute or contract.

**Exceptions to at-will termination**:

- Termination for filing or testifying in wage/workers' compensation/unemployment claims.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (speech, assembly, petition, voting).
- Termination for jury duty, military duty, or witnessing court proceedings.
- Termination in violation of public policy (refusing illegal act, whistleblowing per ORS 659A.199).
- Retaliation for filing discrimination complaints or exercising civil rights.

**Required notices at hire and on pay/role change**:

- ORS 659A.355: Employer cannot retaliate for wage inquiry or discussion; wage discussion is protected.
- SB 906 (eff. Jan. 1, 2026, ORS 652.610): On hire, written explanation of all payroll codes, deductions, benefits, pay rates employee may receive.
- No statutory requirement for written employment offer or role-change notice, but recommended for defensibility.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage (ORS 653.025, indexed annually by CPI; current as of July 1, 2026)**:

- Portland metro (Clackamas, Multnomah, Washington counties urban growth boundaries): $16.80/hour.
- Standard (most Oregon counties): $15.55/hour.
- Nonurban (rural counties: Baker, Coos, Gilliam, Klamath, Malheur, et al.): $14.55/hour.
- Employees paid rate for county where they work 50%+ weekly hours.

**Overtime (ORS 653.261)**:

- 1.5x regular rate for hours exceeding 40 per workweek (no daily overtime threshold).
- Manufacturing: 1.5x for 10+ hours per day or 40+ per week (whichever favors employee) unless collective-bargaining agreement states otherwise.
- Exemptions: executive, administrative, professional employees (federal FLSA test).

**Pay frequency (ORS 652.120)**:

- Payday at least once per calendar month; may not exceed 35 days apart.
- Regular payday must be established in advance.
- If regular payday falls on non-workday, must pay on preceding workday.

**Final pay on termination (ORS 652.140)**:

- **Discharge/mutual agreement**: All earned, unpaid wages due by end of next business day after termination.
- **Resignation with 48+ hours' notice** (excluding weekends/holidays): Wages due immediately.
- **Resignation without notice**: Due within 5 calendar days or next regular payday (whichever earlier); if employee submits time records, estimate due within 5 days, final within 5 days of submission.
- **Penalty wage (ORS 652.150)**: 200% of unpaid wages if employer willfully fails to pay; plus interest and attorney fees.

**Permitted deductions (ORS 652.610)**:

- Deductions required by law (tax, garnishment, court order) or written authorization.
- No deductions for tools, uniforms, employer costs unless written consent and no reduction below minimum wage.
- Must pay deducted amounts to recipient within 7 days after wages due (or per law/agreement).

**Pay statement content (ORS 652.610, per SB 906 effective Jan. 1, 2026)**:

- Name, dates of payment and pay period, rate(s), employer ID/registry number, address, phone.
- Itemized deductions (purpose and amount).
- Gross, net, commissions, bonuses.
- At hire: detailed written explanation of all payroll codes used and all deduction types that may apply.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage range disclosure in job postings**:

- Oregon does not mandate salary ranges in job postings (HB 2746 pending but not enacted as of 2026).
- However, employees have protected right to discuss and disclose wages without retaliation (ORS 659A.355).

**Salary history ban (ORS 659A.357)**:

- Unlawful to inquire about or seek prior compensation from applicant or current/former employer.
- Unlawful to screen applicants based on current/past compensation.
- Exception: Post-offer written authorization to verify compensation after offer made.
- Unsolicited voluntary disclosure by candidate not actionable.

**Equal pay statute (ORS 652.220)**:

- Unlawful to pay protected-class employees lower rates than others for "work of comparable character."
- Protected classes: race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, national origin, marital status, veteran status, disability, age.
- Permissible reasons for pay differences: seniority system, merit system, production-based pay, workplace location, necessary travel, education, training, experience, or any combination that accounts for entire differential.
- Pay equity analysis: Compare employees performing substantially similar work; identify discrepancies; justify by bona fide factor.

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state-mandated pay equity audit; federal EEO-1 (100+ employees) applies if applicable.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Paid Family/Medical Leave, Other Mandated Leave, Interactions with Pay/Role Changes

**Oregon Sick Time (ORS 653.601–653.661)**:

- All employers must provide minimum 40 hours per benefit year (accrued 1 hour per 30 hours worked).
- Employers 6+ employees: paid leave (employers ≤5 employees: may be unpaid).
- Use permitted: employee/family member health condition, diagnosis, care, treatment; domestic violence, harassment, sexual assault, stalking; school/workplace closure due to public health emergency; quarantine during public health emergency.
- Employer may require certification if employee uses 3+ consecutive days.
- No statutory carryover cap (verify employer policy at hire).

**Paid Leave Oregon (ORS 657B, effective Jan. 1, 2024 with premium contributions since 2023)**:

- 12 weeks of job-protected, paid leave per 12-month period for birth/adoption bonding, serious health condition (employee or family), domestic violence/harassment/sexual assault/stalking.
- Employee/employer contribute to state insurance fund (employee pays ~0.4% of wages, employer ~0.5% for employers 10+ employees).
- Replaces OFLA child bonding and serious health condition leave.

**Oregon Family Leave Act—OFLA Redefined (ORS 659A.150–186, SB 1515 effective July 1, 2024)**:

- Now covers only: bereavement (up to 2 weeks per death within 3-month period), pregnancy disability, sick-child leave (up to 40 hours per employee per year for employee's or family member's serious health condition if child under 18 or adult with functional limitation; for non-serious health conditions, separate accrual).
- Child bonding and employee/family serious health condition moved to PLO (cannot stack both).
- Job protection: Employees may use accrued paid sick time, vacation, or other paid leave under employer policy during OFLA leave.

**Domestic violence/harassment/sexual assault leave (ORS 659A.270–285)**:

- Reasonable unpaid leave to seek legal/law enforcement assistance, medical treatment, counseling, safe housing, services related to abuse.
- Job-protected; cannot retaliate for use.

**Interaction with pay/role change**:

- On promotion during leave: Employee returns to same or promoted role; clarify whether wage resets to promoted rate or continues from departure rate (verify case law).
- Leave accrual: Generally continues during paid leave, may pause during unpaid OFLA/PLO (verify employer policy).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Personnel record retention (ORS 652.750)**:

- Employers must retain employee records for at least 60 days after termination.
- Current employees: retention duration determined by employer policy (recommend 3 years minimum for tax/payroll).

**Employee access to personnel files (ORS 652.750)**:

- Employees have right to inspect and copy records during work hours.
- Access within 45 days of written request.
- Employer may charge reasonable fee to recover copying costs.
- Former employees retain access rights for 60 days after termination.

**Record format**:

- No statute mandates electronic vs. paper; electronic acceptable if accessible and printable by employee.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Breach Notification, Monitoring, Social Media

**Data breach notification (ORS 646A.604, Oregon Consumer Information Protection Act)**:

- Applies to "computerized data" containing personal information (name + SSN, financial account, medical record, etc.).
- Notification timeline: As soon as practicable, no later than 45 days after discovery of breach.
- Must notify affected Oregon residents and, if 250+ residents affected, Oregon Attorney General.
- Breach = unauthorized acquisition that materially compromises security, confidentiality, or integrity.
- Exemption: No notification if covered entity reasonably determines residents unlikely to suffer harm (document determination, retain 5 years).
- Penalties: Up to $500,000 for continuing violation.

**Social media and personal account privacy (ORS 659A.330)**:

- Employers cannot require employee or applicant to provide password/access to personal social media account, or establish/maintain personal account.
- Cannot require authorization to advertise on employee's account or add employer to contacts.
- Exceptions: Employer-provided accounts; workplace investigations into employee misconduct (based on specific information about activity); discovery of publicly available information.

**Workplace monitoring**:

- No state statute bans email/internet/video monitoring in workplace; reasonable expectation of privacy applies (federal ECPA relevant).

**Data residency**:

- No Oregon statute mandates employee data residency; in-state/out-of-state storage permitted if security/privacy laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (ORS 659A.360, effective 2016)**:

- Statewide: Cannot inquire about criminal record during initial employment application process (IEAP) unless position-specific exception applies.
- Criminal history questions permitted after initial interview or, if no interview, after conditional offer.
- Cannot exclude applicant solely for past conviction unless position requires criminal history check by law.
- **Portland Ordinance (stricter, effective July 1, 2016)**: Applies to 6+ employee employers; requires deferral until conditional job offer.

**Background checks**:

- No state-level private-sector restrictions; federal FCRA applies (consumer reporting agency rules, notice, dispute, adverse-action procedures).

**Drug testing**:

- No state-level mandate or restriction (federal DOT rules apply if applicable).
- Employers may test for cannabis use; positive test not protected (no medical marijuana cardholder exception under state law for employment).
- Observable impairment standard: Employer may require test upon reasonable suspicion or observable signs.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No state mandate; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mini-WARN (state-level mass-layoff notice)**:

- Oregon has no separate state mini-WARN act; federal WARN Act (60-day notice, 100+ employees or 50+ in 30-day period) applies.
- ORS 285A.510 and 285A.516 address WARN compliance notification (no additional state notice requirement beyond federal).

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance absent employment agreement; generally contractual.

**Non-compete enforceability (ORS 653.295)**:

- Enforceable only if (1) employee annual gross salary + commissions at termination exceed $116,427 (indexed annually, currently $116,427 as of Feb. 2025); (2) written notice 2 weeks before employment commencement; (3) duration max 12 months from termination; (4) garden leave clause (employer pays 50%+ base salary during non-compete period) or other independent consideration; (5) signed copy provided within 30 days after termination.
- Void if salary threshold not met, notice not given, or duration/consideration insufficient.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; enforced under common-law reasonableness standard (legitimate business interest, reasonable scope/duration/geography).

**Workplace Fairness Act restrictions (ORS 659A.370–375)**:

- Non-disparagement and NDA clauses void if applied to discrimination/sexual assault conduct except: (1) aggrieved employee voluntarily requests with 7-day revocation period, or (2) employer made good-faith finding of employee misconduct; NDAs/non-disparagement permitted for other reasons (trade secrets, wage/hour violations).
- Whistleblower protection: Retaliation for reporting violations (wage theft, safety, etc. to BOLI or other agency) unlawful.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification (ORS 670.600 economic-reality/right-to-control test for most purposes; ORS 657.040 for unemployment insurance; verify)**:

- Worker deemed employee unless employer proves all three:
  - (A) Worker exercises total control over when/how work is performed.
  - (B) Work is outside employer's usual business or performed away from usual worksite.
  - (C) Worker has own separate business, occupation, or trade.
- Failure on any prong = employee classification.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine. Each state's law applies to work performed in that state.
- Platform should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable rules.

**Remote workers**:

- No Oregon statute specifically addresses remote-work classification.
- If employee works remotely in Oregon for any employer, Oregon law applies to that work.
- Standard employment law applies regardless of work location.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Obligations

**Mandatory platform features and field restrictions**:

1. **Final Pay Timing Trigger** (ORS 652.140): On termination action, auto-calculate deadline based on discharge type (end of next business day), resignation notice status (48+ hours = immediate, <48 hours = 5 days or next payday). Notify employee and payroll.

2. **Pay Statement Update** (ORS 652.610, SB 906): On any pay rate change (promotion or otherwise), system must generate itemized statement showing new rate, effective date, deductions breakdown. Before Jan. 1, 2026: itemization sufficient; after: add written explanation of payroll codes used and all deduction types.

3. **Salary History Block** (ORS 659A.357): Disable any form field requesting prior salary from external candidate; log as compliance risk if manager attempts inquiry.

4. **Equal Pay Audit Flag** (ORS 652.220): On promotion + base-pay change, if new rate differs >15% from peer rates for "comparable character" work (same role/department/location), flag for manager justification or HR override with documented bona fide reason (seniority, merit, production, location, travel, education, training, experience).

5. **Wage Discussion Protection** (ORS 659A.355): Do not retaliate against or restrict employee discussion of wages; log if manager reports such discussion as "misconduct."

6. **Non-Compete Threshold Recheck** (ORS 653.295): On promotion, if new salary crosses $116,427 threshold, auto-flag: if employee lacks signed NCA with 2-week pre-hire notice and garden-leave clause, mark as unenforceable going forward. If promotion happens, require garden-leave adjustment if NCA exists.

7. **Sick Time Carryover** (ORS 653.601–661): On promotion within same employer, sick time accrual carries forward; do not reset. On cross-company transfer: verify if accrual owed as lump-sum payout per prior employer policy or carries to new employer.

8. **OFLA/PLO Continuity**: On promotion during active leave, employee retains job protection and pay protection under OFLA (bereavement, pregnancy disability, sick-child) or PLO (12-week leave); role may change but leave remains protected. Do not reduce pay rate retroactively if employee in unpaid leave during promotion announcement.

9. **Domestic Violence/Harassment Leave** (ORS 659A.270–285): Job-protected; cannot retaliate for approved leave use. On promotion, do not use leave history in performance/wage decision.

10. **Workplace Fairness Act Compliance** (ORS 659A.370–375): If promotion involves post-signing of any NCA, non-disparagement clause, or "at-will" reaffirmation that would cover discrimination/sexual assault, ensure clause allows employee to report to BOLI, attorney, or law enforcement without penalty.

11. **Record Documentation**: Log all pay change notices, justifications, equity analysis, NCA status, leave continuity decisions for 60 days post-termination audit trail.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether sick time must be paid out on termination or only if employer policy/CBA requires; statute silent on payout obligation.
- Clarify "work of comparable character" geographic scope (same employer location, county, or statewide) for equal pay analysis.
- Confirm whether PLO/OFLA wage replacement applies during unpaid leave; employee cost-of-living impact on promotion timing.
- Verify whether garden-leave payment must continue during promoted role if NCA triggered.
- Confirm interaction of ORS 653.295 salary threshold with bonuses/commissions (base salary alone or total compensation).
