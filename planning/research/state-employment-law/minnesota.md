# Minnesota Employment Law Research

**State:** Minnesota | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

**Key compliance obligations for Human Capital Management Suite operating in Minnesota:**

- **Wage notice timing (§ 181.032)**: Mandatory written notice at hiring detailing all pay terms (rate, basis, payday, deductions, employer contact, ESST rights, anti-retaliation statement). For promotion/pay change: written notice **prior to effective date** (advance notice required; cannot be retroactive). Violation triggers civil liability. System must enforce pre-effective-date notice timing.

- **Pay frequency (§ 181.101)**: Payment required at least once every 31 days on designated payday. Commissions due ≥quarterly. § 181.032 also requires itemized earnings statement each pay period (gross, net, deductions, hours worked, pay period dates, employee name, employer legal name).

- **Final pay (§ 181.13–181.14)**: Discharge = payment within 24 hours of written demand (or first board meeting if public employer). Resignation = payment on first/second regularly scheduled payday (max 20 days from termination). Penalties: 1 day's average wages per day in default, max 15 days. Accrued ESST and PFML must be included as "earned wages" unless pre-termination forfeiture policy exists.

- **Minimum wage and overtime (§ 177.24–177.25)**: Statewide $11.41/hour (Jan 1, 2026); Minneapolis $16.37/hour; St. Paul $16.37 (large), $15–$16.37 (small/micro). Overtime = 1.5x for hours >48/week (not 40/week—Minnesota threshold is 48 hours). No tip credit allowed. Meal breaks ≥30 min for >6 hrs; rest breaks ≥15 min per 4 hrs.

- **Non-compete void (§ 181.988)**: All non-compete agreements are void except sale of business or partnership dissolution. HR system cannot impose or enforce non-compete on promotion. Pre-existing non-competes void and unenforceable retroactively (effective July 1, 2023).

- **Earned Sick and Safe Time (ESST) (§ 181.9445–181.9448)**: Effective Jan 1, 2024. ≥1 hour per 30 hours worked, max 48 hours/year; carry-over to 80-hour cap. Payment at base wage rate (excludes commissions, bonuses). Use for own/family health condition, preventive care, domestic abuse. Accrual does not reset on promotion/demotion; balance transfers. Payout required on termination.

- **Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML) (Ch. 268B)**: Effective Jan 1, 2026. Up to 12 weeks paid leave per year for bonding, family care, pregnancy, serious health condition, qualifying exigency, safety leave. Benefit = 90% of wage up to 50% of state average weekly wage, plus 66% above. Job-protected; employer cost-share with employee (verify premium structure). Pending legislation may repeal effective July 1, 2025 (verify status).

- **Salary history and pay transparency (§ 363A.08, § 181.173, § 181.172)**: No salary history inquiries permitted (effective Jan 1, 2022). § 181.173 (effective 2024) requires salary range in job postings for 30+ employee employers. § 181.172 protects employee wage disclosure from retaliation; no non-disclosure agreements enforceable.

- **Personnel file access (§ 181.960–181.966)**: Current employees may inspect records once per 6 months; former employees once per year, free copy available. Inspection within 30 days of written request (extendable to 35). No refusal permitted; violation = injunctive relief + damages possible.

- **Drug testing (§ 181.950–181.957)**: Limited to safety-sensitive positions only (impairment would threaten health/safety). Cannabis testable for safety positions, peace officers, firefighters, vulnerable population care, CDL holders. Employer cannot charge employee for test. Off-duty cannabis use protected (no discipline for residential use within state law).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Required Notices

Minnesota is at-will employment with limited exceptions for public policy violations, whistleblower retaliation (§ 181.932), and discrimination (§ 363A). At-will means employer may terminate employee for any reason or no reason, and employee may quit for any reason, unless statute, regulation, or contract provides otherwise. No written employment contract required at hire unless employer chooses to create one (verbal employment agreement valid and enforceable). Implied contracts may be formed by employee handbook or repeated conduct (e.g., progressive discipline if handbook requires).

**Required notice at hiring:** § 181.032 mandates written notice in English (or requested language) at time of hire containing: (1) pay rate(s) and basis (hourly/salary/commission/piecework); (2) work schedule and payday (when pay is distributed); (3) permitted deductions and pre-authorized deductions; (4) employer legal name and any business aliases; (5) employer main office address and mailing address; (6) employer phone number; (7) workers' compensation insurance carrier name; (8) ESST accrual rights (as of Jan 1, 2024); (9) anti-retaliation policy statement. Notice must be on individual sheet or combined with other onboarding materials; must be clear and understandable. Notice must be in language of employment communication (if employee hired through Spanish-language communication, notice must be in Spanish). Employers must retain copy of signed notice; employee may be required to sign acknowledgment of receipt and return signed copy, but signature not mandatory per statute (many employers request for documentation). Failure to provide notice: wage violation subject to civil remedy.

**Notice for pay rate or terms change:** § 181.032 requires written notice of any change to compensation **prior to the date the changes take effect** (mandatory timing; no exceptions). Notice lists only the changed information (not entire pay structure, just changes). No employee signature required for change notices (unlike initial notice). Changes triggering notice requirement: (1) pay rate increase/decrease; (2) change in pay basis (hourly to salary conversion); (3) new payday schedule; (4) addition/removal of authorized deductions; (5) change in work schedule affecting pay. "Prior to" = notice must be delivered before effective date; if notice delivered same day as effective date, it is insufficient (must be advance notice with reasonable lead time, typically 5+ business days). Notice delivered via email to employee's known email address or provided in person satisfies delivery requirement. Failure to provide advance notice: violation of wage laws; employee may pursue civil action for damages.

**At-will employment exceptions:** Limited exceptions to at-will doctrine include: (1) whistleblower protection (§ 181.932—cannot terminate for reporting safety violations, OSHA matters, illegal activity); (2) discrimination (§ 363A—cannot terminate based on protected class); (3) retaliation for ESST use (§ 181.9447); (4) retaliation for PFML leave (Ch. 268B); (5) retaliation for wage disclosure (§ 181.172); (6) retaliation for pregnancy accommodation request (§ 181.939). Also, implied contract may exist if handbook/repeated practice creates expectation of job security for "just cause" only (verify fact-specific analysis).

**PFML notice requirement:** Ch. 268B requires employers to provide notice of PFML rights and eligibility to all employees; notice content specified in administrative rules (verify final rule text for specific notice language and timing, expected mid-2026). Notice should include: eligibility criteria, covered events, benefit calculation, job protection, timeline for filing, confidentiality of medical info, anti-retaliation policy.

**Handbook and policies:** No state law mandating employee handbook, but if provided, handbook becomes part of employment terms and may override at-will doctrine if it promises job security or requires "just cause" for termination. Recommend disclaimers that handbook is not a contract and employment remains at-will; however, courts may view disclaimers skeptically if handbook details specific termination procedures (verify effectiveness of disclaimers under Minnesota law—courts may find implied contract despite disclaimer).

**Protected class discrimination (§ 363A):** Minnesota Human Rights Act prohibits discrimination in employment based on: race, color, creed, religion, national origin, sex, gender identity, marital status, disability, status with regard to public assistance, sexual orientation, familial status, age (40+), or lawful off-duty conduct. Applies to employers with 1+ employee. Covers all employment actions: hiring, promotion, compensation, discipline, termination, leave, training. "Gender identity" = inherent sense of being man, woman, both, or neither. Retaliation for complaint also prohibited. Enforcement: Minnesota Department of Human Rights (administrative complaint, investigation, conciliation, hearing). Private right of action available; civil damages include back pay, front pay, compensatory damages, punitive damages (if discrimination was intentional), and attorney's fees. Charge deadline: within 2 years of discriminatory act (verify—may be 1 year for federal EEOC charge-filing deadlines).

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions, and Pay Statements

**Minimum wage:** § 177.24 sets base rate adjusted annually for inflation (no tip credit allowed). Effective Jan 1, 2026: $11.41/hour statewide; $16.37/hour in Minneapolis (all employer sizes); St. Paul tiered by size: $16.37 (large/macro, 101+ employees and small, 6-100 employees), $14.25 (micro, ≤5 employees). Effective Jan 1, 2025: $11.13/hour statewide; St. Paul tiered $15.97 (large/macro), $15.00 (small), $13.25 (micro). No tip credit allowed. Training wage for employees under 20 is 90% of state minimum; applies first 90 days only. Applies to all employees unless specifically exempted; agricultural workers covered. No piece-rate or commission deduction permitted to fall below minimum wage.

**Overtime:** § 177.25 requires 1.5x regular rate for hours exceeding 48/week. Threshold is 48 hours per workweek (not 40 as under FLSA; Minnesota is a 48-hour state). Regular rate includes all compensation except: bonuses, gifts, severance, payments for time not worked. No overtime pay required for agricultural labor, certain exempt categories, or workers with valid collective bargaining agreement providing alternative compensation. Public employers and political subdivisions may offer compensatory time off (1.5 hours per overtime hour) in lieu of overtime pay if employee agrees; employee may cash out unused comp time on termination. Motor vehicle salespeople, healthcare facility workers (14-day agreed period), silo constructors (piece-rate), and air carrier employees (voluntary hour-trading) exempt.

**Meal and rest breaks:** § 177.254 (effective Jan 1, 2026) mandates unpaid meal break ≥30 minutes for work exceeding 6 consecutive hours; employer must provide proactively, not conditional on employee request. If work exceeds 12 consecutive hours, second 30-minute meal break required. § 177.253 (effective Jan 1, 2026) mandates paid rest break ≥15 minutes or reasonable access to nearest restroom (whichever longer) for each 4 consecutive hours worked; rest period must be scheduled within the 4-hour period. Collective bargaining agreements may specify different break schedules; no waiver by employee without agreement. Break periods must be counted as work time and compensated at regular rate. Employer cannot dock pay for break time or deduct break time from hours. Violations subject to civil action for unpaid break wages; employee may recover damages plus attorney's fees.

**Pay frequency:** § 181.101 requires payment at least once every 31 days on a regular designated payday clearly communicated in advance. Commissions and gratuities due at least once every 3 months. Final paycheck follows § 181.13/181.14 rules (separate from regular pay frequency). "Employee" includes agricultural labor. Part-time and seasonal workers subject to same frequency rule.

**Earnings statements:** § 181.032 requires written statement (hard copy or electronic) at each pay period showing: (1) gross pay (total wages before deductions); (2) net pay (take-home amount); (3) deductions itemized by type and amount (taxes, insurance, garnishments, authorized deductions); (4) hours worked (unless exempt employee, in which case "salaried" notation sufficient); (5) pay period dates and payday; (6) employee name and identification; (7) last 4 SSN digits or equivalent employee identifier; (8) employer legal name and address; (9) if commission/piecework, number of pieces/units and rate. Statement must be detachable from paycheck if check is issued; electronic delivery acceptable if accessible to employee. Must be delivered at or before payday. Retained for 3 years by employer (accessible for wage audit). Violations subject to civil remedies; employee may sue for damages and recover attorney's fees. Employer penalty: civil liability for each violation (damages plus possible civil penalty).

**Final pay on discharge:** § 181.13 mandates all earned and unpaid wages due "immediately upon demand" (within 24 hours of written demand for regular employees). Employer in default if not paid within 24-hour window. Discharged employee may recover penalty equal to average daily earnings (at applicable rate) for each day employer is in default, max 15 days. Special rule for employers requiring board approval (public entities): 24-hour clock starts on first regular/special board meeting date after discharge, not the termination date itself. Accrued paid leave (ESST, PFML, vacation) earned during employment must be included as "earned wages" unless employer has a written forfeiture policy established pre-termination.

**Final pay on resignation:** § 181.14 requires payment on first regularly scheduled payday following final day of work, unless first payday is within 5 days of separation (then second payday permitted, but max 20 calendar days total from termination). Migrant workers receive final pay within 3 days of quitting (expedited). Penalty for late payment: average daily earnings for each day in default, max 15 days total, if not paid within 24 hours of written demand. Employer may audit employee accounts (if entrusted with money/property) for up to 10 additional calendar days before final pay deadline.

**Deductions:** § 181.79 prohibits deductions for lost/stolen property or damage unless: (1) employee authorizes in writing _after_ loss occurs; (2) court finds employee liable; or (3) collective bargaining agreement permits. Deductions capped at wage-garnishment limits (Minnesota wage attachment law). Employers who violate owe 2x the deducted amount to employee in civil action. Commissioned salespeople may have performance-based fines per negotiated terms (verify written agreement required). Union dues, tax withholding, and court-ordered garnishments permitted. Repayment plans for advance wages, loans, or uniform costs must be negotiated in advance and not reduce pay below minimum wage.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity: Salary Range, History Ban, Pay Data Reporting, Equal Pay

**Salary history ban:** § 363A.08 subd. 8 (effective Jan 1, 2022) prohibits inquiring into, considering, or requiring disclosure of applicant's prior wage, salary, earnings, benefits, or compensation for applicants for employment. Employees cannot be required to disclose pay history to employer; ban covers all inquiry methods (direct, indirect, third-party reference checks, background check vendors). "Pay history" includes: current/prior wages, salary, earnings, benefits, bonuses, stock options, or any other compensation from any source. Exceptions: (1) if applicant voluntarily discloses (not solicited), employer may consider; (2) employment verification for background check purposes (verify prior earnings amount only for eligibility, not for setting compensation). Penalties: civil remedies available; employee may sue for damages and attorney's fees; penalties include actual damages plus civil penalty per violation (verify exact penalty structure—penalties vary by source). Applies regardless of employer size (no employee-count threshold).

**Pay range disclosure:** § 181.173 (effective 2024) requires employers with 30+ employees to include starting salary range and general description of benefits/compensation in all job postings. "Salary range" = employer's good faith estimate of minimum and maximum annual salary or hourly compensation based on role. Employers may alternatively list fixed pay rate instead of range; ranges cannot be open-ended. Third-party recruiters posting on behalf of 30+ employee employers must also disclose range. No requirement to disclose range in individual applicant inquiries if not yet posted. Penalty: civil enforcement by labor commissioner or private action; damages available.

**Wage and job-title record retention:** § 181.032 and § 363A.08 require employers to maintain job title and wage rate history (including start date and end date for each rate) for each employee throughout employment plus 3 years post-separation, accessible for wage audit by Minnesota labor commissioner. Cannot be discarded or destroyed during retention period. Records must identify: employee name, job title, pay rate, pay frequency, effective date of each change.

**Equal pay statute:** § 181.67 (Equal Pay for Equal Work Law) prohibits employers from paying employees of one sex less than employees of the opposite sex for equal work on jobs requiring equal skill, effort, and responsibility under similar working conditions. Wage differentials permitted if based on seniority system, merit system, quantity/quality production, or any factor other than sex (§ 181.66–181.71). Retaliation for wage discrimination complaints also prohibited. Applies to all employers regardless of size.

**Pay equity reporting:** No mandatory state equal-pay audit or pay equity pay-gap data reporting to state agency. No equivalent to California's SB 973 pay data transparency rules. Federal EEO-1 filing applies to employers with 100+ employees (federal, not state, requirement).

**Wage disclosure protection:** § 181.172 prohibits employer from: (1) requiring nondisclosure of own wages as employment condition; (2) requiring employee to waive right to disclose wages; (3) retaliating against employee for disclosing own wages; (4) retaliating for discussing voluntarily disclosed wages of coworkers. "Retaliation" includes discharge, discipline, penalty, interference, threat, restraint, coercion, or discrimination. Does not prohibit employer from keeping wage information confidential in system or from limiting payroll staff access (legitimate business purpose); protects employee's right to discuss, not employer's obligation to disclose. Applies to current and former employees. Remedies: civil action, back pay, front pay, reinstatement, expungement of adverse records.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Pregnancy, Other Mandated Leave

**Earned Sick and Safe Time (ESST):** § 181.9445–181.9448 (effective Jan 1, 2024) requires ≥1 hour accrual per 30 hours worked, up to 48 hours/year minimum; carry-over to next year permitted until 80-hour cap reached (additional accruals above 80 may be forfeited if employer provides front-loaded leave or 2-year use-it-or-lose-it policy). Payment at same base rate as employment (min. applicable minimum wage); excludes commissions, shift differentials, premium pay, bonuses, gratuities. Employees anticipated to work ≥80 hours/year in Minnesota are covered (excludes independent contractors, fire/police emergency responders, elected officials, farm workers ≤28 days/year, certain temporary positions <120 days). Use permitted for: (1) employee's own serious health condition; (2) family member's serious health condition; (3) preventive care; (4) victim of domestic abuse, harassment, or sexual assault (including legal proceedings, relocation); or (5) public health emergency. Employer cannot require medical certification unless leave is for serious health condition; employer cannot delay ESST use, limit use to specific days, or require advance notice longer than notice customary for regular leave. Accrual does not reset on demotion or pay cut; accrued balance transfers to new role. Payout on termination required for accrued but unused ESST (unless employer has written carve-out in handbook). Rules clarified by administrative rules effective July 6, 2026 (Minn. Rules Ch. 3313.5000 et seq.).

**Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML):** Ch. 268B (effective Jan 1, 2026) provides up to 12 weeks paid leave per benefit year for: (1) bonding with newborn/newly placed child (up to 12 months after event); (2) family care (serious health condition of family member); (3) medical leave for employee's own serious health condition; (4) medical care related to pregnancy; (5) qualifying exigency (military); or (6) safety leave (domestic abuse, harassment, sexual assault). Benefit calculation: 90% of wages up to 50% of state average weekly wage, plus 66% above 50% up to 100%. Eligibility requires ≥$5,300 (5.3% of state average annual wage) in Minnesota wages in benefit-year base period (prior 12 months). Applicant may file 60 days before leave; benefits retroactive to leave start if filed after. Employer contribution required (verify premium structure finalization: cost-share between employer and employee; detailed rules effective mid-2026 per administrative rulemaking). Job protection applies; employer cannot terminate, discipline, or retaliate for taking PFML leave. Interacts with FMLA (federal 12-week rule); state leave runs concurrently—federal and state leave both count toward the 12-week annual maximum if both are applicable. Note: Pending legislation (HF 1263, SF 1771, 2025 session) may repeal PFML effective July 1, 2025 (verify status as of implementation date).

**Pregnancy accommodations:** § 181.939 requires employers to provide reasonable pregnancy accommodations without requiring medical documentation for standard accommodations, including: (1) limits on lifting >20 pounds; (2) seating access; (3) frequent or extended breaks for restroom use; (4) frequent or extended breaks to eat and drink water. Employer cannot discharge, discipline, penalize, interfere, threaten, or retaliate for requesting accommodations. Additional reasonable accommodations negotiable (temporary transfer, modified schedule, unpaid leave, flexible hours). Accommodation requests trigger interactive process; employer must respond timely. Applies to all employers regardless of size (no employee-count threshold).

**Reproductive health:** § 145.409 (effective 2024) provides employees the fundamental right to make autonomous decisions about reproductive health, including use/refusal of reproductive health care and right to continue pregnancy or obtain abortion without employer interference. Employer cannot discriminate against employee for reproductive health choices.

**FMLA (federal):** Applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75-mile radius; 12 weeks unpaid job-protected leave per year for own serious health condition, family member care, bonding, or military. Minnesota leave runs concurrently with FMLA eligibility.

**No state parental leave (unpaid):** Minnesota has no separate unpaid parental leave statute beyond PFML and FMLA.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Format, Employee Inspection Rights

**Personnel file access:** §§ 181.960–181.966 grant current and former employees right to review personnel records at reasonable times within 30 calendar days of written request (extendable to 35 days by written mutual agreement). Current employees may inspect once per 6 months; former employees may inspect once per year or receive free copy once per year if record is maintained (for employees separated <1 year). Personnel records include: job applications, wage history, payroll records, discipline records, leave records, performance evaluations, employment history, salary and promotion details, training records, grievances, termination notice. Inspection must occur at workplace or mutually agreed location; employer must provide copies within same 30–35 day window at reasonable reproduction cost (cost cannot exceed actual copying expense). Employer cannot refuse inspection or delay beyond 35 days; violation may result in civil action for injunctive relief and damages. Employer must maintain records in accessible format (hard copy or electronic). Records for employees separated >1 year need not be maintained by statute, but common-law duty of care applies; recommend 3–7 year retention to account for claim statute of limitations.

**Personnel file disputes (effective July 1, 2024):** Employees may dispute accuracy of any contents of personnel record; employer must note the dispute in the file or record in the system. Employer cannot retaliate for filing dispute.

**Payroll record retention:** § 181.032 requires retention of earnings statements and wage history at least 3 years; violations subject to civil penalty. § 363A.08 (salary history) requires job-title and wage-rate history 3 years post-separation (accessible to labor commissioner for wage audits). Recommend 4-year retention to cover wage claim statute of limitations in Minnesota.

**Record format and accessibility:** No state mandate for electronic vs. paper storage; both formats acceptable. Electronic records must be accessible to employee upon request (printable, downloadable, or searchable database access). No requirement for specific software or cloud storage. For records subject to retention rules, employers must maintain copies in accessible format and location during required retention period.

**Excluded from personnel file (not subject to inspection rights):** (1) References or recommendation letters; (2) Incomplete investigation records (until investigation concluded and adverse action taken); (3) Education records under FERPA; (4) Test results except cumulative scores; (5) Employer salary/budget planning systems; (6) Personal information about other employees/third parties; (7) Supervisor notes held solely by author (not shared); (8) Privileged attorney-client communications or litigation strategy; (9) Coworker statements that would reveal their identity (redact names); (10) Medical records and health information (distinct from leave records). Employer may redact excluded content but must provide all other contents.

**Retention of wage-related records:** Earnings statements (§ 181.032), tax forms (federal requirement), and timesheets must be retained minimum 3 years. Payroll journals, payment records, and hours-worked records similarly 3 years. If dispute or claim arises, preserve records until claim is resolved.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Privacy Law Coverage, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data privacy law:** Minnesota Consumer Data Privacy Act (Minn. Stat. ch. 325O, effective July 31, 2025) establishes comprehensive privacy rights for personal data collected by businesses. Employees and HR data processed in employment context are exempt from most MCPA requirements (verify exact exemption scope—employment exemption likely applies to payroll, benefits, hiring data; clarify whether training/development/performance data subject to MCPA). Additionally, state data-handling practices governed by public records law (Minn. Stat. § 13.01 et seq.) for government employers; federal privacy rules apply (HIPAA for health data, FCRA for background checks). Employers should maintain reasonable data security for employee data in HCM systems. Personnel file access rights (§§ 181.960–181.966) allow employees to inspect and copy records within 30 days.

**Breach notification:** § 325E.61 requires any person/business conducting business in Minnesota (even if data stored elsewhere) that owns or licenses "personal information" of Minnesota residents to notify affected residents without unreasonable delay upon discovery of breach of security. "Personal information" = unencrypted or unredacted data that can identify individual (SSN, driver's license, financial account, etc.). Timeline: "most expedient time possible" and "without unreasonable delay," consistent with legitimate law enforcement needs and scope-of-breach assessment (typically 30–60 days standard practice). Applies to employee/payroll data breaches (SSN, bank account info in HCM systems). Written notice to employee (email to known address or registered mail) required. Notice must include: description of breach, types of information compromised, approximate number of affected individuals, recommended protective actions (credit monitoring, etc.), employer's breach response plan. Government entities notifying of breach must include: type of data, number of affected individuals, employee's name if disciplined, disposition of disciplinary action. No private right of action (government enforcement only), but employers should proactively notify to avoid regulatory action.

**Biometric data:** No Minnesota state statute specifically restricting biometric collection (fingerprint clocks, facial recognition, etc.) or requiring consent for biometric time-tracking. Employers should follow federal standards (BIPA applies in Illinois; no equivalent Minnesota statute) and obtain employee consent for biometric collection as best practice. HCM systems storing biometric data should implement reasonable security (encryption in transit/at rest).

**Employment data processing:** No data residency requirement (data may be stored in cloud, out-of-state, or internationally per employer choice). However, if HCM vendor processes employee data, vendor should be subject to data processing agreements (DPA) covering confidentiality, security, and permitted uses.

**Polygraph and honesty testing:** § 181.75 prohibits employers from requiring, soliciting, or requesting polygraph, voice stress analysis, or any test purporting to test honesty/integrity of employees or applicants; violation is misdemeanor offense. Does not prohibit pre-employment integrity assessments (written tests, reference checks); applies to mechanical/electronic "honesty" tests only. § 181.76 restricts disclosure of test results (even if employee-requested test) except to tested individual and authorized recipients named by employee (e.g., attorney). Disclosure of fact that employee took test or test results without consent is violation. Violators may be liable for civil damages.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (conviction history):** § 364.021 prohibits public and private employers from inquiring into, considering, or requiring disclosure of applicant's criminal record or history until applicant has been selected for interview or, if no interview, before conditional offer of employment is made. Employer may notify applicants upfront that certain criminal histories disqualify applicant from specific positions (conditional disqualification permitted). Exceptions: Department of Corrections and employers with statutory duty requiring criminal background check during hiring. If conviction inquiry is used to screen out applicant post-interview, employer must assess: job-relatedness, nature/severity of crime, time since conviction, rehabilitation. Federal FCRA compliance required if third-party criminal background check used (accuracy, dispute rights, adverse action notice).

**Background checks:** No state-specific background check limitations beyond federal FCRA compliance (Fair Credit Reporting Act, 15 USC § 1681 et seq.). Employer must: (1) obtain written consent; (2) provide FCRA disclosure and authorization form; (3) provide "Notice of Adverse Action" if background check will be used to deny employment (including right to dispute/explain); (4) provide final adverse action notice if denied. Minnesota courts apply federal FCRA standards. No additional state-level restrictions on scope of background check (driving records, credit reports, conviction history all permissible if job-related and compliant with FCRA).

**Drug and alcohol testing:** §§ 181.950–181.957 restrict testing to safety-sensitive positions only (defined as jobs where employee impairment caused by drug, alcohol, or cannabis would threaten health/safety of any person). Cannabis is testable for: (1) safety-sensitive work; (2) peace officer positions; (3) firefighter positions; (4) face-to-face care to vulnerable populations; (5) positions requiring commercial driver's license. Impairment testing (breath alcohol, drug screen) permissible; random testing permissible for safety positions. Pre-employment drug screen allowed. Employer cannot charge employee or applicant for test cost (any cost borne by employer). Marijuana use off-duty outside work and employer premises does not violate; employer cannot discipline for off-duty use. Workplace marijuana use/impairment may be restricted. Positive test results must be confirmed by medical review officer (MRO) with MRO review and opportunity to explain (e.g., prescribed medication, lab error). Testing must comply with established protocols (DOT/SAMHSA standards or equivalent). Applicant/employee has right to split specimen (retain portion for independent lab test).

**Alcohol and cannabis use:** Employers may restrict employee use during work hours and on premises. Off-duty cannabis use (residential use within limits of state cannabis law) generally protected from discipline. Off-duty alcohol use also generally protected unless it affects job performance.

**E-Verify:** No Minnesota state E-Verify mandate for all employers (verify if federal I-9 now requires E-Verify in Minnesota—currently not federally mandated nationwide for all private employers, though some federal contractors must use). Private employers not required to use E-Verify; I-9 paper verification satisfies federal requirement. If employer voluntarily uses E-Verify, must comply with E-Verify procedures and notice requirements (federal law).

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**WARN notification (federal and state):** Minnesota has no state-specific WARN act separate from federal WARN Act (29 USC § 2101 et seq.). Federal WARN requires 60-day advance notice for plant closings (permanent shutdown) or mass layoffs affecting 50+ employees (at a single site) who work 500+ hours in prior 12 months. Notice must go to: affected employees, employee representatives (union), state labor commissioner, local workforce development office. Minnesota Statutes § 116L.976 defines "plant closing" as announced/actual permanent/temporary shutdown of single site of employment or one or more facilities within site, if shutdown causes employment loss of 50+ employees (excluding those <20 hours/week) in any 30-day period. State law incorporates federal WARN by reference; employers must comply with federal WARN requirements. No additional state-specific notice requirements or penalties beyond federal WARN (federal penalties apply: up to 60 days back pay + benefits, attorney's fees).

**Severance packages:** No state mandate for severance pay; severance negotiable between employer and employee. Any offered severance: (1) must not offset final pay obligations (final pay must be paid in full per § 181.13/181.14 regardless of severance amount); (2) may be conditioned on release of claims/confidentiality (verify enforceability under state law; non-compete/non-solicitation conditions in severance are subject to voidability rules below); (3) if employer offers severance, payment timeline follows § 181.13/181.14 (payment within 24 hours or next payday).

**Non-compete agreements:** § 181.988 (effective July 1, 2023) voids all non-compete covenants (agreements that restrict employee from performing work for competitor post-termination) in employment except: (1) agreed upon **at the time of sale of business** (seller/shareholder may restrict for reasonable period/geography after sale); (2) dissolution of partnership/LLC/corporation (partners/members/shareholders may restrict each other post-dissolution). Non-compete **cannot restrict** employee post-termination from competing with employer after termination unless sale/dissolution exception applies. Definition: "covenant not to compete" = agreement restricting employee, after termination, from performing work for another employer for specified time period, geographic area, or capacity similar to prior work. Does not include: non-disclosure agreements, trade secret protection, customer non-solicitation, client list restrictions (those remain enforceable if reasonable). **All non-competes signed before July 1, 2023 are void** (retroactive application). Agreements violating statute are unenforceable; employee may sue for attorney's fees. Notably: HR system cannot impose or enforce non-compete on promotion; if pre-existing non-compete exists in employee record, promotion workflow should note: "Non-compete per Minn. Stat. § 181.988 is void unless related to sale of business or business dissolution."

**Non-solicitation of employees:** No state statute restricting non-solicitation of employees (employer can restrict employee from soliciting coworkers post-termination if reasonable). Minnesota allows reasonable non-solicits between employer and employee.

**Non-solicitation of customers:** § 181.9881 (effective July 1, 2023) prohibits service providers from restricting customers from directly or indirectly soliciting or hiring service-provider employees; any contract clause attempting to restrict customer solicitation is void. Does **not** restrict employer from restricting employee non-solicitation of customers post-termination (employer-employee non-solicits differ from customer-employee restriction). Service-provider defined as person/business providing contracted services; applies to employment relationships where services are provided on behalf of customer.

**Final paycheck on termination:** See § 181.13 (discharge) and § 181.14 (resignation) in Section 3 above. Severance not required but if offered must be paid timely per § 181.13/181.14 timeline (within 24 hours or next payday).

**References and information disclosures:** No statute restricting employer's right to provide employment references or information to future employers; employers generally have qualified immunity for truthful reference information provided without malice (common law).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote-Worker Rules, Reciprocity

**Contractor vs. employee (general labor):** Minnesota does not have a comprehensive ABC test (alternative independent contractor test) for general labor statewide (ABC test applies only to workers' compensation under § 181.722, not employment classification). For general employment classification, Minnesota courts apply **common-law control test**: if employer controls manner/means of work, individual is employee; if individual controls how work is done, contractor status possible. Factors: (1) control over work; (2) right to discharge; (3) whether work is part of employer's usual business; (4) whether work requires special skills; (5) provision of tools/materials; (6) duration of relationship; (7) method of payment; (8) tax treatment. No single factor determinative; all factors weighed together. Default presumption: employee unless contractor demonstrates independence.

**Construction worker classification (§ 181.723, amended 2024):** Construction workers have special protections. **Through Feb 28, 2025:** individual providing commercial/residential building construction services presumes employee status unless meets ALL nine criteria: (1) control over performance; (2) opportunity for profit/loss; (3) operates independently/offers services to others; (4) supplies own equipment/tools; (5) maintains business location; (6) maintains business license; (7) operates business for period; (8) invests capital; (9) performs work in usual course of business. Failure on ANY factor = employee.

**Starting Mar 1, 2025:** construction worker classification test expands to 14-factor test IF individual operates as business entity (LLC, S-corp, etc.). Individual contractors (sole proprietors) still subject to 9-factor test through Feb 28, 2025; test may change in 2025 legislative session—verify updated rules.

**Misclassification penalties:** Employer cannot require or request individual who is employee to enter into agreement/document misclassifying as independent contractor. Each misclassifying agreement = separate violation. Penalties: administrative fines, wage & hour violations, workers' compensation violations.

**Remote workers and multi-location employees:** No state remote-work restrictions; employees working from home in Minnesota subject to Minnesota wage/hour laws. If employee works partly in Minnesota and partly in another state, Minnesota law applies to hours worked in Minnesota (minimum wage, overtime, breaks, leave). Employer must track work location and apply appropriate state law. For remote employees residing in Minnesota but working for out-of-state employer: Minnesota minimum wage and hour laws apply to work performed for Minnesota residents/businesses.

**Work location and wage requirements:** If employee works in Minneapolis or St. Paul (even part-time), minimum wage for hours worked in those cities = local minimum wage ($16.37 and variable by St. Paul tier, respectively). If employee works statewide headquarters in Minneapolis but does field work statewide, track hours by location and apply appropriate minimum.

**Multi-state reciprocity:** No interstate employment reciprocity agreement. Employer operating in multiple states must comply with each state's wage/hour laws, leave laws, and notice requirements for employees in that state. Multi-state employers should maintain separate compliance calendars for: minimum wage increases (each state on different schedule), ESST accrual/payout (Minnesota-specific), pay frequency requirements (may vary by state), final pay timing (may vary by state), notice requirements.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

When Human Capital Management Suite processes a promotion and/or base-pay increase for a Minnesota employee, the following obligations apply:

**Pre-effective-date notice (P1A trigger):** § 181.032 requires written notice of pay rate or pay-basis change **prior to the date the change takes effect** (not after, not concurrent). Notice is mandatory; no exemption by contract. The system must enforce this timing requirement: generate and send notice at least 5–10 business days before the effective date to ensure timely receipt and employee comprehension. System should validate that notice-send date is before effective date in workflow and prevent workflow completion if timing violates statute. Notice must be in English and any language requested by employee (verify if employee language preference is captured in system; if not, assume English + offer translation).

**Notice content and format:** Record and communicate in written form (hard copy or electronic): (1) new pay rate ($/hour or annual salary); (2) pay basis (hourly, salary, commission, etc.); (3) new payday (if changed from prior schedule); (4) any new deduction authorizations (if promotion adds benefits with employee contribution); (5) effective date of change; (6) reference to ESST accrual continuation (if applicable); (7) confirmation that non-compete agreements are void under state law (if employee has pre-existing non-compete). Do not require employee signature on change notice (unlike initial notice); however, document employee's receipt (email read receipt, system acknowledgment, or date of delivery).

**ESST interaction:** Promotion does not reset, forfeit, or reduce accrued ESST balance; carry-over continues at 80-hour cap. If promotion includes hourly wage increase, recalculate ESST payout rate (use new hourly rate going forward for future accruals and payouts). Accrual rate remains ≥1 hour per 30 hours worked regardless of promotion. If employee moves from hourly to salaried, recalculate accrual base rate (new salary per year ÷ 2,080 hours [40 hrs/week × 52 weeks] = hourly equivalent for accrual). No proration of accrual for partial-year status on promotion; continue to accrue at regular rate. If promotion changes role classification (e.g., full-time to part-time or vice versa), ESST balance transfers; accrual rate continues based on new status and hours expected.

**PFML interaction (if employee on PFML or approaching eligibility as of Jan 1, 2026):** Wages increases granted during or after PFML leave do not apply retroactively to the leave period itself (leave benefit calculated on wages earned before leave began). If promotion occurs while employee is actively on PFML leave, new base rate applies upon return to work and for future PFML benefit calculations if another leave is taken in the same benefit year. Job status must not change as a result of PFML leave; promotion/pay increase cannot be conditioned on or withheld during/after leave. Communicate clearly: "Returning from PFML leave does not affect your job title or pay rate; any promotion or raise is separate from leave." If employer policy ties promotion dates to performance year, ensure PFML absence does not disqualify or delay consideration.

**Minimum wage floor check:** System must flag if new pay rate (post-promotion) falls below applicable minimum wage (state $11.41/hour or local Minneapolis $16.37/hour or St. Paul tiered rate for work location). Promotion system should auto-validate against employee's work location and applicable minimum and prevent pay rates below floor. If promotion is in Minneapolis or St. Paul and new rate would be below local minimum, system should halt and escalate to compliance.

**Salary history field restriction:** Do not store, query, or reference employee's prior salary history in promotion/pay-change workflow (§ 363A.08). Any historical compensation data (prior salary, salary history from other employers, negotiated range from hire) must be quarantined from the pay-decision logic. HCM system should: (1) flag if legacy salary-history field is accessed during promotion workflow; (2) log access as potential compliance violation; (3) prohibit using salary history as a justification variable in pay-increase decision. Compensation justification must be based on market data, role requirements, performance (if documented separately), or internal equity (peer roles, not historical pay).

**Wage disclosure:** Record promotion/pay change in system; inform employee that their pay rate may be disclosed to coworkers (§ 181.172 protects such disclosure from retaliation). Employer cannot require non-disclosure agreement, gag clause, or agreement to keep pay confidential as a condition of promotion. If company policy has confidentiality clauses, ensure legal review that they comply with § 181.172 protections (clauses restricting discussion of own or coworkers' disclosed pay are void).

**Pay-range posting (if promotion creates new or changed role):** If promotion creates a new title or materially changes the role and employer has 30+ employees, system must flag that any new job posting for that role must include salary range per **§ 181.173** (effective 2025-01-01, enacted by the 2024 Omnibus Labor and Industry Policy Bill, SF 3852). **Correction (2026-09-03 contradiction closure):** § 181.9414 was repealed effective 2021 (2020 Minn. Stat., amended 1Sp2021 c.10 art.3 s.22) and is not the pay-range posting statute; the operative citation is § 181.173, consistent with the Summary and § 4 above. "Salary range" = employer's reasonable estimate of hourly rate or annual salary for the position. If new title is not posted externally, no posting requirement. Recommendations: (1) integrate salary-range requirement into job-posting workflow; (2) flag roles that have never had a salary range posted; (3) require range to be entered before job posting goes live.

**Non-compete void notice:** If employee has pre-existing non-compete agreement in file, promotion should not trigger re-signing, re-confirmation, or enforcement of the non-compete. System should generate compliance note: "Non-compete per Minn. Stat. § 181.988 is void unless related to sale of business or business dissolution. No non-compete enforcement on role change or promotion. Do not require employee to re-sign non-compete." If system encounters a non-compete flag on employee record during promotion, escalate to legal for review; do not proceed with re-signing.

**Overtime classification review:** If promotion moves employee from overtime-eligible (48-hour threshold under § 177.25) to exempt classification, system must trigger a review workflow: (1) verify that § 177.21 exemption criteria are met (executive, administrative, professional duties test—not merely salary-based); (2) require manager attestation that role duties meet exemption standard; (3) document duties change in personnel file; (4) do not rely on salary alone for exemption. Conversely, if promotion moves exempt to non-exempt, recalculate overtime eligibility. System should flag if classification change lacks documented duties justification.

**Compensation timing and effective date:** Final paycheck (if termination) follows § 181.13/181.14 (within 24 hours or next payday, depending on quit vs. discharge). Promotion pay increase typically effective on promotion date or next paycheck; system should verify that pay increase is reflected in first full pay period post-effective date. Do not delay pay increase to future pay period without documented business reason (e.g., payroll system lag); timely payment is required.

**Record retention and documentation:** System must maintain: (1) signed initial wage notice (at hire); (2) timestamped copy of all pay-change notices (showing issue date before effective date); (3) wage history (job title + rate + effective date) for 3 years post-termination; (4) ESST accrual and payout history; (5) any performance or justification documentation for the pay increase (if documented separately from notice). Store pay-change notices in employee's digital personnel file with metadata (issue date, effective date, approval chain).

**Multi-state or multi-location employees:** If promotion involves work in multiple states (Minnesota + neighboring state), ensure pay rate meets higher applicable state minimum wage and overtime threshold. If employee works partly in Minneapolis/St. Paul and partly statewide, apply Minneapolis/St. Paul minimum to hours worked in those cities (wage is location-based, not employer-based). Document work location in promotion record.

**Example workflow: Promotion from hourly employee in Minneapolis to salaried manager**

1. **Pre-promotion**: Capture current hourly rate, ESST accrual balance, and work location in HCM system.
2. **Promotion approval**: Manager initiates promotion with new salary ($60,000/year). System calculates: new hourly equivalent for ESST ($60,000 ÷ 2,080 = $28.85/hr accrual rate). System flags: "Minneapolis minimum wage $16.37/hour required—new salary $28.85/hr equivalent meets floor."
3. **Pre-effective notice (P1A)**: System generates written notice 5 business days before promotion effective date. Notice includes: new salary ($60,000/year), new pay basis (salaried), new payday (if changed), new position title (Manager), effective date, and statement: "Accrued ESST balance ($1,200) carries over to new role; accrual continues at equivalent rate. Non-compete per Minn. Stat. § 181.988 is void; no re-signing required."
4. **Pay processing**: First paycheck post-promotion reflects new salary in gross pay calculation.
5. **ESST recalculation**: System updates ESST payout rate to new salary ($28.85/hr equivalent). Employee may use accrued balance (balance does not expire or reset).
6. **Record retention**: System retains: signed initial wage notice from hire, dated promotion notice (pre-effective), wage history entry (prior hourly rate → new salary, effective date).

**Overtime classification during promotion:** If promotion is to exempt managerial role, system must verify: (1) new role duties include managing 2+ full-time employees (executive test); (2) new salary meets federal minimum (currently ~$864/week; Minnesota has no separate state minimum for exempt); (3) primary duty is management (not sales, manual work, or isolated management tasks). Manager completes duties attestation; HR/legal reviews before promotion is finalized. If duties do not meet exempt test, classification remains non-exempt even if salaried (salary alone does not create exemption).

**Promotion and PFML eligibility:** If employee approaches 12+ months tenure and anticipated 1,250+ hours worked in prior 12 months (federal FMLA) or has Minnesota PFML wage credits ($5,300+ in base period), promotion does not reset eligibility or accrual. Employee's leave-use and benefit year continue unaffected. If promotion increases wages, future PFML benefits calculated on new wage base if additional leave is taken in same benefit year (but prior leave period benefit amount not recalculated retroactively).

**Promotion and minimum-wage compliance review:** System should perform audit before promotion effective date: compare new pay rate against applicable minimum wage (state, Minneapolis, St. Paul based on work location). Flag if new rate falls below minimum; escalate to HR/Payroll to correct before promotion takes effect. Document audit result in promotion record for compliance trail. If promotion is to remote/multi-location role, identify primary work location and apply that jurisdiction's minimum; if work split across locations, apply highest applicable minimum or track hours by location in payroll.

**Notice delivery and record-keeping:** Promotion notice must be delivered in written form (email, printed letter, or system notification documented in HCM). Recommend: (1) email to employee's known email address with read receipt; (2) printed copy if employee works on-site; (3) system notification with acknowledgment checkbox. Maintain dated copy of notice in employee's digital file with delivery evidence (email metadata, timestamp of system acknowledgment). If employee disputes receipt, employer should demonstrate good-faith notice attempt (email delivery, system timestamp, signature on printed copy).

**Compliance audit checklist for promotion/pay-change transactions:**

- [ ] Salary history field quarantined (not accessed or displayed in promotion workflow)
- [ ] Pre-effective-date notice generated and sent 5+ business days before effective date
- [ ] New pay rate ≥ applicable minimum wage (state, local Minneapolis/St. Paul) for work location(s)
- [ ] ESST accrual balance calculated and confirmed to carry forward (not forfeited)
- [ ] ESST payout rate recalculated for new salary (if applicable)
- [ ] Non-compete voidability noted in system (if pre-existing non-compete in file)
- [ ] Overtime classification reviewed (if moving exempt/non-exempt)
- [ ] PFML eligibility/benefit-year status confirmed (no reset on promotion)
- [ ] FMLA eligibility confirmed (no reset on promotion)
- [ ] Wage history entry recorded (prior rate → new rate, effective date, promotion noted)
- [ ] Notice delivered and receipt documented (email, system acknowledgment, or signature)
- [ ] All records retained in personnel file (initial notice, all pay-change notices, wage history)

**Unresolved/verify-required items:**

- Whether pre-existing non-compete agreements signed before July 1, 2023 (when § 181.988 took effect) remain void on their face vs. any grandfathering rule (current understanding: void retroactively, but verify with legal).
- Whether PFML employer premium cost-share structure (employer %, employee %) is finalized by Department of Employment and Economic Development; if not yet finalized, defer final PFML cost line-item calculation in HR systems pending final administrative rules (expected mid-2026).
- Whether Minneapolis/St. Paul minimum-wage rates are tied to automatic annual CPI/inflation adjustment; if yes, system should auto-flag if promotion brings employee below indexed local minimum for work location (rates recalculated annually, typically Jan 1 Minneapolis, Jan 1/July 1 St. Paul).
- Whether Minneapolis has independent ban-the-box ordinance restricting conviction-history inquiry (verify local ordinance separate from state law).
- Whether federal E-Verify mandate applies in Minnesota; current state law has no state-specific E-Verify requirement, but federal I-9 verification applies to all employers.
