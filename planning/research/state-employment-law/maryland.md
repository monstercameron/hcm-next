# Maryland Employment Law Research

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with statutory exceptions**: Maryland recognizes employment-at-will but prohibits termination for exercising constitutional rights, filing wage/workers' compensation claims, jury duty, military service, or other public-policy violations.
- **Wage transparency mandate (Oct 2024)**: Employers must disclose wage range, benefits description, and other compensation in all public and internal job postings (Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-304.2); applicants may request salary range at any time.
- **Salary history ban**: Employers cannot inquire about prospective employees' wage history unless voluntarily disclosed (§ 3-304.1); retaliation against candidates who refuse is prohibited.
- **Earned sick and safe leave**: Employers with 15+ employees must provide paid leave; employers with <15 must provide unpaid leave; employees earn 1 hour per 30 hours worked, max 40 hours annually (§ 3-1301 et seq.).
- **FAMLI (Family and Medical Leave Insurance)**: State-funded program with contributions starting January 1, 2027, benefits payable no later than January 3, 2028; covers up to 12–24 weeks leave at 90% wage replacement (Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. Title 8.3).
- **Equal pay and protected classes**: Employers prohibited from sex/gender-based pay discrimination; protected classes include race, color, religion, national origin, age, disability, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, military status, genetic information (Md. State Gov't § 20-606).
- **Non-compete restrictions**: Non-competes unenforceable for employees earning ≤150% of state minimum wage ($22.50/hour; $46,800 annually); banned for healthcare workers earning ≤$350,000 and veterinary professionals (§ 3-716).
- **Mini-WARN act**: Employers with 50+ employees must provide 60 days' notice of reductions affecting 25% or 15 workers over 3 months (§ 11-301 et seq.); penalties up to $10,000/day.
- **Ban-the-box**: Employers with 15+ employees cannot inquire about criminal records before first in-person interview (Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-402); exemptions for state/federal legal requirements.
- **Local rules**: Montgomery County minimum wage $17.65/hour (51+ employees); $16.00 (11–50); $15.50 (≤10); earned leave 1 hour per 30 hours, max 56 annually for 4+ employees. Baltimore enforces state minimum wage; no local enhancement for leave.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Maryland is an employment-at-will state: employers and employees may terminate the relationship without cause (Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 31-101, common law).

**Exceptions to at-will termination**:

- Termination for filing a wage theft complaint, workers' compensation claim, or unemployment benefit claim.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religious freedom).
- Termination for jury duty, voting, military service, or serving as a witness.
- Termination in violation of public policy (e.g., refusing illegal act, whistleblowing).
- Implied employment contract: oral or written promises of job security, detailed employment practices, or course of dealing may create enforceable duties.

**Required notices at hire and on pay/role change**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-504(a): Employers must provide written notice of wages, paydays, and employment policies _in advance_ of any change to payday or wage rate. Notice must be at least 1 pay period in advance; exceptions do not require advance notice if wage is _increased_ (only decreases require notice).
- At time of hire, employers should document employee's rate of pay, pay frequency, and any deductions or withholdings authorized.
- No general written offer-of-employment statute in Maryland, but § 3-504 notice requirements apply to pay and role changes.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-413: $15.00/hour (2026 rate; adjusted annually). Tipped employees: $3.63/hour direct wage; employer must supplement tips to reach minimum wage.
- Minors under 18: 85% of state minimum wage ($12.75/hour).
- Montgomery County (local): $17.65/hour (51+ employees), $16.00 (11–50), $15.50 (≤10).
- Baltimore City: $15.00/hour (state rate adopted by City Code, Article 28, § 3-1), auto-matches if state minimum exceeds city rate.

**Overtime**:

- § 3-415: Time-and-a-half (1.5x regular rate) for hours over 40/week. Exemptions: concerts, theaters, music festivals, amusement parks (≤7 months/year operation), agricultural workers.
- No daily overtime (8 hours/day) or 7th-day overtime unless state or federal law applies.

**Pay frequency and timing**:

- § 3-502: Employers must pay at least every 2 weeks (biweekly) or twice monthly. If payday falls on a non-workday, pay on preceding workday.
- Wages must be in U.S. currency or check convertible at face value; no Social Security number on check or notice.

**Final pay on termination**:

- § 3-505: All wages earned for work performed must be paid on or before the day the employee would have been paid if employment had not terminated (i.e., next regular payday following termination).
- Accrued leave: Employer may withhold accrued leave payout if (1) written policy exists limiting leave compensation, (2) employee was notified of policy under § 3-504(a)(1), and (3) employee not entitled under policy terms. Otherwise, accrued leave must be paid.

**Permitted deductions**:

- § 3-503: Employers cannot deduct wages except as required by law (tax, garnishment, court order), with written employee authorization on approved form, or for court-ordered child support/alimony.
- No deductions for tools, uniforms, or other employer-imposed costs unless employee agrees in writing and deduction does not reduce wages below minimum wage.

**Pay statement content** (verify):

- No Maryland statute mandates itemized pay stubs; federal FLSA rules apply. Employers should maintain records of hours, wage rate, deductions, and net pay per § 3-424.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage range disclosure**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-304.2 (effective October 1, 2024): Employers must include in all public and internal job postings the wage range (minimum and maximum hourly rate or salary), a general description of benefits, and any other compensation for the position.
- Wage range may be based on a pay scale, past minimum/maximum for the role, comparable position, or budgeted amount.
- Applicants may request wage range at any time; refusal or retaliation (refusing to interview/hire) is unlawful.

**Salary history ban**:

- § 3-304.1 (2020): Employers cannot inquire about or direct third parties to inquire about prospective employees' wage/salary history unless the candidate voluntarily discloses.
- Retaliation (refusing to interview, hire, or employ someone who asks for wage range or refuses to provide history) is prohibited.

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state-level pay equity audit requirement; federal EEO-1 applies if applicable (federal: 100+ employees).

**Equal pay statute**:

- § 3-304: Employers prohibited from paying employees of one sex or gender identity at lower rate than other sex/gender for equal work in same establishment (same county), unless differential is based on seniority, merit, quality production, or system independent of sex/gender.
- Applies to comparable work in same operation, same business, or same type; employees at same worksite in same county are deemed same establishment.
- Protected classes under Md. State Gov't § 20-606: race, color, religion, sex, age, national origin, marital status, sexual orientation, gender identity, genetic information, military status, disability unrelated to job performance.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, FAMLI, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Earned Sick and Safe Leave (HWFA)**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-1301 et seq.: All employers must provide sick and safe leave; employers with 15+ employees must provide _paid_ leave, employers with <15 must provide _unpaid_ leave.
- Accrual: 1 hour per 30 hours worked, maximum 40 hours per calendar year. Employees may carry over unused balance into next year (up to 64 hours carried over; employers may cap total accrual at 64 hours).
- Permitted uses: Employee's own physical/mental illness, injury, medical condition; health condition of family member; domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, human trafficking (for employee or family member); workplace closure due to public health emergency.
- Employers may require health-care provider certification if employee uses >3 consecutive days for one reason.

**Montgomery County (local enhancement)**:

- Montgomery County Ordinance § 27-68: Employers with 4+ employees must allow 1 hour earned leave per 30 hours worked, max 56 hours/year, carryover to next year. Employers with 1–3 employees have no local requirement (state HWFA applies: unpaid leave only).

**Family and Medical Leave Insurance (FAMLI / Time to Care Act)**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. Title 8.3: State-funded paid family and medical leave program. Contributions from employers and employees effective January 1, 2027, at 0.90% of covered wages up to Social Security wage base (employee pays up to 0.45%, employer pays remainder).
- Benefits payable no earlier than January 3, 2028. Employees with 680+ hours in covered employment during base period eligible for up to 12 weeks leave in rolling 12-month period; additional 12 weeks if serious health condition + new child bond. Replaces up to 90% of average weekly wages (initial range: $50 min, $1,000 max, adjusted annually).
- **Verify**: Final phase-in schedule and benefit caps for 2027–2028.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- If employee takes HWFA leave or FAMLI and returns to a different pay or role, employer must not reduce wages below contractually due or legally required amounts.
- Paid leave accrual may continue during unpaid FMLA-type leave (verify case law on integration of FAMLI with state FMLA).
- On promotion during leave, employee generally returns to promoted role; confirm whether wage must match original or promoted rate.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-424: Employers must maintain payroll records for 3 years documenting employee name, address, occupation, hours worked (daily/weekly), wage rate, gross and net pay, deductions, and leave accrual.
- Personnel records (performance reviews, disciplinary notes, hire/termination documentation) may be retained indefinitely unless employee disputes information; disputed items must be accompanied by employee's written statement.
- Medical records: Store separately from general personnel files; retain at least 3 years post-termination (ADA/HIPAA compliance).

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- Maryland recognizes employee right to inspect personnel and medical records upon written request (common law; not statutory but established in case law).
- Access within reasonable time (typically 10–15 business days) at or near worksite during regular business hours.
- Employees may obtain copies; employers may charge reasonable copying fee.
- **Limit on frequency**: Clarify whether employees may inspect files as often as desired or only periodically (verify specific case law).

**Disputed information**:

- If employee disputes information in file, employer should allow employee to submit written statement of disagreement, which must be retained in file and accompany any disclosure to third parties.

**Record format**:

- No Maryland statute mandates electronic vs. paper format; electronic records acceptable if retention and access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Maryland Personal Information Protection Act**:

- Md. Code, Com. Law § 14-3501 et seq.: Requires businesses to implement reasonable security procedures to protect personal information and notify individuals of data breaches "without unreasonable delay" if personal information (name + SSN, credit card, etc.) is accessed/acquired by unauthorized person.
- **Applicability to employee data**: Law targets consumer/customer data; applicability to employee records is evolving. **Verify** whether employee data triggers breach notification.

**Employee privacy—monitoring and social media**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-712 (User Name and Password Privacy Protection Act, 2012): Employers _cannot_ request or require employees/applicants to disclose personal social media usernames, passwords, or other means to access personal accounts. Disciplining employee or refusing to hire applicant for non-disclosure is prohibited.
- Exception: Employers _may_ require disclosure of usernames/passwords for _non-personal_ (employer-provided) accounts that access employer's internal systems.
- Employers _may_ view publicly available social media content but should avoid discrimination issues (verify case law on monitoring external posts).
- No state statute bans email/internet monitoring of work accounts or general workplace video monitoring; reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies.

**Data residency and processing**:

- No Maryland law mandates data residency for employee records. Employers may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided data security and privacy laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-402 et seq. (Fair Criminal Record Screening Act, effective Feb 29, 2020): Employers with 15+ full-time employees cannot require disclosure of criminal record or accusations on job application or before first in-person interview.
- Exemptions: Legal requirement or authorization by state/federal law (e.g., certain professional licenses, bonding requirements).
- Penalties: Commissioner may order compliance; civil penalty up to $300 per applicant/employee per violation if violation is repeated.
- "Full-time" definition includes contractual, temporary, seasonal, contingent, vocational/educational training (paid or unpaid) through temp agencies.

**Background checks**:

- No state statute restricting private-sector background checks; federal FCRA applies. Employers using third-party consumer reporting agencies must provide FCRA notice, dispute rights, and adverse-action procedures.
- Some municipalities may have local ban-the-box ordinances; employers in those jurisdictions must comply locally (verify local rules).

**Drug testing**:

- No state-level statute regulating private-sector drug testing; federal law (pre-employment, reasonable suspicion, post-accident) and federal DOT rules apply.
- Employers should verify local ordinances in jurisdiction where employees work.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No state mandate for private-sector E-Verify; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice (Economic Stabilization Act, "mini-WARN")**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 11-301 et seq. (effective, verify date): Employers with 50+ employees who have operated for 1+ year must provide 60 days' written notice before workforce reduction affecting 25% or 15+ workers over 3 months.
- Notice to: all affected employees (including part-time <20 hrs/week and <6 months tenure), union representatives, state Division of Employment and Training's Dislocated Worker Unit, elected officials in jurisdiction.
- Penalties: Secretary of Labor may assess civil penalty up to $10,000/day for non-compliance.

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance except in narrow circumstances (verify case law). General principle: severance is contractual, not mandatory, unless employment contract provides.
- Federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for employers with 100+ employees, mass layoff/plant closing affecting 50+ workers) also applies.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- Md. Code, Lab. & Empl. § 3-716: Non-competes unenforceable for employees earning ≤150% of state minimum wage.
  - Current threshold: $22.50/hour ($46,800 annually).
  - Applies to non-competition clauses only; non-solicitation and confidentiality clauses _not_ covered by wage threshold.
  - Healthcare workers providing direct patient care earning ≤$350,000 annually: non-competes banned.
  - Veterinary professionals: non-competes banned (verify specific scope).
- For higher-wage employees: non-competes enforceable if reasonable in duration, geography, and scope; legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential info, customer relationships); no "less restrictive alternatives" doctrine in statute (contrast to Connecticut).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness standard (duration, scope, geography).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Maryland applies common-law test and federal IRS test. No statutory ABC test in labor law (contrast to Connecticut).
- Factors: control over work, method of payment, provision of tools/materials, nature of relationship (permanent vs. temporary), whether services are integral to employer's business.
- **Misclassification enforcement**: Maryland Department of Labor, Department of Revenue Services, Attorney General, Workers' Compensation Commission enforce; penalties include back wages, penalties, and reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No Maryland statute specifically addressing remote-work classification. Standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee works in Maryland (even remotely) for any employer, Maryland law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine. If employee works across state lines, each state's laws apply to work performed in that state. Platform should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable state rules.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling: Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

**Mandatory obligations and field restrictions**:

1. **Pay transparency notice** (§ 3-304.2): Before creating or posting a position, require input of wage range (min/max) and benefits description; block job posting if these fields empty. On offer extension, timestamp wage range disclosure.

2. **Pay rate change notice** (§ 3-504): If base pay _decreases_, generate automated notice 1+ pay periods in advance with new rate and effective date; permit pay _increases_ without advance notice (system default).

3. **Salary history inquiry block** (§ 3-304.1): Disable or flag any form field attempting to request prior salary/wages from external candidates; log any such queries as compliance risk.

4. **Equal pay audit flag** (§ 3-304): On promotion + base-pay change, system should flag if new rate differs significantly from peer rates for "equal work" (comparable role in same establishment/county); require manager justification or approval from HR/legal.

5. **Non-compete enforceability check** (§ 3-716): On hiring or role change, if employee earns ≤$22.50/hour (or $46,800 annually), flag any non-compete clause as unenforceable; mark as void. Healthcare/veterinary workers: void all non-competes regardless of wage.

6. **Earned leave accrual** (§ 3-1301, § 3-424): Track accrued HWFA leave (1 hour per 30 hours worked, max 40/year for large employers, unpaid for small employers). On termination, if no written leave-payout policy exists, automatic payout of accrued balance. On promotion within same employer, leave balance carries forward.

7. **FAMLI contributions and eligibility** (Title 8.3): Effective Jan 1, 2027, deduct FAMLI contribution (up to 0.45%) from payroll; benefits gate: employee must have 680+ hours in base period. Track eligibility and flag when benefits become available (no earlier than Jan 3, 2028).

8. **Montgomery County enhancements** (local ordinance): If employee works in Montgomery County with 4+ employer headcount, ensure earned leave accrual at 1 hour per 30 hours, max 56/year (vs. 40 state minimum). Local minimum wage: $17.65 (51+), $16.00 (11–50), $15.50 (≤10).

9. **Ban-the-box compliance** (§ 3-402): On initial application, do _not_ ask about criminal record; if employer has 15+ employees, require 1st interview before any criminal history inquiry. Log interview date and track timing.

10. **Mini-WARN trigger** (§ 11-301): If planned layoff affects 25% or 15+ employees over 3 months and employer has 50+ employees, auto-flag and require 60-day notice scheduling; notify state Dislocated Worker Unit.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether HWFA leave accrual continues during unpaid FAMLI leave or other state family leave.
- Confirm whether employee returning from FAMLI to same or promoted role must receive same salary as at departure or promoted salary.
- Clarify exact calculation of "same establishment" for equal-pay comparisons (same county, same employer—geographic limits?).
- Verify whether final-pay accrued-leave exception applies to HWFA-accrued leave or only to discretionary vacation.
- Confirm status of implied employment contracts in recent Maryland case law and factual triggers.
