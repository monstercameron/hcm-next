# Texas Employment Law Research

**State:** Texas | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with statutory exceptions**: Texas allows termination without cause absent an enforceable agreement; public-policy exceptions prohibit discharge for workers' compensation claims (§ 451.001), OSHA complaints, jury duty (§ 52.051), voting (EC § 276.004), and discrimination-related opposition (Ch. 21).
- **Minimum wage**: $7.25/hour (federal rate); § 62.151 confirms state law defers to FLSA for covered employees; no local override except preempted ordinances.
- **No state overtime**: Federal FLSA applies (40-hour weekly threshold, 1.5x premium); no daily or weekly override in state law.
- **Pay frequency**: Semi-monthly for non-exempt (§ 61.011); monthly for exempt employees; paydays must be designated (§ 61.012); bonuses/commissions may be paid annually if prescribed in advance.
- **Final pay on separation**: Discharge requires payment within 6 calendar days (§ 61.014); voluntary resignation requires payment on next regular payday; no accrued PTO payout required unless promised in writing.
- **Discrimination threshold**: 15+ regularly employed employees for race/color/religion/national origin/age/sex/disability (Ch. 21); sexual harassment expanded to 1+ employees effective September 1, 2021 (§ 21.141); 300-day charge deadline for sexual harassment, 180 days for other claims.
- **Non-competes**: Must be ancillary to enforceable agreement (§ 15.50); reasonable in time/territory; physicians restricted to 1-year, 5-mile radius, capped buyout by 2025 SB 1318 (effective 2025-09-01); applies to dentists, APRNs, RNs, LVNs, and PAs.
- **Data breach notification**: 60 days to individuals; 30 days to Texas Attorney General if breach affects 250+ residents (§ 521.053, amended by SB 768).
- **No paid sick leave mandate**: Local Austin/Dallas/San Antonio ordinances preempted by HB 2127 ("Death Star") and enjoined by courts since 2018–2021.
- **Jury duty and voting**: Unpaid jury duty leave (§ 52.051); up to 2 hours paid voting leave if polls open < 3 hours outside work hours (EC § 276.004).
- **Workers' compensation retaliation**: § 451.001 prohibits discharge/discrimination for filing good-faith claim or hiring attorney; 2-year limitation period.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Texas recognizes at-will employment: employers and employees may terminate without cause or notice unless an enforceable written agreement or public-policy exception applies.

**Public-policy exceptions to at-will termination**:

- Workers' compensation claim filing (§ 451.001): Discharge, demotion, or retaliation prohibited for good-faith claim, attorney hiring, or proceeding institution.
- OSHA and wage-and-hour complaints: Protected activity; retaliation prohibited.
- Jury duty (§ 52.051): Discharge or penalty prohibited.
- Voting (Election Code § 276.004): Early voting and election-day voting protected; refusal to permit absence is a Class C misdemeanor.
- Discrimination opposition (Ch. 21): Discharge, demotion, or discipline for opposing discriminatory practice or filing/participating in TCHRA proceeding prohibited.
- Whistleblowing (Government Code § 554): Public employees reporting law violations to appropriate authority protected from retaliation (90-day filing window).
- Firearms in locked vehicles (Penal Code § 30.06): Employer cannot prevent employees from storing firearms in locked private vehicles in employer-provided parking.

**No state-mandated written notice at hire or change of pay**: Federal wage-and-hour law applies; recommended practice is written offer letter specifying rate and pay frequency per § 61.012.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (§ 62.001, 62.151): $7.25/hour (federal FLSA rate); § 62.151 explicitly states Texas minimum wage law does not apply to persons covered by FLSA. No state-specific minimum wage or local override except preempted ordinances in Austin/Dallas/San Antonio.

**Overtime**: No state law establishing overtime liability. Federal FLSA applies: time-and-a-half for hours exceeding 40 per workweek. No daily overtime, no weekly threshold beyond 40 hours.

**Pay frequency** (§ 61.011, 61.012):

- Exempt employees: at least once per month.
- Non-exempt employees: at least twice per month, with pay periods as equal in days as possible.
- Paydays: Employer must designate (daily, weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly); if not designated, default is 1st and 15th of each month.
- Posting: Conspicuous notice of paydays required.
- Bonuses/commissions: May be paid as infrequently as annually if prescribed in advance.

**Final pay timing** (§ 61.014):

- **Involuntary termination** (discharge): Payment due within 6 calendar days after discharge date; includes weekends and holidays.
- **Voluntary resignation**: Payment due on next regularly scheduled payday.
- Accrued PTO: No state mandate to pay out unused vacation or PTO unless promised in writing by employer (handbook, offer letter, employment contract).

**Permitted deductions** (§ 61.018): Deductions allowed only if (1) court-ordered, (2) required by state/federal law, or (3) authorized in writing by employee for lawful purpose. Deduction cannot reduce wages below applicable minimum wage.

**Payment method** (§ 61.017):

- United States currency, check/negotiable instrument, or electronic transfer permitted.
- Direct deposit: Employer may elect if employee maintains account; 60 days' notice required before implementation.
- Payroll card: Permitted if linked to federally insured institution.
- Alternate payment: If employee requests alternate form, employer must accommodate within 30 days or sooner as practicable.

**Pay statement content**: No state-mandated itemized format; federal FLSA disclosure requirements apply.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary history ban**: No state law prohibits inquiring about or relying on prior wages (verify whether any pending legislation as of 2026).

**Pay transparency mandate**: No statewide requirement to disclose salary ranges in job postings (federal Equal Pay Act applies for equal work; no state supplement).

**Discrimination and equal pay** (Ch. 21, Labor Code):

- **TCHRA coverage**: Applies to employers with 15+ regularly employed employees for discrimination based on race, color, religion, national origin, age (40+), sex, disability.
- **Sexual harassment exception** (§ 21.141, effective 2021): Applies to employers with 1+ employees; covers all industries.
- **Charge deadline**: 180 days for non-sexual-harassment discrimination; 300 days for sexual harassment (§ 21.201).
- **Enforcement**: Texas Workforce Commission; no private right of action (verify whether current statutory updates).
- **Equal pay**: Federal Equal Pay Act (FLSA § 206(d)) applies; no separate Texas statute.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Paid Family/Medical Leave, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave**: No state mandate for private employers. Local ordinances in Austin (2018), Dallas, and San Antonio (2019) enacted requirements but have been **enjoined and are not in effect** as of 2026. HB 2127 ("Death Star") preempts local ordinances on wages and benefits (verify ongoing litigation status and any new legislation).

**Paid family and medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate; federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid, 50+ employees) is statutory floor.

**Jury duty leave** (§ 52.051):

- Employer cannot discharge, penalize, or threaten employee for attendance.
- Unpaid leave; employer has no obligation to pay during jury service.
- Protected activity; retaliation prohibited.

**Voting leave** (Election Code § 276.004):

- Up to 2 hours paid leave on election day if employee requests and polls are open < 3 hours outside work hours.
- Employer may specify timing; employee request for beginning or end of shift must be granted.
- No paid leave required if employee has ≥ 3 hours non-work time when polls are open.
- Early voting: Employer must not refuse absence for early voting purposes (Class C misdemeanor).

**Interaction with promotion**: On promotion, verify that wage increase applies to any in-progress leave (e.g., if employee takes unpaid leave post-promotion, post-leave wage reflects new promoted rate per employer policy).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**: No specific state mandate for general payroll record retention period established in Texas Labor Code § 61. Federal FLSA requires 3 years minimum for wage-claim defense; Texas courts expect compliance with federal standard. Texas Workforce Commission guidance (if available) may address retention; verify current TWC recommendations. Recommended practice: 3 years minimum for FLSA compliance, 7 years for tax/compliance audit preparedness.

**Record content requirements** (§ 61.001 et seq.): Employers must keep records of:

- Hours worked per pay period.
- Wages earned and rates of pay.
- Overtime compensation (if applicable).
- Deductions made.
- Pay period and payday designation.
- Any written authorization for deductions (§ 61.018).
- Any written compensation agreements or promotion documents.

**Employee access to personnel files**: No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic right to inspect, copy, or challenge personnel records (applies only to public/state employees). Employers may establish voluntary access policies and should document the policy.

**Record format**: No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; either acceptable if retention, access, and compliance requirements met. Electronic storage permitted; cloud storage acceptable if breach notification procedures in place (§ 521.053).

**Wage claim documentation** (§ 61.051, 61.053): Wage claims must be filed with TWC within 180 days of date wages became due (statutory deadline is jurisdictional; if deadline is amended to 365 days, verify current status). Bad-faith non-payment: TWC may assess administrative penalty up to wages owed, capped at $1,000 per violation. First-time violators may receive notice and reasonable cure period before penalty assessed.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Texas Data Privacy and Security Act (TDPSA)** (2024, effective July 1, 2024):

- Applies to consumer data only (personal/household capacity); explicitly exempts employee data processed in employment context.
- Does not govern employee records, benefits data, or contractor information.
- No employer obligation under TDPSA for workforce data; state attorney general enforces.

**Data breach notification** (§ 521.053, amended by SB 768):

- **Individual notification**: 60 days after breach determination; must notify without unreasonable delay and no later than 60th day.
- **Texas Attorney General**: Notify as soon as practicable and no later than 30 days if breach affects 250+ Texas residents (electronic form required on AG website).
- **Trigger**: Data breach of "personal information" = first name/initial + last name + one sensitive element (SSN, account, driver license, passport, biometric, financial/medical data, unique ID) if not encrypted.
- **Safe harbor**: No notification if data encrypted and encryption key not compromised.

**Workplace monitoring and privacy**: No state statute restricting email/internet monitoring or video surveillance; common law reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies (narrow). Employers have broad discretion to monitor workplace.

**Data residency**: No state law mandating data residency for employee records; out-of-state storage permitted if breach notification and cybersecurity obligations met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box for private employers**: No state statute for private sector. Executive Order RP-80 (state agencies only) prohibits inquiring about criminal history on initial application; applies to state hiring and some contractors.

**Background checks**: Federal FCRA applies; no additional state-specific restrictions or requirements for private employers beyond FCRA notice, dispute, adverse-action procedures.

**Drug testing**: No state law prohibiting or mandating pre-employment drug testing for private employers. Employers have broad discretion: pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, random all permitted. Workers' compensation § 411.091 allows drug-free workplace policy but does not create mandate.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- **Private employers**: No state mandate; optional.
- **State agencies**: Required by Executive Order RP-80; applies to state hiring and vendors.
- **Contractors**: If state contractor, may be required per contract terms.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mini-WARN act**: No state law; federal WARN Act (100+ employees, 60 days' notice for plant closing or mass layoff of 50+ employees or 25%+ workforce) is sole requirement.

**Severance**: No state mandate; contractual and voluntary only.

**Non-compete enforceability** (§ 15.50):

- **Core requirement**: Covenant must be **ancillary to or part of otherwise enforceable agreement** (employment, sale of business, etc.); cannot stand alone.
- **Reasonableness test**: Time, geographical area, and scope of activity restrained must be reasonable and not exceed what is necessary to protect goodwill or legitimate business interest.
- **Legitimate interests**: Trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information, goodwill.
- **Blue-pencil doctrine**: Texas courts generally do not modify overly broad provisions; unenforceability stands as drafted (restrictive approach).
- **No specific time/territory standard**: Reasonableness determined case-by-case; courts enforce narrowly.

**Physician and healthcare practitioner non-competes** (§ 15.50, amended by SB 1318, effective Sept. 1, 2025):

- Applies to Texas-licensed physicians, dentists, nurses (APRNs, RNs, LVNs), and physician assistants.
- **Duration cap**: Covenant expires no later than 1-year anniversary of termination.
- **Geography cap**: No more than 5-mile radius from location where practitioner primarily practiced before termination.
- **Buyout cap**: Buyout amount cannot exceed practitioner's total annual salary and wages at termination.
- **Involuntary discharge without good cause**: Covenant is "void and unenforceable" (cannot be enforced even if within 1-year/5-mile/buyout requirements).
- **Effective date**: Applies to agreements entered or renewed on or after Sept. 1, 2025.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**: No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness test (time, scope, geography, legitimate business interest).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**: Texas courts apply common law "right-to-control" test; no single statutory factor is determinative. Eight-factor test: (1) control/direction by hiring entity; (2) integral to business; (3) permanency of relationship; (4) contractor's investment in facilities; (5) opportunities for profit/loss; (6) initiative and judgment in market competition; (7) degree of independent operation; (8) totality of circumstances.

**Misclassification remedies**: Back wages, reclassification, penalties enforced by the Texas Workforce Commission (Labor Code ch. 214 misclassification penalties for staffing/government contractors), the Comptroller, and the Attorney General.

**Remote workers**: No state-specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location. If work is performed in Texas (remote or on-site), Texas law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**: No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state. Promotion or pay change must comply with all applicable state laws where employee is based or works.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints for promotion and base-pay changes**:

1. **Minimum wage compliance**: On promotion or base-pay change, verify wage meets $7.25/hour state minimum. Flag if promotion results in wage below minimum or if deductions would create underpayment.

2. **Written authorization for deductions** (§ 61.018): If promotion involves new deduction (e.g., health insurance, savings plan), obtain written employee authorization for lawful purpose; cannot reduce wages below minimum.

3. **Pay frequency verification** (§ 61.011, 61.012): Confirm payday schedule remains designated (daily through monthly). If promotion changes classification (exempt to non-exempt, vice versa), update pay frequency per statute and notify employee.

4. **Final pay on termination during promotion period** (§ 61.014): If termination occurs within 6 days of promotion effective date, ensure final check includes promoted wage rate for days worked at that rate; discharge deadline is 6 days, resignation deadline is next regular payday.

5. **TCHRA discrimination compliance** (Ch. 21): If promotion involves wage increase, verify no discriminatory basis (pay equity for same work; if employee in protected class, document business reason for promotion and wage change). No discrimination complaint filed within 180 days (300 for sexual harassment)?

6. **Sexual harassment prevention** (§ 21.141, effective 2021): Applies to employers with 1+ employees; if promotion involves supervisory role, ensure trained in harassment prevention (verify state training requirements).

7. **Workers' compensation retaliation check** (§ 451.001): If employee recently filed claim, record promotion timing and business rationale to rebut inference of retaliation. Timing within days of claim may invite scrutiny.

8. **Non-compete enforceability** (§ 15.50): If employee is subject to non-compete on hire or during employment, verify at promotion: (a) is it ancillary to employment agreement (yes, employment relationship is underlying agreement); (b) does promotion change job duties, location, or scope in a way that affects enforceability? If healthcare practitioner (effective 2025-09-01), apply 1-year/5-mile/buyout caps or non-compete is unenforceable post-Sept-1-2025.

9. **Data protection if promotion involves access change** (TDPSA § 2026, § 521.053): If promotion grants access to personal information (e.g., HR data, SSN, customer lists), ensure data security, breach notification procedures documented, and 60-day/30-day notification timelines in place.

10. **Electronic payment method** (§ 61.017): If promotion changes pay frequency (e.g., monthly to semi-monthly), update direct deposit or check timing; 60 days' notice required before implementing direct deposit if not already in place.

11. **Records retention and documentation**: Retain promotion document (effective date, new rate, justification, wage history) per employer policy (recommend 3 years for FLSA alignment). Support for wage-claim defense if disputed.

12. **Jury duty and voting leave interaction**: If employee on approved jury duty or voting leave, promotion wage increase applies to post-leave return; do not reduce post-leave wage below pre-promotion level or applicable minimum.

**Open questions and verification items**:

- Verify whether Texas Labor Code § 61.053 was amended to extend wage-claim deadline from 180 to 365 days (proposed legislation mentioned in research; check current status).
- Confirm whether state training mandate exists for sexual harassment prevention for supervisors/managers (verify TCHRA requirement or TWC guidance).
- Verify SB 1318 healthcare practitioner non-compete enforcement timeline and whether retroactive to agreements signed before Sept. 1, 2025.
- Clarify whether "prescribed in advance" for annual bonus/commission (§ 61.011) requires written notice at hire or calendar-year advance specification.
- Verify whether implied employment contracts (handbooks, verbal promises) survive promotions or are limited to hire date.
