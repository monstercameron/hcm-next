# Iowa Employment Law Research (2026-09-03)

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment dominates**: Iowa recognizes at-will employment by default; no employment contract required at hire unless parties agree to one.
- **Minimum wage defers to federal**: Iowa's statutory minimum ($7.25/hour, Iowa Code § 91D.1) equals federal FLSA, so federal floor applies uniformly; no state premium.
- **Final pay timing mandated**: Iowa Code § 91A.4 requires final wages paid by the next regular payday or within 5 days, whichever occurs first; fail to comply invokes civil penalty.
- **Overtime governed by federal FLSA**: No state-specific overtime rules; federal 1.5× over 40 hours applies.
- **Pay-rate change notice required**: Iowa Code § 91A.3 requires written notice of any change in pay rate at least 3 days before the change becomes effective.
- **Personnel-file access right**: Iowa Code § 91B.1 grants employees right to access their personnel files within 3 business days; employers may charge reasonable copy fees.
- **Civil rights protection expanded 2025**: Iowa Code Chapter 216 now covers gender identity and pregnancy discrimination (verify 2025 amendments); enforced by Iowa Civil Rights Act Division.
- **Multi-state WARN rule**: Federal WARN Act (60 days for 50+ employee separations) applies; Iowa Code § 84C adds state-specific 30-day notice for layoffs affecting 25+ employees.
- **No state paid-leave mandate**: No mandatory paid sick leave or paid family leave for private employers (federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees within 75 miles).
- **Noncompetes enforceable under conditions**: Iowa Code Chapter 553 permits noncompetes and nonsolicitation if "reasonable as to time, area, and line of business" (case-law dependent; verify current enforceability).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Iowa recognizes at-will employment by default. Either party may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any reason or no reason, **except** termination cannot be based on illegal grounds (discrimination, retaliation, public-policy violations, filing a workers' comp claim).

**Written Notices at Hire**: No state law requires a written at-will statement at hire. However, employers should document any employment agreement, offer letter, or wage-agreement terms to avoid disputes.

**Pay-Rate Change Notice**: Iowa Code § 91A.3 mandates **written notice of any change in pay rate** (increase, decrease, or structure change such as shift from hourly to piecework) **at least 3 days before** the change becomes effective. Employers must provide written, signed notice; electronic notice is acceptable if employee can retain a copy. Failure to comply is a violation of wage-payment law; employees may file a civil action for damages.

**No General Change-of-Terms Notice Requirement**: Beyond pay-rate changes, no statute requires advance notice of other role or reporting-line changes (manager assignments, promotions). However, clear documentation is prudent.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: Iowa Code § 91D.1 sets minimum wage at $7.25/hour, which matches federal FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 206). Employers must pay whichever is higher; in practice, the federal floor is the binding floor.

**Overtime**: Federal FLSA rules apply without state deviation. Overtime is 1.5× regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek; FLSA exempt classifications (executive, administrative, professional, outside sales) follow federal definitions.

**Pay Frequency**: No state-specific mandate on pay frequency (weekly, biweekly, monthly). Employers may set their own schedule and must follow it consistently. Employers must provide pay stubs (or electronic notice) itemizing gross wages, deductions, and net pay (verify exact statutory requirement).

**Final Paycheck on Termination or Resignation**: Iowa Code § 91A.4 requires all earned and unpaid wages to be paid to the employee by the **next regular payday or within 5 days, whichever occurs first**, following separation. This applies to both voluntary and involuntary termination. Failure to pay timely invokes a civil penalty of up to $100 per day (verify current penalty cap). Employers must provide a written itemized pay statement showing all deductions.

**Deductions**: Iowa Code § 91A.5 restricts permissible deductions. Only deductions mandated by law (taxes, child support, garnish), ordered by court, or **authorized in writing by the employee in advance** are permitted. Uniform/tool deductions, disciplinary fines, and "shortages" are generally prohibited unless the employee authorizes them. Any deduction that reduces wages below minimum wage is illegal.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Iowa state law restricts inquiries about salary history. Employers may ask prior compensation; no explicit ban exists (verify recent 2025 legislative changes).

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Iowa state law requires salary ranges in job postings or on request. Federal rules do not mandate this either; pay transparency remains optional.

**Equal Pay Law**: Iowa Code § 91D.2 (Wage Discrimination Based on Sex) prohibits employers from paying employees of different sexes at different rates for "substantially similar work." The comparison is functional: roles require substantially similar skill, effort, and responsibility in similar working conditions. Exceptions exist for seniority systems, merit systems, or systems based on production quantity/quality. Enforcement is through civil action or complaint to the Iowa Civil Rights Act Division (verify recent 2025 amendments expanding protected classes to include gender identity).

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Iowa state law mandates pay-equity data reporting. Federal EEO-1 reporting applies to employers with 100+ employees.

**Protected Classes under Chapter 216**: 2025 amendments (verify) expand Iowa's civil rights law beyond sex, race, color, creed, national origin, disability, and age to include **gender identity** and **pregnancy discrimination**. Retaliation for reporting discrimination is prohibited. Enforced by the Iowa Civil Rights Act Division.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

No Iowa state law mandates paid sick leave or paid family leave for private-sector employees.

**Federal FMLA** (12 weeks unpaid per 12 months for serious health condition, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver, childbirth/adoption) applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles).

**State Employees**: Iowa state government employees receive specific leave policies (vacation, sick, family), but these are not mandates for private employers.

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional; no state mandate. Employers may set policies.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: If an employee is promoted or receives a base-pay change, existing leave balances (if any) are typically preserved. Iowa law does not specify carryover or payout rules; these are governed by employer policy and employment contract. Verify employer policy on promotion/pay-change impact on leave.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: Iowa Code § 91A.6 requires employers to retain payroll records (gross wages, deductions, net pay, pay dates, hours worked) for **at least 4 years**. This aligns with federal FLSA (3 years) but extends to 4 under Iowa law. Federal law applies to covered employers; Iowa rule is stricter.

**Personnel-File Access**: Iowa Code § 91B.1 grants employees (or former employees) the **right to access their personnel files within 3 business days** of a written request. Employers may charge a reasonable fee for copying. Employers may withhold information that would violate third-party privacy (e.g., reference letters if confidentiality was promised, internal performance notes if disclosure would impair business relations). However, the scope of withholding is narrow; employee's own performance history, discipline, and evaluation are generally accessible.

**Record Format**: No state requirement for paper vs. electronic. Employers may maintain records digitally, provided they remain accessible and authentic (federal FLSA and Iowa law allow electronic records).

**Separation and Termination Records**: Employers should retain separation notices, final-pay records, and related documentation as part of the 4-year payroll-retention cycle.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Iowa has no comprehensive state data privacy statute equivalent to CCPA. However, Iowa Code § 715C.1 imposes **data-breach notification** requirements.

**Breach Notification**: When an employer (or vendor handling personal information on the employer's behalf) experiences unauthorized access or disclosure of unencrypted digital personal information (e.g., social security number, driver's license number, financial account number, credit card number), the employer must notify affected individuals **without unreasonable delay** and in the **most expedient time possible**. Notification must be in writing or by email if the employer has email addresses on file. Employers should also notify the Iowa Attorney General if the breach involves 100+ Iowa residents. No specific day count is mandated, but "expedient" implies under 5 business days; verify current guidance.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Iowa-specific statute regulates employee biometric collection (fingerprints, facial recognition) or electronic monitoring (keystroke logging, location tracking, video surveillance). Federal Electronic Communications Privacy Act (Title I, wiretap law) and Iowa common law on privacy tort apply.

**Employee Record Confidentiality and Data Residency**: No state-specific employee-data residency or cross-border processing restrictions; federal law and ordinary data-protection practices apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Iowa has **no ban-the-box law** as of 2026. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history on applications or during interviews at any stage. **verify**: Check for recent legislative changes (2025–2026).

**Background Checks**: Permitted. Employers commonly use criminal-history checks via the Iowa Division of Criminal Investigation with fingerprint or name-based consent. No state law restricts timing or scope of criminal-record inquiries for most private employers.

**Drug Testing**: No Iowa law prohibits pre-employment, post-accident, or reasonable-suspicion drug testing. Employers may test; however, the ADA and state disability discrimination law may limit testing if accommodation-related (e.g., testing an applicant with a known disability history). Reasonable-suspicion testing for current employees should follow documented policy.

**E-Verify**: **Not mandated by Iowa state law for private employers.** However, federal law requires all federal contractors and subcontractors (on federal contracts > $3,000) to use E-Verify. Non-federal employers may voluntarily use E-Verify; there is no state penalty for non-compliance.

**Negligent Hiring**: Iowa recognizes negligent-hiring liability; use of background checks and reasonable vetting mitigates risk.

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**WARN Act**: The federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires covered employers (100+ employees) to provide **60 days' written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers in a 30-day period. Applies nationwide, including Iowa. Failure to notify carries penalties.

**Iowa State WARN (§ 84C)**: Iowa Code § 84C.2 adds a **state-level notice requirement** for employers with **25 or more employees** affected by a "layoff" (involuntary separation not for cause) lasting **7 or more consecutive days**. The employer must provide **written notice at least 30 days in advance** to affected employees, the Iowa Workforce Development agency, and local government. This is stricter than the federal rule (lower threshold, longer notice). Failure to provide notice may result in civil liability (verify penalty amount).

**Severance Pay**: No state law mandates severance. Severance is a matter of contract or employer discretion.

**Notice Period to Employee on Termination**: No state-specific notice period required; at-will employment permits immediate termination. However, employers should follow any notice period specified in an employment contract or established policy.

**Noncompete and Nonsolicitation Agreements**: Iowa Code Chapter 553 permits noncompete and nonsolicitation covenants. Enforceability is determined by Iowa case law: agreements must be (1) reasonable in scope (legitimate business interests), (2) reasonable in geographic area, and (3) reasonable in duration. Iowa courts apply a **"reasonableness" test**; overly broad agreements may be unenforceable or severable. **verify**: Consult current Iowa case law (e.g., state Supreme Court rulings) on enforceability; enforcement varies by fact pattern and may have been modified by recent court decisions.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Iowa's Employment Security Law (Iowa Code § 96.19) defines "employee" for unemployment-insurance purposes. The **right to control** test applies: if the employer controls the manner and means of work, the individual is an employee; if the individual controls their work and is engaged in an independent trade, the individual is a contractor. Misclassification of employees as contractors triggers wage-reporting and tax liabilities and may expose employers to audit and penalties.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Iowa-specific rules govern remote workers or employees working partly in Iowa. Federal FLSA applies to wages; Iowa income-tax withholding is required if the employee is working in Iowa (verify with Iowa Department of Revenue). Multi-state employers must comply with all applicable state laws in states where work is performed.

**Reciprocity**: Iowa recognizes only Iowa law for employment disputes involving Iowa-based work. Out-of-state employment contracts may invoke choice-of-law provisions, but if work is performed in Iowa, Iowa law applies to statutory protections.

## 11. Implications for HCM Next (P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

When HCM Next processes a **promotion** and **base-pay change** for a worker in Iowa:

### Obligations and Constraints:

1. **Pay-Rate Change Notice (§ 91A.3)**: Mandatory. Before the base-pay change becomes effective, the system must:
   - Generate and deliver a **written notice** (email acceptable if archived) stating the new pay rate.
   - Timestamp notice delivery at least **3 days before** the change takes effect.
   - Flag in the UI if the 3-day window is violated; block execution if less than 3 days remain.
   - Document the notice in the employee's record.

2. **Promotion and Role Changes**: No state notice requirement for manager or title changes; these are not "pay-rate" events under § 91A.3. However:
   - Record the change in the employee's profile (effective date, new manager, new title).
   - If promotion includes a pay adjustment, apply the notice requirement above.

3. **Final Pay and Leave on Potential Termination**: If the system models a future termination scenario:
   - Model § 91A.4 final-pay calculation: all earned wages due by next regular payday or within 5 days, whichever first.
   - Check for accrued but unused leave (sick, PTO); Iowa law does not mandate payout on termination, but check employer policy.
   - Document final pay calculation and timing.

4. **Record Retention**: Maintain all pay-rate-change notices, promotion records, and wage data for **4 years** per § 91A.6.

5. **Personnel-File Access (§ 91B.1)**: Model a 3-business-day response window for employee requests to access their personnel file (which includes pay history, promotions, performance notes). The system should flag employee access requests and ensure compliance.

6. **Equal Pay Compliance (§ 91D.2)**: When entering a promotion or pay change:
   - Capture comparable role(s) and wage(s) to support equal-pay defense.
   - Flag if the employee's new wage significantly differs from peers in substantially similar roles; escalate for legal review.
   - Document business rationale for wage differentials (seniority, merit, performance).

7. **Leave Impact**: On promotion or pay change:
   - Preserve existing leave balances (sick, PTO, FMLA eligibility).
   - No state rule mandates forfeiture; follow employer policy.
   - If promoted to exempt status, model any leave-policy changes (e.g., shift from hourly PTO to salaried).

8. **Noncompete and Offer Changes**: If promotion involves expansion of noncompete or nonsolicitation:
   - Flag for legal review (Iowa reasonableness test; overbroad agreements may be unenforceable).
   - If new or amended, require employee acknowledgment.

9. **Iowa WARN (§ 84C) - Layoff Scenario**: If the system models a future layoff:
   - For 25+ employees, trigger 30-day notice to employees, Iowa Workforce Development, and local government.
   - Distinguish from federal WARN (60 days for 50+ employees, 100+ company size).
   - Escalate to HR for coordination.

10. **Breach Notification (§ 715C.1)**: If a personnel-file or wage-record breach occurs:
    - Notify affected employees within 5 business days ("expedient" standard).
    - Notify Iowa Attorney General if 100+ Iowa residents affected.
    - Log the incident.

### Data Model / Field Restrictions:

- **pay_rate_change_effective_date**: Must be at least 3 days from notice date.
- **pay_rate_change_notice_sent**: Boolean; required before change effective date. Timestamp and method (email, paper).
- **pay_rate_change_notice_delivered**: Timestamp; must precede effective date by 3+ days.
- **pay_rate_change_reason**: Free text (merit, promotion, market adjustment, etc.); used for equal-pay audit trail.
- **comparable_roles_for_audit**: Link to employees in similar roles and their wages; supports § 91D.2 defense.
- **leave_balance_on_change**: Snapshot of accrued leave at time of change; preserved unless employer policy specifies forfeiture.
- **noncompete_agreement_present**: Boolean; if true, attach document and flag for legal review.
- **iowa_warn_threshold_25_employees**: Boolean; if true and layoff scenario, trigger 30-day notice workflow.

### Open Questions / Verify:

- What is the employer's documented policy on final-pay timing (next payday, 5 days, or immediate)?
- Does the employer have written noncompete agreements in use? Review for Iowa reasonableness.
- Is the employer subject to federal WARN (100+ employees)? If so, coordinate with state WARN.
- Does the employer use E-Verify for federal contracts or voluntarily?
- Has Iowa expanded protected classes under Chapter 216 in 2025? Verify gender-identity and pregnancy discrimination scope.
- What is the current Iowa court precedent on noncompete enforceability (case law)?
- Is the employer covered by FMLA (50+ employees within 75 miles)? If so, coordinate leave eligibility with role/pay changes.
