## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Kentucky is an at-will employment state under common law (no specific statute codifies this, but it is the default presumption). Absent a written contract specifying a term of employment, either employer or employee may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any reason, or for no reason, **except** termination cannot be based on:

- **Illegal discrimination** (race, color, sex, national origin, religion, age 40+, disability, genetic information under Title VII, ADEA, ADA, GINA)
- **Public policy** (jury duty, witness duty, serving in National Guard, voting, reporting safety violations under OSHA)
- **Retaliation** (reporting wage violations, workplace safety violations, or illegal activity; filing workers' compensation claims)
- **Bad faith/Covenant of good faith and fair dealing** (Kentucky recognizes an implied covenant in employment contracts, though application is limited)

No Kentucky state law requires written notice to an employee at hire regarding at-will status. Federal law (FLSA, under 29 U.S.C. § 206–214) requires certain wage disclosures, but no state-specific form is mandated. Wage-theft prevention notices are not required in Kentucky; federal FLSA applies. No state-specific notice form or timing mandate exists for wage-change announcements or role changes, though best practice is prompt, documented communication. **KRS 337.020 requires semi-monthly pay schedules; changes to pay frequency should be communicated well in advance** to allow employee adjustment.

**Offer Letter and Contract Terms**: When an employment relationship begins with a written offer letter or employment contract, those terms override at-will status if they specify duration, severance, or notice requirements. Employers should ensure that all offer letters and contracts are carefully drafted and reviewed for enforceability.

**Separation Documentation**: When an employee is separated, employers must file separation documentation (typically Separation Notice, UK-ES form) with the Kentucky Department of Unemployment Insurance for unemployment-insurance purposes, but **no statutory advance notice period to the worker is required** under Kentucky law. Federal WARN Act applies only to covered employers (50+ employees across all US locations; mass layoffs affecting 500 in a facility or 50 in a 30-day period), and Kentucky has no supplementary state mini-WARN act (verify: no Kentucky-specific mass-separation notice requirement found as of 2026-09-03). Employers may separate workers immediately upon at-will termination unless contracted otherwise.

**Public Employees**: Kentucky public-sector employees may have greater statutory protections (e.g., just-cause removal, notice periods, appeals processes); these do not extend to private-sector employees and are governed by state personnel law and union contracts where applicable.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: Kentucky's statutory minimum wage is $7.25/hour (KRS 337.275), matching the federal FLSA floor. Kentucky does not set a higher minimum; the federal rate applies uniformly across the state.

**Overtime and Seventh-Day Premium**: Kentucky requires overtime at 1.5× regular pay for hours over 40 in a workweek, consistent with federal FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 207). **Unique to Kentucky**: KRS 337.285 mandates premium pay (time-and-a-half) when an employee works all seven consecutive calendar days in a workweek, regardless of hours. This is a stricter state requirement than FLSA and affects any week-long shifts (e.g., a worker on call or in healthcare who works seven consecutive days incurs seventh-day premium liability even if all days are under 40 hours). Exemptions (executive, administrative, professional, outside sales) follow FLSA definitions.

**Pay Frequency**: KRS 337.020 requires employers to pay employees **at least semi-monthly** (twice per month). Employers may not adopt longer pay cycles (e.g., monthly for non-exempt workers). Dates must be fixed and regular. No state law prohibits weekly or biweekly pay (more frequent is permissible).

**Final Paycheck on Termination or Resignation**: KRS 337.055 mandates that all earned wages be paid in full to a separated employee **within 14 days of termination or on the next regular payday, whichever is earlier**. Verification: confirm whether "next regular payday" after 14 days triggers payment or if 14 days is an absolute cap. Disputes over final pay (e.g., disputed commissions, benefits valuations) should be clarified in writing.

**Deductions and Pay Statements**: KRS 337.060 permits only deductions required by law (federal/state income tax, Social Security, child support), court order, or **written employee authorization**. Employers may not deduct for uniforms, equipment, cash shortages, or "training clawbacks" unless explicitly authorized in a signed agreement. No specific pay-statement content requirement found in Kentucky statute; federal FLSA minimum applies (must show gross, deductions, net).

**Tipped Employees**: Federal FLSA allows a minimum wage of $2.13/hour for tipped employees (provided tips bring the employee to the federal minimum of $7.25/hour). Kentucky law does not appear to override this federal tipped minimum in the available statutes; employers may therefore use the federal $2.13 tipped wage floor for Kentucky employees in tipped positions (e.g., restaurant servers, bartenders, bellhops). However, **employers must ensure that tips + base wage meet or exceed $7.25/hour**; if tips fall short, the employer must make up the difference. All tips remain the property of the employee (KRS 337.065 addresses tips; verify: confirm whether Kentucky requires a higher base wage than $2.13 for tipped employees).

**Deduction Rules**: KRS 337.060 is strict on permissible deductions. Employers may deduct only:

- Taxes (federal, state, local income tax)
- Social Security and Medicare (FICA)
- Court-ordered deductions (child support, wage garnishment, judgment)
- Written employee authorization (e.g., voluntary contribution to health plan, 401k, union dues)

Employers **cannot** deduct for:

- Uniforms or work clothes (unless employee authorized or uniform is required only for work)
- Tools or equipment (unless employee authorized or tool use is incidental)
- Cash register shortages or "till taps"
- Breakage or damage (unless the employee negligently caused it and there is a written agreement allowing recovery)
- Training clawback (penalty for leaving after employer-funded training)
- Parking, meals, or lodging (unless employee authorized and amounts do not reduce pay below minimum wage)

Violations of KRS 337.060 may constitute wage theft and expose employers to civil liability and potential wage-and-hour claims.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Kentucky state law restricts inquiries about salary history or prohibits employers from asking about prior compensation. Federal EEOC guidance discourages such inquiries as a proxy for discrimination, but Kentucky has not enacted a categorical ban.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Kentucky state law requires salary ranges in job postings or on request. Federal rules do not mandate pay transparency; this remains employer discretion.

**Equal Pay Law**: **KRS 344 (Kentucky Civil Rights Act)** Chapter 34, Section 344.260 (verify exact cite) prohibits sex-based wage discrimination. The statute protects against paying employees of one sex at a lower rate than the opposite sex for "substantially equal work" requiring "the same or essentially the same knowledge, skills, efforts and responsibilities," considering skill, effort, and responsibility and working conditions. Enforced by the **Kentucky Commission on Human Rights (KCHR)** (verify: exact agency name and enforcement timeline as of 2026-09-03). Exceptions to equal pay may apply for:

- Seniority systems (longer tenure justifies higher pay)
- Merit systems (measurable performance differences)
- Production systems (output-based pay)
- Factors other than sex (e.g., educational credentials, licenses, geographic location)

Courts in Kentucky review these exceptions strictly; the burden is on the employer to prove that a pay differential is based on a non-sex factor. Remedies include back pay, liquidated damages, and attorney fees.

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Kentucky state law requires employers to report pay-equity data to the state (no audit requirement). Federal EEO-1 reporting applies to employers with 100+ employees (current threshold per EEOC; verify if changed in 2026).

**Protected Classes**: Kentucky law (KRS 344) and federal law (Title VII, ADA, ADEA, GINA) protect against discrimination based on:

- Race and color
- Religion
- Sex (including pregnancy, sexual harassment, gender identity under recent federal guidance)
- National origin
- Age (40 and over)
- Disability (physical and mental, with reasonable accommodation requirement)
- Genetic information
- Retaliation (for filing complaints or participating in investigations)

No Kentucky-specific expansion of protected classes beyond federal law has been identified. Sexual orientation and gender identity may receive protection under sex discrimination theories in some Kentucky courts (verify current case law as of 2026-09-03).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

No Kentucky state law mandates paid sick leave or paid family leave for private-sector employees.

**Federal FMLA** (29 U.S.C. § 2601 et seq.) applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles). Eligible employees receive up to 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period for serious health conditions, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver leave, or childbirth/adoption. Employers must maintain health-insurance benefits during FMLA leave. Intermittent or reduced-schedule leave is permitted when medically necessary.

**Kentucky State Employees** (public sector) receive statutory leave benefits (e.g., 15 days/year sick leave, up to 12 weeks family leave per KRS 61.600–650), but these are not private-sector mandates and do not apply to private employers.

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional under Kentucky law. Employers are **not required** to observe any specific holidays (e.g., Christmas, Labor Day, Thanksgiving). Employers may set policies (e.g., office closed on Christmas, "floating holidays") and communicate them at hire or in employee handbooks. If an employer **does** provide holiday pay, that pay must comply with wage-and-hour rules (cannot be deducted without authorization per KRS 337.060).

**Vacation and Accrual**: Accrued vacation is typically governed by employment contract or employer policy, not by Kentucky statute. **Important: Kentucky does not mandate payout of accrued vacation on termination** (unlike California, Illinois, and some other states). Employers may forfeit unused vacation if the employment contract or handbook explicitly permits forfeiture, provided the policy is in writing and communicated to the employee before the employee begins work. However, if an employer voluntarily provides vacation and the contract states "vacation is paid on termination," that contractual promise is enforceable. Best practice: adopt a clear, written vacation policy and communicate it to all employees at hire.

**Sick Leave Flexibility**: No Kentucky law mandates paid sick leave, but federal FMLA (if applicable) permits unpaid leave for serious health conditions. Some employers voluntarily provide paid sick leave; if so, rules on accrual, carryover, and payout should be clearly stated in the handbook.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: When an employee is promoted or experiences a base-pay change:

- Existing leave balances (if any) should be **preserved** in the employee record unless the employment contract specifies forfeiture.
- Any promised vacation payout on termination remains enforceable at promotion (e.g., if handbook says "unused vacation paid on separation," that promise continues).
- Accrual rates (e.g., 1 day/month) may be adjusted with advance notice (ideally communicated in writing and 30 days before the pay-change effective date).
- FMLA leave eligibility (if employer is covered) continues through role/pay changes.

**Best Practice**: Document leave balance updates (accrual, use, adjustments) at time of promotion or pay change; flag if forfeiture is intended at separation.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: Employers must retain payroll records, timesheets, and documents related to remuneration for **at least 4 years** (KRS 337.320). Federal FLSA requires 3 years minimum (29 U.S.C. § 211); Kentucky's 4-year standard is more stringent and applies. Records must include hours worked, wages paid, deductions, and overtime calculations.

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**: Kentucky law does not mandate a statutory personnel-file access right equivalent to some other states. However, common-law principles (privacy tort, public-records law for public employees under KRS 61.878) and contract law may grant employees limited access. Employers should adopt a reasonable access policy (e.g., "within 5 business days of request") and may withhold certain records (reference letters, confidential evaluations, internal investigations, third-party information) if disclosure would violate confidentiality or privacy. **Verify: No state statute explicitly mandates employee access to personnel records in Kentucky.**

**Record Format**: No Kentucky requirement for paper vs. electronic. Employers may maintain records digitally provided they remain accessible, authentic, and non-alterable (compliance with federal FLSA and IRS standards applies). Digital records must be retained for the full 4-year period.

**Separation Records**: Employers should retain separation notices, final-pay records, and related documentation as part of the 4-year payroll-retention cycle.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Kentucky has no comprehensive state data-privacy statute equivalent to CCPA or GDPR. However, **Kentucky requires data-breach notification** (verify: likely KRS 365.732 or under Kentucky consumer protection law).

**Breach Notification**: When an employer or its vendors collect, transmit, or maintain unencrypted personal information (e.g., Social Security number, driver's license number, financial account number, payment card number) and a breach occurs, notification to affected individuals is required. **Timing is "without unreasonable delay"** (verify: exact language and whether specific day count is stated; common interpretation is 30–60 days, but "expedient" is the statutory standard if KRS 365.732 applies).

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Kentucky-specific statute regulates employee biometric collection (fingerprints, iris scans, facial recognition) or electronic monitoring (keystroke logging, location tracking via GPS, email monitoring). Federal law (Title I of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2511) and Kentucky common law on privacy tort apply. Best practice: Obtain written consent for biometric collection and monitoring.

**Employee Record Confidentiality**: Kentucky does not impose state-specific employee data residency or cross-border processing restrictions; federal law and ordinary commercial data-protection practices apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Kentucky has **no ban-the-box law**. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history on applications, during interviews, or via background check at any time in the hiring process. Conviction history is not a protected class under Kentucky law (verify: no recent legislative changes as of 2026-09-03).

**Background Checks**: Permitted without state-specific restrictions. Employers commonly obtain criminal-history checks (via Kentucky State Police or national databases) with written consent. No state law restricts scope or timing for most private employers. Care should be taken to comply with federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) if a third-party vendor is used.

**Drug Testing**: No Kentucky law prohibits pre-employment drug testing. Employers may test all job applicants or classes of applicants without a specific reason. Federal law (ADA) applies: applicants with disabilities cannot be singled out for mandatory testing before an offer of employment. Employers testing applicants must follow FCRA procedures if third-party labs are used.

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**WARN Act**: The federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires covered employers (50+ employees across all US locations; layoffs affecting 500 in a single facility or 50 in a 30-day period) to provide **60 days' written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs. Applies nationwide, including Kentucky. No Kentucky state mini-WARN act has been identified (verify: no separate Kentucky mass-separation statute found as of 2026-09-03).

**Severance Pay**: No Kentucky state law mandates severance pay or severance formulas. Severance is entirely a matter of employment contract, written employer policy, or employer discretion. **If an employer chooses to provide severance**, the severance amount must be documented and paid according to the terms offered (e.g., "2 weeks pay per year of service"). Severance is subject to the same wage-and-hour rules as regular wages (must be paid timely, cannot be deducted without authorization). No obligation to provide severance upon termination absent a written agreement, offer letter, or written policy.

**Notice to Employee on Termination**: No state-specific advance notice period is required for at-will termination under Kentucky law; employers may separate workers immediately. However:

- If an **employment contract specifies a notice period** (e.g., "2 weeks' notice required," "30 days' notice for termination without cause"), that contractual obligation is enforceable.
- If an **employee handbook or company policy** states a notice period, Kentucky courts may consider it an enforceable contract if the employee relied on it.
- Federal WARN Act (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires **60 days' advance notice** for mass layoffs (500+ employees in a facility or 50+ in a 30-day period); verify whether your employer meets the threshold.
- **Best practice**: Provide written notice of termination (even if not required) and document the separation reason for unemployment-insurance and discrimination-defense purposes.

**Noncompete and Nonsolicitation Agreements**: Kentucky permits noncompete and nonsolicitation covenants under **Kentucky common law** (no comprehensive statute governs non-competes). Enforceability is determined case-by-case; agreements must satisfy all of the following to be enforceable:

1. **Reasonable in Scope**: The restriction must protect a legitimate business interest, such as:
   - Trade secrets (confidential processes, formulas, technical know-how)
   - Confidential business information (customer lists, pricing, strategies)
   - Customer goodwill and relationships
   - Substantial relationships with prospective or existing customers
   - Nondisclosure of business methods

2. **Reasonable in Geographic Area**: The restricted territory must be limited to:
   - The geographic area in which the business currently operates
   - Territory where the employee worked or had customer contact
   - States/regions reasonable to the business's actual footprint
   - National or even international scope may be reasonable for national/multinational companies (verify: depends on business scope)

3. **Reasonable in Duration**: The time restriction must be appropriate to the legitimate business interest:
   - 1 year is typically presumed reasonable
   - 2–3 years is often enforceable, depending on industry and business justification
   - 5+ years is scrutinized and often found unreasonable (verify: case-by-case analysis)
   - No fixed rule; courts consider how quickly business relationships decay in the industry

4. **Supported by Consideration**: The agreement must be:
   - Ancillary to an existing employment relationship (if provided at hire, employment itself is consideration)
   - Provided at time of hire with a signed offer letter (consideration is employment)
   - Supported by independent consideration if provided after hire (e.g., promotion, bonus, raise, stock options)
   - A bare non-compete (no employment, no pay, no benefit) is likely unenforceable

**Kentucky courts may **"blue pencil"** (reform) an overly broad non-compete agreement**, reducing its scope or duration to make it enforceable, or they may strike it entirely if the agreement is unreasonable. **Verify: Current Kentucky case law as of 2026-09-03; leading cases include cases from Kentucky Supreme Court and Court of Appeals.** Enforcement varies significantly by industry and fact pattern.

**Nonsolicitation Agreements** (restrictions on hiring former employees' co-workers or soliciting customers) are analyzed under similar reasonableness standards but may be enforced more readily than non-competes if narrowly tailored to legitimate business interests.

**Garden Leave**: No Kentucky statute requires paid leave ("garden leave" or "gardening leave") during a notice or non-compete period. A worker subject to a non-compete or notice period who is not actively working typically continues to receive wages only if the employment contract specifies payment during that period (verify: this is unusual in US law and would require explicit contractual commitment).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Kentucky employment law (KRS 341.010 et seq., Unemployment Insurance Law) defines "employee" for unemployment-insurance purposes. **Neither contract labels nor the parties' intent controls; liability is based on the true underlying nature of the working relationship.** Factors include (1) control over work methods and schedules, (2) provision of tools and equipment, (3) integration into the business, (4) permanence of the relationship, and (5) whether the worker sets own rates/terms. Misclassification of employees as independent contractors triggers unemployment-insurance, wage, and tax liabilities and may expose employers to audit and penalties.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Kentucky-specific rules govern remote workers or employees working partly in Kentucky. Employers must comply with all applicable state laws where work is performed. Kentucky income-tax withholding is required if an employee is working in Kentucky (verify current guidance from Kentucky Department of Revenue). Multi-state employers should ensure compliance with wage-and-hour, non-compete, and discrimination laws of each jurisdiction.

**Reciprocity**: Kentucky law applies to employment disputes where work is performed in Kentucky, regardless of choice-of-law provisions in an employment contract. Out-of-state employees working remotely for Kentucky employers may be subject to Kentucky wage laws (verify: depends on employee location and nature of work).

## 11. Implications for HCM Next (P1A/P1B)

When the platform processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in Kentucky:

### Promotion + Base-Pay Change Workflow

**At-Will Status and Contract Terms**:

- **Record the at-will status** in the worker profile; default to "at-will" for most Kentucky workers unless a written employment agreement (offer letter, employment contract) is attached.
- If a **written employment agreement exists** (specifying term, severance, notice period, non-compete, etc.), record and flag it; ensure changes comply with contract terms.
- Document the **effective date** of the promotion and pay change, and ensure it aligns with the next regular payroll date if possible (to avoid mid-cycle pay complications).

**Pay Frequency and Wage Payment Timing**:

- **Enforce KRS 337.020 semi-monthly requirement**: Ensure the new pay schedule pays wages at least semi-monthly (twice per calendar month). If the employer currently pays biweekly, that is compliant (more frequent than semi-monthly). If the employer intends to switch to monthly pay, flag as **non-compliant** and require correction before the change is effective.
- **Communicate pay-change timing**: If pay frequency is changing, notify the employee in writing at least one pay cycle (ideally 30 days) before the change is effective. Document this notification in the employment record.
- **Ensure wage payment by next payday**: Wages earned through a pay period must be paid by the next regular payday or within 7 days (standard practice); no longer gaps are permitted under KRS 337.020.

**Overtime and Seventh-Day Premium Calculation**:

- **Model overtime correctly**: Compute overtime at **1.5× the regular rate** for all hours over 40 in a workweek (consistent with federal FLSA).
- **Apply seventh-day premium** (unique to Kentucky): If an employee works **all seven consecutive calendar days** in a workweek (e.g., Monday through Sunday), the employee is entitled to premium pay (1.5×) for hours worked on the seventh day (KRS 337.285). This is **in addition to overtime** and is a common source of compliance mistakes.
  - Example: Employee works Mon–Sun, 6 hours/day = 42 hours total. Hours 1–40 are regular rate; hours 41–42 are overtime (1.5×); any day worked on the 7th day gets seventh-day premium (1.5×).
  - The platform must track workweek calendars (Monday–Sunday by default, or employer's defined workweek) and flag seventh-day scenarios.
- **Alert logic**: When a promotion or pay change occurs, check if the employee's historical or projected schedule includes seventh-day work; if so, flag for special compensation calculation.

**Equal Pay and Wage Equity**:

- **Capture comparable roles, skills, responsibilities, and pay** for employees of the same sex in similar positions (KRS 344 compliance).
- When a **promotion involves a pay raise**, audit whether the raise aligns with historical patterns for that role. If a male employee in the same role has been paid 10% more historically, flag for equal-pay review.
- Maintain an **audit trail** of pay-change justifications (e.g., "promotion based on experience," "market adjustment," "performance bonus"). Document comparability analysis (role, skill level, responsibilities) for potential wage-equity defense.
- If the employer has multiple job levels, build a **pay-band matrix** by role and experience to ensure systematic, non-discriminatory pay decisions.

**Record Retention and Data Governance**:

- **Retain all wage and separation records for 4 years** (KRS 337.320): Promotion date, old pay, new pay, effective date, overtime calculations, seventh-day calculations, final-pay records.
- Implement **automated archival** in the platform backend to mark records as "archive-eligible" after 4 years for compliance review and purge.
- Link all wage changes to the affected employee's payroll records to maintain the audit trail.

**Final-Pay Calculation on Termination**:

- **Compute final wages** (all earned wages through the termination date):
  - Regular wages for hours worked
  - Overtime (1.5× for hours over 40/week)
  - Seventh-day premium (1.5× if employee worked all 7 days in the final week)
  - Accrued vacation payout (if employer policy or contract mandates payout; not required by law but check the employee handbook)
  - Bonus or incentive compensation (if earned or contractually promised)
- **Ensure payment within 14 days or next regular payday** (whichever is sooner) per KRS 337.055. Model "next regular payday" as the standard final-pay deadline; if the employer extends beyond 14 days, flag as **potential violation**.
- **Flag any deductions** that are not tax-mandated or written-authorized (KRS 337.060); prohibited deductions (uniforms, shortages, damage) should raise an alert.

**Leave Balance Handling**:

- If the employee has **accrued PTO, vacation, or sick leave**, record the balance at the time of promotion in the employee's profile.
- **Preserve the balance** unless the employment contract explicitly permits forfeiture (e.g., "vacation forfeited upon termination").
- If the employer **mandates vacation payout on separation** (per policy or contract), calculate the payout and include in final pay (subject to wage-payment timing rules).
- If leave is **not forfeited**, it carries forward through the promotion and into the next separation event.
- Flag if the employer has no written leave policy, since this creates compliance ambiguity at separation.

### Manager Change Workflow

**No special legal requirements**: Kentucky law does not impose specific restrictions on manager changes. When an employee's manager changes:

- Update the employee's **direct-report relationship** in the organizational hierarchy.
- Ensure the **employee is notified** of the new manager and any changes to reporting, expectations, or performance-review schedules.
- **Do not use a manager change as a pretext for discrimination** (e.g., changing a manager solely to create a basis for termination); such actions may expose the employer to retaliation or constructive-discharge claims.
- Carry forward all **leave balances, accrued compensation, and employment terms** to the new manager's team; no reset or forfeiture is permitted.
- Ensure the **new manager is briefed on the employee's compensation, non-compete, and any employment-agreement terms** to avoid accidental violations.

### Separation Workflow

**At-Will Termination**:

- **Document termination rationale** (performance, reduction in force, misconduct, voluntary resignation) for internal compliance and to defend against wrongful-termination or discrimination claims.
- **No advance written notice is required** under Kentucky law unless:
  - The employment contract specifies a notice period (e.g., "2 weeks").
  - Federal WARN Act applies (60 days' notice for mass layoffs involving 50+ or 500+ employees; verify threshold).
  - Employer policy or handbook specifies notice (courts may enforce this as an implied contract).
- **Immediate separation is permissible** for at-will employees absent a contractual obligation.
- **Provide final paycheck** within 14 days of termination or next regular payday (KRS 337.055).

**WARN Act and Mass Layoffs**:

- **Federal WARN Act applies** (29 U.S.C. § 2102) if the employer has 50+ employees (aggregate) and the layoff affects **500 or more** in a facility or **50+ in a single 30-day period**.
- If WARN applies, **provide 60 days' written advance notice** to affected employees, unions (if any), and state/local workforce agencies.
- **No Kentucky supplementary state mini-WARN law** has been identified; federal WARN is the only mass-layoff notice requirement (verify: no recent Kentucky statutory changes).
- Failure to comply with WARN carries penalties (back pay, benefits, attorney fees); prioritize compliance if layoff meets WARN thresholds.

**Non-Compete and Non-Solicit Enforcement**:

- If the employer uses **non-compete or non-solicit agreements**, attach the agreement to the employment record and flag for legal review **before separation**.
- **Consult employment counsel** before enforcing a non-compete; Kentucky enforceability depends on:
  - Reasonableness of scope (protecting legitimate business interest)
  - Reasonableness of geography (limited to business territory)
  - Reasonableness of duration (1–3 years typically enforceable; longer terms scrutinized)
  - Adequate consideration (employment at hire, or independent consideration if imposed after hire)
- Overly broad non-competes may be **"blue penciled"** (reformed) by courts or struck entirely; enforcement is **not guaranteed**.
- **Do not attempt to enforce a non-compete unilaterally**; notify the departing employee in writing of the restriction and seek legal review.
- Consider whether the employee is in a **confidential, customer-facing, or trade-secret role** where enforcement is most defensible.

**Separation Notice and Unemployment Insurance**:

- File a **Separation Notice (UK-ES form or equivalent)** with the **Kentucky Department of Unemployment Insurance** within a specified timeframe (typically within 10 days of separation).
- Include the separation reason (voluntary resignation, reduction in force, misconduct, etc.); this affects the employee's unemployment-insurance eligibility.
- Keep the notice on file with the employment record for at least 4 years (KRS 337.320).

**Severance Pay (if offered)**:

- If the employer **chooses to provide severance**, calculate and pay according to the company policy, contract, or offer (e.g., "1 week per year of service").
- Severance is subject to wage-and-hour rules: must be paid timely (within 14 days or next payday), cannot be deducted without authorization.
- Ensure the severance amount is clearly documented in a **separation letter** or **severance agreement**, including any conditions (e.g., non-disparagement, non-solicitation, non-compete waiver, ADEA release).
- If the employer requires a **release or waiver** as a condition of severance, ensure it complies with federal law (ADEA Older Workers Benefit Protection Act requires specific language and consideration for 40+ employees).

### Termination Data Model (P1A/P1B Fields and Constraints)

**Employee Base Data**:

- **Employee.AtWillStatus**: Boolean (default `true` for Kentucky); mark `false` if a written employment contract, offer letter, or union agreement specifies a term or notice period.
- **Employee.EmploymentContractAttachment**: File or link to written employment agreement (if exists); flag for legal review on termination.
- **Employee.ContractEffectiveDate**: Date on which the contract (if any) took effect.
- **Employee.ContractTermLength**: Duration of contract (e.g., "1 year," "indefinite term"); relevant for calculating notice or severance obligations.

**Pay Change Data**:

- **PayChange.NoticeDate**: Date the pay change was communicated to the employee; should precede effective date by one pay cycle minimum (ideally 30 days).
- **PayChange.ChangeReason**: Enum (Promotion, Raise, Reclassification, Adjustment, Market Correction); for audit trail.
- **PayChange.OldRate** and **PayChange.NewRate**: Previous and new wages/salary.
- **PayChange.PayFrequencyCode**: Enum (Weekly, BiWeekly, SemiMonthly, Monthly); **enforce KRS 337.020 minimum of semi-monthly** (flag if Monthly selected and validate with employer).
- **PayChange.NewPayCycleStartDate**: First date on which the new pay schedule applies.
- **PayChange.OvertimeEligible**: Boolean; `true` for non-exempt employees (most of workforce); mark `false` only for executive, administrative, professional, or outside-sales exemptions.
- **PayChange.SeventhDayWorkTracker**: Boolean indicator; if employee's schedule includes potential all-7-days work, flag for seventh-day premium monitoring (KRS 337.285).

**Termination and Final Pay Data**:

- **Termination.TerminationDate**: Date employment ended (last day worked or separation effective date).
- **Termination.TerminationReason**: Enum (Voluntary Resignation, Reduction in Force, Performance Termination, Misconduct, Retirement, Other); affects unemployment-insurance eligibility and potential legal defenses.
- **Termination.NoticeProvidedDate**: Date written notice (if any) was provided; compare to TerminationDate to verify contract compliance.
- **Termination.FinalPayDueDate**: Calculate as `Min(TerminationDate + 14 calendar days, NextRegularPayday)` per KRS 337.055; flag if FinalPayAmount is not paid by this date.
- **Termination.FinalPayAmount**: Gross amount (before taxes/deductions):
  - Earned regular wages through TerminationDate
  - Unpaid overtime at 1.5× (hours over 40 in final workweek)
  - Seventh-day premium at 1.5× (if employee worked all 7 calendar days in final week)
  - Accrued vacation payout (if employer policy mandates; calculate per leave policy)
  - Any earned bonus or incentive compensation
- **Termination.Deductions**: Array of deductions applied; verify each deduction is law-required (taxes, FICA, court-ordered garnishment) or has written employee authorization; flag any non-compliant deductions (KRS 337.060).
- **Termination.NetFinalPayAmount**: FinalPayAmount minus authorized deductions.
- **Termination.PaymentMethod**: Enum (Check, Direct Deposit, Other); ensure payment is received by the employee or refunded if rejected.
- **Termination.LeaveBalanceForfeited**: Boolean; if true, record which types of leave were forfeited and on what legal basis (if any).

**Leave and Accrual Data**:

- **Employee.LeavePolicy**: Link to company handbook or written policy governing vacation, sick leave, PTO accrual, carryover, forfeiture.
- **Employee.AccruedVacationBalance**: Hours or days accrued as of the promotion or termination date.
- **Employee.VacationPayoutOnTermination**: Boolean; does employer policy mandate payout of unused vacation at separation? (Kentucky law does not require this, but contract/policy may.)
- **PayChange.LeaveBalanceCarryforward**: Flag to ensure leave balances are preserved and updated in the employee record following a promotion or pay change.

**Equal Pay and Wage Equity Data**:

- **PayChange.ComparableEmployees**: List of employees in the same or similar role, with same sex/gender, showing their pay, experience, and job level; used for equal-pay audit (KRS 344).
- **PayChange.PayAdjustmentJustification**: Text field capturing the business reason for the pay change (e.g., "promotion to lead role," "market adjustment," "performance-based"); supports equal-pay defense.
- **PayChange.EqualPayAuditRequired**: Boolean; flag to true if the pay change is to a new or existing comparable role for an employee of the same sex as other employees in that role; trigger HR review to confirm pay parity (KRS 344).

**Non-Compete and Restrictive Covenant Data**:

- **Employee.NonCompeteAttachment**: File link to signed non-compete agreement (if exists).
- **NonCompete.RestrictionScope**: Text (e.g., "Cannot work for competitors in software development within Kentucky and Tennessee").
- **NonCompete.RestrictionDuration**: Duration (e.g., "2 years from termination date").
- **NonCompete.RestrictionTerritory**: Geographic area (e.g., "Kentucky," "50-mile radius of Louisville").
- **NonCompete.FlagForLegalReview**: Boolean; set to true upon separation if non-compete exists; require legal counsel sign-off before enforcement.
- **NonCompete.EnforcementStatus**: Enum (Not Enforced, Under Review, Enforced, Waived); track the outcome of any legal review.

**WARN and Mass-Separation Data**:

- **Termination.WARNApplicable**: Boolean; set to true if employer has 50+ employees (aggregate) and separation is part of a layoff affecting 50+ in 30 days or 500+ in facility.
- **Termination.WARNNoticeIssuedDate**: Date 60-day WARN notice was issued (if applicable).
- **Termination.WARNNoticeRecipients**: List of recipients (employees, unions, state/local workforce agencies).
- **Termination.StateNoticeRequired**: Boolean; if yes, file Separation Notice (UK-ES) with Kentucky Department of Unemployment Insurance within required timeframe.

### Open Questions for Employer

1. Does the employer have a written semi-monthly pay schedule, and is it communicated to employees?
2. Does the employer track seventh-day work patterns (all 7 calendar days in a week)? If so, how is seventh-day premium calculated and paid?
3. What is the employer's final-pay policy: payment on termination day, next regular payday, or a set number of days?
4. Does the employer offer PTO/vacation, and if so, is accrued leave paid out on termination?
5. Is the employer subject to federal WARN (50+ employees with mass-layoff potential)? If so, coordinate with 60-day notice.
6. Does the employer use non-compete or non-solicit agreements? If so, have they been reviewed for Kentucky enforceability?
7. Has the employer reviewed pay equity by gender/race for comparable roles (KRS 344 compliance)?
8. Is the employer aware of the Kentucky seventh-day overtime requirement, or are seventh-day scenarios currently unmanaged?
