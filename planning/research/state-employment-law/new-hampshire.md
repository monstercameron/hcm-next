# New Hampshire Employment Law Research

**State:** New Hampshire | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **At-will employment**: New Hampshire recognizes employment-at-will; employment may be terminated without cause and without notice (common law). Exceptions include termination for exercising constitutional rights, jury duty, military service, filing wage/workers' compensation claims, or whistleblowing (RSA 275-E).
- **No state paid sick leave mandate**: Unlike many neighboring states, New Hampshire imposes no requirement for paid or unpaid sick leave. Employers must comply with federal FMLA only if applicable (50+ employees, 12-month history).
- **Pay rate change notice required**: Employers must provide written notice of any wage, salary, or pay schedule changes prior to the effective date and obtain employee signature (RSA 275:49).
- **Non-compete bans for low-wage workers**: Non-compete agreements are void and unenforceable for employees earning ≤200% of federal minimum wage (~$14.50/hour); physicians and dentists subject to additional restrictions (RSA 275:70-a, RSA 329:31-a).
- **Minimum wage**: Federal minimum wage ($7.25/hour) applies; no state override (RSA 279:21).
- **Federal WARN compliance**: State-level notification requirement mirrors federal WARN Act for employers with 100+ employees planning mass layoffs or plant closings; 60 days' advance notice required (RSA 275-F).
- **Equal pay enforcement**: Sex-based pay discrimination prohibited under same standards as federal law (RSA 275:37); wage discussion protection enforced (RSA 275:41-b).
- **Personnel file access**: Employees have statutory right to inspect personnel records; employers with 15+ employees must retain records for 1 year (RSA 275:56).
- **Discrimination protections**: Age, sex, gender identity, race, color, religion, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, marital status, genetic information, military status covered (RSA 354-A:7).
- **Voluntary paid family leave**: Granite State PFML program offers voluntary 60% wage replacement for up to 6 weeks annually; no employer mandate (voluntary, insurance-based, effective 2023).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

New Hampshire is an employment-at-will state; both employers and employees may terminate the relationship without cause or notice (common law). Exceptions exist for termination in violation of public policy or statutory rights:

- Termination for filing a wage theft complaint, workers' compensation claim, or unemployment benefit claim.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religious freedom).
- Termination for jury duty, voting, military service, voting, or appearing as a witness.
- Termination in violation of public policy (whistleblowing under RSA 275-E).
- Termination for refusal to perform illegal act.

**Required written notices at hire and on pay/role changes**:

- RSA 275:49: Employers must provide written advance notice of any changes to wages, salary, paydays, or employment practices/policies. Notice must precede the effective date. Employer must obtain employee's signed acknowledgment and retain copy in personnel file.
- RSA 275:43: Employers must pay wages at least weekly or biweekly; alternative pay frequencies require written petition to Commissioner of Labor and approval showing "good cause" (no less frequently than monthly).
- No general written employment offer statute, but § 275:49 applies to all wage/role changes.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- RSA 279:21: New Hampshire minimum wage is set by statute to match the federal minimum wage ($7.25/hour); automatically adjusts if federal rate increases.
- Tipped employees: Base wage of $3.27/hour permitted if tips customarily exceed $30/month; employer must supplement to reach federal minimum.
- No state overtime standard; federal FLSA applies (1.5x regular rate for hours over 40/week).

**Pay frequency and timing**:

- RSA 275:43: Employers must pay wages within 8 days after expiration of week worked (weekly) or within 15 days after expiration of week (biweekly). Commissioner may approve less frequent pay (no less than monthly) upon written petition for good cause.
- No requirement that payday be on a specific day of week; must be consistent and predictable.

**Final pay on termination**:

- RSA 275:44: On discharge, all wages must be paid within 72 hours of termination. On voluntary resignation with notice, wages due at next regular payday; if resignation without notice, wages due within 72 hours of last day worked. If employee laid off, wages due on next regular payday.
- Penalties: Employer liable for liquidated damages of 10% of unpaid wages per day (excluding Sundays/holidays) if willfully fails to pay without good cause, or amount equal to unpaid wages, whichever is smaller.

**Permitted deductions**:

- RSA 275:48: Employers may deduct wages only if required/empowered by law (taxes, garnishments, court orders) or with written employee authorization for lawful purpose benefiting employee (union dues, health insurance, charitable contributions, voluntary retirement/savings funds, voluntary non-required clothing rental, voluntary uniform cleaning).
- Deductions for employer-required logos or distinctive designs on garments prohibited; no deduction from final wages if garment not returned.
- No deduction for tools, uniforms, or other employer-imposed costs unless employee consents in writing and deduction does not reduce wages below minimum wage.

**Pay statement content**:

- No state-mandated itemized pay stub format; federal FLSA requirements apply (employer should document hours, wage rate, deductions, net pay per RSA 275:56 record-retention obligation).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage range disclosure**:

- No state mandate for job posting wage ranges or benefits disclosure (contrast to neighboring Connecticut, Massachusetts, Maryland). However, applicants may request wage range information at any time, and refusal to disclose is not unlawful.
- Employer may use internal pay scales, past ranges, or market comparables to determine rates.

**Salary history inquiry**:

- No state prohibition on inquiring about prior salary/wages. Proposed bill (SB 211) would have banned inquiries; vetoed in 2019. Employers may legally request applicants' wage history.

**Equal pay**:

- RSA 275:37: Employers prohibited from paying employees of one sex at a rate lower than opposite sex for equal work requiring equal skill, effort, and responsibility under similar working conditions.
- Exceptions: Seniority system, merit/performance system, quality/quantity production system, demonstrable factor other than sex (education, training, expertise).
- Employer cannot reduce other employees' wages to comply with equal pay rule.
- Wage discussion protection (RSA 275:41-b): Employees protected from retaliation for disclosing or discussing wages/benefits; employers cannot require confidentiality of pay as condition of employment.

**Protected classes and discrimination**:

- RSA 354-A:7: Discrimination prohibited based on age, sex, gender identity (protected since 2018), race, color, marital status, physical/mental disability, religious creed, national origin, sexual orientation (protected since 1998), ancestry, genetic information, military status.
- Applies to employers with 6+ employees.
- Pregnancy/childbirth/related medical conditions: Employers must grant temporary disability leave and restore to same or comparable position upon return to work (RSA 354-A:7-VI).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**:

- No state-mandated paid or unpaid sick leave for private employers. Employers may offer voluntary policies; if offered, policy terms control.

**Granite State Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML)**:

- Voluntary, insurance-based program effective January 1, 2023. Not employer-mandated; employers and employees may opt in.
- Coverage: Up to 6 weeks of leave per year at 60% wage replacement for employee's own serious health condition, family member's serious health condition, new child bonding, or military family leave.
- Premium capped at $5/week ($260/year) for individual coverage; state allows flexible premium-sharing models.
- Wages used to calculate replacement benefit capped at Social Security Taxable Wage Maximum.
- No integration with federal FMLA; distinct program.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- If employee takes voluntary PFML leave and returns to same or promoted role, employer must not reduce wages below pre-leave amount.
- No statutory requirement that promoted wage exceed pre-leave wage; verify case law on whether promotion changes apply at return.
- Accrued paid leave (if employer provides) typically continues accruing during paid PFML; unpaid PFML may suspend accrual (employer policy controls, verify).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- RSA 275:56: Employers with 15+ employees must retain all personnel and employment records for 1 year. Smaller employers may have no statutory requirement, but best practice is 3+ years.
- Personnel file includes employment applications, internal evaluations, disciplinary documentation, payroll records, injury reports, performance assessments.
- Health, fitness, lifestyle, and wellness program information obtained for health risk assessment are NOT considered personnel records and must not be retained in personnel file (RSA 275:56).
- Payroll records must document name, address, occupation, hours worked (daily/weekly), wage rate, gross/net pay, deductions, leave accrual (RSA 275:56, Lab. 802.08).

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- RSA 275:56: Employees have statutory right to inspect and receive copies of personnel file upon written request. Employers may charge reasonable copying fee. Access must be provided within reasonable time (verify specific timeline; typically 10–15 business days) at or near worksite during regular business hours.
- Exemptions: Information that would prejudice law enforcement investigation; government security investigation information.

**Record disputes**:

- If employee disputes information in file, employer should allow employee to submit written statement of disagreement; statement must be retained in file and accompany any disclosure to third parties.

**Record format**:

- No state mandate for electronic vs. paper format; either acceptable if retention and access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Breach notification**:

- RSA 359-C:20: Any person or business in New Hampshire that owns or licenses computerized data containing personal information must notify affected individuals "as soon as possible" and "without unreasonable delay" when security breach occurs and likelihood that information has been or will be misused exists.
- Notification to: affected individuals, state attorney general (for persons not subject to trade regulation), primary regulator (for regulated businesses).
- Timing: Expedient manner without unreasonable delay; delays permitted only when necessary to determine breach scope, restore system integrity, or comply with law enforcement request.
- Encryption safe harbor: No notification required if compromised personal information was encrypted and encryption key was not also acquired.
- Applicability to employee data (verify): Statute targets personal information generally; unclear whether employee records (payroll, SSN, address) trigger notification. Recommend treating employee data as personal information subject to law.

**Employee privacy—monitoring, social media**:

- No New Hampshire statute bans workplace email/internet monitoring or general video monitoring of work areas; reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies (common law).
- Social media: Employers may view publicly available content but should avoid discrimination claims.

**Data residency and processing**:

- No state law mandating data residency for employee records; employers may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided data security and breach notification laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- As of July 1, 2025, New Hampshire repealed its statewide ban-the-box law (RSA 275-H). No current state restriction on inquiring about criminal records at application stage or before interview. Federal FCRA rules and common-law tort law apply.
- Background checks conducted by third parties must comply with federal FCRA (notice, dispute rights, adverse-action procedures).

**Background checks**:

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions for private employers. Annulled records (RSA 651:5) must not be disclosed by candidate or used against applicant.

**Drug testing**:

- No state law restricting private-sector drug testing. Employers have broad discretion to test pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, or random. Drug testing policy should be written, consistent, and communicated to applicants/employees.
- Medical marijuana legalized (2013) but no employer accommodation required.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No state mandate for private-sector E-Verify; federal I-9 requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice (NH WARN Act)**:

- RSA 275-F: Applies to employers with 100+ full-time employees. Requires 60 days' advance written notice before plant closing or mass layoff affecting 25+ employees or 25%+ of workforce over 3-month period.
- Notice recipients: all affected employees (including part-time and those employed <6 months), union representatives, state Commissioner of Labor, state Attorney General, chief elected official of each affected municipality.
- Administrative rulemaking: Lab. 1700 specifies content and procedures (verify specific notice format/content requirements).

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance except in narrow circumstances (verify case law). Severance is contractual; requires explicit agreement.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- RSA 275:70-a: Non-competes void and unenforceable for "low-wage employees" (employees earning ≤200% of federal minimum wage, currently ~$14.50/hour).
- RSA 275:70: Non-compete agreements must be provided to employee prior to acceptance of offer; if not, unenforceability may be established.
- For non-low-wage employees: common law enforceability applies; courts enforce non-competes if reasonable in duration, geography, and scope with legitimate business interest (trade secrets, customer relationships).
- RSA 329:31-a: Physician non-competes entered into after August 5, 2016 are void and unenforceable, regardless of wage or terms; applies to restrictive covenants on physicians' practice.
- Dentist/healthcare non-competes: Verify scope of restrictions (may fall under broader healthcare provider rules).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness standard (duration, scope, geography, legitimate business interest).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- New Hampshire courts apply the "ABC Test" (common law), though IRS, NHES, NHDOL, and USDOL each have separate independent contractor tests. An employer must satisfy all three prongs: (A) worker free from control/direction both contractually and in fact; (B) work outside usual course of employer's business or outside all employer's places of business; (C) worker customarily engaged in independently established trade/business of same nature.
- For discrimination, harassment, retaliation claims: courts apply common law "right to control" test.
- Misclassification enforcement: NHDOL, Department of Revenue, Attorney General, Workers' Compensation Commission; penalties include back wages, penalties, reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute addressing remote-work classification. Standard employment law applies regardless of work location. If employee works in New Hampshire (remotely or otherwise) for any employer, New Hampshire law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine. If employee works across state lines, each state's laws apply to work performed in that state. Platform should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable state rules.
- New Hampshire has no income tax (tax advantage for remote workers in NH).
- Employers may specify in writing that New Hampshire law governs employment relationship (recommended for multi-state arrangements).

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling: Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

**Mandatory obligations and field restrictions**:

1. **Pay rate change notice** (RSA 275:49): On any base-pay change (increase or decrease), generate automated written notice stating new rate and effective date. For decreases, require 1+ pay period advance notice; increases may be made immediately. Capture employee signed acknowledgment in system (mandatory field for compliance).

2. **No salary history inquiry** (best practice, though not prohibited): While not legally banned, do not require or flag applicant/candidate salary history fields during hiring. If data exists from external sources, flag for HR/legal review before use.

3. **Equal pay audit flag** (RSA 275:37): On promotion + base-pay change, system should flag if new rate differs >15% from peer rates for equal work in same establishment (same employer). Require manager justification or HR approval for outlier increases.

4. **Non-compete enforceability check** (RSA 275:70-a, RSA 329:31-a): On hiring or role change, system must: (a) Calculate employee hourly wage (or annualize if salary); (b) If ≤$14.50/hour (200% federal min), flag any existing non-compete clause as VOID and unenforceable; mark in system as unenforceable; (c) If employee is physician or dentist, flag all non-compete clauses (regardless of wage) as likely VOID per RSA 329:31-a; (d) On promotion affecting wage, recalculate threshold and update enforceability status (e.g., promotion across $14.50 threshold may activate enforceability).

5. **Non-compete disclosure** (RSA 275:70): If non-compete exists and enforceable, ensure copy provided to employee prior to offer acceptance (or upon hiring if not at offer stage). Document disclosure in personnel file.

6. **Wage discussion protection** (RSA 275:41-b): Do not restrict or flag employee discussions of wages/benefits. Ensure policy language does not prohibit pay disclosure among coworkers; audit existing policies for retaliation clauses.

7. **Personnel file retention and access** (RSA 275:56): For employers with 15+ employees, system must: (a) Retain all personnel and payroll records for 1 year minimum; (b) Flag records for deletion after 1-year threshold if employer policy allows purge; (c) Enable employee access requests via self-service or HR portal; (d) Maintain audit log of access requests and documents provided.

8. **Promotion + pay change timing**: No statutory advance notice for promotion itself; RSA 275:49 applies only to wage/salary changes. If promotion includes role change without wage change, no notice required. If role change includes wage change, advance notice (for decreases) or concurrent notice (for increases) required.

9. **Final pay on separation** (RSA 275:44): If termination occurs during or shortly after promotion, ensure final paycheck includes all wages earned at promoted rate from effective date forward. No hold-back allowed; all wages due within 72 hours of discharge.

10. **PFML eligibility tracking** (Granite State PFML): System should track employee opt-in to voluntary PFML program; if enrolled, deduct premiums per agreement and flag benefit eligibility after coverage period begins.

11. **No paid sick leave requirement**: Do not auto-provision paid sick leave; only if employer policy provides. If policy exists, track accrual per policy terms (no statutory minimum).

12. **Disclosure of non-enforce roles to legal**: Flag positions subject to non-compete restrictions (low-wage ban, physician ban) before non-compete agreements are drafted or executed. Require pre-offer review for non-compete language validity.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether non-solicitation agreements (separate from non-competes) are enforceable for low-wage employees under RSA 275:70-a.
- Clarify whether "same establishment" for equal-pay comparisons under RSA 275:37 includes multi-site employers or only same physical worksite/county.
- Verify scope of healthcare worker non-compete ban: does it extend to nurses, therapists, or only physicians (RSA 329:31-a scope)?
- Confirm whether employee promotion to low-wage threshold invalidates existing non-compete (verify case law on retroactive application).
- Verify current list of occupations subject to federal E-Verify requirements (IRCA) and whether NH state IRCA-equivalent exists.
