# Nevada Employment Law Research

**State:** Nevada | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Job postings and promotions** must disclose wage range to applicants after interview (NRS 613.133); employers cannot inquire about or rely on salary history when setting pay.
- **Base-pay changes** cannot use applicant prior salary; wage decreases require 7 days' written notice before the employee works at the lower rate (NRS 608.100); all pay changes must be documented and recorded for wage-history compliance.
- **Manager changes** may trigger pay or title change; verify whether lateral reassignment at same pay requires notice under wage-transparency rules.
- **Termination** requires final pay immediately upon discharge (NRS 608.020); resignation allows 7 days or next payday, whichever is earlier (NRS 608.030). No severance is mandated; no state mini-WARN act exists (federal WARN applies for 100+ employees).
- **Non-competes** are generally unenforceable; limited exceptions for sale-of-business only; hourly employees cannot be bound (NRS 613.195, amended 2021); blue-pencil revision is available to courts.
- **Paid leave** is mandatory: private employers with 50+ employees must provide 0.01923 hours per hour worked (minimum 40 hours/year); usable after 90 days for health, caregiving, or personal needs (NRS 608.0197). Separate: domestic violence leave (up to 160 hours/12 months for victims, NRS 608.0198).
- **Payroll records** must be retained for 2 years; personnel files can be inspected by employees, though copies may be denied if employment under 60 days (NRS 608.115, NRS 613.075).
- **Pregnant workers accommodation**: employers with 15+ employees must provide reasonable accommodations for pregnancy-related conditions; no undue hardship exemption, timely good-faith interactive process required (NRS 613.4353–613.4383, effective 2017).
- **Voting and military leave**: employees entitled to paid voting leave (1–3 hours by distance, NRS 293.463); National Guard members protected from termination (NRS 412.139, up to $5,000 penalty per violation).
- **Wage transparency** ban: employers cannot enforce pay-secrecy policies; discussion of wages is protected (NRS 613.330). Marijuana pre-employment screening is permitted (with narrow exceptions: firefighters, EMTs, safety-sensitive roles, federal grants—NRS 613.132).
- **Ban the box** applies: no criminal history questions on initial application; criminal history may be evaluated later if procedure followed (NRS 613.330).
- **Minimum wage** is $12.00/hour flat (constitutional amendment effective 2024, NRS 608.250); overtime applies at 8-hour daily or 40-hour weekly threshold (1.5x rate) for employees under 1.5x minimum wage (NRS 608.018).

## 2. Employment Relationship

Nevada is an at-will employment state (NRS 608.010). Employment may be terminated by either party without advance notice or stated cause.

**No mandatory hire notice.** Nevada law does not require a written offer letter, at-hire notice, or employee handbook beyond federal requirements (I-9, tax withholding). Employers should document terms in writing to avoid disputes.

**Wage decrease notice.** Any reduction in agreed or earned wages requires written notice at least 7 days before the employee performs work at the reduced rate (NRS 608.100). Failure to provide notice subjects employer to penalties.

**Pay-secrecy prohibition.** Employers cannot adopt or enforce policies prohibiting employees from discussing their wages or the wages of coworkers; wage discussion is a protected activity under NRS 613.330.

**Semimonthly pay schedule.** Wages must be paid semimonthly on the 15th and last day of the month or on other fixed paydays established by the employer and posted conspicuously (NRS 608.060).

## 3. Wages

**Minimum wage:** $12.00 per hour statewide as of July 1, 2024 (NRS 608.250, amended by voter-approved constitutional amendment). No local jurisdictions currently set higher floors; verify if any are enacted.

**Overtime:** Employees earning less than 1.5× minimum wage ($18.00/hour as of 2024) are entitled to 1.5× their regular rate for hours exceeding 8 in a 24-hour period OR 40 hours in a workweek (NRS 608.018). Employees earning $18.00+ receive overtime only for hours exceeding 40 per week. When federal FLSA and Nevada rules differ, the employee-favorable standard applies.

**Pay frequency:** Wages are due semimonthly (15th and last day of month) by law (NRS 608.060). Employers must establish fixed paydays and post them conspicuously.

**Final pay on involuntary termination (discharge):** Compensation for labor earned, vested, determinable, and unpaid at discharge is due immediately (NRS 608.020). If payroll department is not operational on the day of discharge, wages are due within 3 days.

**Final pay on resignation:** Wages are due by the regular payday or within 7 days, whichever is earlier (NRS 608.030). If the employee does not provide notice, the employer may withhold wages until the next regular payday.

**Failure-to-pay penalties:** If wages are not paid as required, wages continue to accrue at the same rate until paid or for 30 days, whichever is less (NRS 608.040). Employer is liable for penalties and court costs.

**Pay statement requirements:** Employers must provide an itemized statement showing gross pay, net pay, deductions (with reason and authorization for each), and hours worked per period (NRS 608.115). Electronic statements are permitted.

**Permitted deductions:** Deductions are allowed only for legally required withholdings (taxes, FICA, unemployment insurance, wage garnishments, court orders) and authorized voluntary deductions (NRS 608.110). Deductions for union dues, savings plans, or relief associations require written authorization.

**Record retention:** Employers must maintain wage records (gross pay, deductions, net pay, hours worked) for at least 2 years (NRS 608.115).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage/salary history ban (NRS 613.133):** Employers cannot ask prospective employees about their wage or salary history; cannot rely on prior salary to set pay; cannot discriminate against applicants who refuse to disclose history. Violations are subject to complaints to the Labor Commissioner with potential remedies.

**Wage-range disclosure requirement (NRS 613.133):** After a job applicant completes an interview, the employer must provide the wage or salary range or rate for the position. Employers who post job advertisements must include the wage or salary range (verify current guidance for posting rules; the statute requires disclosure upon interview completion at minimum).

**Wage discussion protection (NRS 613.330):** Employers cannot prohibit or penalize employees for discussing wages with coworkers; wage discussion is protected activity. Policies enforcing wage secrecy are unlawful.

**Pay-secrecy policy prohibition:** Any employer policy requiring confidentiality of wages or disciplining employees for wage discussion violates NRS 613.330.

**No state equal-pay statute.** Nevada law does not contain an explicit equal-pay law equivalent to the federal Equal Pay Act. Discrimination on the basis of sex in pay is covered under NRS 613.330 as a general anti-discrimination provision; federal EPA applies.

**Lawful off-duty product use (NRS 613.333):** Employers cannot fail to hire, discharge, or otherwise discriminate against employees for engaging in lawful use of any product outside the employer's premises during nonworking hours, provided the use does not adversely affect job performance or the safety of other employees. Note: Nevada courts have ruled that "lawful use" means lawful under both Nevada AND federal law; federal illegality (e.g., marijuana) may override state protection. Remedies include lost wages/benefits, reinstatement, additional damages, and attorney's fees.

## 5. Leave and Time

**Paid leave requirement (NRS 608.0197, employers with 50+ employees):** Private employers with 50 or more employees must provide at least 0.01923 hours of paid leave for each hour of work performed (equivalent to ~40 hours per year). Employees may accumulate or receive leave in a lump sum at the start of the benefit year. Leave may be used for any reason: mental or physical health, medical diagnosis or care, preventative care, caregiving, or other personal needs. Employees cannot use paid leave until 90 days of employment have elapsed. Employers may limit use to 40 hours per year; unused leave need not roll over (use-it-or-lose-it policies are permitted under NRS 608.0197).

**Paid leave interaction with pay/role change:** A promotion or base-pay change does not reset accrual or eligibility; accrued leave continues to vest and be available regardless of pay change. Verify interaction with involuntary demotion.

**Domestic violence leave (NRS 608.0198):** Employees employed 90+ days who are victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, or whose family/household member is a victim, are entitled to not more than 160 hours of leave in a 12-month period. Leave may be paid or unpaid; used for diagnosis, treatment, counseling, legal proceedings, relocation, or safety planning. Employer cannot require disclosure of details or proof; retaliation is prohibited.

**Pregnant workers' fairness act (NRS 613.4353–613.4383):** Employers with 15+ employees must provide reasonable accommodations for conditions related to pregnancy, childbirth, or related medical conditions unless the accommodation would impose undue hardship. Unlawful for employer to refuse accommodation, take adverse action against an employee for requesting or using accommodation, or require an employee to take leave if an accommodation is available. Employer must engage in timely, good-faith interactive process to determine effective reasonable accommodation. Employees may file complaints with the Nevada Equal Rights Commission (NERC); remedies include back pay, reinstatement, and punitive damages. Effective October 1, 2017.

**No state family/medical leave insurance.** Nevada law does not mandate a state-run or employer-provided family/medical leave insurance plan. Federal FMLA applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles.

**Meal and rest periods (NRS 608.019):** Employees working 8+ continuous hours must receive at least one unpaid meal period of at least 30 minutes. Rest periods are calculated at 10 minutes per 4 hours of work (or major fraction thereof) and are paid, counted as work time; employers must authorize and permit these in the middle of each work period where practicable. No rest period is required if daily work time is less than 3.5 hours.

**No paid vacation law.** Nevada law does not require employers to provide paid vacation or PTO beyond the paid leave mandate (NRS 608.0197). If an employer offers vacation, it must be paid out upon termination; verify whether accrued but uncompensated leave is treated as earned wages.

**Voting leave (NRS 293.463):** Registered voters may take paid time off to vote if voting before or after work hours is impracticable. Time allowed varies by distance: 1 hour if polling place is 2 miles or less; 2 hours if more than 2 miles but not more than 10 miles; 3 hours if more than 10 miles. Employers cannot discharge, discipline, penalize, or make deductions from wages for voting leave. Employees must request leave prior to the day of election. Violation is a misdemeanor.

**Military leave (NRS 412.139):** Employers cannot terminate employment of Nevada National Guard or other state National Guard members because they assemble for training, participate in field training, active duty, or other required meetings, or are ordered to active service. Violation is a misdemeanor; employers face civil penalties up to $5,000 per violation. State and local government employees receive up to 15 days of paid military leave per calendar year and must be reinstated to the same or comparable position upon return.

## 6. Records and Access

**Payroll record retention:** Employers must retain records showing gross wages, deductions, net pay, pay dates, and hours worked for at least 2 years (NRS 608.115).

**Personnel file access (NRS 613.075):** Employees have the right to inspect and receive copies of their own personnel records (qualifications, disciplinary records, performance reviews, etc.). However, employers may refuse to provide copies to employees employed less than 60 days. Employers may charge reasonable copying costs. Employees may also submit written explanations of any disputed records.

**Selection and promotion records:** Employers should retain job descriptions, wage rate history, and selection records for each position to support wage-transparency and equal-pay compliance, though no specific statute mandates retention of hiring records beyond standard payroll requirements.

**Record format:** No state rule mandating paper vs. electronic; electronic records must meet accuracy and availability standards. Payroll records and personnel files may be maintained electronically.

**Wage-history record retention for pay-transparency compliance:** Beyond the 2-year statutory retention for payroll, employers should maintain records of wage-setting decisions, promotion rationale, and pay-range disclosures (interview date, date range disclosed) to defend against wage-transparency or equal-pay challenges under NRS 613.133 and 613.330.

- Retention practice: keep the wage-change notice (NRS 608.100) and the final-pay computation with the payroll record for the two-year NRS 608.115 window, and longer where FLSA (three years) applies.

## 7. Privacy and Data

**Nevada privacy law (NRS 603A):** Nevada does not have a comprehensive state privacy law parallel to CCPA. However, NRS 603A.200 et seq. addresses data security and breach notification.

**Breach notification (NRS 603A.220):** Data collectors (including employers) who maintain personal information must notify affected Nevada residents and consumers in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay if a breach of security compromises the confidentiality or integrity of unencrypted personal information. If a breach affects 1,000+ persons, the data collector must also notify consumer reporting agencies. Law enforcement may delay notification if it would impede criminal investigation.

**Employee data considerations:** NRS 603A.220 applies to employee personal information (Social Security numbers, financial account information, etc.). Employers should have a data-security and breach-notification policy. Verify whether employee records are subject to the full scope of NRS 603A.220 or if narrower rules apply.

**No state biometric data law.** Nevada law does not specifically restrict employer collection or use of biometric data (fingerprints, facial recognition, etc.) as of 2026-09-03; federal rules and general privacy principles apply.

**Monitoring and surveillance:** No Nevada-specific restrictions on electronic monitoring (email, GPS, keystroke logging), though NRS 613.135 prohibits employers from requiring employees to disclose personal social media account credentials.

**Personnel file privacy:** Employees can inspect their own files; no rule restricts employer access or use within the business.

## 8. Hiring and Background

**Ban the box (criminal history, NRS 613.330):** Employers cannot inquire about criminal history on the initial written or electronic application. Criminal history may be considered later in the hiring process if the employer follows required procedure: evaluating the nature of the offense, time elapsed, relationship to the position, and evidence of rehabilitation. Public employers must provide written notice of any decision to deny or revoke a job offer based on criminal history; verify whether this applies to private employers.

**Background checks:** No Nevada-specific licensing, training, or approval requirement for background check companies; federal FCRA rules apply.

**Drug testing:** No Nevada state law prohibiting private-sector drug testing; federal requirements apply.

**E-verify and I-9 compliance:** Nevada law does not mandate E-Verify participation. Federal I-9 and Form I-9 verification rules apply to all employers. E-Verify is optional and available to Nevada employers.

**Marijuana pre-employment screening (NRS 613.132):** Employers may use marijuana screening tests; however, employers cannot refuse to hire based on a positive marijuana screening test EXCEPT for:

- Firefighters and emergency medical technicians (permit refusal based on testing)
- Safety-sensitive positions (verify scope; statute is narrow and specific)
- Positions funded by federal grants where federal law prohibits use

All other employees: a positive marijuana screening test cannot be a sole basis for non-hire.

**Salary history inquiry ban:** Applicants cannot be asked about prior wage or salary history (NRS 613.133). Wage-range disclosure is required after interview.

## 9. Separation

**At-will termination:** Nevada employers may terminate employees without advance notice, cause, or severance (NRS 608.010). At-will status applies unless a written contract, union agreement, or specific statute creates an exception.

**Final pay timing:** Immediate upon involuntary discharge (or within 3 days if payroll office closed); by regular payday or within 7 days if resignation (NRS 608.020, NRS 608.030). Failure triggers wage-penalty liability.

**Severance:** No state law requiring severance pay or notice. If severance is promised in a written agreement or implied contract, it must be paid; otherwise, it is discretionary.

**No Nevada mini-WARN act:** Nevada does not have a state-specific mass-layoff or plant-closure notice statute. Federal WARN Act (29 USC § 2101–2109) applies: employers with 100+ employees must provide 60 days' written notice to affected workers, state labor agency, and local government for plant closures or mass layoffs affecting 50+ employees. Penalties: back pay/benefits for each day of insufficient notice + up to $500/day per employee for failure to notify government agencies.

**Non-competes (NRS 613.195, amended 2017 and 2021):**

- Generally unenforceable in Nevada; courts disfavor restrictive covenants
- Limited exceptions: non-compete enforceable only if employer is selling the business/goodwill and the buyer acquires a legitimate protectable interest
- **Hourly employees:** Cannot be bound by non-competes (blanket prohibition)
- **Blue-pencil revision:** Courts may modify overly broad non-competes to make them enforceable
- All non-competes must be supported by valid consideration and not impose undue hardship

**Non-solicits:** Nevada law does not specifically address non-solicitation agreements; non-competes guidance (disfavoring restrictive covenants) may apply by analogy; verify enforceability.

**Overtime accrual and final pay:** Overtime compensation must be calculated at the rate in effect during the period worked; final pay must include all earned overtime at the applicable overtime rate.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State

**Independent contractor presumption (NRS 608.0155):** A person is presumed to be an independent contractor if:

1. The person possesses or has applied for an employer identification number (EIN), social security number, or filed a business/self-employment tax return with the IRS in the prior year, AND
2. The person holds any necessary state or local business license, occupational license, insurance, or bonding, AND
3. The person satisfies THREE OR MORE of the following:
   - Control: Worker controls the means and manner of performance; result (not means) is the primary element bargained for
   - Discretion: Worker has discretion in scheduling and methods
   - Investment: Worker contributes substantial capital (tools, equipment, materials)
   - Profit/loss: Worker's income derives from profit/loss of work
   - Availability: Worker offers services to the general public or multiple clients

The burden of proof is on the employer to establish contractor status. A written contract is necessary but insufficient. Note: Misclassification as a contractor when the worker is actually an employee can expose the employer to wage-and-hour claims, unemployment-insurance disputes, and statutory penalties under NRS 608 provisions.

**Remote worker / multi-state employment:** If a Nevada employee works remotely in another state, Nevada wage and hour laws apply to the primary employment relationship unless the other state has stricter standards (e.g., higher minimum wage). Payroll records and final pay must comply with Nevada rules. Multi-state withholding and unemployment-insurance rules apply (verify specifics with Nevada Department of Employment, Training and Rehabilitation). Verify which state's employment law governs if the employee's primary residence and work location are in different states.

**Out-of-state employer hiring Nevada worker:** If the employer lacks Nevada presence but hires a Nevada resident, Nevada wage, hour, non-compete, and anti-discrimination laws apply to that employee.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

**P1A (Promotion):**

- If the promotion involves a pay change, verify the new pay does not rely on prior salary history (NRS 613.133 prohibition).
- After the interview, notify the promotee of the wage or salary range for the new position (NRS 613.133 requirement).
- Model wage-rate history recording: capture promotion date, previous and new rates, and position for 2-year retention (NRS 608.115).
- If the promotion is to a manager role or changes the job title, verify whether it triggers pay transparency notice or posting.
- Non-compete enforceability: if the promoted employee is hourly, non-competes are void (NRS 613.195); if salary, non-competes are generally unenforceable unless sale-of-business exception applies.
- Paid leave (NRS 608.0197): promotion does not reset accrual; earned leave carries forward.
- Verify whether promotion is accompanied by a pay increase (triggering wage-rate-history requirements) or lateral title change (simpler record).

**P1B (Base-Pay Change):**

- **Wage-history prohibition:** Never use the employee's prior salary (from any employer or role) to set the new pay rate (NRS 613.133).
- **Decrease notice:** Any pay decrease requires written notice at least 7 days before the employee works at the lower rate (NRS 608.100). Failure to provide notice makes the decrease unenforceable; employee remains entitled to prior rate or penalties apply.
- **Increase notice:** No notice period required for increases; document the new rate in writing before the effective date.
- **Wage-rate-history recording:** Capture the pay-change date, previous rate, new rate, and reason (if any) for 2-year retention as a payroll record (NRS 608.115).
- **Semimonthly pay schedule:** The new rate takes effect on the next regular payday (15th or last day of month) unless employer establishes an earlier custom payday.
- **Equal pay compliance:** Verify the new rate does not create unlawful pay disparity based on sex or other protected class (NRS 613.330 general anti-discrimination rule).
- **Paid leave accrual:** Base-pay change does not affect paid-leave accrual rate (0.01923 hours/hour worked for 50+ employee employers); only hours worked matter.
- **Overtime threshold:** If the new rate pushes the employee above or below $18.00/hour, overtime calculation method changes (daily 8-hour threshold vs. weekly 40-hour only).
- **Termination scenario:** If the employee is terminated shortly after a pay increase, final pay must include any earned wages at the new rate plus accrued paid leave (if applicable, calculated using the most recent rate).

**P1A/P1B with pregnancy accommodation:** If a promoted or base-pay-changed employee is pregnant or has a pregnancy-related condition, verify whether the role change triggers re-evaluation of reasonable accommodations under NRS 613.4353. Document any accommodation requests and interactive-process discussions in payroll/personnel files. If termination occurs during pregnancy, ensure final pay includes all accrued leave and verify no retaliation claim arises under PWFA.

**Voting and military leave during onboarding/offboarding:** Ensure payroll systems capture voting leave taken (must be paid; no wage deduction). For National Guard members, document leave taken for training/duty and verify no adverse employment action is triggered; reinstatement to same/comparable role is mandatory upon return.

**Open questions:**

- Verify the current scope of "wage range" disclosure requirement in NRS 613.133: must postings include the range, or only interviews?
- Clarify whether lateral manager reassignment (no pay change) requires wage-range notification under NRS 613.133.
- Confirm whether paid-leave accrual resumes immediately after an unpaid leave (e.g., unpaid FMLA or personal leave).
- Verify whether overtime compensation is calculated on gross pay or base pay if bonuses or commissions are in place.
- Confirm the penalties for wage-rate-decrease notice violation (whether the decrease is void entirely or just the period before notice).
- Verify whether implied oral contracts (e.g., a verbal promise of raise) are enforceable under Nevada law.
- Verify whether Nevada SB 2 (2025 session) amended non-compete enforceability; current law (2021 amendments) already prohibits hourly employee non-competes and allows blue-pencil revision.
