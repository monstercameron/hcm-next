# Alaska Employment Law: State Requirements for Workforce Changes

**Researched:** 2026-09-03  
**File status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Minimum wage:** $14.00/hour as of 2026-07-01; increases to $15.00/hour 2027-07-01. No tipped credit allowed; server minimum = regular minimum.
- **Overtime:** 1.5x for hours > 40/week or > 8/day; applies to all employers regardless of size, with narrow occupational exemptions (agricultural, seamen, healthcare, etc.). No exemption for businesses under 4 employees.
- **Paid sick leave:** Mandatory 1 hour per 30 hours worked; annual cap 40 hours (< 15 employees) or 56 hours (≥ 15 employees); accrual began 2025-07-01; carryover required each year; not paid out on termination unless employer has a voluntary policy.
- **At-will employment:** Default rule; no state notice requirement unless contract, handbook, or implied agreement says otherwise; Alaska courts recognize public-policy exceptions and implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing.
- **Final pay:** All wages due within 3 working days of employer-initiated termination; within 3 days of the next regular payday if employee-initiated (minimum 3 days after notice received).
- **Pay frequency:** Semi-monthly minimum or more frequent; employers must notify employees in writing of pay day, place, and rate at hire and before any change.
- **Background checks:** No state ban-the-box law (except Anchorage municipal law); employers may ask about criminal history at any stage; no state limit on how old convictions can be reported.
- **Non-competes:** No state statute; Alaska courts will enforce if deemed "reasonable" and necessary to protect legitimate business interests; reasonableness assessed case-by-case on duration, geography, and nature of employee role.
- **Remote work:** Alaska has no state income tax, simplifying remote-worker payroll; employers must still handle federal withholding, Social Security, Medicare, and Alaska unemployment insurance (note: Alaska requires employer SUTA withholding from employee wages).
- **Pay transparency:** No state law mandating salary-range disclosure in job postings or prohibiting salary-history inquiries; only federal Equal Pay Act and Title VII discrimination rules apply.

## 2. Employment Relationship

**At-will employment:** Alaska follows the presumption that employment is at-will, meaning either party can terminate for any lawful reason without cause or advance notice (Alaska Statutes § 23.10.100–23.10.110). Exceptions include:

- **Public policy exception:** Wrongful discharge claims if termination violates public policy (e.g., retaliation for workers' compensation claims, jury service, or reporting legal violations).
- **Implied contract:** Company handbook, written policy, or verbal assurances suggesting job security or termination-for-cause-only language may create an implied contract limiting the at-will presumption.
- **Implied covenant:** Alaska courts recognize an implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing in all contracts, including employment.

**Discrimination and retaliation:** Protected classes include race, color, national origin, religion, sex, age, disability, marital status, changes in marital status, pregnancy or parenthood, and veteran status (Alaska Human Rights Act; Alaska Commission for Human Rights administers). Employers cannot retaliate for opposing discriminatory practices or for exercising statutory rights (workers' comp, jury duty, whistleblower protections).

**Notice at hire:** Employers must provide all employees, in writing, at the time of hire:

- Day and place of pay
- Rate of pay (Alaska Statute § 23.05.140)

**Notice of pay or role changes:** Employers must provide written notice of any change to pay day, place, or rate before the next pay period is processed (AS § 23.05.140).

No state statute requires advance notice to employees before a termination; the federal WARN Act (60 days) applies only to employers with 100+ employees experiencing a mass layoff or plant closing.

## 3. Wages

**Minimum wage:** As of 2026-07-01, $14.00/hour (Alaska Statute § 23.10.065(b)). Annual adjustments for inflation begin 2028-01-01, using the Anchorage CPI-U. School bus drivers must receive 2× minimum wage.

**No tip credit:** Employers may not credit tips or gratuities toward the minimum wage obligation (AS § 23.10.065(d)); differs from federal FLSA.

**Overtime:** Compensation at 1.5× regular rate for hours exceeding 40 per week or 8 per day (AS § 23.10.070(b)). Applies to all employers. Narrow exemptions include agricultural workers, seamen, employees of businesses with fewer than 4 employees, and certain occupational categories (verify specific exemptions in AS § 23.10.070 and Alaska Administrative Code § 8 AAC 15.320).

**Pay frequency:** Employers must pay employees at least semi-monthly (AS § 23.05.140); more frequent payment is permitted. Upon hire and before any change, employers must provide written notice of payday, place, and rate.

**Final pay on termination:** If the employer terminates, all wages due immediately and payable within 3 working days (weekends, holidays, and termination day excluded). If the employee terminates, wages due at the next regular payday at least 3 days after the employer receives notice of resignation (AS § 23.05.140(f)). Failure to timely pay triggers a penalty of regular wages from demand until paid, capped at 90 working days (AS § 23.05.140(g)).

**Deductions:** No state statute explicitly itemizes permissible deductions. Federal standards apply; unlawful deductions that reduce wages below minimum wage are prohibited. (Verify specific deductions allowable under Alaska law.)

**Pay statement content:** Employers must provide written or electronic pay stubs showing:

- Straight-time and overtime hours worked
- Rate of pay
- Gross wages
- Tax deductions and other authorized deductions
- Beginning and ending dates of the pay period
- Current sick-leave balance (as of 2025-07-01) (8 AAC 15.160(h))

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary-range disclosure:** No state requirement to disclose salary ranges in job postings or on request. Alaska employers are not prohibited from inquiring about or relying on salary history.

**Salary history ban:** No state statute; federal law only.

**Equal pay:** Alaska Human Rights Act (AS § 18.80.200 et seq.) prohibits discrimination on the basis of sex in wages. Federal Equal Pay Act (29 U.S.C. § 206(d)) and Title VII of the Civil Rights Act (42 U.S.C. § 2000e-2(h)) apply. Alaska recognizes no additional pay-equity or pay-transparency obligations beyond federal law; no state mandate for pay-data reporting or audit. (Verify if proposed Alaska Salary Transparency Act has been enacted since this research date.)

**Protected classes:** Race, color, national origin, religion, sex, age (40+), disability, marital status, pregnancy or parenthood, veteran status. Employers with 2+ employees are subject to harassment protections; 15+ employees subject to discrimination protections under Alaska law.

## 5. Leave and Time

**Paid sick leave (mandatory):** Effective 2025-07-01, all employees accrue 1 hour per 30 hours worked, capped annually at:

- 40 hours for employers with < 15 FTE employees
- 56 hours for employers with ≥ 15 FTE employees

Exempt employees are presumed to work 40 hours/week for accrual purposes. All unused leave carries over to the next year unless the employer front-loads the full annual allotment upfront. Permitted uses: injury, illness, family care, and legal services related to domestic violence, sexual assault, or stalking (Alaska Statute § 23.10.135).

**Final pay on termination:** Employers are not required to pay out accrued sick leave on termination unless the employer has a voluntary cash-out policy.

**Reinstatement after rehire:** If an employee is rehired within 6 months of termination and did not accept a voluntary cash-out, their previous sick-leave balance must be reinstated (verify if there are additional conditions).

**Paid family and medical leave:** No state mandate; federal FMLA applies if employer has 50+ employees within 75 miles. No state sick-leave requirement for family or medical leave beyond the mandatory paid sick leave accrual.

**Other mandated leave:** No state statute for jury duty, voting, or military service leave; federal rules apply where applicable.

## 6. Records and Access

**Payroll record retention:** Employers must keep accurate records for at least 3 years, including name, address, occupation, daily/weekly hours worked, and wages paid each pay period (Alaska Statute § 23.05.080).

**Employee access to personnel files:** Employees and former employees may inspect and copy personnel files under reasonable rules during regular business hours (AS § 23.10.430). Employers may charge the reasonable cost of duplication (verify if there is a statutory fee cap).

**Record format:** No state mandate; electronic records are acceptable (verify if there are specific format or retention requirements for electronic records).

**Sick-leave record requirements:** Employers must track sick-leave accrual and usage and provide the current balance on each pay stub (as of 2025-07-01).

## 7. Privacy and Data

**State privacy law:** Alaska does not have a comprehensive state consumer data privacy law. Constitutional privacy rights under Article I Section 22 of the Alaska Constitution provide some protection.

**Biometric data:** Alaska Statutes § 18.13.200–270 contain targeted rules for biometric identifiers (verify scope and compliance requirements). Biometric data is NOT covered by the general data breach notification statute but has separate statutory protections.

**Data breach notification:** Any entity collecting personal information must notify affected individuals "in the most expeditious time possible" of a breach. Notification by first-class mail or email (if agreed); timeline guidance suggests within 60 days of discovery. Entities with 10 or fewer employees may be exempt; if 1,000+ Alaska residents affected, must notify consumer reporting agencies (AS § 45.48.010–900, verify specific timeline language).

**Employer data security:** No state statute specifically mandates employer monitoring restrictions or employee data security protocols; federal standards (e.g., FCRA for background checks, HIPAA for health data) apply.

**Recordkeeping confidentiality:** Employee records maintained by state agencies (e.g., personnel actions, performance reviews, disciplinary records) are confidential and require written employee authorization for release (verify scope of confidentiality for private employers).

## 8. Hiring and Background

**Ban-the-box:** No state law. Exception: Anchorage Municipal Code § 5.20 prohibits city employers and contractors from inquiring about criminal convictions before a conditional job offer.

**Background checks:** No state limit on the types of information screened or how old criminal convictions can be reported (Alaska allows reporting of non-convictions for 7 years; convictions indefinitely unless expunged).

**Drug testing:** No state statute regulating or restricting drug testing in private employment; federal FCRA standards apply to third-party screening; pre-employment testing is generally permissible.

**E-Verify:** No state mandate. Alaska allows participation in the federal E-Verify system; not required except for federal contractors and certain industries.

**Conviction consideration:** No state statute limiting consideration of criminal history; courts may apply public-policy principles in discrimination claims.

## 9. Separation

**WARN Act:** Federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act applies (60-day notice for employers with 100+ employees; mass layoff = 50+ or 500+ employees depending on threshold). Alaska has no state mini-WARN. Violations incur liability for back pay and benefits up to 60 days per affected employee.

**Severance:** No state statute requiring severance; voluntary severance is a contractual matter.

**Notice requirements:** No state statute mandating advance notice before termination for at-will employees. Exception: contracts or handbooks may impose notice obligations.

**Non-compete agreements:** Alaska has no statute regulating non-compete agreements. Courts will enforce if deemed reasonable in duration, geography, and scope and necessary to protect legitimate business interests (e.g., trade secrets, confidential information, customer relationships). Reasonableness is assessed case-by-case; courts may "blue pencil" (modify) overbroad terms to make them enforceable.

**Non-solicitation agreements:** Enforceable if reasonable. Court analysis similar to non-competes.

**Implied covenant of good faith:** Alaska courts recognize an implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing; discharge in bad faith or for a malicious motive may expose the employer to tort liability (verify applicability to non-compete context).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State

**Contractor classification test:** Alaska Statute § 23.20.525(a)(8)(A–C) applies the ABC test for unemployment-insurance and workers'-compensation purposes. All three prongs must be satisfied to classify a worker as an independent contractor:

- **(A)** Worker is free from control in performing the work
- **(B)** Work is outside the usual course of the hiring entity's business
- **(C)** Worker is engaged in an independently established trade or business

Failure to satisfy all three prongs results in employee classification (presumption of employment by default).

**Remote work:** Alaska imposes no state income tax, eliminating state withholding for remote work. Employers must withhold federal income tax, Social Security, Medicare, and Alaska unemployment insurance. Unique to Alaska: employers must withhold SUTA taxes from employee wages and remit them to the state (unlike most states where only employers contribute).

**Multi-state compliance:** Employers with Alaska-based workers must comply with Alaska wage and hour law (including minimum wage, overtime, sick leave, pay frequency, final pay). Employers in other states with Alaska remote workers may need to register for Alaska unemployment insurance.

**Reciprocity:** No state reciprocity agreements for unemployment or payroll-tax purposes; multi-state employers must register separately in each state.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

**Promotions and base-pay changes:**

- **Written notice requirement:** Any change in pay rate, payday, or place of pay must be communicated in writing before the next pay period. HCM Next must support a pre-promotion notification workflow and log the date and method of delivery.
- **Sick-leave accrual continuity:** Promotion or role change does not reset sick-leave accrual; accrual continues at 1 hour per 30 hours worked. Platform must track and display current sick-leave balance at all times.
- **Overtime reclassification:** If a promotion or role change affects overtime exemption status (e.g., shifting to executive/administrative exempt role), the platform must validate exemption criteria and alert to the minimum-salary threshold ($1,120/week as of 2026-07-01 for exempt employees).
- **Pay frequency verification:** If a role change alters pay frequency, the platform must enforce the requirement that final notice goes out before the next pay period.

**Terminations:**

- **Final-pay timer:** Platform must flag terminations and enforce the 3-working-day deadline for employer-initiated terminations. Calculate deadline excluding weekends and holidays.
- **Sick-leave payout:** Prompt user that sick leave is not automatically paid out on termination unless the employer has a cash-out policy; if policy exists, calculate and include in final pay.
- **Pay-statement accuracy:** Final pay statement must include all deductions, tax withholdings, and sick-leave balance as of termination date (per 8 AAC 15.160(h)).
- **WARN Act flag:** If the termination is part of a mass layoff or plant closing affecting 50+ employees, alert user to 60-day federal notice requirement (for employers with 100+ employees).

**Compensation and equity:**

- **No salary-history restriction:** Alaska places no restriction on inquiring about or relying on prior salary in setting pay; platform need not enforce salary-history ban (unlike Colorado, California, etc.).
- **Equal-pay audit:** No state mandate for pay-data reporting or equity audits; federal Equal Pay Act and Title VII audits apply if employer conducts them.
