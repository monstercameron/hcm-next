# Rhode Island Employment Law Research

**State:** Rhode Island | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **Employment-at-will with limited exceptions**: Rhode Island recognizes at-will employment but prohibits termination for filing wage claims, workers' compensation claims, jury duty, military service, or exercising constitutional rights; § 28-50-1 (Whistleblowers' Protection Act) protects disclosure of legal violations.
- **Pay frequency**: Weekly (default); employers may petition to pay biweekly/semi-monthly under § 28-14-2 with surety bond if average payroll meets thresholds; advance written notice of any change required.
- **Minimum wage $16.00/hour (2026)** effective January 1, per § 28-12-3; increases to $17.00 in 2027 (annual CPI-adjusted). Tipped employees must receive $16.00 minimum; tip credit not permitted.
- **Pay Equity Act (§ 28-6-17 et seq., effective January 1, 2023)**: All employers must disclose wage ranges at hire, promotion, and upon employee request; salary history inquiry and reliance banned; wage-range disclosure violations trigger $10,000 special damages + attorney fees per § 28-6-19.
- **Paid sick and safe leave (§ 28-57-1 et seq.)**: Employers with 18+ employees must provide 1 hour per 35 hours worked (40 hours/year max); job-protected for health, care, domestic violence, school events; 150-day waiting period before use.
- **Temporary Disability and Caregiver Insurance (§ 28-41-34)**: State-funded; 7 weeks (through 2025) or 8 weeks (effective January 1, 2026) wage replacement to bond with child or care for family; base period $19,200+ minimum (2026).
- **Parental and Family Medical Leave (§ 28-48-1 et seq.)**: 13 workweeks in 2 years for employers with 50+ employees; 12-month tenure + 30 hours/week; may be unpaid after required paid portion; advance 30-day notice; health benefits maintained.
- **Sunday and holiday premium pay (§ 25-3-3)**: 1.5× for work on Sundays/designated holidays; retail employers exclude Sunday hours from weekly overtime calculation; non-retail employers apply both premiums independently (pyramiding).
- **Non-competes (§ 28-59, Rhode Island Noncompetition Agreement Act)**: Void for employees earning <$37,650/year or non-exempt workers; common-law enforceability otherwise; income threshold updated 2024.
- **Ban-the-box (§ 28-5-7(7))**: Cannot inquire about criminal record prior to first interview; applies to 4+ employee employers; exceptions for law-enforcement positions or criminal background legally required.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Rhode Island recognizes employment-at-will: employers and employees may terminate without cause absent contractual limitation or statutory/public-policy exception.

**Exceptions to at-will termination** (§ 28-50-2):

- Discharge or discrimination for filing wage-theft, workers' compensation, or unemployment benefit claims.
- Discharge for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religion) or performing jury duty, voting, or military service.
- Discharge in violation of public policy (refusing illegal act, whistleblowing, participating in agency investigation of illegal activity).
- Retaliation for disclosing immigration status to ICE or law enforcement (§ 28-50-4).
- Discharge violating express written employment contract or implied contract created by detailed handbook, course of dealing, or oral promises of job security.

**Required notices at hire and on change**:

- § 28-14-2 requires written notice at hire of wages, pay rate, and frequency; advance written notice in advance of any change to wage, deduction, or pay frequency.
- Failure to provide notice triggers Wage Theft Act penalties (verify whether 2019 wage-theft statute applies; statute of limitations and damages not found in results).
- No specific statutory offer-of-employment form, but written documentation recommended for defensibility.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (R.I. Gen. Laws § 28-12-3):

- $16.00/hour effective January 1, 2026.
- $17.00/hour effective January 1, 2027 (subject to annual CPI adjustment).
- Tipped employees: no tip credit; employers must pay full minimum wage of $16.00/hour (2026).
- No subminimum for learners, students, or other categories.

**Overtime** (§ 28-12-4.1; § 25-3-3):

- Time-and-a-half (1.5×) for hours over 40/week (federal FLSA rule applies).
- Sunday and holiday premium (1.5×) under § 25-3-3 for all work on Sundays and designated holidays.
- For retail businesses: Sunday/holiday hours are carved out of weekly overtime calculation (no pyramiding). Non-retail: both premium and overtime apply independently, resulting in combined premium (verify exact calculation).
- Exemptions: agricultural employees, professional/executive/administrative employees under federal FLSA test.

**Pay frequency and timing** (§ 28-14-2):

- Default: at least weekly on a regular designated payday.
- Employers whose average payroll exceeds 200% of state minimum wage may petition director for permission to pay biweekly/semi-monthly with surety bond and proof; must designate payday in advance and maintain consistency.
- If payday falls on non-work day, payment due on preceding work day.
- Advance written notice required for any change to frequency.

**Final pay on termination** (§ 28-14-4):

- All wages due must be paid by the next regular payday following termination (whether voluntary or involuntary discharge, layoff, or suspension).
- Covers all wages earned up to date of termination.
- Accrued unused sick leave: employer must pay out accrued balance at termination unless written policy limiting payout provided at hire.

**Permitted deductions** (§ 28-14-3):

- Only deductions required by law (tax, garnishment, court order) are permitted without employee consent.
- Deductions for uniforms, tools, or employer costs require written authorization and must not reduce wage below minimum.
- Wage Theft Act (verify statute) criminalizes unlawful deductions; violations trigger civil remedies.

**Pay statement content** (§ 28-14-2.1):

- Employers must provide statement showing hours worked during pay period.
- Record of all deductions with explanation of basis or reason for each deduction.
- For commercial construction industry employers: hourly regular rate of pay required.
- May be electronic if employee consents; employer must provide printed copy at no cost if employee requests.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage range disclosure** (§ 28-6-19, effective January 1, 2023):

- All employers must disclose wage range (minimum and maximum) at hire, upon promotion or internal transfer, and upon employee written request.
- "Wage range" defined as lowest and highest salaries or wages paid for the position.
- Applies to all employers (no employee threshold).
- Disclosure required before discussing compensation with external candidates and internally promoted employees.
- Violations trigger employee complaint to Rhode Island Department of Labor and Training; penalties $1,000–$5,000 depending on prior violations.

**Salary history ban** (§ 28-6-19):

- Employers cannot inquire about prior wage history, require disclosure, or rely on salary history when considering job applicants.
- Exceptions: prior wage history voluntarily disclosed by applicant to support a wage higher than initially offered (employer may confirm).
- Retaliation (refusal to hire, interview, or employ for non-disclosure) prohibited.
- Violations: compensatory damages, $10,000 special damages, equitable relief, attorney fees.

**Equal pay statute** (Pay Equity Act, § 28-6-17 et seq.):

- Unlawful to pay employees at lower rates than other employees performing substantially similar work (composite of skill, effort, responsibility).
- Protected classes: all classes under R.I. Fair Employment Practices Act (race, color, national origin, creed, sex, age, marital status, disability, religious creed, genetic information, and others); 2023 amendments broadened coverage.
- Defenses: seniority system, merit system, system measuring productivity, factor other than sex (bona fide).
- Safe harbor through June 30, 2026: employer may review pay practices internally and remedy discrepancies within 2 years before lawsuit filed, limiting damages.
- Enforcement: Rhode Island Commission for Human Rights; treble damages possible.

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state pay-equity audit requirement; federal EEO-1 filing applies to employers with 100+ employees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick/Safe Leave, Family Leave, Caregiver Insurance, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Earned Sick and Safe Leave** (R.I. Gen. Laws § 28-57-1 et seq., effective July 1, 2018):

- Applies to employers with 18+ employees (unpaid for <18 employee employers).
- Accrual: 1 hour per 35 hours worked, minimum 40 hours per calendar year.
- Permitted uses: employee's own physical/mental health; care for family member; domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking; child school conference; workplace/school closure due to public health emergency; quarantine during public health emergency.
- Job-protected; employer may not retaliate.
- 150-day waiting period before use (accrual begins on day 1).
- Employer may require certification if employee uses >3 consecutive days.
- No carryover cap specified (verify whether unused hours must carry forward to next year or reset).

**Family Leave Insurance (FLI) and Temporary Disability Insurance (TDI)** (§ 28-41-34 et seq.):

- State-funded separate from employer sick leave; employee and employer contributions via payroll.
- FLI: wage replacement (partial) to bond with newborn/adoptive/foster child or care for seriously ill family member.
- TDI: wage replacement for employee's own serious illness/injury.
- Benefit duration: 7 weeks through December 31, 2025; 8 weeks effective January 1, 2026.
- Minimum weekly benefit (2026): $148; maximum: $1,103; dependents allowance 7% of weekly benefit ($20 minimum per child <18 or disabled 18+, up to 5 dependents).
- Eligibility (as of January 1, 2026): prior 3-month employment; base-period earnings $19,200+; 250+ hours worked.
- Application: within 30 days of leave commencement.

**Parental and Family Medical Leave Act (§ 28-48-1 et seq.)**:

- Applies to employers with 50+ employees; 13 consecutive workweeks of leave in any 2 calendar years.
- Employee eligibility: 12 consecutive months employment + average 30 hours/week.
- Permissible leave: parental leave (birth, adoption, foster placement), family medical leave (serious health condition of employee or family member).
- Job-protected; employer must maintain health benefits during leave.
- Leave may be unpaid; if employer provides paid leave <13 weeks, additional weeks unpaid.
- Advance notice: 30 days unless medical emergency prevents.
- Employer may require medical certification.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- On promotion during earned sick leave, employee generally carries forward leave balance and returns to promoted role (verify wage rate upon return: promoted or original).
- Earned leave accrual likely continues during unpaid family leave, but not confirmed (verify).
- Final pay on termination during leave: all accrued sick leave paid out unless written policy limiting compensation provided at hire.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention** (§ 28-14-19; § 28-6.4-1):

- Payroll records: retain 3 years (hours worked, wage rate, gross/net pay, deductions) per § 28-14-19.
- Earned sick leave records: retain for duration of employment plus 1 year post-termination.
- Personnel files: employer not statutorily required to retain after termination (verify common-law document duty).
- Medical records: retain separately; best practice 3 years post-termination for ADA/privacy compliance.

**Employee access to personnel files** (§ 28-6.4-1):

- Employees may inspect and copy personnel files upon 7 days' written notice (excluding weekends/holidays), at reasonable time outside work hours, in employer's presence.
- Access limited to 3 occasions per calendar year.
- Employer may charge reasonable copying fee.
- Exceptions: records of criminal investigations, civil/grievance/criminal proceeding records, letters of reference, managerial planning records, confidential reports from prior employers.
- Employer may not permit removal of file from business premises.

**Record format**:

- Electronic and paper formats acceptable; no statutory mandate for format.
- Payroll records should document hours, rate, deductions, gross/net for audit trail and wage-theft defense.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Breach Notification, Biometric/Monitoring, Social Media

**Data privacy (Rhode Island Data Transparency and Privacy Protection Act, effective January 1, 2026)**:

- Comprehensive privacy law applying to businesses collecting personal information from Rhode Island residents.
- Employees may not be explicitly exempted in statute (verify coverage of employee data).
- Requires data protection assessments for heightened-risk processing (targeted advertising, sale of personal data, certain profiling, sensitive data).
- No private right of action; enforcement by Rhode Island Attorney General; penalties up to $10,000/violation; no cure period.
- Applies to payroll, medical, benefit records stored by employers.

**Data breach notification** (R.I. Gen. Laws § 11-49.3-4):

- Notification required in most expedient time possible, no later than 45 calendar days after confirmation of breach and ability to ascertain required information.
- (Private entities; state/municipal agencies: 30 days maximum.)
- Applies to unauthorized disclosure of personal information (name + SSN, credit card, financial account, driver's license).
- Notification to: Rhode Island Attorney General if 500+ residents affected; major credit reporting agencies.
- Must include timing, content, and distribution of notices and approximate number affected.

**Social media and personal accounts** (R.I. Gen. Laws § 28-56 [Employee Social Media Privacy]):

- Employers cannot compel disclosure of personal social media usernames, passwords, or access to personal accounts.
- Employers cannot require employees to add employer to contacts or alter privacy settings affecting third-party visibility.
- Exceptions: work-related accounts (employer email, data storage) may be required; employer access to personal accounts permitted if reasonably necessary to investigate workplace misconduct or legal violation.
- Violations: termination or refusal to hire prohibited; enforcement by Rhode Island Commission for Human Rights.

**Workplace monitoring**:

- No state statute banning email/internet monitoring of work devices or workplace video monitoring; reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies.

**Data residency**:

- No Rhode Island statute mandates employee data residency; employers may store in-state or out-of-state if security and privacy laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box** (Opportunity to Compete Act, § 28-5-7(7), effective January 1, 2014):

- Employers with 4+ employees cannot inquire about applicant criminal record (verbal or written) during initial employment application process.
- Exceptions: law enforcement, corrections, judiciary, homeland security roles; positions requiring criminal background check by law; arrests/convictions legally preclude position.
- Criminal history may be discussed at or after first interview.
- Violations: $300 per applicant per violation for repeated offenses; enforcement by Rhode Island Commission for Human Rights.

**Background checks**:

- No state statute restricting private-sector background checks; federal FCRA applies.
- Employers using third-party consumer reporting agencies must provide FCRA notice, dispute rights, adverse-action procedures.

**Drug testing** (§ 28-6.5-1(a)(1)):

- Employer may require drug test only if "reasonable grounds to believe based on specific aspects of job performance and specific contemporaneous documented observations concerning appearance, behavior or speech that employee may be under influence of controlled substance impairing ability to perform job."
- No routine pre-employment or random drug testing permitted; reasonable suspicion/grounds required.
- Observations must be contemporaneous with performance issues (not historical).
- Rhode Island Supreme Court (_Colpitts v. W.B. Mason Co., Inc._, 2020) held that reasonable grounds need not constitute certainty (odd behavior, failure to report injury, incoherence sufficient).

**E-Verify mandate**:

- Rhode Island does not mandate E-Verify for private employers (voluntary E-Verify ended in 2011).
- Federal I-9 requirements apply to all employers; must complete Form I-9 within 3 business days of hire; retain 3 years post-hire or 1 year post-termination (whichever later).

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice (Federal WARN Act applies; no Rhode Island mini-WARN)**:

- Federal WARN Act (29 U.S.C. § 2101 et seq.) applies: 60 days' notice for employers with 100+ employees.
- Triggering events: facility closure/operating unit discontinuance with 50+ workers; layoff of 50–499 workers ≥33% of site workforce; layoff of 500+ workers.
- Notification to: affected employees, union representatives, state Department of Labor and Training, local elected officials.
- Rhode Island adds: employer must provide affected employees/representatives written notice of right to bid to purchase business and information on forming workers' cooperative (§ 28-58-4).
- No separate state WARN law or shorter notice period (federal 60-day requirement applies).

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance except under federal WARN Act; generally contractual.

**Non-compete enforceability** (Rhode Island Noncompetition Agreement Act, § 28-59):

- Void and unenforceable for employees earning <$37,650/year annually (2024 threshold; verify 2026 inflation adjustment).
- Non-exempt (hourly) employees: non-competes generally void.
- For eligible (exempt, ≥$37,650) employees: common-law enforceability applies; must be reasonable in duration, geographic scope, and not impose undue hardship on employee or injury to public.
- Courts apply "blue penciling" (judicial modification of overly broad clauses).
- Typical enforceability: 6 months to 2 years depending on nature of business and role.
- Pending legislation (verify status): broader ban proposals; healthcare providers (advanced practice registered nurses) already banned from non-competes (verify current scope).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; common-law enforceability standard applies (reasonableness in duration, scope, geographic area).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- No Rhode Island-specific ABC test in statute results (verify whether common-law control test applies).
- Federal FLSA classifications apply; federal DOL guidance on employee vs. contractor status controls for federal law purposes.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine. If employee works across state lines, each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Platform should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable state rules.

**Remote workers**:

- No Rhode Island statute specifically addressing remote-work classification.
- Standard employment law applies regardless of work location; if employee works in Rhode Island (even remotely) for any employer, Rhode Island law applies to that work.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling: Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

**Mandatory obligations and field restrictions**:

1. **Pay Transparency Disclosure** (§ 28-6-19): Before posting any job or offering promotion, require wage-range input (min/max); block job posting or promotion posting if range empty; timestamp disclosure. On internal promotion, ensure disclosure before compensation discussion with promoted employee.

2. **Pay Rate Change Notice** (§ 28-14-2): On any pay increase or decrease, generate automated notice in advance of effective date (or concurrent with increase) with new rate, frequency, and effective date; document and retain for audit.

3. **Salary History Inquiry Block** (§ 28-6-19): Disable any form field attempting to request prior salary from external candidates; log queries for compliance risk flagging; only permit external candidate to voluntarily disclose.

4. **Minimum Wage Compliance** (§ 28-12-3): System should flag if any promotion + pay change results in rate <$16.00/hour (2026) and prevent entry unless override justified (verify).

5. **Pay Equity Audit Flag** (§ 28-6-17 et seq.): On promotion + base-pay change, flag if new rate differs significantly from peer rates for "substantially similar work" in same employer/county; require manager justification or HR/legal approval to override; maintain audit trail for potential defense under safe-harbor provision (through June 30, 2026).

6. **Earned Sick Leave Accrual and Payout** (§ 28-57-1): Track accrued sick leave (1 hour per 35 hours worked). On termination, automatic payout of accrued balance. On promotion within same employer, leave balance carries forward. Do not permit termination record to be finalized until sick-leave payout calculated and documented.

7. **Sunday/Holiday Premium and Overtime Calculation** (§ 25-3-3): If promoted employee works retail, system should apply retail overtime rule (Sunday hours carved out); if non-retail, apply pyramiding (both Sunday premium and overtime). Document business classification (retail vs. non-retail) in employee record.

8. **Final Pay Timing** (§ 28-14-4): On termination, schedule final paycheck by next regular payday; include all wages due through termination date plus accrued leave payout.

9. **Ban-the-Box Compliance** (§ 28-5-7(7)): On initial application, do not ask about criminal record; if position requires criminal record inquiry by law, defer until after first interview. Log interview date and timing for audit compliance.

10. **Non-Compete Enforceability Check** (§ 28-59): At hire and promotion of exempt employees, flag if salary <$37,650/year and non-compete in offer; system should warn that agreement likely unenforceable and recommend removal or legal review.

11. **Pay Statement Generation** (§ 28-14-2.1): Ensure all pay statements/earnings records show hours worked, rate, all deductions with explanation, and gross/net; retain for 3 years for wage-theft audit defense.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether earned sick leave accrual continues during unpaid family leave under § 28-41 or § 28-48.
- Confirm whether employee returning from family leave to same or promoted role receives promoted salary or original salary upon return.
- Verify exact calculation of "substantially similar work" for equal-pay comparisons (geographic limits, job-title similarities, composite factors).
- Clarify whether non-compete $37,650 threshold adjusts annually for inflation; if so, what is 2026 threshold.
- Verify status of pending bills proposing broader non-compete bans or narrowing healthcare exceptions.
- Confirm whether pay-transparency posting requirement (§ 28-6-19) applies to all postings (job board, internal, email, print, social media) or limited categories.
- Verify whether Wage Theft Act penalties and statute of limitations apply to wage-payment violations (§ 28-14 deductions, misclassification).
