# Maine Employment Law Research

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with exceptions**: Maine recognizes wrongful termination for public-policy violations (wage claims, workers' comp claims, jury duty, OSHA complaints, union activity, refusal to violate law).
- **Pay frequency and final pay (26 M.R.S. § 621-A, § 626)**: Wages paid at least weekly; final paycheck due on next regular payday unless employee resigns without notice or is discharged (verify timing for discharge final pay).
- **Vacation and paid leave payouts (26 M.R.S. § 626)**: Accrued paid leave and vacation must be paid on termination; 2023 amendment clarifies payout timing. Earned paid leave (EPL) accrues per § 637.
- **Paid Family and Medical Leave (26 M.R.S. ch. 7, subch. 6-A)**: Mandatory program with employer/employee contributions starting 2025-01-01; employee benefits available from 2026-05-01; 12 weeks paid leave for birth, adoption, family care, serious health condition, military qualifying exigency, bereavement.
- **Earned Paid Leave (26 M.R.S. § 637)**: Employees earn 1 hour per 30 hours worked (or 3%, whichever is greater); minimum 40 hours per year for employers with 5+ employees; can be used for any reason.
- **Salary history ban and equal pay (26 M.R.S. § 628-A)**: Employers cannot inquire about or rely on prior wage history; equal pay law prohibits sex-based wage discrimination; Maine allows pay data for internal pay equity analysis.
- **Non-compete restrictions (26 M.R.S. § 599-A, § 599-B)**: Non-competes enforceable only if employee earns at least 3x minimum wage (verify current threshold); maximum 2-year term; reasonable scope and duration required; notice of non-compete to employee must be given.
- **Restrictive covenants (26 M.R.S. § 599-B)**: Non-solicitation and non-disclosure agreements permitted if reasonable; must be provided in writing before or contemporaneously with employment.
- **Records retention and access (26 M.R.S. § 630-A, § 631)**: Payroll records retained 6 years; personnel files accessible to employee; employer may charge reasonable fee for copies; employee entitled to notice of right to review file.
- **Severance for plant closings (26 M.R.S. § 635)**: Employers with 100+ employees at a worksite must provide 90 days' notice and severance equal to 1 week's pay per year of service (up to 26 weeks) if closing/relocating operations; federal WARN Act also applies.
- **Minimum wage and overtime (26 M.R.S. § 664, § 665)**: State minimum wage indexed to federal minimum; overtime 1.5x for hours over 40/week (no daily overtime trigger); tip credit available for tipped employees (verify current rates and tip credit amount).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Maine is an employment-at-will jurisdiction: either party may terminate without cause unless an exception applies (26 M.R.S. § 626-A; common law).

**Statutory exceptions to at-will employment** (26 M.R.S. § 626-B):

- Termination for filing a workers' compensation claim or pursuing workers' compensation benefits.
- Termination for filing a wage or hour claim or unemployment insurance claim.
- Termination for jury duty, voting, or military service.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religious freedom).
- Termination for refusing to commit or participate in illegal act.
- Termination for reporting safety violations, environmental violations, or public health violations to a government agency (whistleblowing).
- Termination for pursuing union activity or labor organizing (verify exact statutory language).

**Written notice requirements at hire and on change**:

- At hiring, employer must provide written notice of: rate of remuneration, hours of work, payday schedule, how wages will be paid, and a summary of leave policies (26 M.R.S. § 621-A).
- On any change to pay rate or job classification, employer should provide written notice of new terms; no specific statutory notice period for pay changes except final pay timing requirement.
- If employer requires wage decrease, 7-day advance written notice is required (verify this timing exactly in statute).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage and indexing**:

- Maine minimum wage is linked to federal minimum wage; if federal minimum is raised, Maine's rises to match or to 1.1x federal, whichever is higher (26 M.R.S. § 664).
- As of 2025, verify the current minimum wage rate on Maine Department of Labor website (index tied to federal).
- No subminimum wage for minors or other classes; tip credit available for tipped employees (currently verify amount, typically lower than minimum wage).
- Tipped employees must be paid at least the tip-credit wage; employer is liable if tips do not bring employee to state minimum wage.

**Overtime**:

- Employees must be paid 1.5x regular rate for all hours worked over 40 per week (26 M.R.S. § 665).
- No daily overtime trigger (Maine does not require daily overtime for hours over 8 per day).
- Exempt categories include: agricultural workers, executives, professionals, outside salespeople, certain domestic workers (verify full exemption list in § 664).

**Pay frequency**:

- Wages must be paid at least weekly on a designated payday (26 M.R.S. § 621-A).
- Payment via cash, check, or direct deposit (with written employee authorization).
- Employer may switch payroll frequency if notice is given (verify timing of notice).

**Final pay on termination**:

- **On discharge or layoff**: Full wages earned to date plus accrued and unused paid leave must be paid by the next regular payday or within 7 business days (verify exact timeline; 2023 amendment may have updated this) (26 M.R.S. § 626).
- **On resignation without notice**: Employer may delay final pay to next regular payday.
- **Vacation and EPL payout**: All accrued paid leave, vacation, and earned paid leave must be included in final paycheck.
- Employer cannot forfeit accrued leave on termination.

**Permitted deductions**:

- Employers may deduct only taxes, garnishments, and court-ordered support (26 M.R.S. § 623).
- Employers cannot require employees to pay for uniform, tools, or other job-related equipment.
- No deductions for uniform cleaning or equipment damage (unless employee grossly negligent; verify this exception).

**Pay statement requirements**:

- Employers must maintain payroll records for 6 years documenting employee name, address, hours worked, rates, wages, and deductions (26 M.R.S. § 630-A).
- Itemized pay stubs must be provided at each payday showing gross pay, deductions, and net pay (26 M.R.S. § 631).
- Electronic pay stubs permitted if employee has access and can print/retain them.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**:

- Maine has no statewide salary-range-in-posting requirement (unlike Connecticut or California); federal guidelines apply to federal contractors.
- Verify if any recent Maine legislation on salary transparency effective 2025-2026.
- Employers may choose to post ranges for competitive recruiting; no legal mandate currently.

**Salary history ban** (26 M.R.S. § 628-A):

- Employers cannot inquire about a prospective employee's wage or salary history, including from references or third parties.
- Exception: Employee may voluntarily disclose prior wage history without employer initiation.
- Applies to private, public, and quasi-public employers.
- Violation may result in penalty (verify amount and statute language for penalties).

**Equal pay and protected classes** (26 M.R.S. § 628):

- **Equal pay statute**: Employers cannot pay employees at different rates based on sex for equal work in same establishment. Defense: seniority, merit-based systems, production-quality pay, or other bona fide occupational qualification.
- **Protected classes** (5 M.R.S. § 4571, Maine Human Rights Act): Employment discrimination prohibited based on race, color, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, religion, familial status, mental or physical disability, or genetic information.
- No state pay-equity reporting requirement for private employers; federal EEO-1 applies if applicable.

**Pay data collection and use**:

- Employers may collect internal pay data for equity analysis and may use aggregated data to adjust pay without disclosing to employees (verify if Maine has narrative-inquiry requirement).
- Employees are generally protected from retaliation if they disclose wages to coworkers (implied under § 626-B public policy exception; verify statutory language).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Earned Paid Leave (EPL) (26 M.R.S. § 637)**:

- All employees working in Maine must earn paid leave at 1 hour per 30 hours worked (or 3% of hours, whichever is greater).
- Minimum accrual: 40 hours per year for employers with 5 or more employees.
- Employers with fewer than 5 employees are exempt (verify this threshold).
- EPL may be used for any reason: illness, family care, prevention, safe time (domestic violence/sexual assault/stalking), or any other reason employee chooses.
- Accrual begins on first day of employment; employees may use accrued leave after 90 days of employment.
- Employers may cap annual carry-over at 40 hours (verify cap rules; some states allow 80 hours).
- Payout on termination: All accrued, unused EPL must be paid.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFMLA) (26 M.R.S. ch. 7, subch. 6-A)**:

- **Mandatory program**: As of January 1, 2025, Maine employers must participate in state PFMLA (contributions begin then).
- **Employee benefits**: Available from May 1, 2026 (verify exact implementation timeline). 12 weeks of paid leave in a 12-month period.
- **Qualifying reasons**: Birth or adoption of child; placement of foster child; care for spouse, parent, or child with serious health condition; employee's own serious health condition; military qualifying exigency or military caregiver leave; bereavement leave for death of family member (verify exact list).
- **Contribution structure**: Employer contributes a percentage of payroll; employee contributes a percentage (verify exact employer/employee split; typically 50/50 or similar).
- **Wage replacement**: Typically 66% of average weekly wage, capped at state average weekly wage (verify exact replacement rate).
- **Eligibility**: Employees employed by employer with 1+ employee in Maine for 12+ months; verify if continuous employment is required.
- **Job restoration**: Employee must be restored to same or equivalent position upon return.

**Interaction with pay and role changes**:

- If employee takes PFMLA or EPL leave during a pay-change or promotion period, the platform must:
  - Preserve employee's accrued leave balance (no forfeiture on return).
  - Calculate final pay to include all accrued leave if termination occurs during leave.
  - If promotion occurs during leave, salary or role change applies upon return from leave (verify case law).
  - EPL accrual continues during unpaid PFMLA leave (if unpaid portion exists; verify).
  - Ensure leave-usage tracking does not conflict with new pay rate or job classification.

**Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) leave**:

- Federal ADA applies; Maine has no separate state disability leave requirement beyond ADA.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- **Payroll records**: Must be retained for 6 years, documenting employee name, address, job classification, rate of pay, hours worked (by day and week), deductions, and gross and net wages (26 M.R.S. § 630-A).
- **Timekeeping records**: If employer uses time clocks or timesheets, records must be retained for 6 years.
- **Personnel files**: Must be retained during employment and for a reasonable period after termination; no maximum retention period specified in statute (verify if industry or case law has established standard).
- **Medical records and leave records**: If employer maintains medical records related to leave or disability accommodations, they must be kept separate from general personnel files and retained per applicable law (verify Maine-specific medical record retention requirement).

**Employee access to personnel files** (26 M.R.S. § 631):

- Employees have the right to inspect and copy their personnel records within a reasonable time after written request.
- Access must be provided at or near the employee's worksite during regular business hours.
- Employee may obtain copies; employer may charge a reasonable copying fee (not to exceed actual cost).
- Employees entitled to know their right to access their file; employer should provide notice at hiring or upon written request.
- **Limit on access frequency**: Employees may access their file once per year unless they have a specific reason to access more frequently; employer may require reasonable notice (verify exact frequency restriction in statute).

**Disputed information**:

- If employee disputes accuracy of information in the personnel file, employee may request correction or removal.
- If not agreed, employer must allow employee to submit written rebuttal/statement, which must be maintained in file and provided to any third party who receives information from the file.

**Record format**:

- Maine has no specific statute mandating electronic vs. paper format for personnel or payroll records.
- Employers may maintain records electronically if retention periods and access rights are maintained.
- Electronic records must be accurately retained and retrievable (verify if Maine has e-records authentication requirement).

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Maine Privacy Law (10 M.R.S. § 1346, Maine Uniform Information Practices Act)**:

- Applies to custodians (individuals/entities holding records on behalf of persons); not a comprehensive consumer privacy law like CCPA.
- **Employee records**: Generally covered if held by public sector; private-sector employee data coverage is limited (verify exact applicability to private employers).
- Individuals have right to know if their personal information is held and right of access (verify if this applies to private-sector employee records).

**Biometric and monitoring data**:

- No Maine state law specifically restricting private-sector biometric collection (fingerprints, facial recognition, voice prints).
- **Electronic monitoring of employees**: No specific Maine statute banning workplace email, internet, or video monitoring of work areas.
- **Workplace privacy expectation**: Common law privacy claims may apply if monitoring is in areas of high privacy expectation (restrooms, locker rooms, changing areas); employers should avoid monitoring in these areas.
- **GPS and location tracking**: No specific Maine statute restricting employer use of GPS or location-tracking devices for work vehicles or equipment (verify if any recent legislation).

**Breach notification law** (10 M.R.S. § 1346(A)):

- Entities holding personal information must notify individuals if there is unauthorized access, use, or disclosure of personal information, "without unreasonable delay."
- **Personal information** includes name with social security number, financial account number, or other identifier.
- **Employee records**: Verify if employee personal information (if containing SSN or financial data) triggers breach notification requirement.
- Notification must include description of information involved, steps individual should take to protect themselves, and contact information for the entity.

**Data residency and processing**:

- No Maine state law mandates data residency for employee records within Maine or United States.
- Employers may process and store employee data on servers located in-state or out-of-state, provided federal privacy laws (if any) and data security practices are met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Criminal history inquiry (ban-the-box)**:

- No comprehensive Maine statewide ban-the-box law for private employers.
- **State employment**: Maine Public Law Chapter 304 restricts state agency inquiries about criminal history; state agencies must comply with "ban-the-box" principles.
- **Private sector**: No statewide restriction; federal EEOC guidance on criminal history applies.
- Some municipalities (verify which Maine cities) have adopted ban-the-box ordinances; employers in those jurisdictions must comply locally.

**Background checks**:

- No Maine statute restricting private employers from conducting background checks on job applicants or employees.
- Federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) applies to third-party consumer reporting agencies; employers must provide notice, opportunity to dispute, and adverse-action notice if background check results in adverse employment decision.
- Employers should conduct background checks consistently and consider job-relatedness.

**Credit history checks**:

- No Maine statute restricting employer use of credit history or credit reports in hiring or employment decisions (verify if recent legislation enacted).

**Drug testing**:

- No state law regulating private-sector drug testing; federal law applies.
- Federal Department of Transportation (DOT) rules apply to transportation industry.
- Employers may conduct pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, or post-accident drug testing (verify if any Maine state restrictions on reasonable-suspicion testing).
- Employees with positive results should be provided opportunity to explain results.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No Maine state mandate for private-sector E-Verify use; federal I-9 and E-Verify requirements apply to all employers.
- Verify if any Maine federal contractor compliance requirement.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Plant closing and mass layoff notice and severance** (26 M.R.S. § 635):

- **Trigger**: Employers with 100 or more employees at a single worksite closing or relocating operations must provide:
  - Minimum 90 days' advance written notice to affected employees.
  - Severance equal to 1 week's pay per year of service (up to 26 weeks maximum).
- **Calculation**: Severance based on employee's regular hourly rate or salary at time of notice.
- **Notice content**: Must include reason for closure/relocation, expected date, severance calculation, last day of work, benefits continuation.
- **Federal WARN Act**: Also applies to employers with 100+ employees; 60 days' notice for mass layoff (50+ employees) or plant closing; federal notice supersedes state if more protective.
- **Smaller employers** (fewer than 100): No state severance requirement; federal WARN Act applies if thresholds met.

**Notice and opportunity to cure before termination**:

- Maine is at-will; no notice period required before termination except for cause (if any).
- Employers may terminate without notice or opportunity to cure unless:
  - Termination violates public policy (§ 626-B exceptions), in which case retaliation may be actionable.
  - Employment contract provides for notice or cause.
  - Implied contract theory (verify if Maine recognizes implied employment contracts).

**Non-compete agreements enforceability** (26 M.R.S. § 599-A):

- Non-competes are enforceable if:
  - Employee earns at least 3 times the state minimum wage (verify current threshold; as of 2025, approximately $22/hour x 3 = $66/hour equivalent or annual salary threshold).
  - Agreement is in writing and provided before or contemporaneously with employment.
  - Agreement is reasonable in duration (typically 2 years or less enforced; verify exact judicial standard), geographic scope, and scope of prohibited activity.
  - Employer has legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential information, customer relationships, substantial relationships with clients/customers).
- **Non-compete duration**: Maximum enforced period is typically 2 years; longer periods generally unenforceable (verify exact Maine judicial standard).
- **Blue-pencil doctrine**: Maine courts may modify overly broad non-competes to make them enforceable (verify if Maine applies this doctrine).

**Non-solicitation agreements** (26 M.R.S. § 599-B):

- Non-solicitation (restriction on soliciting employees or customers) agreements are enforceable if:
  - In writing and provided before or contemporaneously with employment.
  - Reasonable in duration (typically 2 years or less), geographic scope, and scope of persons/entities prohibited from soliciting.
  - Employer has legitimate business interest.
- Typically easier to enforce than non-competes because they restrict less conduct.

**Non-disclosure agreements**:

- Non-disclosure (confidentiality) agreements are enforceable if they protect legitimate trade secrets or confidential information (26 M.R.S. § 599-B).
- Must be reasonable in scope and duration.

**Notice of restrictive covenants**:

- Employers must provide employee with written copy of any non-compete, non-solicitation, or non-disclosure agreement before or contemporaneously with employment (verify "contemporaneously" does not mean employee must sign before first day of work; typically means at or before start of employment).
- Post-employment non-competes (signed after hire) are generally disfavored unless supported by new consideration (e.g., promotion, raise, or continued employment if at-will) (verify Maine's position on post-employment covenants).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Maine applies the **economic realities test** to determine contractor vs. employee status for purposes of wage-and-hour law and workers' compensation (26 M.R.S. § 602; Workers' Compensation Act).
- Key factors (all must weigh toward employee status or contractor status):
  - **Control**: Degree of control exercised by hiring entity over manner and means of work.
  - **Investments**: Whether contractor has invested in tools, equipment, or business infrastructure.
  - **Profit/loss**: Whether contractor has opportunity to profit or risk of loss.
  - **Permanence**: Whether relationship is ongoing or project-based.
  - **Integral to business**: Whether work is integral to hiring entity's business.
- Misclassification as contractor when employee status is appropriate can result in wage-and-hour violations and workers' compensation liability.
- Verify if Maine has adopted ABC test for any purposes or maintains economic realities test across all employment classifications.

**Remote workers**:

- No Maine statute specifically addressing remote work classification; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee works remotely from Maine for out-of-state employer, Maine law applies to work performed (or for employee based in Maine).
- Employers should track where work is performed to ensure compliance with applicable state laws (multistate nexus issue).

**Multi-state employment**:

- If employee works across multiple states (e.g., part-time in Maine, part-time in New Hampshire), the state in which work is performed governs that portion of employment.
- **Choice of law**: Maine contracts may specify choice of Maine law; absent agreement, state where work is performed typically applies.
- Platform should flag multistate workers and apply compliance rules for each state separately (e.g., different minimum wage, overtime, leave rules).
- **Payroll complexity**: Multistate workers require separate payroll tracking per state, separate final pay calculations, and separate leave accrual if states differ on leave policies.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

When a promotion and base-pay change are processed through HCM Next for a Maine-based employee:

**Required typed obligations and notices**:

1. **Pay change timing and notice**:
   - Platform should allow minimum 7-day advance notice before pay decrease (if applicable; verify if this applies to all pay changes or only decreases).
   - Calculate effective date of new pay rate; ensure it is reflected in next paycheck.
   - Generate confirmation showing old rate, new rate, reason, and effective date.

2. **Salary history prohibition**:
   - Block any form field or workflow step that asks for candidate's prior wage history.
   - If platform is reviewing compensation for equity purposes, flag salary history if entered and require user to confirm they did not rely on it in making compensation decision.

3. **Earned Paid Leave (EPL) interaction**:
   - Maintain EPL accrual during pay-change period (no forfeiture or reset).
   - On termination following pay change, ensure accrued EPL is calculated and paid based on all hours worked (include hours at both old and new rates if change occurred mid-period).
   - If employee is on EPL leave during promotion, ensure leave balance is preserved and new pay rate applies to any new leave earned after return.

4. **Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFMLA) interaction** (effective 2026-05-01):
   - If employee is on PFMLA leave during promotion, track leave usage in separate account.
   - On return from PFMLA, apply new pay rate to any future work; do not reset leave balance.
   - If employee is promoted and then takes PFMLA, ensure salary increase applies to leave-benefit calculation (wage-replacement rate may be based on average weekly wage; verify if increase affects benefit amount).

5. **Final pay calculation (if termination follows pay change)**:
   - Calculate final pay using rate in effect at time of termination (not prior rate).
   - Include all accrued EPL, vacation, and other paid leave earned during the employment period (use proportional rates if employee worked under both old and new rates in final period).
   - If termination is discharge, pay must be due by next regular payday (or within 7 business days; verify exact timing).

6. **Non-compete and restrictive covenant validation**:
   - If promotion triggers re-signing of non-compete or non-solicitation, ensure:
     - Employee earns at least 3x minimum wage (approximately $66,000 annually based on verify current threshold).
     - Agreement is in writing.
     - Agreement provided before or contemporaneously with promotion/new employment terms.
     - Agreement is reasonable in duration (2 years or less), geography, and scope.
   - Store date of signing, date agreement was reviewed, and confirmation that employee had opportunity to seek legal advice.
   - Flag any non-competes signed post-employment (after hire but without promotion) and flag as potentially unenforceable unless accompanied by new consideration.

7. **Equal pay verification**:
   - On pay-change entry, platform should check: Are there other employees in same job classification with same tenure/performance receiving different pay based on sex or protected class?
   - Flag for review if pay disparity exists; allow employer to document business reason (seniority, merit, production, bona fide occupational qualification).
   - Maintain records showing reasoning for pay decisions for potential equal-pay audit or litigation.

8. **Records retention and documentation**:
   - Ensure all pay-change records are retained for 6 years (payroll records), including:
     - Change request with date, approval authority, old rate, new rate, effective date.
     - Reason for change (promotion, merit increase, market adjustment, etc.).
     - Any supporting documentation (job description change, performance review, market analysis).
   - Ensure employee receives pay statement showing new rate in next paycheck.

9. **Field restrictions and validations**:
   - **Wage/salary field**: Allow entry of new rate; validate against minimum wage requirement (verify current minimum wage, approximately $14.15 + as of 2025; provide error if below minimum).
   - **Pay frequency field**: Restrict to at least weekly payroll (no less frequent than weekly).
   - **Effective date**: Validate that pay change is not retroactive unless employer explicitly authorizes (and employee agrees).
   - **Promotion field**: If classification changes (e.g., from hourly to salaried), flag overtime-exempt status review; validate that new classification is accurate.
   - **Leave balance field**: Do not allow manual override of EPL accrual calculation; accrual is statutory and cannot be waived.
   - **Non-compete field**: Validate salary threshold; do not allow non-compete for employees earning less than 3x minimum wage.

10. **Notifications to generate**:
    - **To employee**: Written notice of new pay rate, effective date, impact on paycheck, impact on leave accrual (if any), impact on benefits (PFMLA, retirement, etc.).
    - **To payroll**: Change order with old rate, new rate, employee ID, effective date, reason, and final-pay instructions if termination follows.
    - **To compliance**: Flag any equal-pay review needed; flag any non-compete re-signing; flag any multi-state pay-change needs (if employee works across states).

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify exact timing for final pay after discharge (is it "next regular payday" or "within 7 business days," or both—whichever is sooner?).
- Clarify whether pay-decrease notice (7 days) applies to all pay reductions or only specific circumstances.
- Confirm whether "contemporaneously with employment" for non-compete signing means at or before first day of work, or within a reasonable period after hire.
- Verify current minimum wage threshold for non-compete enforceability (is it 3x the state minimum wage, or a fixed dollar amount, or index-adjusted?).
- Confirm whether post-employment non-competes (signed after hire, without promotion or other new consideration) are enforceable in Maine case law.
- Clarify EPL cap rules: Can employers cap carryover at 40 hours per year, and what happens to excess? Is payout mandatory even if capped?
- Verify PFMLA wage-replacement calculation: Is it 66% of average weekly wage? What is the cap amount as of 2026?
- Confirm whether multistate employees' leave accrual must be tracked per state or can be unified under Maine's more generous EPL rule.
- Verify whether implied employment contracts are recognized under Maine law and what patterns trigger them (e.g., detailed handbook, promises of job security, course of dealing).
