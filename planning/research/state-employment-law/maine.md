# Maine Employment Law Research

**State:** Maine | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **At-will employment with exceptions**: Maine recognizes wrongful termination for public-policy violations (wage claims, workers' comp claims, jury duty, OSHA complaints, union activity, refusal to violate law).
- **Pay frequency and final pay (26 M.R.S. § 621-A, § 626)**: Wages paid at least weekly; final paycheck due on next regular payday unless employee resigns without notice or is discharged (verify timing for discharge final pay).
- **Vacation and paid leave payouts (26 M.R.S. § 626)**: Accrued paid leave and vacation must be paid on termination; 2023 amendment clarifies payout timing. Earned paid leave (EPL) accrues per § 637.
- **Paid Family and Medical Leave (26 M.R.S. § 850-A et seq.)**: Mandatory program with contributions split equally (1% total, 0.5% employer/0.5% employee) beginning 2025-01-01; employee benefits available from 2026-05-01; 12 weeks paid leave within 12-month period for birth, adoption, family care, serious health condition, military qualifying exigency, bereavement; wage replacement 90% of average weekly wage up to 50% of state average weekly wage (SAWW), 66% of wages above that threshold.
- **Earned Paid Leave (26 M.R.S. § 637)**: Employees earn 1 hour per 40 hours worked (or equivalent accrual method); minimum 40 hours per year accrual for employers with 10+ employees; can be used for any reason (illness, family care, safe time, prevention); carryover capped at 40 hours per year; payout required on termination for all employers.
- **Salary history ban and equal pay (26 M.R.S. § 628-A)**: Employers cannot inquire about or rely on prior wage history; equal pay law prohibits sex-based wage discrimination; Maine allows pay data for internal pay equity analysis.
- **Non-compete restrictions (26 M.R.S. § 599-A, § 599-B)**: Non-competes enforceable only if employee earns at least 400% of federal poverty level (approximately $62,920 annually for single filer in 2026) OR negotiated individually; employees earning at or below threshold cannot be required to sign non-competes; must provide notice 3 business days before signing; 1-year duration after start of employment or 6 months after signing, whichever is longer; maximum 2-year term; reasonable scope and duration required.
- **Restrictive covenants (26 M.R.S. § 599-B)**: Non-solicitation and non-disclosure agreements permitted if reasonable; must be provided in writing before or contemporaneously with employment.
- **Records retention and access (26 M.R.S. § 630-A, § 631)**: Payroll records retained 6 years; personnel files accessible to employee; employer may charge reasonable fee for copies; employee entitled to notice of right to review file.
- **Severance for plant closings (26 M.R.S. § 635)**: Employers with 100+ employees at a worksite must provide 90 days' notice and severance equal to 1 week's pay per year of service (up to 26 weeks) if closing/relocating operations; federal WARN Act also applies.
- **Minimum wage and overtime (26 M.R.S. § 664, § 665)**: State minimum wage $14.65/hour in 2025, $15.10/hour in 2026 (indexed to federal minimum + 1.1x or federal, whichever higher); tip credit $7.33/hour in 2025 (half of minimum wage); overtime 1.5x for hours over 40/week (no daily overtime trigger); salaried exemption threshold $43,951/year in 2025 (3,000x minimum wage).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Maine is an employment-at-will jurisdiction: either party may terminate without cause unless an exception applies (26 M.R.S. § 626-A; common law).

**Statutory exceptions to at-will employment** (26 M.R.S. § 626-B):

- Termination for filing a workers' compensation claim or pursuing workers' compensation benefits.
- Termination for filing a wage or hour claim or unemployment insurance claim.
- Termination for jury duty, voting, or military service.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religious freedom).
- Termination for refusing to commit or participate in illegal act.
- Termination for reporting safety violations, environmental violations, or public health violations to a government agency (whistleblowing).
- Termination for pursuing union activity or labor organizing (verify exact statutory language).

**Written notice requirements at hire and on change**:

- At hiring, employer must provide written notice of: rate of remuneration, hours of work, payday schedule, how wages will be paid, and a summary of leave policies (26 M.R.S. § 621-A).
- On any change to pay rate or job classification, employer should provide written notice of new terms; no specific statutory notice period for pay changes except final pay timing requirement.
- If employer requires wage decrease, 7-day advance written notice is required (verify this timing exactly in statute).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage and indexing** (26 M.R.S. § 664):

- Maine minimum wage is $14.65/hour in 2025 and $15.10/hour in 2026 (indexed annually; rate increases tied to federal minimum wage or 1.1x federal whichever is higher).
- Tip credit for tipped employees: $7.33/hour in 2025 (exactly half of the minimum wage); employer is liable to make up difference if tips plus tip-credit wage do not equal full minimum wage.
- No subminimum wage for minors or other employee classes; fair labor standards apply equally.
- Tipped employees must receive written notice of tip-credit amount and employer's obligation to ensure total compensation (tips + wage) meets or exceeds minimum wage.

**Overtime and exemptions** (26 M.R.S. § 664, § 665):

- Employees must be paid 1.5x regular rate for all hours worked over 40 per week.
- No daily overtime trigger (Maine does not require daily overtime for hours over 8 per day).
- **Salaried exemption**: Employee earning at least $43,951 per year (3,000x minimum wage as of 2025) in an executive, professional, or administrative capacity is exempt from overtime.
- **Other exempt categories**: Agricultural workers, outside salespeople, certain domestic workers, and commissioned salespeople.
- **Overtime calculation**: Base rate includes hourly wage or proportional salary; excludes bonuses, benefits, and certain allowances.

**Pay frequency** (26 M.R.S. § 621-A):

- Wages must be paid at least every 16 days on a designated payday (26 M.R.S. § 621-A).
- Payment via cash, check, or direct deposit (with written employee authorization).
- **Pay decrease notice**: One full pay period advance written notice required before reducing wage rate (26 M.R.S. § 623).
- Employer may change payday schedule with reasonable notice to employees.

**Final pay on termination** (26 M.R.S. § 626):

- **On discharge**: Full wages earned through termination date plus all accrued and unused paid leave (vacation, EPL) must be paid on the next regular payday or within 2 weeks of termination demand, whichever is sooner.
- **On resignation**: If employee provides notice, final pay due on next regular payday; if resignation is without notice, final pay may be delayed to next regular payday (not to exceed one pay period).
- **Accrued vacation payout**: All accrued vacation must be paid on termination for employers with 10+ employees (mandatory since 2023 amendment to § 626); smaller employers comply per contract/policy.
- **Earned Paid Leave (EPL)**: All accrued, unused EPL must be paid out at employee's regular hourly rate or proportional salary.
- **Forfeiture prohibited**: Employer cannot forfeit or "use-it-or-lose-it" accrued paid leave; all earned leave must be paid.

**Permitted deductions** (26 M.R.S. § 623, § 625-B):

- Employers may deduct only: federal, state, and local taxes; garnishments; court-ordered child support, spousal support, or debt collection; and authorized employee deductions (health insurance, retirement, etc.).
- **Prohibited deductions**: Employers cannot require employees to pay for uniforms, tools, equipment, or supplies necessary for work.
- **Equipment damage**: Employers cannot deduct for damage to equipment or property unless damage results from employee's gross negligence or willful misconduct (verify exact standard in case law).
- **Cash shortage**: Employers cannot deduct cash register shortages or customer non-payment from employee wages.
- **Deduction notice**: Employer must provide written notice of any permitted deduction before wages are withheld.

**Pay statement requirements** (26 M.R.S. § 630-A, § 631):

- **Payroll records**: Employers must maintain payroll records for 6 years documenting employee name, address, job classification, hours worked (by day and week), rates, wage calculation, deductions, gross pay, and net pay.
- **Pay statement delivery**: Itemized pay stubs must be provided at each payday (or electronically if employee consents) showing: gross pay, hourly rate or salary, hours worked (if hourly), all deductions (with amounts and reasons), net pay, and YTD totals.
- **Electronic pay stubs**: Permitted if employee has ongoing access to view, print, and retain them; employer must provide backup paper copies upon request at no cost.
- **Final pay statement**: Must clearly itemize all final wages including accrued leave payout, unused vacation, EPL, and any severance due.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**:

- Maine has no statewide salary-range-in-posting requirement (unlike Connecticut or California); federal guidelines apply to federal contractors.
- Verify if any recent Maine legislation on salary transparency effective 2025-2026.
- Employers may choose to post ranges for competitive recruiting; no legal mandate currently.

**Salary history ban** (26 M.R.S. § 628-A):

- Employers cannot inquire about a prospective employee's wage or salary history, including from references or third parties.
- Exception: Employee may voluntarily disclose prior wage history without employer initiation.
- Applies to private, public, and quasi-public employers.
- Violation may result in penalty (verify amount and statute language for penalties).

**Equal pay and protected classes** (26 M.R.S. § 628; 5 M.R.S. § 4571-A):

- **Equal pay statute** (§ 628): Employers cannot pay employees at different rates based on sex for equal work in same establishment. Defenses include: seniority, merit-based systems, production-quality pay, or other bona fide occupational qualification. Burden of proof on employer to show legitimate, non-discriminatory reason for pay disparity.
- **Protected classes** (5 M.R.S. § 4571, Maine Human Rights Act): All employers, regardless of size, prohibited from discriminating in hiring, promotion, pay, termination, or other terms/conditions of employment based on:
  - Race, color, or ethnic heritage.
  - Sex (including pregnancy, childbirth, reproductive health decisions).
  - Sexual orientation.
  - Gender identity or expression.
  - National origin.
  - Religion or creed.
  - Familial status (being pregnant or having children).
  - Mental or physical disability (includes perceived disability).
  - Genetic information or genetic testing.
  - Age (40 and over; federal ADEA also applies).
  - Veteran status or military service (verify exact coverage).
- **Pay transparency protection**: Employees have right to discuss wages with coworkers without retaliation (implied under § 626-B public policy exception).
- **Harassment and hostile work environment**: Employer liability for harassment based on protected class characteristics if employer knew or should have known and failed to take corrective action.
- **Retaliation protection**: Employees cannot be retaliated against for reporting discrimination, filing complaint with Maine Human Rights Commission (MHRC), or participating in investigation.
- **No state pay-equity reporting**: No mandatory pay-equity data reporting to state (federal EEO-1 applies to employers with 100+ employees).

**Pay data collection and use**:

- Employers may collect internal pay data for equity analysis and may use aggregated data to adjust pay without disclosing to employees (verify if Maine has narrative-inquiry requirement).
- Employees are generally protected from retaliation if they disclose wages to coworkers (implied under § 626-B public policy exception; verify statutory language).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Earned Paid Leave (EPL)** (26 M.R.S. § 637):

- **Accrual rate**: All employees working in Maine must earn paid leave at 1 hour per 40 hours worked (or 40 hours per year minimum, whichever accrual method employer chooses).
- **Minimum requirement**: Employers with 10 or more employees must provide minimum 40 hours of paid leave per year per employee.
- **Employer size exemption**: Employers with fewer than 10 employees may elect to provide leave or substitute with paid holidays/vacation equal to 40 hours per year.
- **Uses**: EPL may be used for any reason without employer restriction: illness, family care, prevention, safe time (domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking), bereavement, medical appointments, or employee's discretion.
- **Accrual timing**: Accrual begins on first day of employment; employees may use accrued leave after 90 days of employment.
- **Carryover cap**: Employers may cap annual carry-over at 40 hours; balance in excess of 40 hours carried over must be paid out (cannot be forfeited).
- **Payout on termination**: All accrued, unused EPL must be paid out at employee's regular rate of pay.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFMLA)** (26 M.R.S. § 850-A et seq.):

- **Mandatory program**: Effective January 1, 2025, all Maine employers must participate in state-administered PFMLA program; contributions begin then.
- **Contribution split**: 1% total payroll contribution split equally: 0.5% employer, 0.5% employee; contributions mandatory for all covered employees.
- **Employee benefits effective**: May 1, 2026; employees begin accessing 12 weeks of paid leave within a 12-month period.
- **Qualifying events**: Birth or adoption of child; placement of foster child; care for spouse, parent, or child with serious health condition; employee's own serious health condition; military qualifying exigency; military caregiver leave; bereavement leave for death of family member (within 1 year of death).
- **Wage replacement**: 90% of average weekly wage up to 50% of state average weekly wage (SAWW); 66% of wages exceeding 50% SAWW; calculated on 12-month average; benefits capped at maximum weekly amount set annually.
- **Eligibility**: Employees employed in Maine for 12 months (need not be continuous) by employer with any number of employees; prior 12 months wages must average at least the state average weekly wage (verify exact threshold).
- **Job restoration**: Employee must be restored to same or equivalent position with equivalent pay, benefits, and terms of employment upon return from leave.

**Interaction with pay and role changes**:

- If employee takes PFMLA or EPL leave during a pay-change or promotion period, the platform must:
  - Preserve employee's accrued leave balance (no forfeiture on return).
  - Calculate final pay to include all accrued leave if termination occurs during leave.
  - If promotion occurs during leave, salary or role change applies upon return from leave (verify case law).
  - EPL accrual continues during unpaid PFMLA leave (if unpaid portion exists; verify).
  - Ensure leave-usage tracking does not conflict with new pay rate or job classification.

**Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) leave**:

- Federal ADA applies; Maine has no separate state disability leave requirement beyond ADA.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- **Payroll records**: Must be retained for 6 years, documenting employee name, address, job classification, rate of pay, hours worked (by day and week), deductions, and gross and net wages (26 M.R.S. § 630-A).
- **Timekeeping records**: If employer uses time clocks or timesheets, records must be retained for 6 years.
- **Personnel files**: Must be retained during employment and for a reasonable period after termination; no maximum retention period specified in statute (verify if industry or case law has established standard).
- **Medical records and leave records**: If employer maintains medical records related to leave or disability accommodations, they must be kept separate from general personnel files and retained per applicable law (verify Maine-specific medical record retention requirement).

**Employee access to personnel files** (26 M.R.S. § 631):

- Employees have the right to inspect and copy their personnel records within 10 days of written request (26 M.R.S. § 631).
- Access must be provided at or near the employee's worksite during regular business hours; employee may bring representative to review file.
- Employee may obtain copies; employer may charge a reasonable copying fee (not to exceed actual cost of reproduction).
- **Notice requirement**: Employer must provide written notice of employee's right to access personnel file at time of hire and upon written request.
- **Access frequency**: Employees may access their file at least once per calendar year without cause; additional access permitted if employee has specific reason (e.g., dispute resolution, legal proceeding, wage/hour claim).
- **Employer may require notice**: Employer may require reasonable advance notice (typically 3-5 business days) before providing access to allow compilation.

**Disputed information**:

- If employee disputes accuracy of information in the personnel file, employee may request correction or removal.
- If not agreed, employer must allow employee to submit written rebuttal/statement, which must be maintained in file and provided to any third party who receives information from the file.

**Record format**:

- Maine has no specific statute mandating electronic vs. paper format for personnel or payroll records.
- Employers may maintain records electronically if retention periods and access rights are maintained.
- Electronic records must be accurately retained and retrievable (verify if Maine has e-records authentication requirement).

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Maine Privacy Law (10 M.R.S. § 1346, Maine Uniform Information Practices Act)**:

- Applies to custodians (individuals/entities holding records on behalf of persons); not a comprehensive consumer privacy law like CCPA.
- **Employee records**: Generally covered if held by public sector; private-sector employee data coverage is limited (verify exact applicability to private employers).
- Individuals have right to know if their personal information is held and right of access (verify if this applies to private-sector employee records).

**Biometric and monitoring data**:

- No Maine state law specifically restricting private-sector biometric collection (fingerprints, facial recognition, voice prints).
- **Electronic monitoring of employees**: No specific Maine statute banning workplace email, internet, or video monitoring of work areas.
- **Workplace privacy expectation**: Common law privacy claims may apply if monitoring is in areas of high privacy expectation (restrooms, locker rooms, changing areas); employers should avoid monitoring in these areas.
- **GPS and location tracking**: No specific Maine statute restricting employer use of GPS or location-tracking devices for work vehicles or equipment (verify if any recent legislation).

**Breach notification law** (10 M.R.S. § 1348):

- **Trigger**: Any business or organization holding personal information must notify individuals if there is unauthorized access, use, disclosure, modification, or destruction of personal information.
- **Timing**: Notification must be made "without unreasonable delay" and in no case later than 30 days after discovery of breach (enacted 2019 amendment to § 1348).
- **Personal information defined**: Name and social security number; name and financial account number (credit card, bank account, etc.); name and driver's license/passport number; name and health insurance information; or other identifier that compromises personal privacy.
- **Employee records**: Employer must notify employees if breach involves their personal information (SSN, financial data, health information, etc.).
- **Notification content**: Notice must include: description of information compromised, description of how breach occurred, date breach was discovered, steps individual should take to protect themselves, contact information for the entity, and information about credit monitoring or identity theft protection services offered (if available).
- **Method**: Notice may be given by email, phone, or written letter; if notice to large group, may be published in media serving affected area.
- **Attorney General notice**: Employer must also notify Maine Attorney General if breach affects 100+ Maine residents or involves social security numbers.

**Data residency and processing**:

- No Maine state law mandates data residency for employee records within Maine or United States.
- Employers may process and store employee data on servers located in-state or out-of-state, provided federal privacy laws (if any) and data security practices are met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Criminal history inquiry (ban-the-box)**:

- No comprehensive Maine statewide ban-the-box law for private employers.
- **State employment**: Maine Public Law Chapter 304 restricts state agency inquiries about criminal history; state agencies must comply with "ban-the-box" principles.
- **Private sector**: No statewide restriction; federal EEOC guidance on criminal history applies.
- Some municipalities (verify which Maine cities) have adopted ban-the-box ordinances; employers in those jurisdictions must comply locally.

**Background checks**:

- No Maine statute restricting private employers from conducting background checks on job applicants or employees.
- Federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) applies to third-party consumer reporting agencies; employers must provide notice, opportunity to dispute, and adverse-action notice if background check results in adverse employment decision.
- Employers should conduct background checks consistently and consider job-relatedness.

**Credit history checks**:

- No Maine statute restricting employer use of credit history or credit reports in hiring or employment decisions (verify if recent legislation enacted).

**Drug and substance testing** (26 M.R.S. § 683):

- **No blanket state ban**: Maine has no comprehensive state law regulating private-sector drug testing (federal law governs).
- **Statute § 683**: Maine law does not mandate drug testing but permits it under certain conditions if job-related and conducted consistently.
- **Pre-employment testing**: Employers may conduct pre-employment drug testing if: position is safety-sensitive or involves operation of vehicles/equipment, or employee is applying for job requiring DOT certification.
- **Reasonable-suspicion testing**: Permitted if employer has objective, articulable basis for suspicion based on observable behavior, appearance, or documented performance issues.
- **Post-accident testing**: Permitted if employee involved in workplace accident resulting in injury or property damage, conducted within reasonable time frame (typically 2-8 hours after incident).
- **Procedural requirements**: Employer must provide written notice of drug-testing policy before administering test; employee has right to explanation of results; employee may request retest of positive result at own expense or employer's expense (verify exact requirement).
- **Americans with Disabilities Act (ADA) compliance**: Drug testing results may not be used to disqualify otherwise qualified employees with substance-use disorder if employee is in recovery or completes treatment; medical information must be kept confidential.
- **Federal DOT rules**: Transportation industry subject to federal DOT testing requirements; state requirements do not preempt federal law.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No Maine state mandate for private-sector E-Verify use; federal I-9 and E-Verify requirements apply to all employers.
- Verify if any Maine federal contractor compliance requirement.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Plant closing and mass layoff notice and severance** (26 M.R.S. § 625-B):

- **Trigger**: Employers with 100 or more employees at a single worksite closing, relocating, or materially reducing operations must provide notice and severance.
- **Notice requirement**: Minimum 90 days' advance written notice to affected employees and 90 days' notice to State Department of Labor (which notifies state agencies and workforce services).
- **Severance calculation**: Severance equal to 1 week's pay per year of service for employees with 3 or more years tenure; employees with less than 3 years receive no severance. Maximum severance cap: 26 weeks (6 months) of pay.
- **Wage calculation**: Severance based on employee's regular hourly rate or weekly salary at time of notice.
- **Notice content**: Written notice must include: reason for closure/relocation, expected effective date, severance amount and calculation, last day of work, benefits continuation details, and information on unemployment insurance eligibility.
- **Accrued leave**: All accrued vacation, EPL, and paid leave must be paid in addition to severance (severance and leave payout are separate).
- **Federal WARN Act**: Also applies to employers with 100+ employees; 60 days' federal notice required for mass layoff (50+ employees at single site) or plant closing; federal notice is concurrent with state notice (both required).
- **Smaller employers** (fewer than 100): No state severance requirement for plant closing; federal WARN Act applies only if 100+ employees nationwide and 50+ at affected site.

**Notice and opportunity to cure before termination**:

- Maine is at-will; no notice period required before termination except for cause (if any).
- Employers may terminate without notice or opportunity to cure unless:
  - Termination violates public policy (§ 626-B exceptions), in which case retaliation may be actionable.
  - Employment contract provides for notice or cause.
  - Implied contract theory (verify if Maine recognizes implied employment contracts).

**Non-compete agreements enforceability** (26 M.R.S. § 599-A):

- **Salary threshold for enforceability**: Non-competes are enforceable only for employees earning more than 400% of the federal poverty level (approximately $62,920/year for single filer as of 2026, indexed annually).
- **Employees below threshold**: Employees earning at or below 400% FPL cannot be required to sign non-competes; any such agreement is unenforceable and void.
- **Agreement requirements**:
  - Must be in writing.
  - Employer must provide employee written notice at least 3 business days before employee is required to sign (or within 3 days of at-will employment beginning if non-compete presented at hiring).
  - Agreement must specify reasonable duration, geographic scope, and scope of prohibited competitive activity.
  - Employer must demonstrate legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential information, substantial customer relationships, client goodwill).
- **Duration limits**: Non-compete remains in effect for no less than 1 year following start of employment or 6 months after employee signs agreement, whichever is longer, and no more than 2 years from date of signing.
- **Blue-pencil modification**: Maine courts may modify overly broad non-competes to make them enforceable if modified language remains reasonable.
- **Remedies**: Employer may seek injunctive relief and damages for breach; employee may challenge enforceability based on vagueness, overbreadth, or failure to meet statutory requirements.

**Non-solicitation agreements** (26 M.R.S. § 599-B):

- Non-solicitation (restriction on soliciting employees or customers) agreements are enforceable if:
  - In writing and provided before or contemporaneously with employment.
  - Reasonable in duration (typically 2 years or less), geographic scope, and scope of persons/entities prohibited from soliciting.
  - Employer has legitimate business interest.
- Typically easier to enforce than non-competes because they restrict less conduct.

**Non-disclosure agreements**:

- Non-disclosure (confidentiality) agreements are enforceable if they protect legitimate trade secrets or confidential information (26 M.R.S. § 599-B).
- Must be reasonable in scope and duration.

**Notice of restrictive covenants**:

- Employers must provide employee with written copy of any non-compete, non-solicitation, or non-disclosure agreement before or contemporaneously with employment (verify "contemporaneously" does not mean employee must sign before first day of work; typically means at or before start of employment).
- Post-employment non-competes (signed after hire) are generally disfavored unless supported by new consideration (e.g., promotion, raise, or continued employment if at-will) (verify Maine's position on post-employment covenants).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Maine applies the **economic realities test** to determine contractor vs. employee status for purposes of wage-and-hour law and workers' compensation (26 M.R.S. § 602; Workers' Compensation Act).
- Key factors (all must weigh toward employee status or contractor status):
  - **Control**: Degree of control exercised by hiring entity over manner and means of work.
  - **Investments**: Whether contractor has invested in tools, equipment, or business infrastructure.
  - **Profit/loss**: Whether contractor has opportunity to profit or risk of loss.
  - **Permanence**: Whether relationship is ongoing or project-based.
  - **Integral to business**: Whether work is integral to hiring entity's business.
- Misclassification as contractor when employee status is appropriate can result in wage-and-hour violations and workers' compensation liability.
- Verify if Maine has adopted ABC test for any purposes or maintains economic realities test across all employment classifications.

**Remote workers**:

- No Maine statute specifically addressing remote work classification; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee works remotely from Maine for out-of-state employer, Maine law applies to work performed (or for employee based in Maine).
- Employers should track where work is performed to ensure compliance with applicable state laws (multistate nexus issue).

**Multi-state employment**:

- If employee works across multiple states (e.g., part-time in Maine, part-time in New Hampshire), the state in which work is performed governs that portion of employment.
- **Choice of law**: Maine contracts may specify choice of Maine law; absent agreement, state where work is performed typically applies.
- Platform should flag multistate workers and apply compliance rules for each state separately (e.g., different minimum wage, overtime, leave rules).
- **Payroll complexity**: Multistate workers require separate payroll tracking per state, separate final pay calculations, and separate leave accrual if states differ on leave policies.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

When a promotion and base-pay change are processed through Human Capital Management Suite for a Maine-based employee:

**Required typed obligations and notices**:

1. **Pay change timing and notice**:
   - Platform should allow minimum 7-day advance notice before pay decrease (if applicable; verify if this applies to all pay changes or only decreases).
   - Calculate effective date of new pay rate; ensure it is reflected in next paycheck.
   - Generate confirmation showing old rate, new rate, reason, and effective date.

2. **Salary history prohibition**:
   - Block any form field or workflow step that asks for candidate's prior wage history.
   - If platform is reviewing compensation for equity purposes, flag salary history if entered and require user to confirm they did not rely on it in making compensation decision.

3. **Earned Paid Leave (EPL) interaction**:
   - Maintain EPL accrual during pay-change period (no forfeiture or reset).
   - On termination following pay change, ensure accrued EPL is calculated and paid based on all hours worked (include hours at both old and new rates if change occurred mid-period).
   - If employee is on EPL leave during promotion, ensure leave balance is preserved and new pay rate applies to any new leave earned after return.

4. **Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFMLA) interaction** (effective 2026-05-01):
   - If employee is on PFMLA leave during promotion, track leave usage in separate account.
   - On return from PFMLA, apply new pay rate to any future work; do not reset leave balance.
   - If employee is promoted and then takes PFMLA, ensure salary increase applies to leave-benefit calculation (wage-replacement rate may be based on average weekly wage; verify if increase affects benefit amount).

5. **Final pay calculation (if termination follows pay change)**:
   - Calculate final pay using rate in effect at time of termination (not prior rate).
   - Include all accrued EPL, vacation, and other paid leave earned during the employment period (use proportional rates if employee worked under both old and new rates in final period).
   - If termination is discharge, pay must be due by next regular payday (or within 7 business days; verify exact timing).

6. **Non-compete and restrictive covenant validation**:
   - If promotion triggers re-signing of non-compete or non-solicitation, ensure:
     - Employee earns at least 400% of federal poverty level (approximately $62,920 annually in 2026; if employee below this threshold, non-compete cannot be enforced and signing is prohibited by statute).
     - Agreement is in writing.
     - Agreement provided before or contemporaneously with promotion/new employment terms.
     - Agreement is reasonable in duration (2 years or less), geography, and scope.
   - Store date of signing, date agreement was reviewed, and confirmation that employee had opportunity to seek legal advice.
   - Flag any non-competes signed post-employment (after hire but without promotion) and flag as potentially unenforceable unless accompanied by new consideration.

7. **Equal pay verification**:
   - On pay-change entry, platform should check: Are there other employees in same job classification with same tenure/performance receiving different pay based on sex or protected class?
   - Flag for review if pay disparity exists; allow employer to document business reason (seniority, merit, production, bona fide occupational qualification).
   - Maintain records showing reasoning for pay decisions for potential equal-pay audit or litigation.

8. **Records retention and documentation**:
   - Ensure all pay-change records are retained for 6 years (payroll records), including:
     - Change request with date, approval authority, old rate, new rate, effective date.
     - Reason for change (promotion, merit increase, market adjustment, etc.).
     - Any supporting documentation (job description change, performance review, market analysis).
   - Ensure employee receives pay statement showing new rate in next paycheck.

9. **Field restrictions and validations**:
   - **Wage/salary field**: Allow entry of new rate; validate against minimum wage requirement ($14.65/hour in 2025, $15.10/hour in 2026); provide error if below minimum wage.
   - **Pay frequency field**: Restrict to at least every 16 days (Maine law requires payment at least every 16 days, which approximates twice monthly).
   - **Effective date**: Validate that pay change is not retroactive unless employer explicitly authorizes (and employee agrees).
   - **Promotion field**: If classification changes (e.g., from hourly to salaried), flag overtime-exempt status review; validate that new classification is accurate.
   - **Leave balance field**: Do not allow manual override of EPL accrual calculation; accrual is statutory and cannot be waived.
   - **Non-compete field**: Validate salary threshold; do not allow non-compete for employees earning less than 400% of federal poverty level (approximately $62,920/year in 2026, indexed annually); flag for review if employee earning below threshold is presented with non-compete to sign.

10. **Notifications to generate**:
    - **To employee**: Written notice of new pay rate, effective date, impact on paycheck, impact on leave accrual (if any), impact on benefits (PFMLA, retirement, etc.).
    - **To payroll**: Change order with old rate, new rate, employee ID, effective date, reason, and final-pay instructions if termination follows.
    - **To compliance**: Flag any equal-pay review needed; flag any non-compete re-signing; flag any multi-state pay-change needs (if employee works across states).

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether post-employment non-competes (signed after hire, without promotion or other new consideration) are enforceable in Maine case law or if "new consideration" must be present.
- Clarify whether Maine courts apply blue-pencil modification to non-competes found overbroad (can courts narrow scope/duration or must entire agreement fail?).
- Verify exact wage-replacement calculation for PFMLA (is 90% up to 50% SAWW applied to gross wages or net wages?); confirm cap amount as of 2026 and whether it adjusts annually.
- Confirm whether EPL accrual continues during unpaid leave (PFMLA unpaid portion, unpaid military leave, etc.) or only during compensated time.
- Verify whether implied employment contracts are recognized under Maine law (employee handbook language, promises of job security, course of dealing) and what triggers enforceable implied terms.
- Clarify plant-closing severance interaction with PFMLA: if employee is on PFMLA leave when closure notice is given, do they receive severance while on leave, and does leave protection continue during severance period?
- Verify if there are specific Maine wage-and-hour penalty provisions for violations (e.g., liquidated damages, interest, penalty multipliers) per 26 M.R.S. § 626-A or other statute.
- Confirm whether state PFMLA supplements or replaces federal FMLA (12-week entitlement per statute; verify interaction if employee has used federal FMLA in prior period).
- Verify coverage of multistate employees' EPL accrual: if employee works 80% in Maine, 20% in New Hampshire, how is 40-hour minimum EPL calculated—only on Maine hours or total hours?
