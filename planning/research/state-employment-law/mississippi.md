# Mississippi Employment Law Research (2026-09-03)

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment prevails**: Mississippi recognizes at-will employment by default; either party may terminate for any reason, any time, subject to federal discrimination protections and public policy exceptions.
- **No state minimum wage**: Federal FLSA minimum ($7.25/hour) applies; Mississippi's statutory minimum ($5.15/hour) is preempted.
- **E-Verify mandatory for ALL employers**: Miss. Code § 71-11-3 (Mississippi Employment Protection Act) requires every employer, regardless of size, to verify work eligibility via E-Verify; this is the strictest state requirement.
- **Final pay on next regular payday**: No state-specific deadline; federal FLSA applies; most private employers have discretion on pay schedule (weekly, biweekly, monthly) except manufacturers and public service employers (50+ employees must pay bi-weekly or semi-monthly per Miss. Code § 71-1-35).
- **Equal pay for equal work (sex only)**: Miss. Code § 71-17-5 (effective 2022) prohibits sex-based wage discrimination for "equal work" (requires equal skill, effort, education, responsibility); applies to employers with 5+ employees; private lawsuit enforcement only.
- **No state anti-discrimination law for private employers**: Title VII (federal) applies; no state agency enforces anti-discrimination; coverage gap for employers with < 15 employees.
- **No mandated paid leave**: Federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid for 50+ employers within 75 miles) applies; no state paid sick leave or paid family leave for private sector (state employees only).
- **3-year payroll records retention**: Federal FLSA requires 3-year retention; no state-specific mandate supersedes it.
- **Data breach notification without unreasonable delay**: Miss. Code § 75-24-29 requires notice in "most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay"; no specific day count; no private right of action.
- **Noncompetes enforceable if reasonable**: Enforceability determined by reasonableness of scope, duration, geographic area under case law; Mississippi courts scrutinize closely; burden on employer to prove reasonableness.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Mississippi is an at-will employment state. Under common law and statute, either party may terminate the relationship at any time, for any reason, or for no reason, **except** termination cannot be based on illegal discrimination (federal Title VII, ADA, ADEA, Equal Pay Act) or retaliation for reporting illegal acts (public policy exception; common law tort action available).

No Mississippi statute requires written notice to the employee at hire regarding at-will status, wage theft prevention, or pay-rate changes. Federal FLSA guidance applies. Employers may document at-will agreements and pay-rate changes for internal compliance; no state-specific form or timing is mandated.

Employment contracts are permitted and enforceable if they specify term or conditions. Employee handbooks may create enforceable obligations if they promise specific procedures for discipline or termination.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: Federal FLSA floor ($7.25/hour) applies. Miss. Code § 71-1-51 establishes no state minimum; federal law governs.

**Overtime**: Federal FLSA rules apply. Overtime is 1.5× regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Executive, administrative, professional, and outside-sales exemptions follow FLSA definitions (29 U.S.C. § 213).

**Pay Frequency**: Employers have discretion to set pay schedules (weekly, biweekly, monthly, etc.) for most workers. **Exception**: Miss. Code § 71-1-35 requires manufacturers and public service corporations with 50+ employees to pay wages bi-weekly or semi-monthly, or on the second and fourth Saturdays of each month. Payment must include all wages earned within the preceding 10 days (15 days for public service corporations). Tipped minimum is $2.13/hour; if tips do not bring total to $7.25/hour, employer must make up the difference.

**Final Paycheck on Termination or Resignation**: No state-specific timing mandate. Federal FLSA requires all earned wages be paid in full, typically by the next regular payday. No state law mandates severance; it is a matter of contract.

**Deductions and Pay Statements**: Federal FLSA governs permissible deductions (only deductions required by law, court order, or authorized in writing by employee are allowed). Mississippi employers are required to provide an itemized statement of deductions for each pay period, though no formal pay-stub statute exists. Federal FLSA record-keeping applies.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Mississippi state law restricts inquiries about salary history. Employers may ask about and rely upon prior wage information.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Mississippi state law requires salary ranges in job postings or on request.

**Equal Pay Law**: Miss. Code § 71-17-5 (Mississippi Equal Pay for Equal Work Act, effective July 1, 2022) prohibits employers with 5+ employees from paying employees of one sex less than the opposite sex for equal work requiring "equal skill, education, effort and responsibility" and performed under "similar working conditions." Applies to employers with 5+ employees. Exceptions: seniority systems, merit systems, production-based systems, or "any other factor other than sex" (including salary history and continuity of employment). **Note**: This is the only state equal-pay statute; race, ethnicity, and other protected-class pay discrimination are addressed only by federal law. Enforcement is private lawsuit only; no state agency investigates. Damages limited to back pay and attorney's fees (less than federal Equal Pay Act).

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Mississippi state law requires employers to report pay equity data. Federal EEO-1 reporting applies to employers with 100+ employees.

**Protected Classes**: Title VII, ADA, ADEA, Equal Pay Act, and other federal laws apply. No state-specific expansion of protected classes. Verify: No comprehensive state anti-discrimination statute exists for private employers; Title VII coverage gap for employers with < 15 employees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

No Mississippi state law mandates paid sick leave or paid family leave for private-sector employees. **Federal FMLA** (12 weeks unpaid per 12 months for qualifying reasons: serious health condition, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver, childbirth/adoption) applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles). Employers must maintain health-insurance benefits during FMLA leave.

**State employee exception**: Mississippi public-sector employees receive 15 days/year sick leave and up to 12 weeks family leave, plus beginning January 1, 2026, up to 8 weeks paid parental leave (state sector only).

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional; no state mandate. Employers may set policies and communicate them at hire or on change.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: If an employee is promoted or base pay changes, existing leave balances (if any) are typically preserved. No state rule specifies carryover or payout on promotion. Employer policy and contract govern. On termination, Mississippi does not mandate payout of unused PTO/vacation unless employer policy or contract promises it.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: Federal FLSA requires 3 years minimum. Mississippi has no state-specific mandate; federal rule applies. Employers should retain: wage records, hours worked, deductions, final-pay records for at least 3 years.

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**: **Mississippi has NO statute granting employees the right to access their own personnel files.** Employees have no statutory right to review or inspect their records. Personnel files are exempt from Mississippi's Public Records Act (§ 25-1-100). Access is governed entirely by employer policy.

**Record Format**: No state requirement for paper vs. electronic. Employers may maintain records digitally provided they remain accessible and authentic (federal FLSA allows electronic records).

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Mississippi has no comprehensive state employee-data privacy statute (no CCPA equivalent). Miss. Code § 75-24-29 imposes data-breach notification requirements (see below).

**Breach Notification**: When an employer or vendor that collects, maintains, or transmits unencrypted personal information (SSN, driver's license number, financial account number, credit card number, etc.) experiences a breach, the employer must notify affected individuals "**in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay**" unless law enforcement determines notification would compromise a criminal investigation. No specific day count is mandated; "expedient" is the standard. Recommended best practice: within 3 days to avoid "unreasonable delay" disputes. Enforcement is by Attorney General only; no private right of action.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Mississippi-specific statute regulates employee biometric collection (fingerprints, facial recognition for background checks) or electronic monitoring (keystroke logging, location tracking, email surveillance). Federal law (Title I of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act) and common-law privacy tort apply.

**Employee Data Residency**: No state residency or cross-border processing restrictions. Federal law and ordinary commercial data-protection practices apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Mississippi has **no ban-the-box law**. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history on applications, interviews, or background checks at any time. Verify: No recent legislative changes as of 2026-09-03.

**Background Checks**: Permitted. Employers commonly use criminal-history checks via the Georgia Crime Information Center (GCIC) or name-based inquiries. No state law restricts timing or scope for most private employers. Federal FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act) requires written consent and adverse-action disclosure.

**Drug Testing**: Pre-employment drug and alcohol testing is permitted and optional. Miss. Code § 71-7-3 governs testing: employers must provide written policy 30 days before implementation, with standard language on grounds for testing and possible discipline. Applicants must receive notice in writing and may be asked to sign acknowledgment. Refusal or confirmed positive may be grounds for denial of employment.

**E-Verify**: **Required for ALL employers, regardless of size.** Miss. Code § 71-11-3 (Mississippi Employment Protection Act) mandates verification of work eligibility via E-Verify (federal USCIS program) for all new employees. 1099 contractors are also covered. Employers who use E-Verify are exempt from employment-authorization liability. Penalties for non-compliance: contract termination, 3-year public-contract ineligibility, 1-year suspension of licenses and permits. This is the most stringent state E-Verify requirement in the nation.

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**WARN Act**: The federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires covered employers (100+ employees) to provide **60 days' written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers in a 30-day period. Applies nationwide, including Mississippi. Failure to notify carries penalties.

**State Mini-WARN**: Mississippi has **no state mini-WARN law**. Federal WARN Act is the only mass-layoff notice requirement.

**Severance Pay**: No state law mandates severance. Severance is a matter of contract or employer discretion.

**Notice to Employee on Termination**: No state-specific notice period required; at-will employment permits immediate termination. Employers must follow any notice period specified in an employment contract or policy.

**Noncompete and Nonsolicitation Agreements**: Mississippi permits noncompete and nonsolicitation covenants. Enforceability is determined by Mississippi case law: the employer bears the burden of proving the agreement is (1) reasonable in scope (restricted to legitimate business interests), (2) reasonable in geographic area, and (3) reasonable in duration. Mississippi courts scrutinize these agreements closely; overly broad agreements may be unenforceable. A 50-mile radius restriction was found reasonable in one case. **Verify**: Consult current Mississippi case law before enforcing; enforceability is fact-dependent and evolving.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Mississippi applies the **Common Law Test** for unemployment-insurance and workers'-compensation purposes. The test centers on the right to control: does the business have the right to direct and control not just the final result but also how the work is done? Additional factors: the nature of the service (separate from employer's business), the duration (continuous vs. intermittent), and the degree of integration. **Default presumption**: A worker is presumed an employee under MS Code § 71-5-11(J); the party claiming independent-contractor status bears the burden.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Mississippi-specific rules govern remote workers or multi-state work. Federal FLSA applies to wages; state income-tax withholding is required if the employee works in Mississippi. Multi-state employers must comply with all applicable state laws in states where work is performed.

**Reciprocity**: Mississippi recognizes Mississippi law for employment disputes involving Mississippi-based work. Out-of-state employment contracts may invoke choice-of-law provisions, but if work is performed in Mississippi, Mississippi law applies to statutory protections.

## 11. Implications for HCM Next (P1A/P1B)

When HCM Next processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in Mississippi:

**Promotion Workflow:**

- **Record at-will status** in the worker profile. For most Mississippi workers, no contract-term field is required.
- **Verify E-Verify compliance**: Confirm employer has E-Verify registration and individual's work eligibility is verified (mandatory). Flag if employer is not registered.
- **Equal pay audit trail**: If employer uses Miss. Code § 71-17-5 tracking, capture job title, skill level, effort, education, responsibility, working conditions, and salary comparables (for sex-based disputes) for audit.
- **Check for noncompete**: If employer has noncompete agreements, attach to employment record and flag for legal review (enforceability is fact-dependent).
- **Pay frequency validation**: If promoted employee was on weekly/biweekly/monthly, validate that new role respects pay-frequency rules (manufacturers/public service 50+ must stay bi-weekly/semi-monthly).
- **Leave preservation**: Preserve any accrued PTO or sick leave balances unless contract specifies forfeiture; no state mandate to pay out on promotion.
- **No promotion-specific notice required**: No Mississippi state law mandates written notice of promotion terms or timing.

**Base-Pay Change Workflow:**

- **Document pay change reason**: Capture the business reason for the increase (merit, market adjustment, role change, etc.). If sex-based pay disparity is a concern, document the factor other than sex (seniority, merit, production, or other legitimate factor per § 71-17-5 exception).
- **Pay frequency alignment**: Validate new rate conforms to pay-frequency rules (manufacturers 50+ must pay bi-weekly or semi-monthly).
- **Final-pay confirmation**: If employee is terminated during a pay-period, ensure final pay includes all earned wages and commissions and is paid by next regular payday. No state-specific deadline.
- **Retention obligation**: Retain all wage and separation records for 3 years (federal FLSA minimum).
- **No written notice mandate**: Federal FLSA does not require written notice of pay changes; best practice is to document the change internally.
- **Equal-pay compliance**: If the pay change affects employees differently by sex, verify the change does not create a sex-based wage disparity; ensure the change is justified by a factor other than sex under § 71-17-5.
- **Data retention**: If personal information is handled, establish a breach-notification protocol to contact affected parties "in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay" (≤3 days recommended).

**Termination Workflow:**

- **At-will termination**: No notice period required; at-will employment permits immediate termination. Follow any notice period in the employment contract or policy.
- **Mass-separation WARN**: If terminating 50+ employees in a 30-day period and employer has 100+, file 60-day WARN notice (federal). Mississippi has no state mini-WARN.
- **Final paycheck**: Pay all earned wages, commissions, overtime by next regular payday. No state-specific deadline; federal FLSA applies. No mandatory PTO payout unless policy or contract requires it.
- **Record retention**: Retain separation notice and final-pay records for 3 years.
- **Noncompete enforcement**: If employee is subject to a noncompete, ensure it is attached to the employment record; flag for legal review if employer intends to enforce (enforceability is uncertain without case-law review).
- **E-Verify compliance**: Ensure E-Verify records are retained per employment record retention rules.

**Open Questions for HCM Next:**

- Does the employer have a written policy on final-pay timing (immediately, next regular payday, or longer)?
- Is the employer a manufacturer or public service corporation with 50+ employees? (Must pay bi-weekly or semi-monthly per § 71-1-35.)
- Is the employer subject to federal WARN (100+ employees)? If so, coordinate with 60-day notice requirement for mass layoffs.
- Does the employer use noncompete agreements? If so, review for enforceability under Mississippi case law before enforcing.
- Is the employer covered by FMLA (50+ employees within 75 miles)? If so, coordinate leave eligibility with role/pay changes.
- Does the employer track pay-equity data by sex for § 71-17-5 compliance? If so, model the audit trail in the promotion/pay-change workflow.
- Has the employer established a data-retention and breach-notification protocol? (Miss. Code § 75-24-29 requires notice "without unreasonable delay".)
