# Virginia Employment Law Research

**State:** Virginia | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with statutory exceptions**: Exceptions include terminations for wage theft claims, workers' compensation claims, jury duty, military service, whistleblowing, refusal to commit illegal acts, or exercising constitutional rights (Va. Code § 40.1-27.3).
- **Minimum wage indexed annually**: $12.41/hour (2025), $12.77/hour (2026–2027), $13.75/hour (2027–2028), $15.00/hour (2028–2029); thereafter indexed by CPI (§ 40.1-28.10).
- **No state overtime after 2022 repeal**: Virginia follows federal FLSA overtime only; state-level overtime wage act repealed (§ 40.1-29.2 now references federal requirements).
- **Non-competes banned for low-wage and FLSA-covered employees**: Low-wage threshold defined by average weekly wage per § 65.2-500; also any FLSA-covered (overtime-eligible) employee (SB 1218, effective July 1, 2025); health-care professionals also banned from non-competes (§ 40.1-28.7:8). Non-competes unenforceable if employee terminated without cause and no severance/compensation provided.
- **Wage transparency and salary history ban effective September 3, 2026**: All job postings must disclose wage/salary range; salary history inquiry prohibited; civil penalties up to $1,000 per first offense, $5,000 per subsequent; private right of action within 1 year (§ 40.1-28.7:12).
- **Protected classes include sexual orientation and gender identity**: Virginia Values Act (2020) amended Virginia Human Rights Act § 2.2-3905; also protects against pregnancy discrimination (2020), disability (2021), ethnic hairstyles (2020 CROWN Act); 5+ employees subject to non-discharge rule, 15+ for full anti-discrimination duties (§ 2.2-3905).
- **Paid sick leave for home health workers only**: 1 hour per 30 hours worked, max 40/year, carry-over permitted (§ 40.1-33.3 et seq., effective 2021); **no general state-mandated paid sick leave for other employees** (federal FMLA applies to employers with 50+ employees).
- **Pay frequency and final pay (§ 40.1-29)**: Hourly = biweekly or twice monthly; salaried = monthly; final pay on or before next regular payday; treble damages for knowing wage violations.
- **Ban-the-box for public employers only**: § 19.2-392.4 restricts public employers from inquiring about expunged records; marijuana possession inquiry prohibited (§ 19.2-389.3).
- **No state mini-WARN; federal WARN Act applies**: Employers with 100+ employees must provide 60 days' notice for mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Virginia is an employment-at-will state under common law.

**Statutory exceptions to at-will termination** (Va. Code § 40.1-27.3):

- Termination for filing wage, workers' compensation, or unemployment claims.
- Termination for jury duty, voting (election officials), military service, or court appearance.
- Termination for reporting a law violation to a supervisor or government agency in good faith.
- Termination for refusing an illegal order or complying with a legal duty.
- Retaliation for whistleblowing, wage discussion, or misclassification complaints.

**Required notices at hire and pay/role change**:

- Va. Code § 40.1-29: Written notice of wages, pay periods, and deductions required. Pay _decrease_ requires notice 1+ pay periods in advance; pay _increase_ does not require advance notice.
- No general "offer of employment" statute, but § 40.1-29 requires written notice of employment terms affecting pay or deductions.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (§ 40.1-28.10):

- 2025: $12.41/hour (greater of state or federal $7.25).
- 2026–2027: $12.77/hour.
- 2027–2028: $13.75/hour.
- 2028–2029: $15.00/hour.
- **Effective Jan 1, 2029, indexed annually** by CPI-U increase from prior year (Commissioner sets adjusted wage by Oct 1 each year).
- Tipped employees: No tip credit; full minimum wage required (employer must pay full minimum regardless of tips).
- Minors: Federal rules apply (no state subminimum for youth).

**Overtime**:

- **No state overtime after 2022 repeal.** § 40.1-29.2 makes employers liable for violations of federal FLSA overtime (29 U.S.C. § 207); federal rules apply: 1.5x regular rate for hours over 40/week.
- No state-level daily overtime (8 hours/day) or 7th-day overtime; federal FLSA standard only.
- Exempt employees: Must meet salary test (currently $35,568/year under federal FLSA; Virginia has no separate test).

**Pay frequency and timing** (§ 40.1-29):

- Hourly/non-exempt: At least biweekly (every 14 days) or twice monthly (semimonthly).
- Salaried/exempt: At least monthly.
- Exception: Employees earning >150% of state average weekly wage ($1,463 × 1.5 = ~$2,195/week or ~$114,140/year) may be paid monthly if they agree in writing.
- High-earning employees and work-study students: May be paid monthly with written agreement.
- If payday falls on weekend/holiday: Pay on preceding workday (not later than next regular payday).

**Final pay on termination** (§ 40.1-29):

- All wages earned must be paid on or before the date employee would have been paid if employment had continued (next regular payday following separation).
- Accrued leave: No state mandate for vacation/personal leave payout unless employer policy, employment contract, or implied agreement requires it. Paid sick leave (for home health workers) must be paid at same hourly rate + benefits as regular work.
- Employer withholding accrued leave only if: (i) written policy exists limiting leave compensation, (ii) employee was notified under § 40.1-29, and (iii) policy explicitly permits forfeiture.

**Permitted deductions** (§ 40.1-29):

- Only legal withholdings (payroll/income tax, court-ordered garnishment, child support, judgment) without employee authorization.
- Employer may deduct with **written, signed employee authorization** on approved form (no specific form mandated by statute, but authorization must be explicit, documented, and on file).
- No deductions for tools, uniforms, breakage, shortages, or other employer-imposed costs unless employee agrees _in writing_ _and_ deduction does not reduce wages below applicable minimum wage.
- Employer who knowingly fails to pay wages liable for **treble damages** (all wages due + equal amount as liquidated damages + 8% annual interest accruing from due date) (§ 40.1-29.2).
- 3-year statute of limitations for wage claims (§ 40.1-29).

**Pay statement content** (§ 40.1-29):

- Must show: employer name and address, hours worked (if hourly or certain salaried employees), rate of pay, gross and net wages, amount and purpose of all deductions.
- Sufficient detail to enable employee to determine how gross and net pay calculated.
- Employer must retain pay statements for 3+ years and provide copies upon employee request.
- Agricultural employees: Upon request, employer must furnish written statement of gross wages and deduction purposes during any pay period.
- No specific requirement to itemize deductions line-by-line (unlike some states), but sufficient clarity required.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage transparency** (§ 40.1-28.7:12, effective September 3, 2026):

- All public and internal job postings must disclose wage, salary, or wage/salary range.
- Range must be disclosed in good faith based on pay scale, prior range, comparable roles, budget, or other legitimate business factors.
- Employers must disclose wage/salary range upon applicant request; withholding prohibited.
- Employers cannot retaliate against employee or applicant for requesting wage range or disclosing own compensation.
- **Enforcement**: Attorney General may impose civil penalties up to $1,000 (first offense) or $5,000 (subsequent violation); employees may bring private action within 1 year.

**Salary history ban** (§ 40.1-28.7:12, effective September 3, 2026):

- Employers cannot seek, request, or require wage/salary history from prospective employees or applicants.
- Exception: If candidate voluntarily discloses prior compensation, employer may consider it only if applied to justify higher offer (must comply with equal pay law § 40.1-28.6).
- Employer cannot retaliate (refuse to hire, retaliate against employee, or take adverse action) for applicant/employee refusal to disclose salary history.

**Equal pay** (§ 40.1-28.6):

- No pay discrimination between sexes for equal work (equal skill, effort, responsibility, similar working conditions).
- Exceptions: Seniority system, merit system, production quality/quantity, or factor other than sex.
- Violations treated as unpaid wages; employee may bring civil action.

**Protected classes** (§ 2.2-3905, Virginia Human Rights Act):

- Race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, age (40+), disability, marital status, pregnancy/childbirth/lactation, military status, ethnic/national origin.
- Applies to employers with 5+ employees (any employer of domestic workers).
- Prohibited discrimination in compensation, terms, conditions, privileges of employment.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Military, Jury Duty

**Paid sick leave**:

- **Narrow scope**: Only home health workers and companions in consumer-directed MEDICAID services earn statutory paid sick leave (§ 40.1-33.3 et seq., effective 2021). Definition: individual providing personal care, respite, or companion services to consumer receiving state medical assistance; employee must work avg. 20+ hours/week or 90+ hours/month to qualify.
- **Accrual and payout**: 1 hour per 30 hours worked, max 40 hours per calendar year; carry-over to next year permitted (no cap on carry-over specified in statute). Paid leave must be compensated at same hourly rate + benefits as regular work, no less than minimum wage (§ 40.1-28.10 without tip credit).
- **Permitted uses**: Employee's own mental/physical illness, injury, health condition; need for medical diagnosis, care, treatment, or preventive medical care; care of family member with similar needs.
- **General workforce**: No Virginia-mandated state paid sick leave for other employees; federal FMLA (unpaid, 12 weeks/year) applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles. No state paid family leave program yet (federal mandates only).

**Jury duty protection** (§ 18.2-465.1):

- Employer cannot discharge, demote, suspend, threaten, or penalize employee for jury service or court appearance upon reasonable notice to employer.
- No requirement to use accrued leave for jury duty; employer cannot require employee to report to work during jury service.
- **Shift restrictions**: No work shift starting after 5 p.m. same day or before 3 a.m. next day if employee served 4+ hours (including travel).
- Violation is Class 3 misdemeanor (criminal penalty, not private cause of action).

**Voting/election service protection** (Va. Code § 24.2-119.1):

- **Election officials** (electoral board members, deputy registrars, officers of election) protected from discharge and adverse action for serving at polls on election day or at post-election board meetings if they gave reasonable advance notice.
- Same 4+ hour shift restrictions apply.
- **Regular voters**: No statutory "time off to vote" mandate; employees must use personal leave to vote on election day or vote early in person (absentee voting available without employer permission). No employer penalty for voting early, at polls outside work, or during meal breaks.

**Military service leave** (§ 44-93.2):

- Members of Virginia National Guard, Virginia Defense Force, or out-of-state National Guard working in Virginia have right to unpaid leave when called to state/federal active duty.
- **Accrued leave discretion**: Employee chooses whether to use vacation/personal leave during active duty; no forced use (decision is employee's sole discretion).
- Cumulative absence limit: 5 years total over career; service beyond 5 years not reemployment-protected under statute (federal USERRA applies to federal service, 5 years).
- Employer refusal to comply: Circuit court may order compliance, back wages/benefits, and attorney fees/costs. Employer also subject to penalties under § 44-93.5.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll record retention** (§ 40.1-29):

- Employers must keep payroll records for 3 years showing employee name, address, occupation, hours (daily/weekly), rate, gross/net pay, deductions.
- Minor employees: Records kept on premises for 36 months from last work period.
- Statute of limitations for wage claims: 3 years.

**Employee access to records**:

- Common law right to inspect personnel file within reasonable time (10–15 business days) at or near worksite during business hours.
- Employee may obtain copies; employers may charge reasonable copying fee.
- Disputed information: Employee may submit written disagreement, which must accompany any disclosure to third parties.

**Record format**:

- No Virginia mandate for electronic vs. paper; electronic acceptable if accessible and retention met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Breach Notification, Monitoring

**Breach notification** (§ 18.2-186.6):

- Personal information (name + SSN, credit card, driver license, biometric data, etc.) breach must be disclosed to affected residents and Virginia Attorney General **without unreasonable delay** if unauthorized access compromises security/confidentiality and causes, or reasonably believed to cause, identity theft or fraud.
- **Encryption safeguard**: If encrypted information accessed in unencrypted form, or encryption key holder breaches, disclose if identity theft/fraud likely. Encrypted data is not breach-reportable if key remains secure.
- **Reasonable delay exception**: May delay notification to allow determination of scope and restoration of system integrity, but only if delay does not impede investigation.
- Notice requirement: Written or electronic notice to each affected Virginia resident; also notify Virginia Attorney General if breach involves more than minimal risk.

**Virginia Consumer Data Protection Act** (§ 59.1-575 et seq., effective Jan 1, 2023):

- Applies to consumer data (personal data collected from/about individuals acting in individual/household context). **Employee data excluded** (individuals acting in employment/occupational context exempt from VCDPA).
- Does not restrict employer collection/use of employee data for employment purposes (hiring, payroll, benefits, etc.).
- Consumer rights under VCDPA (access, correction, deletion, opt-out): Do not apply to employee data.
- Note: Employer must still comply with § 18.2-186.6 breach notification for employee data containing personal information (SSN, etc.).

**Employee privacy—social media and wage discussion**:

- § 40.1-28.7:4 (Social Media Privacy, 2012): Employers cannot request/require employees or applicants to disclose personal social media username, password, or means of accessing personal account. Disciplining employee or refusing to hire applicant for non-disclosure prohibited.
  - Exception: Employers _may_ require disclosure of credentials for _employer-provided_ accounts (work email, corporate social media) that access employer systems.
  - Employers may view publicly available social media content, but must avoid discrimination issues based on protected class information revealed.
- § 40.1-28.7:9 (Wage Discussion Protection, 2022): Employer cannot discharge, discipline, threaten, discriminate against, or retaliate against employee for inquiring about, discussing, or disclosing own wages, other employees' wages, or filing wage complaint with Commissioner (including hours/pay info).
- **Workplace monitoring**: No state statute banning video surveillance, keystroke monitoring, or location tracking of work devices. Reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies under Virginia tort law; clear notice to employee that monitoring may occur reduces privacy claim risk.
- **Email and internet**: No state statute banning employee work email or internet use monitoring; employer may monitor work-provided devices and accounts if employer owns/controls them and employee has notice.

**Data residency and processing**:

- No Virginia state law mandating employee data residency within Virginia; employers may store/process employee data in-state or out-of-state provided data security and breach notification laws met.
- No state-level requirement to use in-state cloud providers or exclude certain jurisdictions; federal compliance (FCRA for background checks, HIPAA for health data, etc.) applies.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- § 19.2-392.4: **Public employers only** cannot require disclosure of expunged records on application or before first interview.
- **Private sector**: No state ban-the-box; federal FCRA applies. Employers may conduct background checks; must provide FCRA notice and adverse-action procedures.
- Exemptions: Court orders, professional licensure, bonding requirements.

**Marijuana employment protection**:

- § 19.2-389.3 (repeals effective July 1, 2026): Employers (public and private) cannot inquire about, request, or require disclosures of marijuana possession arrests, charges, or convictions if records not open for public inspection.
- § 40.1-27.4: No employer may discharge/discipline employee for lawful medicinal cannabis oil use under valid physician certification, unless work impairment or possession during work hours; does not prevent drug testing or restrict defense-industrial-base employers from enforcing THC limits (>50 ng/ml urine, 10 pg/mg hair).

**Drug testing**:

- No state-level regulation; federal law (pre-employment, reasonable suspicion, post-accident) and DOT rules apply.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No Virginia state mandate; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Non-Compete

**Mass layoff notice**:

- **No Virginia mini-WARN act.** Federal WARN Act applies: employers with 100+ employees must provide 60 days' notice for mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers or 33% of workforce (whichever is lower).

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance outside narrow circumstances; contractual only.

**Non-compete enforceability** (§ 40.1-28.7:8; SB 1218 effective July 1, 2025):

- **Prohibited for low-wage employees**: Average weekly wage determined pursuant to § 65.2-500 (workers' compensation), or median hourly wage for Commonwealth as reported by Bureau of Labor Statistics.
- **Prohibited for FLSA-covered (overtime-eligible) employees**: Any employee "entitled to overtime compensation under 29 U.S.C. § 207 for any hours worked in excess of 40 hours in any one workweek" (SB 1218, effective July 1, 2025; applies to agreements entered on/after that date).
- **Prohibited for all health-care professionals** regardless of wage or FLSA status.
- **Unenforceable if employer terminates without cause and fails to provide severance, compensation, or paid leave** (unless termination for cause and documented).
- **Exceptions**: Employees deriving earnings "in whole or predominant part" from sales commissions, incentives, or bonuses may have enforceable non-competes if reasonable in scope, duration, and geography.
- **Violation**: $10,000 civil penalty; employee may sue within 2 years for damages, lost compensation, and attorney fees.

**Non-solicitation/confidentiality**:

- Common law enforceability (reasonableness in duration, geography, scope); no wage threshold.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers

**Contractor classification**:

- Virginia applies common-law test and federal IRS test (control, payment method, tools, permanence, integral nature).
- No ABC statutory test.
- § 40.1-28.7:7: Misclassification enforcement by Department of Labor, Department of Revenue, Workers' Compensation Commission; penalties include back wages, reclassification, civil actions.

**Misclassification retaliation** (§ 40.1-33.1, effective July 1, 2020):

- Employer cannot retaliate against employee/contractor reporting suspected misclassification in good faith.
- Commissioner may order reinstatement, back wages, and penalties.

**Remote workers**:

- No Virginia statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee works in Virginia (even remotely), Virginia law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (Promotion and Base-Pay Change)

**Mandatory obligations and field restrictions**:

1. **Minimum wage floor check** (§ 40.1-28.10): On any pay change (promotion, base raise, role change), verify new rate ≥ current state minimum ($12.77 for 2026–2027; $13.75 for 2027–2028; $15.00 for 2028–2029; then annual CPI adjustment). Auto-update wage floor on Jan 1 each year. Flag or reject below-minimum offers as compliance violation.

2. **Wage transparency: Posting and range disclosure** (§ 40.1-28.7:12, effective September 3, 2026):
   - Before posting a promotion, transfer, or new role, _require_ wage/salary range input (min/max or single rate with business justification); system blocks posting if range missing.
   - Wage range must be disclosed in good faith based on pay scale, prior range, comparable roles, budget, or other legitimate factors.
   - On job offer, timestamp wage range disclosure to employee/candidate as proof of compliance.
   - Internal transfers/promotions must also disclose range (applies to all internal postings).
   - For roles with variable compensation: Disclose base salary range + general description of bonus/commission structure.

3. **Pay rate change notice** (§ 40.1-29):
   - If base pay _decreases_: Auto-generate written notice 1+ pay periods in advance (timing requirement = pay period length). Notice must include old rate, new rate, effective date, and employee acknowledgment signature/digital sign-off.
   - If base pay _increases_: No advance notice requirement; may take effect immediately (but recommended practice: communicate before pay cycle).
   - Store notice in employee record for 3 years (payroll retention requirement).

4. **Salary history inquiry block** (§ 40.1-28.7:12, effective September 3, 2026):
   - In recruitment/hiring form: Disable or remove any field asking "current salary," "prior salary," "salary history," or similar.
   - For internal candidates (current employees): May reference their current Virginia wage data; external candidates cannot be asked for prior compensation.
   - If candidate volunteers salary history: Platform may capture for internal records but cannot use it as primary basis for offer (must justify through market comparables, budget, or pay scale).
   - Flag violations as compliance risk; audit recruitment for salary-history questions and retaliation risk (e.g., penalizing applicant for refusal to disclose).

5. **Equal pay audit and justification** (§ 40.1-28.6):
   - On promotion + base-pay change, flag if new rate differs >10% (verify threshold) from peer rates for "equal work" (comparable role, same skill/effort/responsibility, similar working conditions in same location/department).
   - Trigger HR review/manager justification if rate outlier (e.g., promoted employee earning less than peer in same/similar role).
   - Accept justifications: Seniority, merit rating, production/sales metrics, individual performance data (all documented).
   - Maintain equal-pay analysis in promotion record for 3 years (recordkeeping).

6. **Non-compete enforceability check and void** (§ 40.1-28.7:8; SB 1218):
   - On hire date input or role change: Automated check of employee earnings and FLSA status.
   - **Low-wage threshold** (determined per § 65.2-500 average weekly wage, or median hourly wage per BLS):
     - If employee salary below threshold: Flag any existing non-compete as unenforceable and recommend removal/amendment.
     - Verify current threshold value with state Department of Labor (indexed; updates annually).
   - **FLSA overtime coverage** (effective July 1, 2025 for new agreements):
     - If employee is FLSA-covered (entitled to overtime under 29 U.S.C. § 207): Flag non-compete as unenforceable and void.
     - Non-exempt hourly employees and many salaried employees FLSA-covered; exempt status depends on duties test.
   - **Health-care professionals**: Void all non-competes regardless of wage or FLSA status.
   - **Termination without cause or severance**:
     - If employee terminated involuntarily without severance, compensation, or paid leave: Non-compete automatically unenforceable (unless termination documented as "cause").
     - System should flag non-compete as void on involuntary separation unless cause documented.
   - **Remedies**: If system enforces unenforceable non-compete, employer liable for $10,000 civil penalty; employee may sue within 2 years for damages + lost compensation + attorney fees.

7. **Paid sick leave tracking (home health workers only)** (§ 40.1-33.3):
   - If employee classification = "Home Health Worker" or "Consumer-Directed Services Companion" AND avg. hours >20/week or >90/month:
     - Accrue paid sick leave: 1 hour per 30 hours worked, max 40 hours/calendar year.
     - Carry-over to next year (no statutory cap on accrual; verify employer policy).
     - On termination: If no written leave-payout policy on file, mandatory payout of accrued balance at regular hourly rate + benefits.
   - All other employees: No state requirement; federal FMLA (unpaid) only.

8. **Treble damages exposure and payroll audit** (§ 40.1-29.2):
   - _Critical for P1B_: Pay must be on or before next regular payday after termination/role change.
   - Any late-pay violation exposes employer to: All wages due + equal amount as liquidated damages + 8% annual interest from due date.
   - Example: $5,000 unpaid bonus due 2026-09-10 but paid 2026-09-20 = owed $5,000 + $5,000 + ~$27 interest = ~$10,027 treble recovery.
   - System should auto-alert if pay run misses scheduled payday for any employee.

9. **Protected class verification on termination/separation** (§ 2.2-3905, § 40.1-27.3):
   - On termination/demotion/role change: Verify decision not based on protected class (race, color, religion, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, national origin, age 40+, disability, marital status, pregnancy, military status).
   - Verify not retaliation for wage claim, whistleblower report, jury duty, military service, misclassification complaint, or refusal to break law.
   - Document business reason (performance, restructure, consolidation, etc.) in termination record.

10. **Wage discussion/disclosure protection** (§ 40.1-28.7:9):

- Employer cannot prohibit employees from discussing/disclosing wages with peers.
- Do not include "wage confidentiality" clauses in promotions or role changes.
- If employee inquires about peer wage rate as part of promotion negotiation, do not retaliate; address through compensation review process.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Does the September 3, 2026 wage transparency rule apply retroactively to promotions/transfers announced before that date, or only to job postings made on/after that date?
- Is the low-wage non-compete threshold (per § 65.2-500 or BLS median) fixed or does it auto-update annually? What is the current threshold for 2026?
- What specific time frame constitutes "1+ pay periods in advance" notice for pay decrease—pay-period start, payday, or any date within that period?
- For equal-pay audits under § 40.1-28.6, does "equal work" comparison include bonus, sales commission, benefits value, or only base salary?
- Does SB 1218 (FLSA non-compete prohibition, effective July 1, 2025) apply to agreements pre-dating that effective date, or only new agreements entered on/after?
