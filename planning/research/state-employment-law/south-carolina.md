# South Carolina Employment Law Research

**State:** South Carolina | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment**: South Carolina is at-will; employers may terminate without cause or notice. Exceptions exist for workers' compensation retaliation, jury duty, military service, and public-policy violations.
- **Minimum wage and overtime**: Federal floor applies ($7.25/hour, federal overtime); no state override.
- **Seven-day written notice required for wage changes**: § 41-10-30 mandates written notice at hire of wages, hours, paydays, deductions; any change requires at least seven calendar days' written advance notice.
- **Final pay timing**: § 41-10-50 requires payment of all wages within 48 hours of separation or next regular payday (not exceeding 30 days).
- **E-Verify mandate**: § 41-8-20(B) requires all private employers to verify work authorization within three business days of hire (federal I-9 + E-Verify program).
- **Human Affairs Law protections**: Applies to employers with 15+ employees (§ 1-13-10 et seq.); prohibits discrimination on race, religion, color, sex, age, national origin, disability; Pregnancy Accommodations Act (§ 1-13-30(P), 2018) requires reasonable accommodations.
- **Handbook disclaimer**: § 41-1-110 allows employers to disclaim implied contract if disclaimer appears in underlined capital letters on first page and employee signs (effective 2004).
- **Non-compete enforceability**: Common law; must be written, signed, reasonable in time/territory; strictly construed against drafter; no blue-pencil modification.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

South Carolina follows at-will employment doctrine: employers and employees may terminate employment at any time without cause or advance notice.

**Public-policy exceptions** (§ 41-1-80, § 41-1-70, § 41-1-85):

- Termination for filing or participating in workers' compensation claim or proceeding (§ 41-1-80).
- Termination for jury duty, jury summons response, or subpoena compliance (§ 41-1-70).
- Termination for lawful off-duty tobacco use (§ 41-1-85); applies only to tobacco, not broader lawful products.
- Termination for refusing to perform illegal act (common law).

**Written notice at hire and on wage change** (§ 41-10-30):

- Employers must provide written notice at hiring specifying "normal hours and wages agreed upon, the time and place of payment, and the deductions which will be made."
- **Seven calendar days' written advance notice required before any change in wages, hours, pay frequency, or deductions.**
- Change may not become effective until seven days after written notice delivered.

**At-will employment may be limited by:**

- Handbook or personnel manual that creates implied contract of employment (§ 41-1-110; disclaimer required to defeat implied contract).
- Written employment contract specifying term or cause-for-termination clause.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (§ 41-10-30): Federal minimum wage of $7.25/hour applies; no state override.

**Overtime**: Federal FLSA overtime (time-and-a-half for hours over 40/week) applies; no state daily or weekly threshold beyond federal law.

**Pay frequency** (§ 41-10-40, § 41-10-30):

- Employers must designate regular paydays: daily, weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly.
- Wages must be paid on regular payday in lawful U.S. currency, negotiable warrant, or check.
- Direct deposit permitted if employees receive earnings statements and at least one free withdrawal per deposit period.

**Final pay timing** (§ 41-10-50):

- Upon employee separation (discharge, resignation, or end of term), employer must pay all wages due "within forty-eight hours of the time of separation or the next regular payday which may not exceed thirty days."
- Includes all accrued wages and bonuses/commissions if vested or earned during employment term.

**Permitted deductions** (§ 41-10-30, § 41-10-40):

- Deductions allowed only if required by law (tax withholding, garnishment, court order) or authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes (e.g., health insurance, retirement plan contributions).
- Deduction must not reduce wages below applicable minimum wage.
- Employers must provide written itemized pay statement or earnings record.

**Treble damages** (§ 41-10-80): Employee may recover in civil action three times the full amount of unpaid wages, plus costs and reasonable attorney's fees; statute of limitations is three years from when wages became due.

**Record retention** (§ 41-10-30(C)): Employers must maintain true and accurate wage and hour records for three years.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary history ban**: No state law; employers may inquire about and rely on prior wages in setting compensation.

**Pay transparency mandate**: No state-wide requirement for salary range disclosure in job postings or on request.

**Equal pay**: no South Carolina equal-pay statute (Title 41 has no such chapter); the federal Equal Pay Act (29 U.S.C. § 206(d)) and Title VII apply.

- Prohibits discrimination in wages on basis of sex for substantially similar work.
- No explicit private right of action stated; enforcement by state agency.
- Federal Equal Pay Act (FLSA § 206(d)) provides baseline.

**Human Affairs Law** (§ 1-13-10 et seq.):

- Applies to employers with 15+ regularly employed employees.
- Prohibits discrimination based on race, religion, color, sex, age, national origin, disability.
- No separate gender-identity or sexual-orientation statute; coverage dependent on legislative updates (verify current status).

**Complaint process**: Claims must be filed within 180 days of alleged violation with South Carolina Human Affairs Commission; agency may pursue conciliation, administrative hearing, or civil suit.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave**: No state rule; employer policy and federal law apply. Employers may offer paid sick leave at their discretion; no state mandate or minimum accrual requirement.

**Paid family and medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate; federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid, 50+ employees, if applicable) is sole statutory floor.

**Jury duty leave** (§ 41-1-70):

- Employee cannot be discharged or demoted for jury duty, jury summons response, or subpoena compliance.
- Remedy: damages up to one year's salary (based on 40-hour week at wages received at time of subpoena).
- Statute of limitations: one year.

**Military leave**: Federal Uniform Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA) applies; no separate state statute requirement verified; federal law is mandatory floor.

**Interaction with promotion**: On promotion during leave, employer must not reduce post-leave wage below the wage rate earned before leave commenced or before promotion, whichever is lower.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll record retention** (§ 41-10-30(C)):

- Employers must maintain true and accurate wage, hour, and employment records for three years.
- Records must be available for inspection by Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation.

**Employee access to personnel files**: No state rule; employer policy applies. South Carolina does not mandate employee access to personnel records or impose timing/format requirements; employers may establish their own disclosure policies.

**Record format**: No state mandate for electronic vs. paper format; either acceptable if retention and access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Workplace monitoring and privacy**: No state statute restricting email, internet monitoring, or video surveillance; common law reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies (narrow).

**Biometric data**: No specific state biometric regulation for employment; federal BIPA does not apply to South Carolina (Illinois-specific); common law applies.

**Data breach notification** (§ 39-1-90):

- Any business conducting business in South Carolina that owns or licenses computerized data containing personal identifying information must notify affected residents "in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay" after discovery of breach.
- Personal identifying information = name + sensitive element (SSN, driver's license, financial account, government ID, biometric, unique identifier) if not encrypted/redacted.
- Notification methods: written, electronic, telephonic, or substitute notice (email, website, media) if cost exceeds $250,000 or affects 500,000+ persons.
- Penalties: civil liability for damages, injunctive relief, attorney's fees, and fines up to $1,000 per affected resident.

**Data residency**: No state law mandating data residency; out-of-state storage permitted if breach notification and security requirements met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box**: No state law for private employers; applies only to state agencies (Executive Order).

**Background checks**: Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions for private employers.

**Drug testing** (§ 41-1-15):

- No state law prohibiting private-sector drug testing.
- Employers may test pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, or random.
- Employer must maintain drug prevention program with written policy.
- Test results must be kept confidential; no release without written employee consent except by court order or state agency disclosure in administrative/civil proceedings.
- Results cannot be used in criminal proceedings against employee.

**E-Verify mandate** (§ 41-8-20(B), Illegal Immigration Reform Act):

- **Applies to ALL private employers** (no employee threshold).
- New hires must be verified within three business days of hire.
- Verification must be completed through federal E-Verify program or federal I-9 process.
- Failure to verify may result in suspension or revocation of business license.
- Private employers must register and participate in E-Verify federal work authorization program.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mini-WARN act**: No state law; federal WARN Act (100+ employees, 60 days' notice for plant closing or mass layoff of 50+ employees or 25%+ of workforce) is sole requirement.

**Severance**: No state mandate; contractual and voluntary only.

**Non-compete agreements** (common law; statutory exceptions):

- **Enforceability test**: (1) In writing, (2) signed by restrained party, (3) based on valuable consideration (employment itself may suffice), (4) reasonable in time and territory, (5) not against public policy.
- Enforced narrowly against drafter; courts do not apply blue-pencil doctrine to modify overly broad provisions; entire provision fails if any element unreasonable.
- Valid legitimate business interests: trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information, goodwill.
- **No specific time/territory standard in statute; reasonableness determined case-by-case; restrictive interpretation favors employee.**

**Statutory exception**: none. S.C. Code ch. 41-7 is the right-to-work law and contains no non-compete provision; non-competes are governed by common law only.

- Non-competes are null and void and unenforceable if employer is a nonprofit corporation with annual gross revenue exceeding $1 billion.

**Non-solicitation agreements**: Enforced under common law reasonableness test (time, scope, geography, legitimate business interest); no separate statute.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification** (common law):

- South Carolina courts apply eight-factor test; no single factor determinative.
- Factors: (1) Control/direction by hiring entity, (2) Integral to hiring entity's business, (3) Permanency of relationship, (4) Contractor's investment in facilities/equipment, (5) Opportunities for profit and loss, (6) Initiative and judgment in market competition, (7) Degree of independent business operation, (8) Totality of circumstances.
- Misclassification enforcement by the South Carolina Department of Labor, Licensing and Regulation (LLR), the Department of Revenue, and the Attorney General.

**Remote workers**: No specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location. If work is performed in South Carolina (remote or on-site), South Carolina law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**: No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints for promotion and base-pay changes**:

1. **Seven-day written notice before wage change** (§ 41-10-30): Any increase or decrease in base wage, bonus structure, or commission rate requires seven calendar days' written advance notice before effective date. HCM must:
   - Generate written notice document with wage change details (from amount, to amount, effective date, business reason).
   - Record delivery date and employee acknowledgment.
   - Flag if effective date is fewer than seven days from notice delivery.

2. **E-Verify status verification** (§ 41-8-20(B)): Before processing promotion or base-pay increase, verify employee's work authorization status in E-Verify or I-9 file is current and no legal hold/revocation is in place; record verification timestamp.

3. **Minimum wage compliance**: Verify promoted wage meets federal $7.25/hour minimum; flag if reduction results in subminimum wage.

4. **Final pay interaction**: If termination occurs post-promotion, ensure all wages at promoted rate due within 48 hours or next regular payday (not exceeding 30 days).

5. **Personnel file documentation** (§ 41-10-30(C), three-year retention): Record promotion decision, wage change, effective date, business rationale, and seven-day notice delivery in personnel file; retain for three years.

6. **Workers' compensation retaliation check** (§ 41-1-80): If employee recently filed workers' compensation claim, injury report, or participated in related proceeding, flag promotion timing to ensure no retaliation inference; timing within 30 days of claim is suspicious.

7. **Jury duty and military service protection** (§ 41-1-70, USERRA): If employee recently served jury duty or activated for military service, do not demote, discharge, or deny promotion; maintain wage from pre-leave position or promoted position, whichever is higher.

8. **Tobacco/lawful products protection** (§ 41-1-85): Promotion cannot be denied or conditioned on employee's off-duty tobacco use.

9. **Human Affairs Law compliance** (§ 1-13-10 et seq., 15+ employees): If organization has 15+ employees, verify promotion does not discriminate based on race, religion, color, sex, age, national origin, or disability; record promotion in compliance audit trail.

10. **Pregnancy accommodation interaction** (§ 1-13-30(P)): If female employee is pregnant or has pregnancy-related condition, provide reasonable accommodations (flexible schedule, modified duties, seating, etc.); promotion cannot be denied if accommodation is available; offer accommodation in writing.

11. **Handbook disclaimer enforcement** (§ 41-1-110): If handbook contains job-security language or cause-for-termination clause, verify disclaimer is conspicuously underlined capital letters on first page and signed by employee; if no valid disclaimer, implied contract may limit at-will status post-promotion.

12. **Records retention**: Retain all promotion and wage-change documentation for three years per § 41-10-30(C) to support labor commissioner inspection or litigation defense.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether § 41-10-30 "seven days" is calendar days or business days; exact effective-date calculation.
- Confirm scope of "wages" subject to seven-day notice: does it apply to bonus/commission changes if not part of base wage?
- Clarify whether handbook implied-contract language survives a promotion or only applies at hire (case law inconsistent).
- Verify whether E-Verify verification timestamp must be within three business days of hire or may be updated on promotion/rehire.
- Determine whether military service retaliation is prohibited beyond USERRA (no additional state statute identified).
- Verify status of pending paid sick leave or family leave bills in 2026 session (verify § 41-1-27 remains only administrative provision, not mandate).
