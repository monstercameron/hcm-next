# Ohio Employment Law Research

**State:** Ohio | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **At-will employment** with public-policy exceptions: termination unlawful if motivated by workers' comp claim (RC 4123.90), whistleblowing (RC 4113.52), OSHA/occupational safety activities (RC 4111 et seq.), jury duty, military service, or refusal to commit illegal act.
- **Semimonthly pay required** by RC 4113.15: wages for 1st–15th due by end of month; 16th–31st due by 15th of next month; no other statutory pay schedule permitted. Final pay due on next regular payday or within 15 days (whichever sooner).
- **Minimum wage $11.00/hour** (2026, indexed annually per Ohio Constitution Art. II § 34a) for employers with gross receipts >$405,000; small employers pay federal minimum ($7.25). Tipped credit $5.50/hour.
- **Ohio mini-WARN Act** (RC 4113.31, effective Sept 29, 2025): 60-day advance notice to state/local officials + employees required for plant closing or layoff of 50+ at one site if employer has 100+ total employees or ≥4,000 weekly hours.
- **Pay statement mandate** (HB 106 RC 4113.14, effective April 9, 2025): itemized earnings and deductions stubs required each pay period; electronic or paper. Non-compliance: director notice, 10-day cure, complaint to Ohio Dept of Commerce.
- **Paid sick/safe leave** (Issue 1, effective 2025): all private employers must allow earned sick time for illness, family care, domestic violence, public health emergencies. Accrual tied to hours worked; retaliation prohibited.
- **Salary history bans in major cities** (not statewide): Columbus (eff. 2024), Cincinnati, Toledo prohibit wage-history inquiry; pay-range disclosure required post-offer in Cincinnati/Toledo but not Columbus.
- **Equal pay statute** RC 4111.17 prohibits wage discrimination on basis of race, color, religion, sex, age, national origin, ancestry; applies to all employers; private right of action.
- **Discrimination complaints** (RC 4112.02 + HB 352 2021): 2-year limitation period; must exhaust Ohio Civil Rights Commission (OCRC) administrative remedies; protected classes include race, sex, disability, age, national origin, religion, and (as of 2021) sexual orientation, gender identity.
- **No state-mandated paid family/medical leave, no state salary-history ban, no E-Verify mandate outside construction, no personnel-file access statute for private employees, no statewide severance mandate.**

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

At-will employment is the default in Ohio. Either party may terminate without cause, notice, or severance.

**Public-policy exceptions** to at-will termination (Wrongful Discharge in Violation of Public Policy doctrine):

- Termination for filing or pursuing workers' compensation claim or occupational disease benefit (RC 4123.90; 90-day notice requirement to employer).
- Termination for reporting occupational safety or health violations to OSHA or state (RC 4111 et seq. wage/hour protections; Ohio Occupational Safety and Health Act).
- Termination for jury duty, military service, or voting.
- Termination for refusing to commit illegal act (common law).
- Termination for exercising statutory rights (whistleblowing under RC 4113.52; wage/overtime claims).
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (speech, assembly, petition).

**Implied contract**: Employment handbooks or offer letters may create implied just-cause-termination clauses; courts evaluate whether employer policies or conduct created reasonable expectation of job security beyond at-will status.

**Wage-theft prevention and wage-change notice** (RC 4113.15): Employers must provide written notice of regular paydays (semimonthly requirement, on or before 1st and 15th) at hire. Any change to payday must be communicated to affected employees in advance. No statutory minimum notice period for advance notification; best practice is 1+ pay period.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (RC 4111.02; Ohio Constitution Art. II § 34a):

- **$11.00/hour** (2026) for employers with annual gross receipts exceeding $405,000.
- **$7.25/hour** (federal minimum) for employers with gross receipts ≤$405,000.
- Tipped employees: $5.50/hour; tips must bring total to applicable minimum.
- Annual adjustment effective Jan 1, tied to Consumer Price Index for urban wage earners and clerical workers (CPI-W), rounded to nearest $0.01; no cap on increases.
- No local minimum-wage authority; political subdivisions may not set rates different from state constitutional mandate (RC 4111.02).

**Overtime** (RC 4111.03; SB 47 2022):

- Federal FLSA standard applies: 1.5× regular rate for hours over 40/week.
- Travel time to/from worksite exempt; Portal-to-Portal Act (29 U.S.C. § 251 et seq.) incorporated by RC 4111.03.
- Except: travel during regularly scheduled work hours or at specific employer direction remains compensable.
- Compensatory time-off not permitted in lieu of overtime pay (unless employer is political subdivision per FLSA).

**Pay frequency** (RC 4113.15):

- Semimonthly mandatory: wages for work done 1st–15th of month due by last day of that month; 16th–31st due by 15th of following month.
- Payday must be on/before designated date; cannot be suspended or delayed.
- Employer may pay more frequently (weekly, biweekly) by written contract; does not eliminate semimonthly requirement unless contract explicitly provides.

**Final pay on termination** (RC 4113.15):

- On discharge or layoff: all earned wages due on next regular payday or within 15 days of separation, whichever is sooner.
- On resignation: all earned wages due on next regular payday.
- If employee absent on payday, wages due upon demand at usual payroll location.
- **Penalties**: If wages unpaid >30 days after payday (or >60 days if no regular payday), employer liable for greater of 6% of unpaid wages or $200, plus attorneys' fees (RC 4113.15(B); first-degree misdemeanor).

**Permitted deductions** (RC 4113.15):

- Legally required deductions (taxes, garnishment, court orders, child support).
- Deductions authorized in writing by employee for lawful purpose (union dues, insurance, retirement, charitable contribution).
- No deduction for employer-required uniforms, tools, equipment, or supplies unless authorized in writing and does not reduce wages below minimum wage.
- No deduction from final wages for damage/loss unless employee at fault and written authorization exists.

**Pay statement content** (RC 4113.14, HB 106 effective 4/9/2025):

- Employers must provide written or electronic statement with each regular payday.
- Must include: employee name, employer name and address, gross pay, total hours worked (hourly employees), pay rate and rates, pay-period dates, **itemized list of all additions to and deductions** (taxes, insurance, garnishments, withholdings, etc.), net pay.
- Hourly employees also require: overtime hours (>40/week).
- Electronic statements must be accessible, printable.
- Non-compliance: employee submits written request; employer has 10 days to cure or violates; employee may report to Ohio Dept of Commerce for director enforcement action.

**Records retention** (RC 4111.08):

- Employers must maintain for ≥3 years: name, address, occupation, pay rate, amount paid per pay period, hours worked daily, other information prescribed by director.
- Records subject to inspection by director at reasonable times; labor commissioner may demand records for criminal charges (2-year window) or civil action (4-year window).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary-history inquiry ban** (municipal ordinances, not statewide):

- **No state law bans** wage-history inquiry; Ohio employers may ask about prior compensation.
- **Columbus ordinance** (effective March 1, 2024): employers with ≥15 employees prohibited from inquiring about applicant's wage history. Violating is unlawful discriminatory practice; civil fine up to $5,000/violation. Does not require pay-range disclosure post-offer.
- **Cincinnati ordinance** (2020): prohibits wage-history inquiry; requires pay-range disclosure upon conditional offer.
- **Toledo ordinance** (2019): prohibits wage-history inquiry; requires pay-range disclosure upon conditional offer.
- **Federal FCRA** applies to all background-check usage; no additional Ohio restrictions.

**Equal-pay statute** (RC 4111.17):

- Applies to all employers in Ohio.
- Unlawful to pay wages to one employee at rate less than another for equal work (equal skill, effort, responsibility, similar working conditions) based on race, color, religion, sex, age, national origin, or ancestry.
- Exceptions: seniority systems, merit systems, production-quality/quantity systems, or wage-rate differential based on factor other than protected class.
- Retaliation prohibited for asserting equal-pay claim or testifying in proceeding.
- Private right of action; damages include back pay, liquidated damages, attorneys' fees.

**Pay equity reporting**:

- No state mandate for pay-equity audit or reporting (no counterpart to California, Illinois, or other state equity initiatives).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Ohio Earned Sick and Safe Leave Act** (Issue 1, effective 2025):

- Applies to all private employers in Ohio; no size threshold.
- Employees accrue 1 hour of paid leave per 30 hours worked; no cap on accrual or carryover (verify if employers may limit usage per year).
- Permitted uses: employee's own physical/mental illness, medical diagnosis/care/treatment, child's school/childcare absences, domestic abuse, sexual assault, stalking, public health emergency.
- Employer may not require employee to use other paid leave before sick leave.
- Employer may not retaliate (discharge, discipline, penalty in performance evaluation) for requesting/using sick leave.
- Employer must comply or face director enforcement; employee may file complaint with Ohio Dept of Commerce.

**Federal FMLA**:

- Applies to employers with ≥50 employees, employees with ≥12 months service and 1,250 hours in past 12 months; covers birth, adoption, child/parent/spouse illness, military caregiver leave, military exigency leave.
- Up to 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave per 12-month period.
- Ohio does not mandate state paid family/medical leave supplement; employers may voluntarily provide.

**Pregnancy and maternity**:

- Ohio anti-discrimination statute (RC 4112.02) covers pregnancy as sex discrimination (4+ employee threshold for coverage; federal PDA requires 15+ but Ohio is broader).
- Employer cannot terminate employee due to pregnancy or temporary disability from pregnancy/childbirth (RC 4112-5-05 administrative rule).
- Policy excluding pregnant workers from employment is prima facie sex discrimination.
- Maternity-leave policies must account for individual medical status and capacity; insufficient/no maternity leave leading to termination = unlawful sex discrimination.
- Federal Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (PWFA, 42 U.S.C. § 2000gg et seq., effective June 27, 2023) allows reasonable accommodations (light duty, modified schedule, etc.) without retaliation; Ohio employers covered.
- No state-mandated paid maternity leave.

**Military leave** (RC 5903; RC 5923):

- Public employees: leave of absence to enter military service; reinstatement without loss of seniority or pay upon not being accepted.
- State employees: paid military leave up to one month per federal fiscal year for active-duty service in uniformed services (National Guard, reserve).
- Private-sector military leave: federal USERRA (38 U.S.C. § 4311 et seq.) governs reemployment rights; Ohio has no additional private-sector requirement.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- If employee promoted while on paid sick leave, wage protection applies: upon return, wage must not be reduced below promoted rate.
- If employee uses paid leave during pay-period of promotion, promote wages from effective date forward regardless of leave period.
- Paid-leave accrual continues during paid leave time (verify if employer policy may differ); does not reset on promotion.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention** (RC 4111.08):

- Minimum 3-year retention for employee name, address, occupation, pay rate, hours worked daily, amounts paid per pay period.
- Records must be made available for inspection by director or authorized representatives at reasonable times.
- Director may demand records within 2-year window for criminal investigation, 4-year window for civil action.
- No format requirement (electronic, paper, or hybrid acceptable).

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- **No general right**: Ohio does not mandate that private-sector employers maintain or provide access to personnel files (contrast with public-sector employees under Ohio Public Records Act, RC 149.43).
- **Medical records exception** (RC 4113.23): Employers must provide copies of any medical report required as condition of employment or arising from job-related injury/disease. Includes physician reports, lab/hospital test results from employer-mandated exams. Employee or representative may request without charge. Employer may charge up to $0.25/page for copies. Refusal is minor misdemeanor per violation.
- **Wage and hour records**: Employees may request copies within 30 business days of written, notarized demand (per RC 4111.14).

**Record content and format**:

- No state mandate for itemized pay-stub format prior to HB 106 (April 9, 2025); RC 4113.14 now requires itemization of earnings and deductions on every statement.
- Employers should maintain daily/weekly hours, rate of pay, deductions, gross and net pay per RC 4111.08.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Breach Notification, Monitoring

**Data breach notification** (RC 1349.19):

- Applies to any person/business owning or licensing computerized personal information of Ohio residents.
- Notification required if unauthorized access/acquisition compromises security and is reasonably likely to result in identity theft or fraud.
- Timing: most expedient manner possible, but no later than 45 days after discovery or notification of breach.
- Notification recipients: affected individuals; if ≥1,000 Ohio residents affected, also notify consumer reporting agencies (Equifax, Experian, TransUnion).
- **Safe harbor**: No notification required if personal information encrypted and encryption keys not compromised.
- Penalties: up to $10,000/day for knowing or reckless violation; up to $150,000 for failure to notify when required.
- Enforcement: Ohio Attorney General and individuals through private right of action.

**Employee privacy—monitoring, social media**:

- No state statute banning workplace email, internet, or video monitoring; reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine (common law) applies.
- Employers may monitor work-provided devices and work-time usage without restriction (verify case law on personal device use during work hours).
- No prohibition on social-media monitoring or consideration of publicly available content in employment decisions (subject to anti-discrimination laws).
- Employer may not discriminate based on lawful off-duty conduct; Ohio has no lawful-off-duty-conduct or tobacco-use statute for private employers (no state rule; federal law applies).

**Data residency and processing**:

- No Ohio law mandating data residency for employee records; out-of-state storage/processing permitted if breach notification requirements and security standards met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box for criminal history** (HB 56 2015 for public employers; limited private-sector coverage):

- **Public employers**: may not ask about criminal background on initial application; background checks permitted after initial interview.
- **Private employers**: Ohio has no statewide ban for private sector. Exceptions exist in major cities:
  - Cincinnati ordinance (2010): private employers cannot ask about criminal history on initial application.
  - Columbus ordinance (effective 2024): private employers with ≥15 employees cannot inquire about criminal record on initial application.
  - Toledo ordinance: private employers cannot inquire about criminal history on initial application.
- **For covered employers**: Criminal history may be considered later in hiring process after discussion with applicant; consideration must be individualized and job-related (verify specificity of municipal ordinances).

**Background checks** (federal FCRA applies):

- Employers using third-party background providers must comply with FCRA notice (disclosure + authorization before running check), dispute procedures, adverse-action procedures.
- No Ohio-specific restrictions beyond FCRA.
- Annulled/sealed/expunged records cannot be used against applicant.

**Drug testing** (RC 2925):

- **No state law restricting** private-sector drug testing; employers have broad discretion.
- Permissible scenarios: pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, random (per written policy).
- Ohio Bur. of Workers' Comp (BWC) Drug-Free Safety Program (voluntary) offers incentives for drug-free workplace certification; not mandatory.
- Policy should be written, communicated, and consistently applied.
- Medical marijuana (Ohio Medical Marijuana Control Program, R.C. ch. 3796; R.C. 3796.28): employers need not accommodate use, may maintain drug-free workplace policies, and may discipline or refuse to hire for use (verify scope of protection).

**E-Verify mandate**:

- **No statewide private-sector mandate**. Federal I-9 requirements apply to all employers (Form I-9 completion for new hires).
- **Construction exception** (2026): Effective March 19, 2026, nonresidential construction employers (contractors, subcontractors, labor brokers) must enroll and verify all covered-project workers in E-Verify. Covers office buildings, highways, bridges, utilities, infrastructure; excludes residential, agricultural, mobile-home construction.
- **Construction penalties**: $250–$1,500 for first failure to enroll; $25,000+ per violation for repeat noncompliance; disqualification from state contracts (2+ years) for willful violations; permanent license revocation for knowingly hiring unauthorized workers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mini-WARN Act—plant closing and mass layoff** (RC 4113.31, effective Sept 29, 2025):

- **Coverage**: Employers with ≥100 employees (excluding certain part-time) OR ≥100 employees with combined ≥4,000 weekly work hours (excluding overtime).
- **Trigger**: Plant closing (≥50 employees losing jobs within 30 days) OR mass layoff (≥50 employees at single site losing jobs within 30-day period).
- **Notice period**: Minimum 60 days' advance written notice.
- **Recipients**: Affected employees (or union representatives); Ohio Director of Job and Family Services; chief elected officials of affected municipal corporation and county.
- **Notice content**: Must comply with federal WARN Act requirements; Ohio adds requirement for "detailed statement explaining reason" for closure or layoff; information about support available to impacted employees.
- **Penalties**: Affected employees entitled to back pay for each violation day (up to 60 days); civil penalties up to $500/day per violation for employer.

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance. Severance is purely contractual; requires explicit agreement (written or implied through handbook/offer letter).

**Whistleblower protection** (RC 4113.52):

- Applies to all employers in Ohio; protects disclosure of violations of state/federal statute, ordinance, regulation, work rule, or company policy reasonably believed to be criminal offense or likely to cause imminent risk of physical harm or hazard to public health/safety.
- Protected disclosures include criminal offenses, felonies by employer or coworker, improper solicitation for contributions, environmental violations.
- **Procedure**: Requires "reasonable and good faith effort to determine accuracy" of reported information; specific statutory reporting procedures must be followed (internal report to supervisor/HR, then external if not remedied; or direct report to relevant authority if imminent danger).
- **Retaliation prohibition**: Employer may not discharge, demote, suspend, threaten, harass, or discriminate against employee for protected disclosure.
- **Remedies**: Reinstatement, back pay (wages and benefits), front pay if reinstatement infeasible, attorneys' fees.

**Workers' compensation retaliation** (RC 4123.90):

- Employer may not discharge, demote, reassign, or take punitive action against employee for filing workers' comp claim or occupational disease claim.
- Protection applies regardless of whether injury actually occurred; only relevant fact is that employee pursued claim.
- **Notice requirement**: Employee must give employer notice within 90 days of retaliatory action or lose right to claim.
- **Remedies**: Reinstatement, back pay, attorneys' fees.

**Non-compete enforceability** (common law per _Raimonde v. Van Vlerah_, 42 Ohio St.2d 21, 325 N.E.2d 544 (1975)):

- Ohio applies reasonableness test for non-compete validity: agreement must be
  - (1) no greater than required to protect employer's legitimate business interest (trade secrets, customer relationships, substantial relationships of personal character);
  - (2) not impose undue hardship on employee;
  - (3) not injurious to public.
- Courts use "blue-pencil" doctrine: may modify overbroad provision to make reasonable (duration, geographic scope, line of business) rather than striking entirely.
- No statutory safe harbor; case-by-case evaluation of reasonableness required.
- Typical enforced agreements: 1–2 year duration, defined geographic territory, reasonable scope of restricted activities.

**Non-solicitation** (common law):

- No specific statute; enforced under common-law reasonableness standard (duration, scope, legitimate business interest). Courts apply similar _Raimonde_ framework as non-competes.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- **ABC Test** (common law): Ohio courts apply this test, though federal (IRS, FLSA), state (BWC), and various regulatory agencies each have separate tests.
- Prongs: (A) worker free from control/direction contractually and in fact; (B) work outside usual course of employer's business or outside all established places of business; (C) worker customarily engaged in independently established trade/business of same nature.
- Misclassification enforcement by Ohio Department of Commerce (Wage and Hour), Department of Taxation, BWC, and Attorney General; penalties include reclassification, back wages, penalties.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute governing remote-work classification or treatment.
- Standard employment law applies regardless of work location; if work is performed in Ohio (remote or on-site), Ohio law applies.
- Multi-state remote workers: each state's law applies to work performed in that state; employers should ensure compliance with all applicable state rules.

**Multi-state employment reciprocity**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws independently govern work performed within that state.
- Employers may designate state law governing overall employment relationship by written agreement (recommended for multi-state workers).

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints for promotion or base-pay change**:

1. **Pay frequency and payday change** (RC 4113.15): System must flag if promotion/pay change coincides with payday change. Semimonthly schedule (1st/15th) is mandatory; any change to regular pay schedule must be communicated to employee in advance. No statutory advance notice period; best practice is ≥1 pay-period notice.

2. **Base-pay change notice and timing** (RC 4113.15): For wage increase, employer may implement immediately (no advance notice required by statute). For wage decrease, must provide written advance notice prior to effective date (best practice: 1+ pay period); capture written acknowledgment in system.

3. **Minimum wage verification**: On any promotion or pay change, system must verify wage meets applicable minimum wage:
   - **Large employer** (gross receipts >$405,000): $11.00/hour (2026; indexed annually Jan 1).
   - **Small employer** (gross receipts ≤$405,000): $7.25/hour (federal minimum).
   - Tipped minimum: $5.50/hour (tips must bring total to applicable minimum).
   - Flag if promotion wage falls below applicable minimum; require manager justification or HR override.

4. **Final pay compliance on separation** (RC 4113.15): If termination occurs within promotion period, ensure all wages at promoted rate paid within 15 days of separation (or on next regular payday if sooner); capture in system. Failure to pay subjects employer to liquidated damages (greater of 6% unpaid wages or $200) plus attorneys' fees.

5. **Equal-pay statute compliance** (RC 4111.17): On promotion or pay change, system should flag if new wage for one gender deviates substantially from peer rates for equal work. No tolerance specified in statute; best practice is to require HR/legal review for >10–15% outlier increases or decreases. Apply to all protected classes (race, color, religion, sex, age, national origin, ancestry).

6. **Paid sick leave interaction** (Issue 1 effective 2025): On promotion + pay change, if employee uses sick leave before or during promotion period, system must ensure wage upon return is not reduced below promoted rate. Sick-leave accrual continues during paid leave; no reduction in accrual on promotion (verify if employer policy allows selective accrual adjustments).

7. **Final pay on resignation** (RC 4113.15): If employee resigns after promotion, all wages earned at promoted rate from effective date forward must be paid on next regular payday (not held pending separation date); system must recalculate final check to reflect promoted wage for worked period.

8. **Record retention** (RC 4111.08): System must retain promotion documentation (offer, effective date, wage-change justification, HR approval, equal-pay analysis if conducted) for ≥3 years to support compliance with director inspection or labor commissioner demand.

9. **Non-compete enforceability review** (common law _Raimonde_): On promotion to non-compete-restricted role, system should identify if employee subject to non-compete clause; flag for legal review on whether new role/compensation level renders agreement unreasonable (verify whether promotion to higher-paying role strengthens reasonableness or undercuts it under Ohio case law).

10. **Implied contract analysis**: On promotion, if employee handbook or offer letter contains language promising "employment for cause only," "job security," or "career advancement," flag for legal review to determine if implied contract limits at-will status and what remedies apply if termination follows promotion.

11. **Pay-statement compliance** (HB 106 RC 4113.14, effective 4/9/2025): On promotion effective mid-pay-period, system must ensure pay stub issued on next regular payday itemizes both pre- and post-promotion wages separately (or clearly identifies promotion date and wage change on stub). Failure to provide compliant stub triggers 10-day cure window; non-compliance is reportable to Ohio Dept of Commerce.

12. **Severance or final-pay policy interaction**: If promotion triggers severance clause or enhanced final-pay obligation (per handbook or contract), system must identify and calculate correctly (e.g., severance based on tenure, title, or wage level). No state law mandates severance, so obligations are contractual only.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Clarify whether "equal work" under RC 4111.17 requires identical duties or substantially similar responsibilities and whether comparison scope is same site, same department, or company-wide.
- Confirm whether wage increase on promotion to address past pay inequality satisfies equal-pay statute or creates additional disclosure/notice obligations (e.g., must employer acknowledge historical underpayment?).
- Verify whether _Raimonde_ reasonableness analysis for non-competes is strengthened or weakened by promotion and wage increase (case law on this point not searched exhaustively).
- Confirm whether implied-contract job-security promises survive promotion or are limited to hire date (verify _Vigil v. Arzola_ or successor doctrine).
- Clarify accrual treatment of paid sick leave during unpaid leave or leave taken concurrently with promotion (policy-dependent; statute does not address).
