# Delaware Employment Law Research

**State:** Delaware | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will with implied covenant**: Delaware recognizes employment-at-will with an implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing exception (common law; no statutory codification).
- **Minimum wage $15.00/hr**: Effective 2025-01-01 (19 Del. C. § 902); tipped $2.23; no state overtime rule (FLSA applies).
- **Salary history ban**: Employers may not inquire about or seek prior compensation history (19 Del. C. § 709B, effective 2017-12-14).
- **Personnel file access**: Employees have right to inspect personnel files during reasonable business hours; penalties $1,000–$5,000 per refusal (19 Del. C. §§ 730–735).
- **Delaware Paid Leave (FMLA)**: 10–24 employees parental only; 25+ parental, family caregiving, medical; 12 weeks/yr parental, 6 weeks/24 months medical/caregiving; 80% AWW, $100–$900/wk (2026–2027); benefits payable from 2026-01-01.
- **Mini-WARN (100+ employees)**: 60 days' notice for mass layoff (50+ and 33% or 500+) or plant closing (50+); penalties $1,000/day or $100/day/worker; back pay up to 60 days (19 Del. C. ch. 19).
- **Service Worker Protection**: 15 days' notice, 90-day retention, just-cause during transition (19 Del. C. ch. 20); penalties $2,500–$5,000.
- **Breach notification**: 60-day notice required if personal information breached; if 500+ residents affected, notify Attorney General; free credit monitoring if SSN involved (6 Del. C. § 12B-102).
- **Ban-the-box (public only)**: Public employers cannot inquire into criminal record before first interview; private employers unaffected.
- **No state paid sick leave**: No mandate beyond federal FLSA.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Delaware is an employment-at-will state; either party may terminate without cause.

**Implied covenant exception**:

- Delaware recognizes an implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing in employment relationships (common law doctrine). Specific statutory exceptions are limited.

**Required written notices at hire and on change**:

- No Delaware statute mandates written notice of at-will status, pay rate, or hours at hire (federal FLSA poster and wage-and-hour rules apply).
- On pay or role change: No specific statutory notice period required; employers should document changes in writing to avoid misunderstanding.
- Wage theft prevention notice: Delaware does not have a specific wage theft prevention notice requirement; federal FLSA applies.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- State minimum wage is $15.00/hr effective 2025-01-01 (19 Del. C. § 902).
- Tipped workers: $2.23/hr; tips must make up the difference to reach minimum wage.
- No subminimum wage allowed.

**Overtime**:

- No state overtime rule; federal FLSA applies (1.5x regular rate for hours over 40/week).

**Pay frequency**:

- Wages must be paid at least monthly (19 Del. C. § 1102).
- Payment method: cash, check, or direct deposit (with written consent).

**Final pay on termination**:

- On layoff: Full wages due on next regular payday (19 Del. C. § 1103).
- On discharge or resignation: Full wages due within 7 days of period close, but not later than next regular payday unless employee fails to provide notice (verify current case law on notice requirement).

**Permitted deductions**:

- Employers cannot withhold wages except as required by law, court order, or with written employee authorization for cash advance, goods, or services (19 Del. C. § 1107).
- No deductions for damaged/unreturned property; such withholding violates § 1107.
- Authorized deductions: tax withholding, garnishment, lawful withholding agreements.

**Pay statement requirements**:

- Employers must provide written pay statement showing pay period, total hours worked, rate of pay, gross wages, and all deductions (19 Del. C. § 1108).
- Records must be maintained for at least 3 years (19 Del. C. § 1108).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**:

- No state requirement to disclose wage range in job postings or to applicants; federal FLSA applies.

**Salary history ban**:

- Employers may not screen applicants based on compensation history or seek compensation history from applicants or prior employers (19 Del. C. § 709B, effective 2017-12-14).
- Applicants may voluntarily disclose prior compensation; employer and applicant may negotiate without employer requesting history.
- Violations enforced by Delaware Department of Labor, Office of Wage & Hour Enforcement.

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state-level pay equity reporting requirement; federal EEO-1 applies if applicable.

**Equal pay statute**:

- Delaware does not have a state equal-pay-for-equal-work statute independent of federal equal-pay law; Title VII and EPA apply.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**:

- No state-mandated paid sick leave requirement; employees covered only if employer voluntarily provides it.

**Delaware Paid Leave (Family and Medical Leave Insurance, 19 Del. C. ch. 37)**:

- **Eligibility**: 12 months tenure, 1,250 hours worked in prior 12 months.
- **Employer size thresholds**:
  - 10–24 employees: parental leave only (12 weeks/yr).
  - 25+ employees: parental, family caregiving, medical leave (12 weeks/yr parental; 6 weeks/24 months medical or family caregiving).
- **Benefit rate**: 80% of average weekly wage, minimum $100/wk, maximum $900/wk (2026–2027 rates; indexed annually).
- **Contributions and payability**: Contributions started 2025-01-01; benefits payable from 2026-01-01.
- **Interaction with pay/role change**: Employee returning from leave must be restored to same or equivalent position; wages must not be reduced below contractual entitlement.

**Paid leave accrual during unpaid leave**: Verify whether accrual continues during unpaid leave periods (likely yes, but confirm).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll record retention**:

- Employers must maintain payroll records for at least 3 years, documenting employee name, address, occupation, hours worked, wage information, and deductions (19 Del. C. § 1108).

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- Employees have the right to inspect their own personnel files at reasonable time during regular business hours (19 Del. C. §§ 730–735).
- **File contents**: Application, salary/wage information, commendations, discipline notices, deduction authorizations, benefits info, leave records, employment history, performance evaluations, medical records.
- **Exclusions**: Criminal investigations, third-party reference letters, pending legal proceedings, other confidential materials.
- **Inspection**: Once per year unless employee justifies additional access; may take notes but cannot remove file.
- **Dispute**: If employee disputes file contents, employer must allow written statement to accompany file; removal/correction requires mutual agreement.
- **Penalties**: Employers refusing access face civil penalties of $1,000–$5,000 per violation; retaliation against complainants also prohibited.

**Record format**:

- No Delaware statute requires electronic vs. paper; employers may use either if retention and access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Delaware Personal Data Privacy Act (6 Del. C. ch. 12D)**:

- Effective 2025-01-01; applies to controllers and processors of personal data (personal information and sensitive data).
- **Employee data applicability**: Verify whether employee data falls under DDPA or whether exemption applies to employment relationships (many state privacy laws exempt employment data; Delaware's status is uncertain pending case law or agency guidance).
- **Sensitive data** includes biometric data (fingerprint, voice print, retina/iris image); controllers must obtain consumer opt-in consent.

**Electronic monitoring**:

- No Delaware statute restricts employer email, internet, or general video monitoring of work areas.
- Federal reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine and ECPA apply.

**Breach notification**:

- Delaware requires notification to affected Delaware residents "without unreasonable delay but not later than 60 days after determination of breach" (6 Del. C. § 12B-102).
- **Personal information definition**: First name/initial + last name combined with account number, credit/debit card, access code, security code, or password to financial account; **SSN breaches require free credit monitoring for 1 year**.
- **Attorney General notification**: If 500+ residents affected, notify AG by breach notification deadline.
- **Substitute notice**: For breaches exceeding $75,000 cost or 100,000+ residents, organizations may use substitute notice (website posting, media, social media).

**Data residency**:

- No Delaware statute mandates data residency; employers may store/process employee records in-state or out-of-state.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- **Public employers**: Cannot inquire into criminal record during initial application or before first interview (executive order and legislation effective 2014); may inquire post-conditional offer.
- **Private employers**: No Delaware ban-the-box law for private sector; federal EEOC guidance applies.

**Background checks**:

- No Delaware statute restricts private employers; federal FCRA applies.

**Drug testing**:

- No Delaware state-level drug-testing regulation for private employers; federal law (DOT, reasonable suspicion) applies.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No Delaware state mandate for private-sector E-Verify; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice (mini-WARN, 19 Del. C. ch. 19)**:

- **Employers with 100+ employees** must provide 60 days' notice of mass layoff, plant closing, or major relocation (50+ miles).
- **Mass layoff definition**: Permanent layoff affecting 50+ employees and 33% of workforce, or 500+ employees.
- **Plant closing**: Shutdown affecting 50+ employees.
- **Notice recipients**: Affected employees, Delaware Department of Labor Division of Employment & Training, Workforce Development Board.
- **Penalties**: $1,000/day or $100/day/worker; back pay up to 60 days.

**Severance**:

- No Delaware general severance mandate; federal WARN Act (60 days' notice) applies concurrently to qualifying employers.

**Service Worker Protection Act (19 Del. C. ch. 20)**:

- Contractors must provide 15 days' notice before terminating services.
- Successor contractors must retain service workers for 90 days minimum on same terms; just-cause protection during transition.
- Penalties: $2,500–$5,000 per violation.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- **General rule**: Delaware common law permits reasonable non-competes (employer must show legitimate business interest: trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information).
- **Physician exception (6 Del. C. § 2707)**: Non-compete covenants between physicians are void; covenants not to practice in a particular locale/duration are unenforceable. Damages may be recoverable if reasonably related to injury from termination, but injunctive relief unavailable.
- **Blue-pencil doctrine**: Delaware courts may modify overbroad restrictive covenants to make them enforceable in limited circumstances, but availability depends on factual context (e.g., business sale, equal bargaining power). Verify recent 2024–2025 Chancery decisions on blue-pencilling trends.

**Non-solicitation**:

- Enforced under common-law reasonableness standard; less scrutiny than non-competes if scope, duration, geography, and customer base are reasonable.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Delaware uses the common-law "control test" and related factors to distinguish contractors from employees; no statutory ABC test.
- Misclassification enforcement by Delaware Department of Labor and Workers' Compensation Commission.

**Remote workers**:

- No Delaware statute specifically addresses remote work; standard employment law applies.
- **Tax treatment**: Delaware has no reciprocity with any state. Remote workers for Delaware employers must file Delaware tax returns on Delaware-source income; convenience-of-employer rule does NOT apply.
- If employee works from another state (not required, but elected), that day may be claimed as outside Delaware only if employer directed or strongly encouraged remote work.
- Delaware residents working in other states may claim credit against Delaware tax liability for taxes paid to other states.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; if employee works across state lines, platform should flag to ensure compliance with each state's laws.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

**Required typed obligations and timing constraints**:

1. **Salary history inquiry ban**: Block or flag any form field requesting prior salary/compensation history from external candidates; document that inquiry prohibition applies under § 709B.
2. **Minimum wage**: Enforce $15.00/hr floor; tipped positions default to $2.23/hr and require tip-credit verification.
3. **Final pay timing**: On layoff, calculate full wages due on next regular payday; on termination, verify whether 7-day rule or next payday applies (confirm current Delaware case law).
4. **Pay frequency**: Default to monthly pay; document employer's chosen payday.
5. **Delaware Paid Leave eligibility**: Gate 12-week parental leave and 6-week medical/caregiving leave to employees with 12+ months tenure, 1,250+ prior-year hours, and employer headcount (10–24 for parental only; 25+ for all three reasons); track accrual and usage in rolling 24-month window for medical/caregiving.
6. **Benefit rate capping**: 80% of AWW, min $100/wk, max $900/wk (2026–2027); auto-update annually per Delaware DPA guidance.
7. **Personnel file access**: Flag if employee requests file inspection; ensure compliance with 30-day response requirement (verify statutory timeline) and document that inspections limited to once/year.
8. **Mini-WARN trigger**: When 50+ employees laid off within 3-month period at single location, trigger 60-day notice workflow and penalty calculation ($1,000/day or $100/worker/day).
9. **Service Worker Protection**: If using successor contractors, track 90-day retention obligation and just-cause protection requirement post-15-day notice.
10. **Breach notification**: If employee personal information (SSN, account, access code) is breached, trigger 60-day notification timer; if 500+ residents affected, auto-notify Attorney General; if SSN involved, provision 1 year credit monitoring.
11. **Salary history in offers**: Block or flag salary history inquiry in offer-generation flow; document that all compensation offered is based on role, qualifications, and market data, NOT prior history.

**Field restrictions and validations**:

- Prior compensation field: Locked/hidden unless applicant voluntarily discloses.
- Wage range in offer letter: Not required by Delaware; optional but recommended to align with § 709B intent.
- Overtime classification: Default non-exempt; exempt categories (executive, professional, outside sales, agricultural) require documentation.
- Personnel file retention: Enforce 3-year minimum retention in system.
