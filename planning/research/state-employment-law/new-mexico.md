# New Mexico Employment Law Research

**State:** New Mexico | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment**: New Mexico recognizes employment-at-will with public-policy exceptions for workers' compensation claims, OSHA complaints, whistleblowing, jury duty, and anti-discrimination (implied contracts also recognized).
- **Semimonthly pay required**: Employers must designate regular paydays not more than 16 days apart (NMSA 1978 § 50-4-2); semimonthly payments due by last day of month (for days 1-15) or 15th of next month (for days 16-31).
- **Final pay timing**: On discharge, fixed wages due within 5 days; other compensation within 10 days (§ 50-4-4). On resignation, final pay due at next regular payday (§ 50-4-5).
- **Statewide minimum wage $12.00/hour**: Local ordinances in Santa Fe ($15.00), Albuquerque ($12.00), Las Cruces ($13.01), and Bernalillo County ($12.00) set higher minimums; employers must pay the highest applicable rate (NMSA 1978 § 50-4-22).
- **Paid Sick Leave (Healthy Workplaces Act)**: 1 hour per 30 hours worked, capped at 64 hours/year carry-over (§ 50-17-1 et seq., effective July 1, 2022); usable for employee's own illness, family medical care, school/childcare issues, domestic abuse, sexual assault, stalking.
- **No state paid family/medical leave mandate**: HB 11 (2025) failed in Senate; federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees and other conditions met.
- **Expanded protected classes**: NMSA 1978 § 28-1-7 prohibits discrimination based on race, age, religion, color, national origin, ancestry, sex, sexual orientation, gender, gender identity, pregnancy-related conditions, disability, genetic information, military status, and (for 50+ employee employers) spousal affiliation.
- **Ban-the-box for private employers**: § 28-2-3.1 prohibits criminal history inquiry on initial application; may be considered after application review and discussion.
- **Non-competes restricted for healthcare**: § 50-4A-1 voids post-employment non-competes for physicians, dentists, podiatrists, certified registered nurse anesthetists, certified nurse practitioners, certified nurse-midwives, psychologists, physician assistants, and pharmacists; 1-year non-solicitation and reasonable liquidated damages permitted.
- **No state salary-history ban, no pay-transparency posting mandate, no personnel-file access statute, no mini-WARN, E-Verify not mandated**.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

New Mexico follows the at-will employment doctrine: both employers and employees may terminate the relationship at any time, without cause or notice.

**Public-policy exceptions** to at-will termination exist when discharge violates a clear mandate of public policy:

- Termination for filing workers' compensation claim or seeking occupational disease benefits.
- Termination for exercising occupational safety and health rights (NMSA 1978 § 50-9-1 et seq., New Mexico Occupational Safety and Health Act).
- Termination for exercising rights under federal FLSA or state wage laws.
- Termination for whistleblowing (reporting illegal conduct or violations of law to law enforcement).
- Termination for jury duty, military service, or voting.
- Termination for refusal to perform illegal act.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religion).
- Termination in violation of written or implied employment contract (implied by handbook, oral promises, course of conduct).

**Implied contract**: An employment contract may be implied through written materials (handbooks, policies), oral promises, and course of conduct; such contracts may limit at-will status.

**Written notice of wage changes** (§ 50-4-2): Employers must notify employees in writing of regular paydays (not more than 16 days apart) at hire; changes to paydays must be communicated in advance to all affected employees.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (§ 50-4-22):

- Statewide minimum wage: $12.00/hour (2026).
- Local minimums (must pay highest applicable): Santa Fe City $15.00/hour (eff. Mar 1, 2025); Las Cruces $13.01/hour; Albuquerque $12.00/hour; Bernalillo County $12.00/hour.
- Tipped employees: Employers may pay cash wage less than minimum provided tips plus wages meet or exceed minimum wage.
- No state overtime standard; federal FLSA applies (1.5x regular rate for hours over 40/week, or 12/day in New Mexico with certain exceptions under common law).

**Pay frequency** (§ 50-4-2):

- Employers must designate regular paydays not more than 16 days apart.
- Semimonthly requirement: wages for services from 1st–15th of month due by last day of that month; wages for 16th–31st due by 15th of following month.
- No requirement for specific day of week; must be consistent and predictable.

**Final pay on termination** (§ 50-4-4, § 50-4-5):

- On discharge: Fixed and definite wages (not based on task, commission, or calculation method) due immediately on demand or within 5 days of discharge; other compensation within 10 days of discharge.
- On resignation: All wages due at next regular payday.
- Failure to pay subjects employer to damages in civil action.

**Permitted deductions** (§ 50-4-3):

- Deductions only if required/empowered by law (taxes, garnishments, court orders) or authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes (union dues, health insurance, retirement, charitable contributions, other voluntary deductions).
- No deduction for tools, uniforms, or employer-required costs unless employee consents in writing and deduction does not reduce wages below minimum wage.
- No deduction from final wages for lost or damaged items if employee not at fault.

**Pay statement content**:

- No state-mandated itemized pay-stub format; federal FLSA requirements and record-keeping obligations under § 50-4-9 apply (employers should document hours worked, wage rate, deductions, net pay).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**:

- No state mandate for job-posting salary ranges or wage disclosure at hire.
- No prohibition on inquiring about prior salary/wages; employers may request applicants' wage history.
- Local ordinances in Santa Fe, Albuquerque, Las Cruces, or Bernalillo County do not impose additional pay-transparency requirements.

**Fair Pay for Women Act** (NMSA 1978 § 28-23-1 et seq., effective July 1, 2013):

- Applies to employers with 4+ employees.
- Prohibits paying employees of one sex at lower rates than opposite sex for equal work requiring equal skill, effort, responsibility, and similar working conditions.
- Exceptions: seniority systems, merit systems, production-quality systems, demonstrable factor other than sex.
- Retaliation prohibited for asserting a claim under the Act.
- Employer may not reduce other employees' wages to comply.

**Equal pay enforcement**:

- Human Rights Bureau (NMSA 1978 § 28-1-1 et seq.) enforces equal pay under broader discrimination framework.
- Private right of action available; recoverable damages include back pay, liquidated damages, attorneys' fees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Healthy Workplaces Act—Paid Sick Leave** (NMSA 1978 § 50-17-1 et seq., effective July 1, 2022):

- Applies to all employers (no size threshold) and all employees (full-time, part-time, seasonal, temporary).
- Accrual: 1 hour per 30 hours worked; up to 64 hours/year may carry over year-to-year.
- Permitted uses: Employee's own physical or mental illness, injury, health condition; medical diagnosis, care, or treatment; child's school/childcare issues related to health/disability; domestic abuse, sexual assault, stalking.
- Employer may not require use of other paid leave before employee uses sick leave.
- Employer may not discharge or retaliate against employee for requesting/using sick leave; policy must not penalize absence in performance evaluation.

**Caregiver Leave Act** (NMSA 1978 § 50-16-3):

- Applies to all employers.
- If employer provides sick leave for employee's own illness, employer must permit use for care of child, spouse, domestic partner, sibling, parent, mother-in-law, father-in-law, grandchild, grandparent, or stepparent.
- Same terms and procedures apply as for employee's own sick leave; retaliation prohibited.

**Domestic Abuse Leave** (§ 50-4A-3):

- Employers must grant unpaid or paid leave (per employer policy) for up to 14 days/calendar year (up to 8 hours/day).
- Permitted uses: obtain order of protection, meet with law enforcement, consult attorneys, attend court proceedings related to domestic abuse of employee or family member.
- Retaliation prohibited; employer must maintain confidentiality unless employee consents or law requires disclosure.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFML)**:

- New Mexico does not mandate state PFML. HB 11 (2025, "Welcome Child and Family Wellness Leave Act") passed House but stalled/failed in Senate (as of Mar 11, 2025).
- Employers may voluntarily offer PFML coverage; employees and employers contribute to state-managed fund.
- Federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees, 12-month employment history, 1,250 hours worked in past 12 months.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- If employee on paid sick leave or caregiver leave is promoted or receives base-pay increase, wages must not decrease from pre-leave level upon return.
- No statutory requirement to apply promotion wage retroactively to leave period (verify case law).
- Accrued paid leave continues accruing during paid leave; unpaid leave accrual policy-dependent (verify).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention** (§ 50-4-9):

- Employers must retain true and accurate records of hours worked and wages paid to each employee for at least 1 year after entry.
- Records subject to inspection by labor commissioner or authorized representatives at all reasonable times.
- Labor commissioner has 2-year window to demand records for criminal charges, 4-year window for civil action.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic right to inspect or copy personnel records.
- Public/state employees may review files during normal business hours in presence of HR administrator.
- Peace officers may file written response to adverse comments within 30 days (§ 29-14-7).
- Personnel files subject to New Mexico Public Records Act (§ 14-2-1) exemptions (medical info, reference letters, pre-hire investigations, etc., may be withheld).

**Record content and format**:

- No state mandate for electronic vs. paper format; either acceptable if retention/access requirements met.
- No state-mandated pay-stub format; employers should document hours worked (daily/weekly), wage rate, deductions, gross/net pay, leave accrual per § 50-4-9.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Breach Notification** (§ 57-12C-1 et seq.):

- Applies to any person/business owning or licensing computerized data with personal information.
- Notification required "in the most expedient manner possible, but no later than 45 days" after discovery of breach.
- Must notify affected individuals (and State Attorney General if breach affects 1,000+ NM residents).
- Encryption safe harbor: No notification required if compromised data encrypted and encryption key not also acquired.
- Penalties: Up to $25,000 for knowing/reckless violations; up to $150,000 for failure to notify when required.
- Enforcement by New Mexico Attorney General.

**Employee privacy—monitoring, social media, tobacco**:

- No state statute bans workplace email/internet monitoring or video monitoring of work areas.
- Reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies (common law).
- Employer may not discriminate against employee for lawful off-duty tobacco use or require tobacco abstinence while off duty (Employee Privacy Act, § 50-11 et seq.).
- No state law restricting employer's ability to monitor social media or publicly available content (subject to discrimination laws).

**Data residency and processing**:

- No state law mandating data residency for employee records; employers may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided breach notification and security laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box—criminal record inquiry** (§ 28-2-3.1):

- Private employers with written/electronic application: May not inquire regarding applicant's arrest or conviction history on initial application.
- Criminal history may be considered after application review and upon discussion of employment with applicant.
- Covers arrests not followed by conviction, sealed/dismissed/expunged/pardoned convictions, and convictions not job-related and not consistent with business necessity (for public employers under § 28-2-3).
- Enforcement under Human Rights Act § 28-1-10 et seq.; applicant may seek relief for violation.

**Background checks**:

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions for private employers.
- Expunged records (NMSA 1978 § 29-3A-1 et seq., Criminal Record Expungement Act, verify) need not be disclosed by the applicant.
- Third-party background check providers must comply with FCRA notice, dispute, adverse-action procedures.

**Drug testing**:

- No state law restricting private-sector drug testing.
- Employers have broad discretion to test pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, or random.
- Drug testing policy should be written, consistent, communicated to applicants/employees.
- Medical marijuana legalized (Lynn and Erin Compassionate Use Act, § 26-2B); no employer accommodation required for lawful off-duty use (see Section 7).

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No state mandate for private-sector E-Verify; federal I-9 requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mass layoff notice**:

- No state mini-WARN law; New Mexico relies on federal WARN Act.
- Federal WARN Act applies to employers with 100+ employees; requires 60 days' advance notice before plant closing or mass layoff affecting 25+ employees or 25%+ of workforce over 3-month period.
- Notice must be given to affected employees, union representatives (if applicable), state dislocated worker unit, and chief elected official of affected municipalities.

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance except in narrow circumstances (verify case law). Severance is contractual; requires explicit agreement.

**Non-compete enforceability** (§ 50-4A-1):

- Healthcare practitioners (physicians, osteopathic physicians, dentists, podiatrists, certified registered nurse anesthetists, certified nurse practitioners, certified nurse-midwives, psychologists, physician assistants, pharmacists): Post-employment non-competes unenforceable.
- Permitted restrictions: Non-disclosure clauses for confidential information; non-solicitation (≤1 year); reasonable liquidated damages for competitive practice post-termination.
- For practitioners employed <3 years, employer may require repayment of loans, relocation, signing bonuses, education/training (does not apply to shareholder/partner agreements).
- For non-healthcare employees: Common law enforceability applies; courts enforce if reasonable in duration, geography, scope with legitimate business interest (trade secrets, customer relationships).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness standard (duration, scope, geography, legitimate business interest). Healthcare practitioners limited to 1 year per § 50-4A-1.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- New Mexico courts apply "ABC Test" (common law), though the IRS, the New Mexico Department of Workforce Solutions, and the USDOL each apply separate tests.
- Prongs: (A) worker free from control/direction contractually and in fact; (B) work outside usual course of employer's business or outside all places of business; (C) worker customarily engaged in independently established trade/business of same nature.
- For discrimination/retaliation/harassment: "right to control" test may apply.
- Misclassification enforcement by NMDOL, Department of Revenue, Attorney General, Workers' Comp Commission; penalties include back wages, reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute addressing remote-work classification.
- Standard employment law applies regardless of work location; if work performed in New Mexico (remote or on-site), New Mexico law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Employers should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable state rules.
- Employers may specify in writing that New Mexico law governs employment relationship (recommended for multi-state arrangements).

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints**:

1. **Pay frequency/payday notice** (§ 50-4-2): On any promotion or base-pay change, system must flag if payday changes. If payday remains regular (≤16 days apart), no additional notice required unless employer policy mandates advance notice.

2. **Base-pay change notice and timing** (§ 50-4-2, § 50-4-3): For any wage increase, employer may implement immediately (no statutory advance notice required). For wage decrease, must provide written advance notice prior to effective date (best practice: 1+ pay period advance notice). Capture written acknowledgment in system.

3. **Minimum wage compliance**: On promotion or base-pay change, system must verify wage meets applicable minimum: statewide $12.00/hour or highest local minimum if employee works in Santa Fe ($15.00), Albuquerque ($12.00), Las Cruces ($13.01), or Bernalillo County ($12.00). Flag if promotion results in wage below applicable minimum.

4. **Final pay on separation during/after promotion**: If termination occurs following promotion, ensure all wages earned at promoted rate from effective date forward are paid within 5 days (fixed wages) or 10 days (other compensation) of discharge per § 50-4-4.

5. **Fair Pay for Women Act compliance** (§ 28-23-1 et seq.): On promotion or base-pay change, system should flag if new wage for one gender differs >15% from peer rates for equal work within same establishment; require manager justification or HR approval for outlier increases (applies to employers with 4+ employees).

6. **Paid sick leave interaction** (§ 50-17-1 et seq.): On promotion + pay change, if employee uses sick leave concurrently, system must not reduce post-leave wage below pre-promotion level. Sick leave accrual continues during paid leave (verify if employer policy dictates pro-rata accrual).

7. **Caregiver leave interaction** (§ 50-16-3): If employee uses caregiver leave and returns to promoted role, employer must not reduce wage below pre-leave promoted wage.

8. **Domestic abuse leave interaction** (§ 50-4A-3): If employee takes domestic abuse leave and returns to promoted role, wage protection applies (do not reduce below promoted wage).

9. **Final pay on resignation** (§ 50-4-5): On promotion, if employee resigns, final pay (including promoted wage if promotion effective before resignation) due at next regular payday.

10. **Record retention** (§ 50-4-9): System must retain promotion records (wage change documentation, effective date, justification) for minimum 1 year; support labor commissioner inspection rights.

11. **Non-compete enforceability check** (§ 50-4A-1): On promotion to healthcare practitioner role, system must: (a) Identify if employee subject to non-compete; (b) If yes, flag as unenforceable per § 50-4A-1 (applies to all listed healthcare roles); (c) Document non-solicitation clause (≤1 year) and liquidated damages as enforceable alternatives; (d) On promotion, check if new role changes healthcare-practitioner status and update enforceability status.

12. **Implied contract review**: On promotion, if handbook or offer letter promises "cause-only termination" or other job security, flag for legal review to determine if implied contract limits at-will status.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether "same establishment" for Fair Pay for Women Act comparisons (§ 28-23-1) includes multi-site employers or same physical worksite only.
- Clarify whether "equal work" under § 28-23-1 requires identical duties or substantially similar responsibilities.
- Confirm scope of § 50-4A-1 healthcare-practitioner list and whether licensed mental health counselors, social workers, or other allied health roles qualify.
- Verify whether implied contract for "cause-only termination" can override at-will status after promotion or only at hire (Vigil v. Arzola doctrine).
- Confirm whether final-pay requirements (§ 50-4-4, § 50-4-5) apply to all wage types or only hourly/salary (commission, bonus, other incentives—verify).
