# Arkansas Employment Law: State Requirements for Workforce Changes

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Minimum wage ($11/hour)** and **overtime (1.5x after 40h/week)** apply to employers with 4+ employees; platform must record hours worked and calculate qualifying overtime based on actual hours, excluding paid time off.
- **At-will employment** is the default; limited exceptions exist (public policy, statutory duty, implied contract from handbook). Platform need not record termination justification but employer handbooks can create implied for-cause contracts.
- **No state sick leave, family leave, or paid time off mandate**; federal FMLA applies (12 weeks unpaid for qualifying conditions, covered employers). Nursing mothers have statutory right to unpaid breastfeeding breaks (Ark. Code § 11-5-116).
- **Final pay due within 7 days of termination or next regular payday** (Ark. Code § 11-4-405); platform must ensure timely processing and include all earned wages; double-wage penalty for non-compliance.
- **Semi-monthly pay frequency required** for most employees; corporations with $500k+ annual income may pay exempt management once per month (Ark. Code § 11-4-401).
- **No state pay transparency law, salary history ban, or mandatory pay equity audits**. Equal pay statute covers sex only; state law covers employers with any size, but federal Title VII standard is broader.
- **Non-compete agreements enforceable** under Act 921 (Ark. Code § 4-75-101) if ancillary to employment, limited in time and scope, and protecting legitimate business interest; courts apply "blue pencil" (may reform overbroad provisions). Physician non-competes now void.
- **No mini-WARN act** in Arkansas; federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for mass layoffs) applies only. E-Verify mandatory for state government starting January 1, 2026.
- **No statewide ban-the-box** for private employers; local (Little Rock, Pulaski County, Pine Bluff) and public sector restrictions apply. Background checks allowed; consider felony timing and relevance.
- **Employee records retention**: Personnel files must be retained 5 years post-separation (state agency standard); federal FLSA requires 3 years for payroll records. Arkansas has no state biometric privacy law with consent requirement but covers biometric data under data-breach statute.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Notice Requirements

Arkansas follows the **at-will employment doctrine** by default (Ark. Code § 11-9-107). Employees may be discharged at any time for any reason, without notice or cause.

### Exceptions to At-Will Employment

Four narrow exceptions apply:

1. Discharge for refusal to violate a criminal statute.
2. Discharge for exercising a statutory right.
3. Discharge for complying with a statutory duty.
4. Discharge in violation of general public policy.

Arkansas does **not** recognize the implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing as a freestanding limit on at-will employment for private-sector workers; however, **implied contract exception** applies when an employee handbook contains a definitive list of grounds and conditions for dismissal (_Gladden v. Arkansas Children's Hospital_, 292 Ark. 130, 1987), or when oral promises are made suggesting job security.

### Notice Requirements at Hire and on Change

Arkansas has **no state law requiring written notice of employment terms at hire** or notice of pay/role changes prior to implementation. Employers are not required to provide any pre-termination warning. Federal wage laws (FLSA) must still be disclosed if employer is covered.

### Wage Theft Prevention and Pay Rate Change Notice

No state wage theft prevention notice is mandated. Pay rate changes may be implemented without advance notice, but must comply with minimum wage and overtime laws.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

### Minimum Wage and Overtime

**Minimum wage: $11.00/hour** for employers with 4+ employees (Ark. Code § 11-4-211). When both state and federal law apply, the higher wage controls.

**Overtime: 1.5x regular rate** for hours worked over 40 per workweek (Ark. Code § 11-4-211). Calculation is based on **actual hours worked**; paid time off (holidays, sick leave, vacation) does not count toward the 40-hour threshold.

### Pay Frequency

**Semi-monthly pay** required for all employees in corporations doing business in Arkansas (Ark. Code § 11-4-401). Corporations with annual gross income ≥$500,000 may pay **management and FLSA-exempt executives** earning >$25,000/year at least once per calendar month. Non-corporate employers (sole proprietors, LLCs) are not bound by the semi-monthly rule but must establish a consistent schedule and provide notice.

Acceptable payment methods: cash, check, direct deposit, payroll debit card.

### Final Pay on Termination or Resignation

**Final paycheck due within 7 days** of discharge if employee demands or requests it; otherwise, due by next regularly scheduled payday (Ark. Code § 11-4-405). Final check must include all earned wages through last day of work, plus accrued overtime.

**Penalty: Double wages** if final pay is not issued within 7 days after the next regular payday.

Unused vacation or PTO is **not required to be paid out** unless the employer has a written policy or contract promising payment.

### Permitted and Prohibited Deductions

**Permitted deductions:**

- Legally required (federal, state, local taxes; Social Security; Medicare; unemployment insurance).
- Court-ordered (wage garnishment, child support, bankruptcy).
- Employee-authorized in writing (union dues, insurance premiums, retirement contributions, charitable donations).
- Deductions for board, lodging, apparel, or items/services provided by employer (if does not reduce below minimum wage).

**Prohibited deductions (that would reduce pay below minimum wage):**

- Cost of medical exam or drug test required by employer.
- Spoilage, breakage, cash/inventory shortages or losses.
- Fines or penalties for lateness, misconduct, or quitting without notice.

Note: verify deduction compliance with federal FLSA if employer is covered.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

### No State Salary Transparency Law

Arkansas has **no state pay transparency law**. Employers are not required to disclose salary ranges in job postings or provide salary information upon applicant request.

### No Salary History Ban

Arkansas permits employers to **ask job candidates about previous wages** during hiring; no state-level salary history ban exists.

### Equal Pay and Wage Discrimination

**Sex-based wage discrimination prohibited** for employers of any size (Ark. Code § 11-4-405 wage discrimination provision). A variation in pay is permitted if based on seniority, experience, training, skill, ability, duties performed, shift/time of day worked, or "any other reasonable differentiation other than sex."

Federal **Title VII** covers employers with 15+ employees and prohibits discrimination on the basis of race, color, religion, age, national origin, sex (including sexual orientation and gender identity under recent interpretations).

**Arkansas Civil Rights Act (1993)** covers employers of any size and prohibits discrimination based on race, religion, national origin, gender, or disability.

No state-mandated pay data reporting or equity audit is required.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, Other Mandated Leave

### Paid Sick Leave and Paid Family Leave

Arkansas has **no state mandate** for paid or unpaid sick leave. Any sick leave is determined by employer policy or contract.

**Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)** applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles). Eligible employees may take up to **12 weeks unpaid leave** in a 12-month period for serious health condition, bonding with new child, or qualifying exigencies. Employees must have worked for employer 1+ year and 1,250+ hours in prior 12 months.

### Nursing Mothers Breastfeeding Breaks

**Reasonable unpaid break time each day** for employees who need to express breast milk for their child (Ark. Code § 11-5-116). Breaks should coincide with existing paid/unpaid breaks when feasible.

Employer must make reasonable effort to provide **private, secure, sanitary location** other than toilet stall, in close proximity to work area. Employee's normal work space may suffice if it meets requirements.

**Exception: Undue hardship** on employer operations may excuse break time requirement. Break time is **unpaid**.

### Jury Duty, Voting, Military Service Leave

**Jury duty:** Employees summoned to jury service **cannot be discharged, lose sick/vacation leave, or suffer penalty** for serving on jury (unpaid; state pays nominal jury fee). Unlawful for employer to persuade employee to avoid jury service or demand use of vacation leave for jury summons response.

**Voting:** Employer must allow **unpaid voting leave** and schedule work to permit employee opportunity to vote. No specific duration mandated; employer discretion applies.

**Military service:** Employees called to active duty state military service entitled to **unpaid leave** with reemployment rights under USERRA. Employer must restore employee to same/equivalent position with full benefits and retirement credit.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format Requirements

### Record Retention Periods

**Personnel files:** State government standard requires **5 years retention after resignation or termination** (State of Arkansas General Records Retention Schedule). Private-sector employers are not required by Arkansas law to follow this standard but should check federal requirements.

**Payroll and wage records:** Federal FLSA requires **3 years retention** for all covered employers.

**I-9 and tax documents:** Must comply with federal/IRS retention periods (typically 3 years for I-9, 4+ years for tax documents).

If record is subject to litigation or official action, retention period is extended until action finalizes.

### Employee Access to Personnel File

No specific Arkansas state law mandates employee access to personnel files. Employers may set their own policies. Federal access rights apply for certain records (e.g., OSHA injury reports).

### Pay Statement/Paycheck Stub Requirements

Arkansas has **no state law mandating pay stub content or format**. Employers are not legally required to provide written documentation of earnings or deductions.

**Best practice:** Despite no mandate, providing itemized pay stubs (pay-period dates, hours worked at each rate, gross pay, itemized deductions, net pay) is advisable for audit compliance and to satisfy federal FLSA recordkeeping if covered.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric, Monitoring, Breach Notification

### Biometric Data Protection

Biometric data (voiceprints, handprints, fingerprints, DNA, retinal/iris scans, hand geometry, faceprints) are covered under Arkansas data-breach statute effective August 9, 2019. Businesses acquiring, owning, or licensing personal information (including biometric) must **implement and maintain reasonable and appropriate security practices** to protect from unauthorized access or disclosure.

**No consent requirement:** Unlike Illinois, Texas, and Washington, Arkansas does **not require informed consent** before collecting biometric data. No obligation to provide written notice of collection, purpose, or retention period. **No prohibition** on sale, lease, trade, or disclosure of biometric data to third parties. verify: no standalone BIPA-style private right of action in Arkansas.

### Employee Monitoring

Arkansas has **no standalone workplace electronic-monitoring statute**. Employers may use electronic surveillance (email, web monitoring, location tracking) subject to federal wiretap laws and common-law privacy torts (intrusion on seclusion). No requirement for employee consent or notice prior to deployment of monitoring technology.

### Breach Notification

Arkansas Personal Information Protection Act (Ark. Code Title 4, Chapter 110) requires notice to affected persons if personal information (including biometric) is breached and poses reasonable risk of identity theft or fraud. Notice must be given **without unreasonable delay**.

### Data Residency or Processing Constraints

No state-specific employee data residency or processing requirements. Employers must comply with applicable federal laws (e.g., GDPR-equivalent restrictions if workforce includes EU residents; CCPA-equivalent if California residents).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

### Ban-the-Box

**No statewide private-sector ban-the-box law** in Arkansas. Private employers may inquire about criminal history on job applications or during hiring.

**Local restrictions:**

- **Little Rock, Pulaski County, Pine Bluff:** Ban-the-box ordinances delay criminal history inquiries to later in hiring process (typically after conditional offer).

**Public sector:** Executive Order 16-04 (Governor Asa Hutchinson, 2016) applies Ban the Box to state government positions.

### Background Checks and Criminal History Consideration

Employers must consider the **nature of the crime, time elapsed since offense, and relevance to the job** when using criminal records in hiring decisions (federal Fair Chance to Compete for Jobs Act applies to federal contractors; Arkansas requires similar consideration for all employers).

Must comply with federal FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act) requirements: obtain written consent, provide notice of adverse action, allow dispute.

### Drug Testing

No specific Arkansas state law addressing drug testing. Employer may test if notice is provided and policy is applied consistently. Must comply with federal laws (ADA, DOT, safety-sensitive positions).

### E-Verify Mandate

**State government positions:** E-Verify **mandatory for all new hires as of January 1, 2026** (Executive Order or statute, verify source).

**Private employers:** No state mandate for E-Verify use. Federal contractors/subcontractors must use E-Verify per federal requirements.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN Acts, Severance, Non-Compete Enforceability

### Mini-WARN Act Status

**No state mini-WARN act.** Federal **WARN Act** (60 days' advance notice to employees and state labor agency for mass layoffs of 50+ employees at a single site within 30 days) is the sole applicable law.

**WARN notice recipient in Arkansas:** Arkansas Workforce Connections (Arkansas Department of Commerce). verify: current recipient and contact information.

### Severance Pay

**Not required by Arkansas law.** Severance is at employer discretion and may be included in employment contract or company policy. No state severance mandate or termination pay requirement beyond final wages due.

### Non-Compete Agreements

**Enforceable** under Ark. Code § 4-75-101 (Act 921, effective September 2015) if:

1. **Ancillary to employment** or part of otherwise enforceable employment agreement.
2. **Protectable business interest:** Trade secrets, customer lists, confidential information, intellectual property, goodwill with customers, knowledge of business practices, methods, profit margins, costs, training, or "other valuable employer data."
3. **Limited in time and scope:** Not greater than necessary to defend protectable interest. Absence of geographic restriction does not make agreement overbroad if time and scope are reasonable.

**Blue-pencil doctrine:** Courts **shall reform overbroad covenants** to make them enforceable (distinguish from prior Arkansas law, which voided overbroad agreements in entirety).

**Physician non-competes:** Void as of March 2025 (Governor Sarah Huckabee Sanders signed bill clarifying that any non-compete "restricting the right of a physician to practice within the physician's scope of practice is void").

**Non-solicit and confidentiality agreements:** § 4-75-101 does not apply. Existing common-law standards govern; courts often apply non-compete enforcement principles.

### Implied Contracts and Wrongful Termination

Implied-contract exception to at-will doctrine applies if handbook or oral promises suggest job security or enumerate specific termination procedures. Platform should flag any employer handbook provisions that might create liability.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor vs. Employee, Remote-Worker Rules, Reciprocity

### Independent Contractor Classification

Arkansas uses the **ABC test** (presume employment by default):

- **(A)** Worker operates free from employer control.
- **(B)** Work is outside employer's usual business activities.
- **(C)** Worker has independently established business.

**All three conditions must be satisfied** to classify as independent contractor. Failure on any factor results in employee status. Misclassification carries penalties (back wages, fines, potential liability).

### Remote Worker and Multi-State Employment

**General principle:** State law where work is physically performed applies. Employee working from Arkansas is subject to Arkansas law regardless of employer location or employee residence.

**Arkansas repealed its "convenience of the employer" rule** in 2021. No bright-line rule; application depends on facts (work location, payroll location, employer presence, etc.). **verify** multi-state analysis for specific scenario.

### Tax and Reciprocity

Arkansas income tax applies to Arkansas residents and non-residents earning Arkansas-source income. No reciprocity agreement with neighboring states.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Obligations, Timing, Field Restrictions

### Promotion and Base-Pay Change (P1A)

**Required fields/recording:**

- Document hours worked and overtime calculations (basis for any pay increase over 40h/week).
- Capture pay rate change with effective date; enforce semi-monthly or monthly pay frequency rules based on employer size/income.
- Record new manager assignment; no statutory notice to employee required but flag handbook implied-contract risks.
- If role change involves health/safety (e.g., hazardous work), flag need for safety training and medical clearance (verify state/federal requirements).

**Timing constraints:**

- Pay rate changes take effect on next regular payday or as agreed; no advance notice required by Arkansas law.
- Nursing mother accommodations: If promotion involves role relocation, ensure private breastfeeding space available at new location (Ark. Code § 11-5-116).

**Restrictions:**

- Ensure final wage payment if role terminates; no wage deductions for training cost or equipment unless authorized in writing and does not reduce below minimum wage.

### Termination (P1B)

**Required fields/recording:**

- Termination date, reason (for internal documentation, even though at-will; helpful for exception analysis and handbook review).
- Accrued unpaid vacation/PTO (if company policy provides payout).
- Final wage amount, calculation of final paycheck due date (7 days from demand or next payday).
- Flag whether handbook or oral agreements created implied for-cause contract.

**Timing obligations:**

- **Final pay due within 7 days of termination** if employee requests it, or by next regular payday (Ark. Code § 11-4-405).
- Double-wage penalty if final pay is late beyond 7 days after next payday.
- Federal WARN notice (60 days) if mass layoff meets criteria (50+ employees, single site, 30 days).

**Non-compete enforcement:**

- Verify non-compete is ancillary to employment and limited in time/scope (Ark. Code § 4-75-101).
- Physician non-competes void (2025 law change).
- Platform should note non-compete in termination record and flag for legal review.

**Field restrictions:**

- Do not include severance requirement (not mandated); if offered, enforce via contract/policy.
- No ban-the-box restriction for private employers (state-wide); but check local jurisdiction (Little Rock, Pulaski, Pine Bluff).
- E-Verify mandatory only for state government starting Jan 1, 2026.

**Open questions (verify):**

- Multi-state remote worker: Does employee work from Arkansas office or home in other state? Law of work location applies; requires clarification for multi-state cohorts.
- Implied contract from handbook: Does employer handbook enumerate termination procedures or promise job security? Review before termination to assess at-will exception risk.
- Contractor classification under ABC test: Is worker truly independent (all three conditions) or employee by default? Misclassification carries back-wage and penalty risk.
