# Washington Employment Law Research

**State:** Washington | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Minimum wage $17.13/hour (2026)**: Annual CPI-W adjustment; youth 14–15 at 85%; no regional multiplier (unlike Oregon/California).
- **Final pay mandate (RCW 49.48.010)**: All wages earned through last workday due by end of pay period; no withholding for unreturned equipment; double damages + attorney fees if willful.
- **Overtime 1.5x after 40/week (RCW 49.46.130)**: No daily threshold; broad exemptions for salaried, seamen, airline/motor carrier, agricultural workers; shift-trading alternative.
- **Paid sick leave 1 hr/40 (RCW 49.46.210)**: Accrual-based, usable 90 days after hire; 2025 (HB 1875 eff. 7/27/25): added immigration proceedings as authorized use; carryover required but no annual cap stated.
- **Equal Pay and Opportunities Act (RCW 49.58, 15+ employees)**: Salary history ban; job-posting pay-range disclosure (since 2023); 2025 (SB 5408 eff. 7/27/25): 5-business-day cure for non-disclosure, $100–$5,000 statutory damages per violation.
- **Personnel file access (RCW 49.12.240/250, amended HB 1308 eff. 7/27/25)**: 21-day access window; $250–$1,000 penalties based on delay; private right of action.
- **Non-competes (RCW 49.62.020)**: Unenforceable below $123,394.17 salary (2025 threshold, annual inflation adjustment); 18-month presumption of unreasonableness; 2024 (SB 5935 eff. 6/6/24): non-solicitation narrowed to current customers only.
- **Paid Family/Medical Leave (RCW 50A, eff. 1/1/26)**: Up to 12 weeks; 2026 premium 1.13% (employer 28.57%, employee 71.43%); claim minimum 4 consecutive hours/week; 25+ employees; 180 days employment required.
- **Washington WARN Act (SB 5525 eff. 7/27/25)**: 60 days' notice for 50+ employee business closure/mass layoff; ESD and employee civil action available.
- **Workplace isolation (RCW 49.60.515 eff. 1/1/26)**: Panic buttons, sexual harassment policy, training for janitors, security, hotel housekeepers, room service; covers 50%+ isolated hours or no-immediate-response settings.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Washington recognizes employment-at-will; employers and employees may terminate without cause except by statute or contract.

**Exceptions to at-will termination**:

- Termination for filing or testifying in workers' compensation, wage, or unemployment claims.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (speech, assembly, petition, voting).
- Termination for jury duty, military duty, or witness duty.
- Termination in violation of public policy (refusing illegal act; whistleblowing).
- Retaliation for filing discrimination complaints or exercising civil rights.

**Required notices at hire and on pay/role change**:

- No specific Washington statute mandates written employment offer or role-change notice at hire (recommend for defensibility).
- Wage discussion protected: employers cannot retaliate against wage inquiry or discussion (RCW 49.58).
- On discharge, final pay timing must be met per RCW 49.48.010 (next pay period end; see §3 below).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage (RCW 49.46.020)**:

- 2026 rate: $17.13/hour (effective January 1, 2026).
- 2025 rate: $16.66/hour.
- Annual adjustment: Indexed by Consumer Price Index for Urban Wage Earners (CPI-W) each January 1.
- Youth: Employees ages 14–15 may be paid 85% of minimum wage; 16+ receive full minimum wage.
- Applies: All employers; tips/service charges do not count toward minimum wage obligation.

**Overtime (RCW 49.46.130)**:

- Standard: Time-and-a-half (1.5x) regular rate for hours over 40 per workweek (no daily threshold).
- Calculation: Regular rate includes base hourly, commissions, bonuses; weekly basis.
- Compensatory time: Employees may request comp time instead of overtime pay.
- Exemptions: Executive, administrative, professional; seamen; motion picture projectionists (union); truck/bus drivers (federal Motor Carrier regulations); agricultural (phased out by 1/1/24); airline voluntary shift-trading; real estate agents (specific contract); retail/service earning 1.5x minimum + commissions >50% compensation; public safety 240 hours/28 days.
- Penalties: $1,000–$20,000 per willful violation.

**Pay frequency (RCW 49.46.020 reference; standard practice)**:

- Payday requirement: At minimum monthly; recommended biweekly or semi-monthly.
- Regular payday establishment: Must be declared in advance.

**Final pay on termination (RCW 49.48.010)**:

- Timing: All earned wages due by end of established pay period or next regular payday after termination.
- Includes: Hourly wages, salary, piece rate, commission, earned bonuses, overtime, shift differentials, hazard pay, agreed compensation.
- Withholding prohibition: Cannot withhold final pay for unreturned keys, uniforms, tools, equipment, or property.
- Penalties: Misdemeanor; double damages + attorney fees under RCW 49.52.070 if unpaid.

**Deductions (WAC 296-126-028)**:

- Permitted: Deductions required by law (tax, garnishment, court order) or written authorization.
- Restrictions: Cannot deduct for tools, uniforms, employer costs unless written consent and no reduction below minimum wage.
- Timing: Deducted amounts must be remitted to recipient within 7 days of wages due or per law/agreement.

**Pay statement content (WAC 296-128-010)**:

- Requirement: Itemized statement provided at each payday, separate from paycheck.
- Must show: Pay basis (hours/days worked), rate(s) of pay, gross wages, itemized deductions (purpose + amount).
- Records: Must retain name, address, occupation, dates of employment, rate(s), amount each period, hours worked.
- Retention: 3 years minimum (WAC 296-126-040).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary history ban (RCW 49.58.100)**:

- Prohibition: Cannot inquire about prior compensation from applicant or current/former employers.
- Screening: Cannot screen applicants based on current/past compensation.
- Exception: Post-offer written authorization to verify compensation after conditional offer; unsolicited voluntary disclosure not actionable.

**Job-posting pay-range disclosure (RCW 49.58.110)**:

- Threshold: Applies to employers with 15+ employees.
- Effective date: January 1, 2023.
- Requirement: Must disclose "wage scale or salary range" in each job posting; fixed amount allowed if only one rate offered; must include general description of benefits and other compensation.
- Upon request: After offer, must provide minimum wage/salary for the position.
- 2025 amendment (SB 5408 eff. 7/27/25): Added 5-business-day cure period for non-compliance (expires 7/27/27); statutory damages $100–$5,000 per violation plus attorney fees.

**Equal pay (RCW 49.58)**:

- Protected classes: Race (including hair traits/styles), color, religion, creed, sex (pregnancy/childbirth/related), national origin, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, age, marital status, political ideology.
- Standard: Unlawful to pay protected-class employees at lower rates for substantially similar work; permissible reasons: seniority, merit, production, location, necessary travel, education, training, experience, or any combination accounting for entire differential.

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state-mandated pay equity audit; federal EEO-1 (100+ employees) applies if applicable.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Paid Family/Medical Leave, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave (RCW 49.46.210)**:

- Accrual: Minimum 1 hour per 40 hours worked.
- Front-loading: Allowed if meets accrual/use/carryover requirements.
- Earliest use: 90 calendar days after hire.
- Authorized purposes: Physical/mental health treatment, preventive care, health condition management, domestic violence/sexual assault/stalking, legal proceedings.
- 2025 update (HB 1875 eff. 7/27/25): Immigration proceedings added (prepare for or participate in judicial/administrative immigration proceeding for self or family member); verification by employee statement or documentation from immigration advocate, attorney, clergy, professional.
- Carryover: Must carry over year to year; no annual cap stated in statute.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave (RCW 50A, administered by ESD)**:

- Benefit weeks: Up to 12 weeks per 12-month period.
- Qualifying events: Birth/adoption, serious health condition (self or family), military exigency, bereavement.
- Premium rates (2026, eff. 1/1/26): 1.13% total; employer pays 28.57%, employee 71.43%.
- Maximum weekly benefit: $1,647 (2026).
- 2026 updates: Claim minimum reduced to 4 consecutive hours/week (from 8); job protection threshold lowered to 25+ employees; eligibility reduced to 180 calendar days employment (6 months) with no minimum hours requirement.
- Job protection: Employee may return to same or equivalent position.

**Domestic violence/sexual assault leave (RCW 49.76)**:

- Eligibility: Victims of domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking, or hate crime.
- Authorized uses: Legal/law enforcement assistance, medical/mental health treatment, shelter, rape crisis services, safety planning, relocation.
- Leave type: Reasonable, intermittent, or reduced schedule; may be paid or unpaid per employer policy.
- Notice: Advance notice per employer policy; emergency notice no later than end of first day if impossible to advance notify.
- Protection: Cannot retaliate or discriminate against victim.

**Military family leave (RCW 49.77)**:

- Eligibility: Spouse of armed forces (active duty, National Guard, Reserves) notified of impending call/order or deployed.
- Entitlement: 15 days unpaid leave per deployment.
- Notice: Within 5 business days of official notice.
- Leave substitution: Employee may substitute accrued paid leave.
- Insurance: If ineligible for employer contributions, employer must allow continuation at employee expense (≤102% premium).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Personnel file access (RCW 49.12.240/250, amended HB 1308 eff. 7/27/25)**:

- Definition: Job applications, performance evaluations, non-active/closed disciplinary records, leave/accommodation paperwork, payroll records, employment agreements.
- Access timeline: Within 21 calendar days after written request by employee, former employee, or designee.
- Penalties: $250 if complete file not provided within 21 days; $500 if not provided within 28 days; $1,000 if provided after 35 days.
- Remedy: Private cause of action; statutory damages and attorney fees available.

**Payroll/wage record retention (WAC 296-126-040)**:

- Duration: Minimum 3 years.
- Contents: Name, address, occupation, dates of employment, rate(s), amount each period, hours worked, pay statements.
- Applies to: All employees subject to RCW 49.46.020 (minimum wage).

**Record format**:

- No statute mandates electronic vs. paper; electronic acceptable if accessible and printable by employee.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Breach Notification, Monitoring, Social Media

**Data breach notification (RCW 19.255.010)**:

- Deadline: No later than 30 days after discovery of breach.
- Attorney General: Notify if breach affects 500+ Washington residents within 30 days of discovery.
- Scope: Applies to any business owning/licensing computerized personal information of Washington residents (name + SSN, financial account, medical record, etc.).
- Delay: Notification may be delayed if law enforcement requests for criminal investigation.

**My Health My Data Act (RCW 19.373, eff. 3/31/24)**:

- Scope: Covers broad range of businesses (not just traditional healthcare); applies to consumer health data (personal information linked/reasonably linkable to past/present/future physical/mental health status).
- Employee data access: Regulated entities and small businesses must restrict employee/processor/contractor access to consumer health data to only those with necessary access for stated purposes (eff. 3/31/24).
- Verify: Whether specific employee-data-handling rules differ from standard healthcare HIPAA.

**Social media (RCW 49.44.200)**:

- Restriction: Employers cannot request/require access to personal social media accounts of applicants or current employees.
- Prohibited: Seeking login info, passwords, contact lists, privacy settings, content.
- Retaliation: Cannot discharge, discipline, penalize, threaten, or refuse to hire for account access.
- Remedy: Private right of action; aggrieved party may recover attorney fees plus $500 statutory penalty (RCW 49.44.205).

**Workplace monitoring**:

- No state statute bans email/internet/video monitoring; reasonable expectation of privacy applies (federal ECPA relevant).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (RCW 49.94, amended HB 1747)**:

- Original effective: 2018.
- 2025 amendments (effective dates: 7/1/26 for 15+ employees; 1/1/27 for smaller employers):
  - Cannot ask criminal history on application, interview, through recruiter, or order background check until after conditional offer extended.
  - Cannot reject applicant/deny promotion solely on adult conviction record without legitimate business reason.
  - Before rejecting based on criminal record: (1) notify of specific record; (2) hold position open minimum 2 business days; (3) allow explanation/rehabilitation evidence.

**Background checks**:

- No state-level private-sector restrictions; federal FCRA applies (consumer reporting agency rules, notice, dispute, adverse-action procedures).

**Drug testing**:

- No state-level mandate or restriction (federal DOT rules apply if applicable).
- Cannabis: Employers may test; positive not protected (no medical-cardholder exception for employment).

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No state or Seattle mandate; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply to all employers (E-Verify voluntary in Washington).

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Washington WARN Act (Senate Bill 5525, eff. 7/27/25)**:

- Mini-WARN statute: Washington's state-level equivalent to federal WARN.
- Threshold: 50+ employees (excluding part-time).
- Notice: Written notice to Employment Security Department and affected employees (or bargaining representative); 60 days before business closure or mass layoff.
- Business closing: Permanent or temporary shutdown of single site resulting in employment loss for 50+ employees (excluding part-time).
- Enforcement: ESD, aggrieved employees, or unions may bring civil action; back pay, canceled benefits (up to 60 days), attorney fees.

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance absent employment agreement; contractual.

**Non-compete enforceability (RCW 49.62.020)**:

- Income threshold (2025): Employees $123,394.17; independent contractors $308,485.43; annual inflation adjustment by Department of Labor & Industries.
- Presumption: 18-month presumption of unreasonableness post-termination; agreements exceeding 18 months void.
- Exception: Threshold does not apply if non-compete is part of sale of business.
- 2024 update (SB 5935 eff. 6/6/24): Non-solicitation now narrowed to current customers only; past/prospective customers subject to non-compete restrictions.
- Sunset: RCW 49.62.020 effective until 6/30/27; new version effective 7/1/27 (stricter rules expected).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- Narrowed by SB 5935 (eff. 6/6/24): Current customers only; common-law reasonableness standard applies for scope/duration/geography.

**Restrictive covenants general**:

- Silenced No More Act (RCW 49.44.211 eff. 6/9/22): Non-disparagement and NDA clauses void if applied to illegal discrimination, harassment, retaliation, wage/hour violations, sexual assault, or conduct against clear public policy; minimum $10,000 damages + attorney fees; retroactive to pre-6/9/22 agreements if agreed at employment outset or during employment.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- ABC Test (unemployment law): Worker deemed employee unless employer proves all three: (A) worker exercises total control over when/how work performed; (B) work outside employer's usual business or away from usual worksite; (C) worker has own separate business/occupation/trade.
- Failure on any prong: Employee classification.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine. Each state's law applies to work performed in that state.
- Flag: HCM should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable rules.

**Remote workers**:

- No Washington statute specifically addresses remote-work classification.
- Standard: If employee works remotely in Washington for any employer, Washington law applies to that work.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Obligations

**Mandatory platform features and field restrictions**:

1. **Final Pay Timing Trigger (RCW 49.48.010)**: On termination action, auto-calculate deadline based on discharge type (end of established pay period). Notify employee and payroll if unpaid wages lapse beyond deadline; system must log compliance risk and auto-escalate to HR if 48+ hours past deadline.

2. **Pay Statement Update (WAC 296-128-010)**: On any base-pay change (promotion, merit increase, etc.), system must generate itemized statement showing new rate, effective date, gross, deductions breakdown. Retain statement in employee record for 3 years.

3. **Salary History Block (RCW 49.58.100)**: Disable any form field requesting prior salary from external candidate in hiring module; log as compliance risk if manager attempts inquiry via notes/comments.

4. **Equal Pay Audit Flag (RCW 49.58)**: On promotion + base-pay change, if new rate differs >15% from peer rates for substantially similar work (same/similar role/location/department), flag for manager justification or HR override with documented bona fide reason (seniority, merit, production, location, travel, education, training, experience). Do not auto-approve if red-flag triggered.

5. **Non-Compete Threshold Recheck (RCW 49.62.020)**: On promotion, if new salary crosses $123,394.17 threshold (2025; adjust annually), auto-flag: if employee lacks signed NCA with enforceability conditions (income threshold met at time of agreement, 18-month max duration, independent consideration/garden leave), mark as unenforceable going forward. If NCA exists and new role extends beyond 18 months, flag for legal review.

6. **Pay-Range Disclosure (RCW 49.58.110)**: When initiating internal transfer/promotion process: if employee requests, must provide minimum wage/salary for promoted role within standard response time. If external posting created for similar role, must include pay range (no omission without 5-business-day cure notification option).

7. **Paid Leave Continuity (RCW 49.46.210 + RCW 50A)**: On promotion within same employer, paid sick leave accrual carries forward; do not reset. If promotion occurs during active PFML leave, employee retains job protection and leave benefit; role may change but leave benefit continues. Do not reduce base pay retroactively if employee in PFML during promotion announcement.

8. **Domestic Violence/Sexual Assault Leave (RCW 49.76)**: Job-protected; cannot retaliate for approved leave use. On promotion, do not use leave history in performance/wage decision; flagged as compliance risk if manager mentions leave as basis for promotion delay.

9. **Immigration Proceedings Leave (HB 1875 eff. 7/27/25)**: On promotion, do not use immigrant-status-related sick leave (preparation for/participation in immigration proceedings) as basis for performance downgrade or wage reduction; flagged as retaliation risk.

10. **Workplace Isolation Requirements (RCW 49.60.515 eff. 1/1/26)**: If promoted employee becomes isolated worker (50%+ hours without coworker/supervisor or no-immediate-response setting) as janitor, security, hotel housekeeper, or room service: auto-flag for mandatory panic-button assignment and training within 30 days; track annual mandatory harassment-prevention training completion.

11. **Record Documentation (WAC 296-126-040)**: Log all pay change notices, justifications, equity analysis, non-compete status, leave continuity decisions, immigration-status considerations, and isolation requirements for 3-year retention; audit trail must show timestamp and approver for compliance.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether non-compete threshold of $123,394.17 applies to base salary alone or total compensation (salary + commissions/bonuses).
- Confirm interaction of PFML/OFLA wage replacement with promotion timing; whether employee must exhaust paid leave before eligible for promoted rate.
- Clarify whether garden-leave payment obligation continues if employee promoted during active non-compete restriction.
- Verify whether paid sick leave must be paid out on termination or only if employer policy requires.
