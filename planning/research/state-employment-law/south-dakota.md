# South Dakota Employment Law Research

**State:** South Dakota | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment**: South Dakota recognizes at-will employment (SDCL 60-4-4); exceptions exist for workers' compensation retaliation (SDCL 62-1-16), public policy violations (whistleblowing, refusal to commit illegal acts), discrimination based on protected class, and tobacco use (SDCL 60-4-11).
- **Minimum wage $11.85/hour (2026)**: South Dakota indexes minimum wage annually based on CPI-U; rate becomes effective January 1 each year (SDCL 60-11-3); no decrease year-over-year permitted (SDCL 60-11-3.2).
- **Tipped wage $5.925/hour (50% minimum, 2026)**: Employers may credit tips toward minimum wage; if tips plus wages fall short, employer must make up difference (SDCL 60-11-3.1).
- **No state overtime mandate**: Federal FLSA 1.5x for 40+ weekly hours applies; no daily, consecutive-hour, or other triggers.
- **Pay frequency**: Monthly or regular agreed payday (SDCL 60-11-9); employer must designate and communicate payday at hire.
- **Final pay on termination**: Due by next regular payday or within reasonable time; employer may withhold until all employer property returned (SDCL 60-11-10); written demand triggers 5-day payment requirement.
- **Non-competes strictly limited**: SDCL 53-9-11 permits 2-year post-employment restriction within specified county, municipality, or area only if employer continues like business therein; voidable for healthcare practitioners (SDCL 53-9-11.2, effective 7/1/2023); courts disfavor reform and may void entirely if unreasonable.
- **Equal pay for sex**: SDCL 60-12-15 prohibits wage discrimination for comparable work; comparable skill, effort, responsibility required, not physical strength.
- **Protected classes (broad, all employer sizes)**: SDCL 20-13-10 reaches all employers (1+ employee) for discrimination based on race, color, creed, religion, sex, ancestry, disability, national origin; notably absent: age, sexual orientation, gender identity (federal protections apply under Bostock v. Clayton County; local ordinances in Sioux Falls, Spearfish, Vermillion extend protections).
- **No state paid sick leave, no PFML mandate, no salary-history ban, no pay-transparency posting requirement, no personnel-file access statute (private sector), no mini-WARN, no ban-the-box (private), no drug-testing statute, E-Verify not mandated**.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

South Dakota recognizes at-will employment: either party may terminate at will without cause or notice (SDCL 60-4-4).

**Exceptions to at-will**:

- **Workers' compensation retaliation**: Employer civilly liable for wrongful discharge if termination retaliates for filing lawful claim or applying for benefits (SDCL 62-1-16); burden of proof on employee; once employee establishes retaliation, employer must prove valid independent reason.
- **Public policy violations**: Discharge violates public policy if employee refuses to commit crime, reports unlawful conduct to supervisor or law enforcement (whistleblowing for "public good"), exercises jury duty or military service, or asserts rights under state wage law.
- **Protected-class discrimination**: Termination based on race, color, creed, religion, sex, ancestry, disability, national origin (SDCL 20-13-10).
- **Tobacco use**: Termination for off-duty use of tobacco products is discriminatory unless restriction is bona fide occupational requirement reasonably related to that employee's activities (SDCL 60-4-11).
- **Implied for-cause contract**: Rare; handbook language promising cause-only termination with mandatory pre-discipline procedures may create contract exception.

**Written notice at hire**: No specific notice statute; best practice is written acknowledgment of at-will status and payday designation (SDCL 60-11-9).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (SDCL 60-11-3):

- Statewide minimum: $11.85/hour effective 1/1/2026 (indexed annually by CPI-U; prior year: $11.50, 2025).
- South Dakota Department of Labor & Regulation publishes adjusted rate by October 15, effective January 1 following year.
- Rate may not decrease year-over-year (SDCL 60-11-3.2).
- No local override.

**Tipped employees** (SDCL 60-11-3.1):

- Tipped employee: one customarily and regularly receiving 35+ dollars monthly in tips; excludes babysitters, outside salespersons, amusement/recreational establishments operating <7 months/year, organized camps, religious/nonprofit educational conference centers.
- Minimum cash wage: $5.925/hour (50% of $11.85 state minimum).
- Tips credited toward minimum wage; if tips + cash wage fall short of $11.85 in any week, employer must make up difference as additional wages each pay period.

**Overtime** (federal FLSA applies; no state mandate):

- Time and one-half for hours exceeding 40 in a single workweek.
- No state daily, consecutive-hour, or other trigger.

**Pay frequency** (SDCL 60-11-9):

- Employers must pay wages at least once each calendar month or on a regular agreed payday.
- Employer must designate payday and communicate to employee at hire.
- Semimonthly, biweekly, weekly, or monthly common; must be consistent.

**Final pay on termination** (SDCL 60-11-10):

- All earned, unpaid wages due by next regular payday or within reasonable time after separation.
- Employer may withhold final pay until employee returns all employer property in employee's possession.
- Employee may make written demand for immediate payment; employer must pay within 5 days of written demand.
- Applies to voluntary resignation, involuntary discharge, and layoff.
- Intentional refusal to pay wages, false denial of amount owed, or withholding to harass/defraud is Class 2 misdemeanor (SDCL 60-11-11).

**Permitted deductions** (implied under SDCL 60-11-10):

- Deductions required by law (taxes, garnishments, court orders) are permitted.
- Deductions authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes (union dues, health insurance, retirement, charitable) are permitted.
- No explicit statute on uniform, tool, or damage deductions; best practice is written authorization and verification deduction does not reduce below minimum wage.

**Pay statement content**:

- No state-mandated itemized format; federal FLSA recordkeeping requires documentation of hours, rate, gross/net, deductions for at least 3 years.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**: No state mandate for job-posting salary ranges or wage disclosure at hire; employers may inquire about prior salary/wages.

**Salary history ban**: None; employers free to request and consider wage history.

**Equal Pay for Men and Women** (SDCL 60-12-15):

- Prohibits paying employees of one sex at lower rates than opposite sex for comparable work.
- Applies to all South Dakota employers.
- Comparable work defined by skill, effort, responsibility, and working conditions; not physical strength.
- Exceptions: seniority systems, merit systems, quality/production-based systems, factor other than sex.
- Retaliation prohibited (SDCL 60-12-16).
- Employer may not reduce other wages to comply.
- Private right of action available; remedies include back pay and liquidated damages.

**Protected-class pay discrimination** (SDCL 20-13-10):

- Applies to race, color, creed, religion, sex, ancestry, disability, national origin discrimination in pay or terms of employment.
- Enforced by South Dakota Division of Human Rights; also subject to federal Title VII (15+ employees), ADA, ADEA (20+ employees via dual-filing).
- Sioux Falls, Spearfish, Vermillion local ordinances add sexual orientation and gender identity protections (not state law).

**Age discrimination**: Federal ADEA applies (20+ employees); no separate state statute.

**Sexual orientation / gender identity**: No state protection; federal Bostock v. Clayton County (Title VII) applies to employers with 15+ employees; local Sioux Falls ordinance covers all employers with 1+ employee.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**: No state mandate for private-sector paid or unpaid sick leave. Employers who voluntarily offer may set terms; state law silent on interaction with promotion/pay change (best practice: ensure wage does not decrease upon return from leave).

**Paid family/medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate for private sector. Federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees, 12-month tenure, 1,250 hours worked in prior 12 months. State employees eligible for up to 12 weeks paid leave/year under SD state PFML program (does not extend to private employers).

**Breastfeeding/lactation**: Federal PUMP Act applies (reasonable break time and private space other than bathroom for expressing milk, one year postpartum); no additional state mandate.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**: State law silent. Best practice: if employee uses voluntarily offered leave and is promoted or receives base-pay increase effective during or immediately after leave, ensure wage does not decrease from pre-leave level upon return.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and wage records** (SDCL 60-12-17):

- Employers must make, keep, and maintain records of wages, wage rates, job classifications, and terms/conditions of employment.
- Retention period: "reasonable period" (not explicitly defined in statute; federal FLSA requires 3 years).
- Employers with 25+ employees must preserve records subject to inspection by Department of Labor.

**Employee access to personnel files** (private sector):

- No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic access to inspect or copy personnel records.
- Public employees covered under S.D. Admin. R. 55:09:02:01 (employment examinations, performance appraisals, competitive materials are confidential; name, classification, salary releasable on written request or with employee authorization).
- Private employers may voluntarily adopt personnel-file access policy or address in handbook.

**Record content and format**:

- No state-mandated electronic vs. paper requirement; either acceptable.
- No state-mandated pay-stub format; federal FLSA recordkeeping applies.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Breach Notification** (SDCL 22-40-20):

- Applies to individuals/entities conducting business in SD that own or license computerized data containing personal information of SD residents.
- Notification required no later than 60 days from discovery or notification of breach, unless law enforcement requests longer delay.
- Must notify affected SD residents in most expedient manner possible.
- If breach affects 250+ SD residents, notification to SD Attorney General required.
- Encryption safe harbor: No notification required if data encrypted and encryption key not compromised.
- Penalties: Up to $5,000 per violation (SDCL 22-40-25).
- Applies to employee personal data (SSN, financial account info, health records, tax IDs, etc.).

**Employee monitoring**: No state statute restricting workplace email, internet, or video monitoring; common-law reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies.

**Biometric data**: No specific state law; federal BIPA applies to employers with operations in Illinois.

**Data residency/processing**: No state law mandating data residency; employers may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided breach notification laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal history inquiry)**:

- **Not applicable to private employers** in South Dakota; private employers may inquire about criminal history at any point.
- Public employers subject to limited restrictions (not relevant to HCM Next private-sector focus).

**Background checks** (private employers):

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions.
- Employers may conduct background checks at any stage (no waiting period for private sector).
- Adverse action notice required under FCRA (not SDCL).

**Drug testing**:

- No comprehensive state statute; employers have broad discretion.
- Pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, random testing all permitted.
- Written policy recommended.
- If employer requires drug test as condition of employment, employer must pay for test and provide medical records (SDCL 34-11.1 context).
- Employers may take adverse action if applicant or employee tests positive for cannabis metabolites, particularly for safety-sensitive positions.

**Medical marijuana**: SD legalized medical marijuana; no employer accommodation required for lawful off-duty medical-marijuana use by cardholders; employer may test for impairment.

**E-Verify mandate**: None in South Dakota. Federal I-9 requirements apply; E-Verify optional.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mass layoff notice (state level)**:

- No South Dakota mini-WARN act; federal WARN Act applies (100+ employees, 60 days' notice, 25+ affected or 25%+ of workforce).
- Federal notice to local dislocated-worker unit and chief elected official required.

**Severance**: No state mandate; severance contractual and requires explicit agreement.

**Non-compete agreements** (SDCL 53-9-11):

- Post-employment non-competes **enforceable only if**:
  - Duration not exceeding two years from termination date.
  - Geographic scope: specified county, first- or second-class municipality, or other specified area.
  - Employer continues to carry on like business therein at termination.
  - Reasonable in scope to protect legitimate business interest (trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information).
- Enforcement disfavored; courts may void entirely if unreasonable rather than reform.
- Out-of-state choice-of-law clauses not enforceable to import foreign non-compete rules (SD courts reject).

**Non-compete void for healthcare practitioners** (SDCL 53-9-11.2):

- Non-competes voidable (with exceptions) for: physicians, advanced practice nurses, dentists, dental hygienists, physician assistants, optometrists, chiropractors, psychologists, and other defined licensed healthcare practitioners for contracts entered on or after July 1, 2023.

**Non-solicitation agreements**:

- Common law applies; enforceable if reasonable in duration, geography, scope with legitimate business interest (no specific statute).

**Restraint of trade doctrine** (SDCL 53-9-8):

- Every contract restraining lawful profession, trade, or business is void except as provided by SDCL 53-9-9 to 53-9-11.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- South Dakota applies common-law test (not ABC test); factors include: integration into business, nature of services, control/direction, hiring/supervising assistants, set working times.
- No presumption of employee status; employers have discretion if facts support contractor status.
- Misclassification enforced by Department of Labor, Department of Revenue; penalties include back wages and reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If work performed in South Dakota (remote or on-site), South Dakota law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Best practice: written agreement specifying South Dakota law governs employment relationship for multi-state workers.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints**:

1. **Payday consistency** (SDCL 60-11-9): On promotion or base-pay change, verify payday remains regular and consistent. If payday changes, communicate advance notice to employee and document in system.

2. **Base-pay increase implementation** (SDCL 60-11-9): Wage increase may be implemented immediately (no statutory advance notice required). For wage decrease, best practice is advance written notice (1+ pay period) and employee acknowledgment; capture in system.

3. **Minimum wage compliance** (SDCL 60-11-3): On promotion or base-pay change, verify wage meets $11.85/hour (2026 rate; indexed annually). Flag if promotion results in subminimum wage. For tipped employees, verify cash wage meets $5.925/hour + tips meet $11.85 minimum.

4. **Tipped-wage interaction**: If promoted to non-tipped or tipped role, recalculate tipped-wage minimum (SDCL 60-11-3.1); if tips change post-promotion, verify tip credit and employer make-up obligations.

5. **Equal Pay compliance** (SDCL 60-12-15): On promotion or base-pay change within same location, system should flag if new wage for one gender differs materially from peer rates for comparable work; require manager justification or HR approval for outlier increases. Document non-discriminatory basis (seniority, merit, production quality, or other factor other than sex).

6. **Protected-class discrimination risk** (SDCL 20-13-10): On promotion or base-pay change, verify wage/terms not discriminatory based on race, color, creed, religion, sex, ancestry, disability, or national origin. Document business justification for all changes.

7. **Wage record retention** (SDCL 60-12-17): System must retain promotion records (effective date, wage change, justification) for minimum 3 years (federal FLSA standard; state "reasonable period" aligns with this). Support Department of Labor inspection rights.

8. **Non-compete review** (SDCL 53-9-11): On promotion, check if employee subject to non-compete or non-solicit. If non-compete exists, verify:
   - Duration ≤ 2 years from termination.
   - Geography: specified county, municipality, or area.
   - Reasonableness in scope.
   - Employer continues like business at location.
   - Document in system; flag if likely unenforceable (healthcare practitioner after 7/1/2023, or overly broad).

9. **Final pay post-promotion** (SDCL 60-11-10): If termination occurs post-promotion, ensure all wages earned at promoted rate from effective date forward are paid by next regular payday. Employee may demand written immediate payment (due within 5 days). If employee returns all property, no holdback permitted.

10. **Workers' compensation retaliation check** (SDCL 62-1-16): If promotion follows any workers' compensation claim filing, document business justification for promotion/wage change to mitigate retaliation risk.

11. **Whistleblower protection** (public policy): If promotion follows any protected whistleblowing activity (good-faith report of unlawful conduct), flag for legal review to ensure retaliation risk mitigated.

12. **Tobacco use protection** (SDCL 60-4-11): If promotion or wage change alters off-duty activities (schedule change, location affecting tobacco use), ensure change does not violate statute unless bona fide occupational requirement.

13. **Voluntary leave and wage return** (best practice): If employee used voluntarily offered leave and is promoted/receives increase during or immediately after leave, ensure wage upon return is no less than pre-leave wage.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify scope of "reasonable period" for wage record retention under SDCL 60-12-17 (federal 3-year standard is safe harbor).
- Clarify whether non-compete "specified area" includes multiple counties or multi-state regions if employer operates therein.
- Confirm whether healthcare non-compete void per SDCL 53-9-11.2 (effective 7/1/2023) applies retroactively to pre-2023 contracts (likely not; statute may be prospective only—verify).
- Verify whether tipped-wage "make up" obligation (SDCL 60-11-3.1) applies retroactively on week-by-week basis or only if annual average falls short (likely weekly per DOL guidance).
- Confirm whether implied contract for "cause-only termination" in handbook can override at-will status after promotion or only at hire.
- Verify scope of "comparable work" for equal pay (SDCL 60-12-15): same job title, same location, same manager, or broader?
