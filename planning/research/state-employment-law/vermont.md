# Vermont Employment Law Research

**State:** Vermont | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with statutory exceptions**: Vermont recognizes employment-at-will under common law, but prohibits termination in violation of public policy (whistleblower protections, jury duty, military service, workers' compensation claims, wage/unemployment claims). No single comprehensive "at-will statute"; exceptions codified in 21 V.S.A. Chapter 5 and Chapter 3 (safety/health).

- **Minimum wage $14.42/hour (2026)**: Indexed annually by the lower of 5% or CPI change (21 V.S.A. § 384). Tipped minimum $7.21/hour (January 2026). Overtime at 1.5× for hours >40/week.

- **Pay frequency and final pay**: Weekly or biweekly payment required by default; semimonthly/monthly with written notice (21 V.S.A. § 342). Final pay on discharge: within 72 hours of termination; on resignation: next regular payday or following Friday (21 V.S.A. § 342a).

- **Earned Sick Time (EST)**: 40 hours/year accrual (1 hour per 52 hours worked) for employees averaging ≥18 hours/week; usable for employee/family medical care, domestic violence/stalking services (21 V.S.A. §§ 481–486). One-year waiting period permitted.

- **Parental and Family Leave**: Unpaid; 10+ employees for parental (birth/adoption, 1 year); 15+ employees for family (serious illness, bereavement up to 2 weeks). Act 32 (2025) expanded definitions and coverage. Short-term family leave: ≤24 hours/year for school/medical appointments (21 V.S.A. §§ 470–474, § 472a).

- **Paid Family & Medical Leave**: Voluntary, insurance-based program via The Hartford (VT-FMLI, effective 2023). Low uptake (~63 employers, 0.6% of workforce as of 2026). No employer mandate; no wage replacement requirement.

- **Pay transparency mandate**: Act 155 (2024, effective 2025-07-01) requires employers with 5+ employees to disclose wage/salary range in Vermont job postings; applies to salary, hourly, commission (with statement), and tipped positions (21 V.S.A. § 495n).

- **Salary history ban and equal pay**: Employers cannot inquire about or seek prior compensation; cannot require compensation minimums/maximums as hiring condition (21 V.S.A. § 495m, 2018). Equal pay for equal work (21 V.S.A. § 495).

- **Ban-the-box (criminal records)**: Employers cannot request criminal history on initial application; may inquire after qualification for interview (21 V.S.A. § 495j, 2017). Federal/state mandatory-disqualification positions exempt.

- **Drug testing**: Strict limits; random/company-wide testing prohibited except when required by federal law. Probable-cause testing only if employee assistance program available; positive result does not mandate termination if employee completes program (21 V.S.A. §§ 512–514).

- **Breach notification**: 45-day notice requirement post-discovery for employee data breaches; attorney general notification within 14 days (9 V.S.A. § 2435, 2018).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Vermont is an employment-at-will state under common law; employers may terminate without cause or notice. However, statutory and common-law exceptions apply:

**Public policy exceptions**:

- Termination for filing wage theft, workers' compensation, or unemployment claims prohibited.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (speech, assembly, religion) prohibited.
- Termination for jury duty, military service, or appearing as witness prohibited.
- Whistleblower protection: Employees reporting safety violations, health hazards, or workplace injuries cannot be discharged or retaliated against (21 V.S.A. § 231, VOSHA).
- Healthcare whistleblower protection: Hospital/nursing home employees protected for reporting legal violations, medical errors, improper care, or safety hazards.
- Wage discussion protection: Retaliation for disclosing or discussing wages/benefits prohibited (21 V.S.A. § 495, subdivision (c)).
- Opposing unlawful employment practices: Retaliation prohibited for opposing discrimination, filing complaints, or participating in agency investigations (21 V.S.A. § 495, subdivision (c)).

**Required written notices**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 342: Employers must provide written notice (name, address, and usual compensation) at hire and prior to any wage/pay-schedule change; employee must sign acknowledgment retained in file.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 384: Vermont minimum wage $14.42/hour as of January 1, 2026 (previously $14.01). Annual adjustment on each January 1: the lower of 5% or CPI-U increase for 12 months prior to preceding September 1, never decreasing.
- Tipped minimum wage: $7.21/hour (January 2026); employers must supplement to reach minimum wage if tips fall short. Base wage set at one-half minimum wage (21 V.S.A. § 384).

**Overtime**:

- No state standard; federal FLSA applies: 1.5× regular rate for hours >40 in workweek.

**Pay frequency**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 342: Employers must pay weekly in lawful money or check, to a day not >6 days prior to payment. After written notice to each employee, biweekly or semimonthly allowed (wages earned to day not >6 days prior). Collective bargaining agreements may allow payment to day not >13 days prior.

**Final pay on termination/resignation**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 342a: On discharge, all wages due within 72 hours. On voluntary resignation with notice, wages due on next regular payday; if resignation without notice, wages due within 72 hours of last day worked. On layoff, wages due on next regular payday.
- Penalties: Employer liable for liquidated damages equal to 10% of unpaid wages per day (excluding Sundays/holidays) if willfully fails to pay without good cause, or unpaid amount, whichever is smaller.

**Wage deductions**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 343: Only legal deductions permitted (taxes, garnishments, court orders) or written employee authorization for lawful purposes (union dues, insurance, charitable contributions, retirement/savings, voluntary uniform cleaning, non-required garment logos/designs).
- No deduction for employer-required tools, uniforms, or costs unless written consent and deduction does not reduce wages below minimum wage.

**Pay statement content**:

- No state-mandated itemized format; federal FLSA requirements apply; employers should document hours, rates, deductions, net pay per retention requirements (21 V.S.A. § 342).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage range disclosure in job postings**:

- Act 155 of 2024 (21 V.S.A. § 495n, effective 2025-07-01): Employers with 5+ employees must include "type and range of monetary compensation" expected to offer for Vermont positions in job advertisements. Range defined as good-faith expectation of minimum/maximum annual salary or hourly wage.
- Commission-based positions: Statement that "employer cannot post expected minimum/maximum salary range" required.
- Tipped positions: Must disclose that fact and state base wage or range of base wages.

**Salary history inquiry ban**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 495m (2018): Employers cannot inquire about, seek, or determine hiring/interview decisions on current or past compensation. Exception: If prospective employee voluntarily discloses, employer may confirm after offer made. Violations subject to enforcement by Vermont Attorney General.

**Equal pay**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 495: Employers prohibited from paying employees of different sexes at different rates for equal work requiring equal skill, effort, and responsibility under similar conditions. Exceptions: seniority/merit systems, production systems, demonstrable factor other than sex.

**Wage discussion protection**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 495, subdivision (c): Employees protected from retaliation for disclosing or discussing wages/benefits; employers cannot require confidentiality of pay as employment condition.

**Protected classes and discrimination**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 495: Discrimination prohibited based on race, color, religion, ancestry, national origin, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, place of birth, crime victim status, age, HIV status, or disability. Applies to employers with 1+ employees.
- Retaliation prohibited for opposing unlawful acts, filing complaints, testifying/assisting in investigations, or disclosing wages (§ 495(c)).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Earned Sick Time (EST)**:

- 21 V.S.A. §§ 481–486 (effective 2017): Employees averaging ≥18 hours/week earn 1 hour per 52 hours worked (40 hours/year after January 1, 2019; 24 hours in 2017–2018).
- Use: Employee or family medical care, obtaining medical/dental/counseling services, or arranging services for domestic violence/sexual assault/stalking victims.
- Compensation: At least the higher of normal hourly rate or minimum wage.
- Waiting period: Employer may require 1-year waiting period for newly hired employees; accrual continues during waiting period but use restricted.
- Carryover: Unused balance carries to following year (no cap specified; verify employer policy override).

**Parental Leave (21 V.S.A. § 470)**:

- Employers with 10+ employees working ≥30 hours/week average: Unpaid leave for birth, adoption, or initial foster-care placement (child age ≤18) to care/bond with child within 1 year of placement.
- Duration: Up to 12 weeks per 12-month period (reduced if employer provides paid leave).
- Job restoration: Same or comparable position upon return.

**Family Leave (21 V.S.A. § 471)**:

- Employers with 15+ employees working ≥30 hours/week average: Unpaid leave for serious illness of family member (spouse, child, parent, in-law).
- Duration: Up to 12 weeks per 12-month period.
- Job restoration: Same or comparable position.

**Bereavement Leave (21 V.S.A. § 471, as amended by Act 32, 2025)**:

- Up to 2 weeks of the 12-week family leave entitlement for death of family member within 1 year; up to 5 workdays consecutive. Includes leave for estate administration/settlement.
- Act 32 (2025) expanded family definition and bereavement scope.

**Short-Term Family Leave (21 V.S.A. § 472a)**:

- All employers: Unpaid leave ≤4 hours per 30-day period, ≤24 hours per year, for school/medical appointments (child, parent, spouse, in-law) or medical emergency.

**Vermont Paid Family & Medical Leave Insurance (VT-FMLI)**:

- Voluntary, insurance-based program effective 2023 (via The Hartford). Not employer-mandated; Phase 3 (2026) targets self-employed/uninsured individuals.
- Benefit: Up to 6 weeks/year at 60% wage replacement for own serious health condition, family member care, new child bonding, or military family leave.
- Uptake: Very low (~63 employers, ~0.6% of private-sector workforce as of April 2026); program status remains voluntary through at least 2026.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- No statute restricting wage reduction upon return from leave; common-law good-faith implied covenant may apply. Promotion during leave does not affect leave entitlement.
- EST accrual typically continues during paid leave; verify employer policy on unpaid leave.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Personnel record retention**:

- No state statute mandating specific private-sector retention period; federal contractors subject to FLSA 3-year requirement (payroll records, tax documents).
- Best practice: 3+ years; verify employer policy for compliance with state litigation hold requirements.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- No specific statute for private-sector employees; public employees have statutory access rights (1 V.S.A. § 317).
- Recommend: Policy allowing employee inspection/copy requests within 10–15 business days at or near worksite.

**Record format**:

- No mandate for electronic vs. paper; either acceptable if retention and access requirements met.

**Payroll record content**:

- No state-mandated format; federal FLSA requires documentation of name, address, hours (daily/weekly), wage rate, gross/net pay, deductions.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Breach notification**:

- 9 V.S.A. § 2435 (Security Breach Notice Act, 2018): Data collectors (including employers) holding employee personally identifiable information or login credentials must notify affected individuals and Attorney General without unreasonable delay, no later than 45 days after discovery.
- Notification: Attorney General within 14 days of discovery; DFR-regulated entities within 14 business days; law enforcement delays permitted upon request.
- Encryption safe harbor: No notification required if compromised information was encrypted and encryption key not compromised.
- Enforcement: Vermont Consumer Protection Act (injunctive relief, $10,000 fine per violation).

**Employee monitoring and privacy**:

- No state statute banning workplace email/internet monitoring or video monitoring of work areas; common-law reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies.

**Social media**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 495l (2018): Employers cannot require employees/applicants to disclose personal social media credentials, access account in presence of employer, divulge content, change privacy settings, or add employer to contacts.
- Exception: Employer may request specifically identified content to comply with legal obligations, investigate unauthorized disclosure of proprietary information, or investigate unlawful harassment/threats/discriminatory content (employee-related).

**Employee data privacy law**:

- No comprehensive Vermont state-level employee data privacy statute currently in force (2024 proposal vetoed); federal privacy standards (FCRA, GLBA, CCPA applicability) apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- 21 V.S.A. § 495j (2017): Employers cannot request criminal history on initial application form. May inquire during interview or after determining applicant otherwise qualified.
- Exceptions: Federal/state mandatory-disqualification positions; employer obligations under federal/state law to exclude convicted individuals. If employer inquires, applicant afforded opportunity to explain circumstances and post-conviction rehabilitation; civil penalty up to $100 per violation.

**Background checks**:

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions for private employers. Expunged or sealed records (13 V.S.A. ch. 230, verify) need not be disclosed by the applicant.

**Drug testing**:

- 21 V.S.A. §§ 512–514 (strict restrictions): Employers cannot request/require drug tests as condition of employment, promotion, or benefit, except where required by federal law.
- Probable-cause exception: Employer may test individual employee if probable cause exists that employee is using/under influence on the job, provided: (1) employer has available bona fide EAP; (2) positive result does not mandate termination if employee agrees to and completes program (suspension limited to time needed for program, max 3 months); (3) test administered per § 514 standards.
- Random/company-wide testing prohibited (except federal requirement).

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No state mandate for private-sector E-Verify; federal I-9 requirements apply.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice (Vermont WARN Act, 21 V.S.A. §§ 411–415)**:

- Applies to employers with 50+ employees. Requires 45 days' advance written notice before mass layoff/plant closing affecting 50+ employees over 90-day period (not 15+ as in some sources; statute specifies 50+).
- Notice recipients: All affected employees, union representatives, Secretary of Commerce and Community Development, Commissioner of Labor, state Attorney General, chief elected officials of affected municipalities.
- Content: Approximate number of employees affected, job titles, anticipated date of job loss.
- Wage obligation: All unpaid wages and compensation due to affected workers must be paid.

**Severance**:

- No state mandate except contractual obligations. Severance requires explicit agreement.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- No statute; common law applies. Vermont courts enforce non-competes if reasonable in duration, geography, and scope and protecting legitimate business interests (trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information).
- Continued at-will employment is sufficient consideration for non-compete covenant.
- No per-se ban for low-wage workers (contrast to some neighboring states). Verify enforceability on case-by-case basis.
- 2025 legislative proposals (e.g., H.205) to establish statutory non-compete restrictions under consideration but not enacted.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness standard.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- No single state statute; Vermont courts apply common-law "right-to-control" test and, for certain contexts, the ABC test. VDOL, the Vermont Department of Taxes, and the USDOL each apply separate tests; employer must satisfy all applicable tests.
- Misclassification: Enforced by Department of Labor, Department of Revenue, Attorney General; penalties include back wages, liquidated damages, reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute. Standard employment law applies regardless of work location. If employee works in Vermont (remotely or on-site), Vermont law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity. Each state's laws apply to work performed in that state. Platform should flag multi-state workers for compliance with all applicable state rules.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling: Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

**Mandatory obligations and field restrictions**:

1. **Pay rate change notice** (21 V.S.A. § 342): On any base-pay change (increase or decrease), generate automated written notice stating new rate and effective date. Require employee signed acknowledgment in system (mandatory field for compliance).

2. **Act 155 pay-range disclosure check**: If promotion includes new job posting, system must flag for pay-range disclosure requirement (5+ employees, effective 2025-07-01). Verify posting includes salary/hourly range or commission/tipped statement.

3. **Salary history inquiry prohibition** (21 V.S.A. § 495m): Do not flag, capture, or use applicant/candidate prior salary in promotion decisions. If historical data exists, route to legal/HR review before use.

4. **Equal pay audit** (21 V.S.A. § 495): On promotion + base-pay change, flag if new rate differs >15% from peer rates for equal work in same establishment (same employer). Require manager justification or HR approval for outlier increases to prevent discrimination claims.

5. **Wage discussion protection** (21 V.S.A. § 495(c)): Ensure system/policies do not restrict employee discussions of wages. Audit existing policy language for retaliation clauses; remove restrictions.

6. **Non-compete risk assessment**: No low-wage or healthcare-worker universal bans, but common-law enforceability is uncertain. On promotion, flag if non-compete exists; recommend legal review before enforcement attempt. Document that review occurred.

7. **EST impact on compensation**: If employee takes EST during review/promotion period, ensure final pay includes all promoted-rate wages earned during absence. No statutory restriction, but verify integration with payroll system.

8. **Parental/Family Leave interaction**: If promotion occurs during or immediately after parental/family leave return, system should log promotion as separate from leave restoration to avoid claims that promotion conditioning on leave completion.

9. **Earned Sick Time accrual continuation**: If promotion changes hours/classification, verify EST accrual continues per statute (21 V.S.A. § 481); system should flag if new classification (e.g., salaried) might affect accrual mechanics.

10. **PFML tracking**: If employee is enrolled in optional VT-FMLI program (low uptake, but verify), system should preserve coverage/premium deduction status through promotion.

11. **Promotion timing and final pay**: If separation occurs during or shortly after promotion, ensure final paycheck includes all wages at promoted rate from effective date. No hold-back permitted; all wages due within 72 hours of discharge (21 V.S.A. § 342a).

12. **Record retention for promotion documentation**: System must retain promotion records (offer, acceptance, effective date, wage change notice, employee signature) for 3+ years (best practice; no state mandate for private sector).

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether common-law non-compete enforceability requires case-by-case review or whether Vermont precedent establishes bright-line rules for scope/duration/geography.
- Clarify whether "same establishment" for equal-pay audits under § 495 includes multi-site employers or only same physical location.
- Confirm whether promotion that moves employee above/below EST eligibility threshold (18 hours/week) affects accrual mid-year.
- Verify current status of pending non-compete statutory proposals (H.205 and others) and effective date if enacted during 2026 session.
