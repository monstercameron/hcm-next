# North Dakota Employment Law Research

**State:** North Dakota | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment**: North Dakota recognizes at-will employment for both employer and employee; exceptions exist for workers' compensation retaliation, whistleblower protection (unlawful conduct reports), jury duty, and discrimination under Human Rights Act.
- **Minimum wage $7.25/hour**: North Dakota tracks federal FLSA floor; no state increase since 2009; no tipped-credit sub-minimum.
- **Overtime 1.5x for 40+ weekly hours**: Weekly threshold only; no daily trigger; paid leave does not count toward the 40-hour threshold (N.D. Admin. Code 46-03-01).
- **Pay frequency**: Regular paydays required; semimonthly or monthly common; must be consistent and predictable (NDCC § 34-14-02).
- **Final pay on termination**: All earned, unpaid wages due by next regular payday or within 15 days of separation, whichever comes first (NDCC § 34-14-03); unused accrued leave must be paid unless narrow exception met (employer informed at hire, employee voluntarily resigned, met tenure/notice conditions).
- **No state paid sick leave mandate**: Private employers have no obligation to provide paid or unpaid sick leave unless voluntarily offered.
- **No state paid family/medical leave mandate**: Federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees and other conditions met.
- **Non-competes void except sale/dissolution**: NDCC § 9-08-06 voids post-employment non-competes and non-solicits outside business-sale/partnership-dissolution contexts (one of the nation's strictest bans).
- **Protected classes**: NDCC § 14-02.4 reaches all employers (1+ employee) for discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, marital status, public assistance status, lawful off-duty activities (unless conflict with essential business interests), and political affiliation.
- **No state salary-history ban, no pay-transparency posting mandate, no personnel-file access statute (private employees), no mini-WARN act, E-Verify not mandated, drug testing permitted**.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

North Dakota follows at-will employment doctrine: either party may terminate without cause or notice.

**Public-policy exceptions** limit at-will termination when discharge violates a clear mandate:

- Termination for filing workers' compensation claim or applying for benefits (NDCC § 65-05-37).
- Termination for exercising occupational safety/health rights (OSHA-type activities).
- Termination for whistleblowing (reporting unlawful conduct in good faith to law enforcement or governmental body; NDCC § 34-01-20).
- Termination for jury duty, military service, voting.
- Termination for refusal to perform illegal act.
- Termination in violation of public policy or constitutional rights.
- Termination based on protected class discrimination (NDCC § 14-02.4).
- Termination for asserting rights under FLSA or state wage laws.

**Lawful off-duty activities protection** (NDCC § 14-02.4): Employers may not discriminate based on participation in lawful activity off employer premises during non-working hours, unless activity directly conflicts with employer's essential business-related interests.

**Written notice requirements**: On hire, employer must inform employee of regular payday (NDCC § 34-14-02). Changes to payday must be communicated in advance to affected employees.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (NDCC § 34-06-22):

- Statewide minimum: $7.25/hour (federal floor; no state increase).
- No tipped-credit sub-minimum; tipped employees must earn at least $7.25/hour when tips are included.
- No local ordinance override authority.

**Overtime** (N.D. Admin. Code 46-03-01):

- Time and one-half for hours exceeding 40 in a single workweek.
- Weekly threshold only; no daily or consecutive-hours trigger.
- Paid holidays, vacation, sick leave do not count toward 40-hour threshold.
- Minimum overtime rate at state minimum wage: $10.88/hour ($7.25 × 1.5).

**Pay frequency** (NDCC § 34-14-02):

- Employers must designate regular paydays and communicate them to employees at hire.
- Semimonthly or monthly common; must be consistent and predictable.
- Direct deposit and stored-value card authorized if employee consents.

**Final pay on termination** (NDCC § 34-14-03):

- All earned, unpaid wages due by next regularly scheduled payday or within 15 days of separation, whichever comes first.
- Applies to voluntary resignation, involuntary discharge, and layoff.
- Unused accrued leave must be paid unless employer met three conditions: (1) informed employee at hire of forfeiture policy, (2) employee voluntarily resigned, (3) employee met tenure and advance-notice requirements. Failure to meet any condition voids the exception.

**Permitted deductions** (NDCC § 34-14-04):

- Deductions only if required by law (taxes, garnishments, court orders) or authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes (union dues, health insurance, retirement, charitable).
- No deduction for tools, uniforms, or costs unless employee consents in writing and deduction does not reduce below minimum wage.
- No deduction from final wages for breakage, loss, or damage (implied).

**Pay statement content**:

- No state-mandated itemized pay-stub format; employers should document hours worked, wage rate, deductions, gross/net pay per NDCC § 34-14-04.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**: No state mandate for job-posting salary ranges or wage disclosure at hire; employers may inquire about prior salary/wages.

**Salary history ban**: No state prohibition; employers free to request and consider wage history.

**Equal Pay for Men and Women** (NDCC § 34-06.1-01 et seq.):

- Applies to all employers in North Dakota.
- Prohibits paying employees of one sex at lower rates than opposite sex for comparable work requiring comparable skill, effort, responsibility, and working conditions within the same establishment.
- Exceptions: seniority systems, merit systems, production-quality systems, factor other than sex.
- Retaliation prohibited; employer may not reduce other wages to comply.
- Employer must retain wage records (wage, rate, job classification, terms) for at least 2 years after employment ends.

**Enforcement**: Human Rights Commission investigates complaints; private right of action available for back pay and liquidated damages.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**: No state mandate for private-sector paid or unpaid sick leave. Employers who voluntarily offer sick leave may set terms; employees cannot be penalized for using earned leave.

**Paid family/medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate. Employers may voluntarily offer; federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees, 12-month tenure, 1,250 hours worked in past 12 months.

**State employees only** (H.B. 1170, effective May 1, 2026): State employers must provide 40 hours new-hire leave within first year of employment (does not apply to private sector).

**Breastfeeding/lactation**: Federal PUMP Act applies (reasonable break time and private space other than bathroom for expressing milk, one year postpartum); no additional state mandate. Infant Friendly Workplace designation (voluntary) available for employers offering flexible scheduling, private location, water/hand-washing, refrigerator.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**: If employee uses voluntarily offered paid leave and is promoted/receives base-pay increase, wage must not decrease from pre-leave level upon return (best practice; state law silent on this scenario).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and wage records** (NDCC § 34-14-04):

- Employers must retain true and accurate records of hours worked and wages paid for at least 2 years after employment ends (for equal-pay enforcement, though general records retention period not explicitly codified beyond this).
- Records subject to inspection by Department of Labor and Human Rights agents at reasonable times.

**Employee access to personnel files**: No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic access to inspect or copy personnel records. NDCC § 44-04-18.1 (personal-information exemption) applies only to public employees, not private.

**Record content and format**: No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; either acceptable. No state-mandated pay-stub format.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Breach Notification** (NDCC § 51-30):

- Applies to any person/business owning computerized data with personal information of ND residents.
- Notification required in the most expedient manner possible and without unreasonable delay (no more than 45 days implied by industry practice; statute says "expedient").
- Must notify affected ND residents; notification to North Dakota Attorney General required if breach affects 250+ ND residents.
- Encryption safe harbor: No notification required if data encrypted and encryption key not also compromised.
- Penalties: Up to $5,000 per violation.

**Employee monitoring**: No state statute restricting workplace email/internet/video monitoring; reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies under common law.

**Medical marijuana**: No employer accommodation required for lawful off-duty medical marijuana use (applies to cardholders).

**Data residency/processing**: No state law mandating data residency; employers may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided breach notification laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal history inquiry)**:

- Applies to **public employers only** (NDCC § 12.1-33-02.2, effective 2019); private employers exempt.
- Public employers may not inquire about criminal history until applicant selected for interview.
- Exceptions if federal/state law mandates background check for the position.

**Background checks (private employers)**:

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions.
- Employers may request background checks at any stage (no waiting period for private sector).

**Drug testing** (NDCC § 34-11.1):

- No comprehensive state statute; employers have broad discretion.
- If employer requires drug test as condition of employment, employer must pay for test and furnishing medical records.
- Pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, random testing all permitted.
- Written policy recommended.

**Medical marijuana**: ND legalized medical marijuana; no anti-discrimination for lawful use off-duty (applies to cardholder status; employer may still test for impairment).

**E-Verify mandate**: None in North Dakota. Federal I-9 requirements apply; E-Verify optional.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mass layoff notice (state level)**:

- North Dakota requires advance notice to Job Service North Dakota for mass separations (25+ workers in single establishment, permanent or expected 7+ days), 48 hours prior (separate from federal WARN Act).
- Federal WARN Act applies to employers with 100+ full-time employees; requires 60 days' notice for plant closing or mass layoff affecting 25+ employees or 25%+ of workforce.

**Severance**: No state mandate; severance is contractual and requires explicit agreement.

**Non-compete agreements** (NDCC § 9-08-06):

- Post-employment non-competes are **void and unenforceable** as a matter of public policy, except in the context of:
  - Sale of business or sale of substantial assets.
  - Partnership, LLC, or corporate dissolution.
- Covenants must be reasonable in duration, geography, and scope to protect legitimate business interest (trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information).
- 2019 amendment expanded permissible geographic scope from "city or county" to "wherever employer actually transacts business."
- Courts repeatedly refuse to enforce out-of-state choice-of-law clauses importing different rules.

**Non-solicitation agreements**:

- Common law applies; enforceable if reasonable in duration, geography, scope with legitimate business interest.
- No specific statute; less protected than non-competes but generally enforceable if reasonable.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- North Dakota applies common-law test (not ABC test); factors include: integration into business, nature of services, control/direction, hiring/supervising assistants, set working times, and others.
- No presumption of employee status; employers have discretion if facts support contractor status.
- Misclassification enforced by Department of Labor, Department of Revenue, Attorney General; penalties include back wages and reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If work performed in North Dakota (remote or on-site), North Dakota law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Best practice: specify in writing that North Dakota law governs employment relationship for multi-state workers.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints**:

1. **Payday consistency** (NDCC § 34-14-02): On promotion or base-pay change, system must verify payday remains regular and consistent. If payday changes, communicate advance notice to employee.

2. **Base-pay increase implementation** (NDCC § 34-14-02): Wage increase may be implemented immediately (no statutory advance notice required). For wage decrease, best practice is advance written notice (1+ pay period) and employee acknowledgment; capture in system.

3. **Minimum wage compliance**: On promotion or base-pay change, verify wage meets $7.25/hour (NDCC § 34-06-22). Flag if promotion results in subminimum wage.

4. **Final pay on separation during/after promotion** (NDCC § 34-14-03): If termination occurs post-promotion, ensure all wages earned at promoted rate from effective date forward are paid by next regular payday or within 15 days of separation (whichever comes first). Include unused accrued leave unless narrow forfeiture exception met (employer informed at hire, employee voluntarily resigned, met tenure/notice conditions).

5. **Equal Pay compliance** (NDCC § 34-06.1): On promotion or base-pay change within same establishment, system should flag if new wage for one gender differs materially from peer rates for comparable work; require manager justification or HR approval for outlier increases.

6. **Wage record retention** (NDCC § 34-14-04): System must retain promotion records (wage change, effective date, justification) for minimum 2 years; support Department of Labor inspection rights.

7. **Non-compete/non-solicit review** (NDCC § 9-08-06): On promotion, check if employee subject to non-compete or non-solicit. If non-compete, note as **unenforceable** and void per statute. If non-solicit exists, verify reasonableness in duration, geography, and scope; document in system.

8. **Whistleblower protection check** (NDCC § 34-01-20): If promotion follows any protected whistleblower activity (good-faith report of unlawful conduct), flag for legal review to ensure retaliation risk is mitigated.

9. **Final pay on resignation** (NDCC § 34-14-03): If employee resigns post-promotion, final pay (at promoted wage if effective before resignation) due by next regular payday or within 15 days. Unused leave must be paid unless exception conditions met.

10. **Human Rights Act compliance** (NDCC § 14-02.4): On promotion, verify wage/terms not discriminatory based on protected class (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability, marital status, public assistance status, lawful off-duty activities, political affiliation). Document non-discriminatory basis.

11. **Lawful off-duty activities** (NDCC § 14-02.4): If promotion affects employee's ability to engage in lawful off-duty activities (schedule change, location), ensure change does not violate statute unless activity directly conflicts with essential business interests.

12. **Workers' compensation retaliation** (NDCC § 65-05-37): If employee recently filed workers' comp claim or applied for benefits, promotion and wage change must not appear retaliatory; document business justification.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether "same establishment" for Equal Pay Act comparisons (NDCC § 34-06.1) includes multi-site employers or same physical worksite only.
- Clarify whether accrued leave forfeiture exception (NDCC § 34-14-03) applies retroactively to promotion effective date or only prospectively.
- Confirm whether implied contract for "cause-only termination" in handbook can override at-will status after promotion or only at hire.
- Verify scope of medical-marijuana protections: whether employer may prohibit working while impaired, or only forbid discrimination for cardholder status.
- Confirm whether non-solicitation agreements are enforceable absent explicit statutory language (common law only).
