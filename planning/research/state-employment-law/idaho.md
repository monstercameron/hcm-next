# Idaho Employment Law Research

**State:** Idaho | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment default**: Idaho recognizes at-will employment as the default relationship; either party may terminate without cause unless an exception applies (common law exceptions: public policy violations, implied contract, breach of good faith).
- **Final pay timing strict (10 days or regular payday)**: Idaho Code § 45-606 requires wages by the earlier of the next regular payday or 10 days after termination; employee request for early payment triggers 48-hour deadline (weekends/holidays excluded).
- **Minimum wage tracks federal**: Idaho Code § 44-1502 sets minimum wage at $7.25/hour and automatically adjusts when federal minimum wage changes; no separate state overtime law (federal FLSA applies).
- **Wage deductions restricted**: Deductions only permitted if required by law or with written employee authorization (Idaho Code § 45-609); must provide itemized statement of deductions; unpaid wages accrue 15-day penalty (up to $750).
- **Non-competes enforceable with limits**: Idaho Code §§ 44-2701, 44-2704 enforce non-competes for "key employees" only if reasonable in duration (18 months max, presumed reasonable if ≤18 months), geography, and scope; must protect legitimate business interests; violations of restraint doctrine may result in unenforceability.
- **No state paid sick leave or FMLA**: Idaho has no mandated paid sick leave or state family/medical leave law; federal FMLA (if applicable) and FLSA apply; employers are free to offer voluntarily.
- **No mini-WARN act**: Idaho has no state mass-layoff notice requirement; federal WARN Act (60 days for 100+ employees, 50+ affected) applies to covered employers.
- **Discrimination: race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability**: Idaho Code § 67-5909 (Idaho Human Rights Act) prohibits employment discrimination in hiring, compensation, and terms/conditions for protected classes; private right of action and administrative complaint process available.
- **Data breach notification required**: Idaho Code § 28-51-105 mandates notification "without unreasonable delay" if personal data accessed/misused; state agencies must notify AG within 24 hours.
- **Three-year record retention**: Idaho Code § 45-610 requires employers maintain employment records for 3 years from date of separation; must provide wage information and notice of wage reductions to employees.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Idaho is an at-will employment state. Either party may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any reason, without cause or notice unless a specific exception applies or the parties have agreed otherwise in a valid employment contract (Idaho Code § 44-901 et seq. addresses employment contracts; common law recognizes at-will as default).

**Exceptions to at-will termination**:

- **Public policy exception**: Termination in violation of established public policy (e.g., retaliation for filing a workers' compensation claim, fulfilling jury duty, or reporting illegal activity) may create liability.
- **Implied employment contract**: Oral or written promises of job security, handbooks with progressive discipline, or course of dealing may create enforceable contract obligations beyond at-will terms; courts apply common law contract principles.
- **Good faith and fair dealing**: Idaho recognizes a covenant of good faith and fair dealing in employment relationships, though scope and enforcement remain unsettled; **verify** whether this doctrine restricts at-will termination rights or applies only to dispute resolution.

**Required written notices at hire and on change**:

- At time of hiring, employers should provide written notice of the rate of remuneration, hours of employment, and pay schedule.
- **Wage reduction notice (Idaho Code § 45-610)**: Employers must inform employees of any wage reductions before the affected work is performed; written notice required if requested by employee.
- **No statutory notice period for pay/role changes**: Idaho does not mandate a specific waiting period or advance notice before a promotion, base-pay change, or role change takes effect; employers may implement changes immediately unless employment contract specifies otherwise.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- Idaho Code § 44-1502 sets the minimum wage at $7.25 per hour.
- The statute explicitly requires Idaho's minimum wage to "conform to, and track with, the federal minimum wage," meaning Idaho's rate automatically adjusts when the federal minimum wage increases.
- **Reduced minimum wage for minors**: $4.25 per hour for employees under 20 years old during their first 90 consecutive calendar days of employment (Idaho Code § 44-1502).
- **Tipped employees**: Minimum of $3.35 per hour in direct wages; if tips combined with direct wages do not equal the applicable minimum wage, employer must make up the difference (Idaho Code § 44-1502).
- No subminimum wage allowed for any other class of worker.

**Overtime**:

- **No separate state overtime law**. Idaho defers to federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) overtime provisions: 1.5x regular rate for hours over 40 per week; executive, administrative, professional, and outside sales exemptions apply under 29 U.S.C. § 213(a).

**Pay frequency**:

- Idaho Code § 45-606 does not mandate a specific pay frequency (weekly, bi-weekly, monthly); employers may set frequency by agreement, but must pay consistently and in accordance with wage payment rules.
- Wages must be paid "in full or in part" at the time specified in the employment arrangement.

**Final pay on termination**:

- **Timing (Idaho Code § 45-606)**: All wages "then due the employee" must be paid by the earlier of (1) the next regular payday, or (2) within ten (10) calendar days of layoff or termination, excluding weekends and holidays.
- **Employee request for early payment**: If an employee makes a written request for earlier payment, all wages then due must be paid within forty-eight (48) hours of receipt of such request, excluding weekends and holidays.
- **Director's discretion**: The Idaho Department of Labor director may grant temporary extensions to these timeframes upon application showing "good and sufficient reasons."
- **Scope of "wages due"**: Includes all compensation for work performed in the pay period immediately preceding separation; employees not paid on hourly or salary basis must receive at least minimum wage for all hours worked.
- **Accrued leave (verify)**: Idaho statute does not explicitly require payment of accrued paid time off (vacation, sick leave, personal days) upon separation unless agreed by employment contract or policy; employers should clarify in handbook.

**Permitted wage deductions**:

- **Idaho Code § 45-609**: Employers may withhold wages only if (1) required or empowered by state or federal law, (2) authorized by written employee consent for a lawful purpose, or (3) withheld for court-ordered garnishment or tax obligations.
- No deductions for "tools of trade," uniforms, or employer-imposed costs are permitted unless employee expressly authorizes in writing.
- **Itemized deduction statement**: Employers must furnish employees a detailed statement of all deductions for each pay period when deductions occur; failure to comply is a misdemeanor.

**Pay statement content requirements**:

- Idaho Code § 45-609 requires a written statement detailing deductions when deductions are made; no separate statute mandates specific pay stub format or content beyond federal FLSA rules.

**Wage theft penalty**:

- **Idaho Code § 45-607**: If an employer fails to pay wages on time, the employee's wages continue to accrue at the same rate for up to 15 days, with a maximum penalty of $750 (or $500 if paid before a lien is filed); penalties forfeit if employee avoids payment or refuses to accept.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure in postings or on request**:

- **No Idaho state law**: Idaho has no statute requiring employers to disclose wage range in job postings or on applicant request.
- Federal EEO-1 reporting (for employers with 100+ employees) and EEOC guidance apply if applicable.

**Salary history ban**:

- **No Idaho state law**: Idaho has no statute prohibiting inquiry into prospective employees' wage or salary history; employers are free to request this information.
- Some municipalities may have enacted local bans; verify local jurisdiction where employee works.

**Pay data reporting**:

- **No state-level pay equity reporting requirement**: Idaho does not mandate pay equity audits or public reporting of salary data by protected class.
- Federal EEO-1 reporting applies if applicable to employer size.

**Equal pay / sex-based wage discrimination statute**:

- **No separate equal pay statute**: Idaho does not have a standalone statute requiring "equal pay for equal work" based on sex.
- **Discrimination prohibition (Idaho Code § 67-5909)**: Idaho Human Rights Act prohibits compensation discrimination based on sex (and other protected classes: race, color, religion, national origin, age, disability) in hiring, discharge, and terms/conditions of employment; private right of action available through Idaho Human Rights Commission or district court.
- Disparate impact and disparate treatment claims available under Idaho common law discrimination theories; burden-shifting framework follows federal Title VII precedent.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**:

- **No Idaho state mandate**: Idaho has no statutory requirement for employers to provide paid sick leave, paid personal days, or paid time off of any kind.
- Employers are free to offer paid sick leave voluntarily and may set their own accrual, usage, and carryover rules subject to employment contract terms.
- Any paid leave entitlement must be honored upon termination unless employer policy explicitly states forfeiture (verify state common law on implied obligation to pay accrued leave).

**State family and medical leave**:

- **No Idaho equivalent to FMLA**: Idaho does not have a state family and medical leave act; employees in Idaho are not eligible for state FMLA leave.

**Federal FMLA**:

- Employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles are covered by the federal Family and Medical Leave Act (29 U.S.C. § 2601).
- Eligible employees (12+ months tenure, 1,250+ hours worked in prior 12 months) may take up to 12 weeks unpaid leave in a 12-month period for qualifying reasons (birth/adoption, serious health condition, military service, domestic violence).
- Employers must provide health insurance continuation during FMLA leave and restore employee to same or equivalent position upon return.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- If an employee takes leave (whether paid, unpaid, FMLA, or voluntary) and is promoted or receives a base-pay increase during absence, upon return the employee is entitled to the higher pay (federal FMLA restoration rules; no separate Idaho statute).
- If an employee is demoted or receives a pay cut during leave, courts apply common law contract principles to determine rights (verify whether salary reduction during leave is actionable under good-faith covenant).
- No Idaho statute specifically addresses leave interaction with role changes; employers should document pay decisions and compliance with FMLA restoration requirements.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and employment record retention**:

- **Idaho Code § 45-610**: Employers must maintain employment records for a minimum of three (3) years from the last date of the employee's service.
- Records must include: employee name, address, occupation, hours worked (if applicable), wage information, and all compensation paid.
- No maximum retention period specified; employers may retain longer at their discretion.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- **No specific Idaho statute**: Idaho does not have a statute granting employees a statutory right to inspect or copy their personnel files.
- Common law good-faith obligation may imply a reasonable right to review information used in employment decisions; **verify** whether case law supports employee access rights in Idaho.
- Employers should establish a written policy on file access and consider allowing reasonable inspection to avoid disputes.

**Disputed information**:

- No Idaho statute addresses removal or correction of disputed personnel file information; employers should establish internal procedures for employee disputes and document any corrections/responses.

**Record format**:

- No Idaho statute specifies electronic vs. paper format; employers may maintain records electronically or in paper if retention and access requirements are met; ensure data security and backup compliance.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Idaho employee data privacy law**:

- **No comprehensive employee privacy statute**: Idaho does not have a consumer privacy act or employee-specific data protection law comparable to CCPA (California) or GDPR (EU).
- Employers may collect and process employee data subject to general data security obligations and federal privacy law (if applicable to the employer's industry: healthcare, financial services, etc.).

**Electronic monitoring and surveillance**:

- **No state law restriction on workplace monitoring**: Idaho does not prohibit electronic surveillance, email monitoring, or video monitoring of employees during work hours.
- Employers should consider federal wiretapping laws (18 U.S.C. § 2511) and reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine when monitoring employee communications.
- **Biometric data (fingerprints, iris scans)**: No Idaho statute specifically regulates biometric collection for timekeeping or identification; employers may collect with employee consent or as part of background check/time-tracking system.

**Breach notification**:

- **Idaho Code § 28-51-105**: If personal information of Idaho residents is accessed, acquired, or used without authorization, the entity must "conduct in good faith a reasonable and prompt investigation" and notify affected residents "as soon as possible...without unreasonable delay."
- **State agency requirement**: State agencies must notify the Idaho Attorney General within 24 hours of discovering a breach.
- **Third-party custodians**: Data processors or custodians holding employee information they do not own must immediately notify the data owner and cooperate by sharing breach details.
- **Law enforcement exception**: Notification may be delayed if law enforcement determines it would impede a criminal investigation; notification must occur once the agency confirms no longer necessary.
- **Criminal penalty**: Government employees who intentionally disclose protected information face misdemeanor charges (up to $2,000 fine or 1 year imprisonment).

**Data residency or processing constraints**:

- **No Idaho data residency requirement**: Idaho does not mandate that employee data be stored in-state; employers may process/store data in-state or out-of-state subject to security obligations and applicable federal law (e.g., HIPAA, FERPA).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- **No Idaho state ban-the-box law for private sector**: Idaho does not prohibit private employers from asking about criminal history on job applications or during interviews.
- Some municipalities in Idaho (verify specific jurisdictions: Boise, Moscow) have enacted local ban-the-box ordinances; employers in those jurisdictions must comply locally.
- Federal FCRA (Fair Credit Reporting Act) applies if employers use third-party consumer reporting agencies for background checks.

**Background checks**:

- **No Idaho statute limiting private-sector background checks**: Idaho does not restrict private employers from conducting pre-employment or ongoing background checks.
- **FCRA compliance required**: If an employer uses a third-party consumer reporting agency (including credit bureaus), the employer must comply with FCRA notice, dispute, and adverse-action procedures (29 CFR Part 1602).
- Employers should ensure compliance with Fair Hiring Practices and equal employment opportunity laws when using background checks.

**Drug testing**:

- **No state-level statute regulating private-sector drug testing**: Idaho does not restrict private employers' ability to conduct pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, or post-accident drug testing.
- **Federal DOT rules apply**: Employers in transportation (motor carriers, railways, aviation, maritime) are subject to federal Department of Transportation drug-testing regulations.
- Employers should verify local ordinances in jurisdictions where employees work; some municipalities may restrict testing.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- **No Idaho state mandate for private-sector E-Verify**: Idaho does not require private employers to use E-Verify (federal Form I-9 verification system).
- **Federal I-9 requirement applies**: All employers must verify employment eligibility and complete Form I-9 under Immigration Reform and Control Act (IRCA).

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice and severance (mini-WARN)**:

- **No Idaho state law**: Idaho has no mini-WARN act requiring advance notice or severance pay for mass layoffs.
- **Federal WARN Act applies**: Employers with 100+ employees must provide 60 days' advance written notice to affected employees and state employment agency if layoff/plant closing affects 50+ employees (29 U.S.C. § 2101 et seq.).

**Severance and notice requirements**:

- **No mandatory severance**: Idaho does not require employers to provide severance pay upon termination.
- Severance is enforceable if provided by written employment agreement, policy, or past practice establishing an implicit contract; employers may condition severance on execution of general release.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- **Idaho Code §§ 44-2701, 44-2704**: Non-compete agreements are enforceable if they meet four criteria:
  1. Party must be a "key employee or key independent contractor" (defined as employee in top 5% of earners, presumable; may be challenged with evidence to contrary).
  2. Agreement must have a legitimate business purpose: protecting trade secrets, confidential information, customer relationships, or substantial relationships with prospective/existing clients.
  3. **Duration**: Postemployment restriction cannot exceed 18 months from date of termination unless additional consideration (beyond employment) is given; agreements of 18 months or less are presumed reasonable as to duration.
  4. **Reasonableness test**: Geographic scope, type of employment/business restricted, and degree of restraint must be reasonable and no greater than necessary to protect employer's legitimate interests; courts apply "blue pencil" doctrine to modify overbroad restrictions if possible.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- **Idaho Code § 44-2704**: Non-solicitation agreements (restricting solicitation of employees or customers) are not considered "non-competes" if they meet reasonableness standards: no more than 18 months duration, limited geographic scope, restricted to employer's business/customers, and do not restrict employee's ability to work in the general field.
- Enforced under same reasonableness standard as non-competes; presumed reasonable if 18 months or less.

**Non-disclosure and trade secrets**:

- Idaho Code § 44-2701 preserves employer's ability to protect trade secrets and proprietary information through trade secrets law (Idaho Uniform Trade Secrets Act, Idaho Code § 48-3901 et seq.) independent of non-compete enforceability.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- **No ABC test in Idaho statute**: Idaho applies common law test (control, integration, economic realities) to classify workers as employees vs. independent contractors for state wage-and-hour and tax purposes.
- **Control test (primary factor)**: Degree of control employer exercises over manner and means of work; independent contractors retain control over work methods.
- **Economic realities test**: Economic dependence on employer, extent of investment, permanence of relationship, and skill required factor into classification.
- **Misclassification enforcement**: Idaho Department of Labor, state tax authority, and workers' compensation commission may audit employment classifications and assess penalties for willful misclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- **No Idaho statute addressing remote work**: Idaho applies standard employment law regardless of work location (on-site, remote, hybrid).
- If employee is remote but performs work for Idaho employer or performs work in Idaho, Idaho law applies to that employment relationship.
- Employers should comply with wage-and-hour laws (minimum wage, overtime, final pay) and other obligations based on employee's physical work location and employer's principal place of business.

**Multi-state employment (reciprocity)**:

- **No reciprocity doctrine**: Idaho does not recognize reciprocity; if employee works across state lines, Idaho applies its laws to work performed in the state.
- **Choice of law**: Employment contracts may specify which state's law governs, subject to conflict-of-laws principles; contract provisions that waive employee rights under the laws of the state where work is performed may be unenforceable.
- **Multi-state compliance**: Platform should flag when workforce changes affect employees in multiple states and ensure compliance with each state's requirements (wage, leave, discrimination, record retention).

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

When HCM Next processes a promotion or base-pay change for a worker in Idaho, the following obligations, timing constraints, and field restrictions apply:

**Required typed obligations and timing**:

1. **Final pay timing**: On termination during or after a pay/role change, platform must flag that final payment deadline is the earlier of (a) next regular payday or (b) 10 calendar days (excluding weekends/holidays); if employee requests early payment in writing, deadline becomes 48 hours (excluding weekends/holidays).

2. **Wage reduction notice**: Before implementing a pay decrease, platform must generate a written notice to employee; no statutory advance period required, but notice must precede the change in pay.

3. **Wage deduction authorization**: If employer policy permits deductions (garnishments, tax, benefit contributions), platform must confirm written authorization on file before processing a pay-change that includes a deduction.

4. **No mandated paid leave**: Idaho has no paid sick leave or state FMLA mandate; if employer offers voluntary paid leave, platform should maintain accrual records and ensure consistency with employer policy.

5. **Non-compete/non-solicitation**: Store date when covenant was executed, date employee received it, and documented reasonableness attributes (duration cap: 18 months presumed reasonable, geography, scope); flag any agreements exceeding 18 months without documented additional consideration.

6. **Discrimination flag**: Before posting salary change, ensure no correlation with employee's protected class (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age, disability); document business reason for pay change.

7. **Record retention**: Maintain employment records for minimum 3 years from date of separation; wage change records must include date, amount, and authorization.

**Field restrictions and validations**:

- **Wage reduction field**: Require written notice to employee before decrease takes effect; no automatic implementation without documented notice.
- **Non-compete duration cap**: Enforce 18-month maximum duration unless additional consideration checkbox is explicitly checked (with documentation).
- **Key employee classification**: When enforcing non-compete, require classification as "key employee" (top 5% earner, or documented management/sensitive-access role) with supporting evidence.
- **Minimum wage floor**: Validate that any base-pay change does not fall below Idaho's $7.25/hour minimum wage (or higher if federal minimum increases); flag if employee is under 20 years old and within first 90 days ($4.25 minimum applies).
- **Tipped employee verification**: If tipped employee, ensure direct wage component (at least $3.35/hour) is properly tracked and tips are credited toward minimum wage.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Confirm whether Idaho's "good faith and fair dealing" covenant in employment relationships restricts at-will termination rights or applies only to dispute resolution.
- Clarify whether accrued paid time off (if offered by employer policy) must be paid upon termination or is subject to employer's forfeiture policy.
- Verify whether employees have a common-law right to inspect/copy personnel files in Idaho.
- Confirm scope of implied employment contract doctrine: what course of dealing or handbook language creates an enforceable contract?
- Determine whether a non-compete executed at time of promotion (not at hire) requires additional consideration or is enforceable under same rules.
- Verify whether discrimination liability extends to pay decisions made during COVID-19 or other force-majeure circumstances (e.g., pandemic-driven pay freeze).
