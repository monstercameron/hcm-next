# Indiana Employment Law Research (2026-09-03)

**State:** Indiana | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **At-will employment default**: Indiana recognizes at-will employment by default; no written employment contract required at hire or change unless employer or employee specifics require one.
- **Minimum wage defers to federal FLSA**: Indiana's statutory minimum ($7.25/hour) is tied to federal FLSA ($7.25/hour); no state floor above federal.
- **Wage payment frequency semimonthly or biweekly**: IC 22-2-5-1 requires payment at least semimonthly or biweekly, with wages earned within 10 business days prior; no other state-specific pay frequency mandates.
- **Final pay timing on resignation**: IC 22-2-5-2 permits delayed payment to next regular payday if employee voluntarily leaves; terminations must follow contract terms or federal FLSA requirements.
- **No state paid-leave mandate**: No state-mandated paid sick leave, paid family leave, or paid time off for private-sector employees; federal FMLA applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles).
- **Payroll record retention 4 years**: IC 22-2-8 requires retention of payroll and wage records for 4 years after the calendar year in which remuneration was paid.
- **Right-to-work state**: IC 22-9-2-1 ensures no employee may be required to join or pay dues to a labor organization as a condition of employment.
- **Physician non-competes heavily restricted**: IC 25-22.5-5.5, amended by SEA 475 effective July 1, 2025, voids any non-compete between a physician and hospital, hospital parent company, or hospital system entered on or after July 1, 2025.
- **General non-competes enforceable under common law**: Non-competes for non-physician employees are enforceable if reasonable in scope, duration, and geographic area (verify current case law).
- **No ban-the-box, no paid-sick-leave, no salary-history ban, no mini-WARN**: Indiana lacks these protections; federal law applies where applicable.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Indiana recognizes at-will employment as the default employment relationship under common law and statutory principles. Absent a written employment contract specifying a term or conditions, either employer or employee may terminate the relationship at any time, for any reason (or no reason), **except** when termination violates public policy (e.g., illegal discrimination under Title VII, ADA, ADEA; retaliation for jury duty, voting, public-policy complaints, workers' compensation claims, or OSHA whistleblowing) or is otherwise unlawful.

Indiana has codified at-will principles in IC 22-2 and related statutes, but the state does not require employers to provide written notice to employees at hire or on role/pay changes beyond federal requirements (FLSA minimum-wage notice, overtime notice). There is no Indiana-specific wage-theft prevention notice statute; the federal FLSA Fair Pay Notice applies to covered employers.

**No written contract required**: Most Indiana employment relationships operate without written employment agreements. Where an employer and employee do not have a written contract, at-will status is presumed. If an employment relationship is at-will, no severance, notice period, or cause requirement applies (absent public-policy exceptions). Employers may document at-will acknowledgments, policy handbooks, or wage-change notifications for internal compliance and to establish clear expectations; no state-specific form or timing is mandated.

**Implied contract and good-faith-and-fair-dealing exception** (verify): Indiana courts have recognized narrow exceptions to at-will employment where an employee can demonstrate reliance on an implied promise of job security or where an employer's conduct violates an implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing. These are fact-specific; consult Indiana case law for current application (verify with legal counsel).

**Worker notification on termination**: When an employee is terminated or resigns, employers must file an Unemployment Insurance Separation Notice (Form UI-14) with the Indiana Department of Labor if applicable; this is administrative, not a worker notification requirement under state law. No statutory severance pay or advance notice period is required under Indiana law for at-will terminations (federal WARN Act applies only to covered mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers). Employers should follow any notice period specified in individual employment contracts, signed offer letters, or established company policies.

**Public policy exceptions**: Termination is unlawful if it is in retaliation for jury duty (IC 34-3-3-1), voting, filing a workers' compensation claim (IC 22-3-2-19), OSHA complaints, or other protected conduct. Employers must not discharge employees for these reasons.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: Indiana's statutory minimum wage under IC 22-2-2 is $7.25/hour (tied to federal FLSA). The state has not raised its own floor since the federal FLSA rate was established in 2009. **Local preemption**: IC 22-2-2-10.5 preempts local or municipal minimum wage ordinances; only the state rate applies, except where federal law requires a higher floor.

**Tipped Employees**: IC 22-2-2-3 permits a minimum wage of $2.13/hour if the employer documents that the employee receives tips sufficient to bring total compensation to at least $7.25/hour.

**Trainee and Youth Subminimum**: IC 22-2-2-4 allows employers to pay employees under age 20 a subminimum wage of $4.25/hour for the first 90 consecutive calendar days after initial employment.

**Overtime**: Indiana has no state-specific overtime statute; federal FLSA overtime rules apply (1.5× regular rate for hours over 40 per workweek). Executive, administrative, professional, and outside-sales exemptions follow federal FLSA definitions (29 U.S.C. § 213).

**Pay Frequency**: IC 22-2-5-1 requires employers to pay each employee "at least semimonthly or biweekly, if requested," in U.S. currency, by negotiable check, draft, money order, or electronic transfer. Payment must include all wages earned to a date not more than 10 business days prior to payday. ("Business day" = Monday–Friday, excluding legal holidays.)

**Final Paycheck on Termination or Resignation**: IC 22-2-5-2 states that if an employee **voluntarily** leaves employment, the employer is not required to pay accrued wages until "the next usual and regular day for payment of wages, as established by the employer." If the employee leaves without providing a forwarding address, the employer has no penalty obligation until 10 business days after the employee demands payment or provides an address. **For involuntary termination**, absent a contract specifying otherwise, final wages must follow the next regular payday per established employer practice; verify employer policy for immediate vs. next-payday payment. Federal FLSA requires timely payment of all earned wages regardless.

**Deductions and Pay Statements**: Federal FLSA governs permissible deductions; only deductions required by law (taxes, garnishments), by court order, or **authorized by employee in writing** are permitted. Unlawful deductions (for cash shortages, customer complaints, uniforms, tools) that reduce pay below the minimum wage are prohibited. No Indiana-specific pay-statement content requirement found in statute; federal FLSA record-keeping standards apply (IC 22-2-8 requires employers to keep wage records). Best practice: provide itemized pay stubs showing gross pay, deductions, net pay, and hours worked per pay period.

**Statute of Limitations**: IC 22-2-2-9 provides a **3-year statute of limitations** for wage claims under Indiana wage-and-hour law. Employees may recover unpaid wages, penalties, and attorney fees if an employer violates minimum wage, overtime, or payment-frequency requirements.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Indiana state law restricts inquiries about salary history or restricts the use of prior pay when setting new wages. Federal EEOC guidance (via press releases and technical assistance) discourages such inquiries to reduce pay equity gaps, particularly for women and minorities, but Indiana has not enacted a state law prohibiting such inquiries. Employers in Indiana may ask about prior compensation; best practice is to avoid the inquiry to eliminate historical pay-bias influence.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Indiana state law requires employers to disclose salary ranges in job postings, in recruitment materials, or on request from candidates or employees. Federal FLSA does not mandate pay transparency in recruitment. Several states (California, Colorado, New York, Washington) have enacted pay-range transparency laws; Indiana has not. This remains optional unless a specific employment contract, offer letter, or employer policy commits to such disclosure.

**Equal Pay Law**: Indiana defers to federal equal-pay standards (Equal Pay Act of 1963, Title VII of the Civil Rights Act, and other federal statutes). No Indiana-specific equal-pay statute codifying an "equal pay for equal work" requirement has been found separate from federal enforcement. However, Indiana Common Law and the Indiana Civil Rights Commission enforce federal equal-pay principles. Employers must avoid **sex-based wage discrimination** and discrimination based on protected classes (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, age) under Title VII, ADA, ADEA, and other federal statutes.

**Protected Classes under Indiana Law**: Title VII, ADA, Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), Genetic Information Nondiscrimination Act (GINA), and other federal laws apply in Indiana. The Indiana Civil Rights Commission (ICRC, also referred to as Indiana Department of Civil Rights under IC 22-9) enforces these federal standards and aligns with federal standards. No state-specific expansion of protected classes found beyond federal law (verify: Indiana case law on common-law discrimination tort). Employers should document pay decisions, job responsibilities, and performance ratings to demonstrate non-discriminatory basis for wage decisions.

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Indiana state law requires employers to report pay equity data or submit pay-equity audits to state agencies. Federal EEO-1 reporting requirements apply to employers with 100+ employees; these require employers to report workforce composition (race, ethnicity, gender, job category) annually to the EEOC. Indiana employers must comply with federal EEO-1 if they meet the 100+ employee threshold.

**Affirmative Action and Diversity**: No Indiana state law requires affirmative action or diversity programs in hiring or pay-setting. Federal contractor requirements (Executive Order 11246) apply to federal contractors meeting a certain threshold.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

No Indiana state law mandates paid sick leave or paid family leave for private-sector employees.

**Federal FMLA** (12 weeks unpaid per 12 months for qualifying reasons: serious health condition, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver, childbirth/adoption) applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles). Employers must maintain health-insurance benefits during FMLA leave.

**No state sick-leave requirement**: Indiana lacks a state-mandated paid sick leave law for private employers as of 2026-09-03. Some Indiana employers voluntarily provide paid sick leave; where an employer establishes a policy, Indiana courts generally hold the employer to the terms of that policy (verify employer policy and employment contract).

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional; no state mandate. Employers may set policies and communicate them clearly at hire or change.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: If an employee is promoted or receives a base-pay change, existing leave balances (if any—e.g., accrued vacation, PTO, or sick leave per employer policy) are typically preserved unless the employment contract or employer policy explicitly states otherwise. Indiana law does **not** specify carryover, use-it-or-lose-it, or payout rules; these are entirely governed by employer policy and employment contract terms. **Verify employer policy** for:

- Whether leave accrual continues through a promotion.
- Whether accrual rates change with a promotion or new job grade.
- Whether unused leave is forfeited or carried over at year-end.
- Whether accrued leave is paid out upon resignation or termination.
- **Upon Termination**: Verify whether accrued PTO, vacation, or sick leave is paid out. Indiana law does not mandate payout of accrued leave upon termination; the employer's written policy controls. If the employer has a policy promising payout, Indiana courts will enforce it. If no policy exists, payout is not required.
- **FMLA Interaction**: If the employer is FMLA-covered (50+ employees within 75 miles), coordinating leave is important. FMLA leave is unpaid (unless the employer allows employees to use accrued leave during FMLA leave). An employee on FMLA leave continues to accrue paid leave benefits only if the employer's policy requires it. Verify employer policy on whether FMLA leave counts toward accrual or is unpaid/outside accrual.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: IC 22-2-8 requires employers to retain payroll records, time records, and documents related to remuneration for **at least 4 years** after the calendar year in which remuneration was paid. Federal FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 211) requires 3 years; Indiana's 4-year rule is more stringent and applies.

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**: Indiana draws from common law and general privacy principles (codified in IC 22-9 and state public-records law principles). Employees have a general right to access their own personnel records upon request. Employers must allow timely review; specific timelines are not codified in statute, but "reasonably prompt" (typically 5–10 business days) is expected practice. Some information may be withheld if disclosure would violate privacy, breach third-party confidentiality (e.g., references, letters of recommendation), or constitute trade secrets. **Verify employer policy** on which records are accessible (wage history, performance evaluations, disciplinary records, medical information) and which are not.

**Record Format and Accessibility**: No state requirement mandates paper vs. electronic storage. Employers may maintain records digitally provided they remain accessible, authentic, and retrievable. Records must be retained in a format that allows for timely retrieval and audit. Electronic systems should have backup and disaster-recovery protections.

**Separation and Final-Pay Records**: Retain separation notices (Form UI-14), final-pay documentation, signed separation agreements, non-compete acknowledgments, and wage statements as part of the 4-year payroll retention cycle per IC 22-2-8. These records protect both the employer (evidence of wage compliance) and employee (documentation of final pay, accrued leave, etc.).

**IOSHA and Safety Records**: In addition to wage records, employers must retain Occupational Safety and Health Administration (OSHA) records (incident logs, injury reports, medical evaluations) for 5 years following the end of the year in which the incident occurred. Indiana's IOSHA program (administered by the Indiana Department of Labor) enforces these requirements.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Indiana has no comprehensive state data privacy statute equivalent to California CCPA or Colorado CPA. However, IC 24-4.9 (verify: often cited as the "Indiana Security Breach Notification Law") imposes data-breach notification requirements.

**Breach Notification**: When an employer or vendor experiences a breach of personal information (e.g., SSN, driver's license number, financial account information, medical records, biometric data), the employer must notify affected individuals **"in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay,"** per IC 24-4.9 (verify exact statute section and timing standard). No specific day count is mandated; "expedient" and "without unreasonable delay" are the legal standards. Law enforcement may request delayed notification if it compromises a criminal investigation.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Indiana-specific statute regulates employee biometric collection (e.g., fingerprints for background checks) or electronic monitoring (keystroke logging, location tracking). Federal Title I of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act (ECPA) and Indiana common law on privacy tort apply. verify: Employers should consult legal counsel before implementing biometric systems or surveillance.

**Employee Record Confidentiality**: Indiana does not impose state-specific employee data residency or cross-border processing restrictions; federal law (no overarching state privacy law equivalent to GDPR or state-level privacy laws like CCPA) and ordinary commercial data-protection practices apply. Employers should implement reasonable data-protection measures (encryption, access controls, secure storage) to safeguard employee personal information and comply with federal laws (GLBA for financial data, HIPAA for health data, FCRA for background-check data, etc.).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Indiana has **no ban-the-box law** as of 2026-09-03. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history on applications, during interviews, or via background check at any time in the hiring process. No state law restricts the timing of criminal record inquiries for private employers.

**Background Checks**: Permitted. Employers commonly use criminal-history checks via the Indiana State Police Records Section or national databases with individual consent. No state law restricts timing or scope of criminal record inquiries for most private employers (federal Fair Credit Reporting Act applies to scope and accuracy).

**Drug Testing**: No Indiana state law prohibits pre-employment drug testing. Federal rules (ADA, FLSA) apply to the extent that testing is not disability discrimination or otherwise unlawful.

**E-Verify**:

- **E-Verify requirement for public contractors and state employers**: IC 22-5-1.7 (Indiana's E-Verify Law) requires employers who contract with or are funded by the state of Indiana to verify work eligibility through E-Verify (U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services, USCIS). This applies to public agencies and private contractors performing work for the state. Verification must occur before hire or assignment of duties. Failure to comply may result in contract termination, fines, or loss of state funding.
- **For private employers without state contracts**: No Indiana state mandate found for private employers that do not have state contracts or public funding. However, federal law (Immigration Reform and Control Act, IRCA) requires all employers to verify work eligibility via Form I-9 (Employment Eligibility Verification) within 3 days of hire. E-Verify is optional for private employers but is a supplementary federal verification system; employers may voluntarily register in E-Verify as part of their I-9 compliance program (verify: consult immigration counsel for current practice).
- Compliance: Employers subject to IC 22-5-1.7 must register and maintain current E-Verify enrollment. The platform should flag any state-contract employees without verified work authorization and escalate to HR/Legal.

**Drug Testing**: No Indiana state law prohibits pre-employment or post-accident drug testing. Federal rules (ADA, FLSA) apply to the extent that testing is not disability discrimination. Some employers voluntarily conduct drug tests; the platform should ensure testing protocols comply with federal law and do not discriminate against qualified individuals with disabilities (verify: ADA Section 46—drug testing is not considered a medical examination under the ADA if the test does not also assess the existence, nature, severity, or permanence of a disability; however, some testing and inquiry may be deemed discriminatory if targeted at specific groups).

**Negligent Hiring**: Indiana recognizes negligent-hiring liability and employers have a duty to exercise reasonable care in hiring. Use of background checks and careful vetting of prior employment, criminal history, and references mitigates liability risk. Employers should document the hiring decision rationale and retain hiring records.

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**WARN Act**: The federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2102 et seq.) requires covered employers (100+ employees within 75 miles) to provide **60 days' written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers in a 30-day period. Covered employers must notify:

- Affected employees (or their representatives).
- The Indiana Department of Labor.
- The local workforce investment board and chief elected official(s) of the affected municipality.
  Failure to provide timely WARN notice may result in civil penalties and liability for employee back pay and benefits. Indiana has no additional state mini-WARN act; federal WARN applies nationwide, including Indiana employers. The platform should include a WARN-compliance workflow that flags potential mass separations and generates the required notice documentation.

**Severance Pay**: No Indiana state law mandates severance. Severance is a matter of contract or employer discretion.

**Notice to Employee on Termination**: No state-specific notice period required; at-will employment permits immediate termination. However, employers should follow any notice period specified in an employment contract or established policy.

**Noncompete Agreements**:

- **Physician non-competes**: IC 25-22.5-5.5, as amended by Senate Enrolled Act No. 475 (effective July 1, 2025), significantly restricts physician non-competes. The statute voids any non-compete agreement **entered on or after July 1, 2025** between a physician and a hospital, parent company of a hospital, affiliated manager of a hospital, or hospital system. For non-compete agreements entered between July 1, 2020 and June 30, 2025, the statute requires inclusion of specific provisions (notice to patients, contact information sharing, medical record access, buyout options, good-faith negotiation). A hospital may not enforce a non-compete if it terminates the physician without cause. Agreements entered before July 1, 2020 remain subject to prior law (verify).

- **Non-physician non-competes**: General non-competes for non-physician employees are **enforced under Indiana common law** and remain valid if (1) the covenant protects a legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential information, substantial relationships with specific prospective or existing customers, extraordinary or unique skills), (2) the geographic scope is reasonable, (3) the duration is reasonable (typically 1–3 years, verify case law), and (4) the covenant does not unduly burden the employee's ability to earn a livelihood. Indiana courts scrutinize these agreements closely; overly broad covenants may be unenforceable or modified by a court. **Verify**: Consult current Indiana case law (state appellate court rulings) before relying on enforceability; enforcement varies by fact pattern and industry.

**Non-Solicitation Agreements**: Similar enforceability analysis as non-competes under Indiana common law; reasonableness of scope, duration, and legitimate business interest apply.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Indiana's Unemployment Insurance Law (IC 34-9-2 et seq.) and wage law define "employee" and "employment" for UI and wage purposes. Neither contract labels ("independent contractor," "1099 contractor") nor the parties' stated belief control; liability is based on the **true underlying nature of the working relationship**. Indiana courts apply an economic-reality test, examining factors such as:

- Control by the employer over work methods, hours, and rate of pay.
- Provision of tools, equipment, or materials by the employer.
- Integration of the worker into the employer's business.
- Permanence or indefiniteness of the relationship (versus a one-time project).
- Whether the worker performs work that is part of the employer's regular business.
- Skill level required and who bears the risk of profit or loss.

Misclassification of employees as independent contractors triggers **wage-reporting liabilities** (failure to withhold payroll taxes, FICA, unemployment insurance), potential **wage-and-hour violations** (unpaid minimum wage, overtime), and **penalties** from the Indiana Department of Labor and IRS. Employers should verify classification with legal counsel if uncertain.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Indiana-specific rules govern remote workers or employees working partly in Indiana from another state. However:

- **Federal FLSA**: Applies to wages, overtime, and minimum-wage requirements regardless of where the employee works.
- **State income-tax withholding**: Indiana Department of Revenue requires withholding if the employee is an Indiana resident **or** is working in Indiana (even part-time or remotely). Consult the Department of Revenue for specific guidance on remote-worker residency tests.
- **Multi-state employers**: Must comply with all applicable state laws in states where work is performed. If an employee works in multiple states (e.g., remote in Indiana plus occasional travel to California), the employer must comply with wage, hour, leave, and discrimination laws of each relevant state.
- **Workers' compensation**: Covered under Indiana law if the employee is working in Indiana (verify with the Worker's Compensation Board).

**Reciprocity and Choice of Law**: Indiana recognizes Indiana law for employment disputes where work is performed in Indiana. Out-of-state employment contracts may attempt to invoke choice-of-law provisions (e.g., "This contract shall be governed by the laws of Delaware"), but if work is performed in Indiana, Indiana law applies to statutory protections (minimum wage, overtime, anti-discrimination, at-will status, public-policy exceptions). Indiana courts will not enforce out-of-state choice-of-law provisions that attempt to waive Indiana statutory protections; any agreement to the contrary is void as against public policy.

## 11. Implications for Human Capital Management Suite (P1A/P1B)

When the platform processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in Indiana:

**Promotion and Base-Pay Change Workflow**:

- **Record at-will status** in the worker profile; no employment-term field required for most Indiana workers absent a written contract.
- **Wage payment compliance**: Ensure new base pay is communicated and recorded in accordance with IC 22-2-5 semimonthly or biweekly payment schedule. Capture the new pay rate and effective date.
- **Leave balance preservation**: If the employee has accrued PTO or leave balances (per employer policy), preserve balances through promotion. Document any changes to leave accrual rates if the new role has different leave policies.
- **No paid-leave mandate interaction**: Promotion or base-pay change does not trigger state-mandated paid-leave obligations (none exist); apply employer policy consistently.
- **Capture equal-pay audit trail**: Document the employee's role, salary band, job title, and comparable roles for audit trails if wage-equity disputes arise (federal Title VII protection is automatic but requires documentation).
- **Non-compete flag**: If the employee is a physician or the employer uses non-compete agreements, attach and flag for legal review:
  - Physicians: Verify agreement pre-dates July 1, 2025 (post-2025-07-01 physician-hospital non-competes are void under IC 25-22.5-5.5).
  - Non-physicians: Ensure non-compete is reasonable in scope, duration, and geographic area per common law; flag for legal review if boundary cases.
- **E-Verify compliance** (if applicable): If the employer has a public contract, verify E-Verify status at hire or change; flag if work-authorization not yet verified.
- **No notice timing mandate**: No state law requires notice of pay change in advance; communicate change before or at effective date per employer practice.

**Termination Workflow** (At-Will):

- **Final-pay calculation**: For voluntary resignation, IC 22-2-5-2 permits payment to next regular payday. For involuntary termination, follow next regular payday or employer policy (verify employer policy for immediate vs. delayed final-pay practice). Ensure all earned wages, overtime, and accrued PTO (if required by policy) are included.
- **Retain termination records**: Preserve separation notice, final-pay documentation, and any signed agreements (non-compete, acknowledgment, etc.) for 4 years per IC 22-2-8.
- **WARN compliance**: If employer has 100+ employees within 75 miles and the separation is part of a mass layoff (50+ workers in 30 days), trigger 60-day federal WARN notice and coordinate with state UI separation notice.
- **No severance mandate**: No state law requires severance; apply employer policy if one exists.
- **Ensure no retaliation**: Confirm termination is not in retaliation for jury duty, voting, public policy (e.g., OSHA complaint), or protected activity under federal law.

**Restrictions and Prohibitions to Enforce**:

- **Do not terminate during protected conduct**: Flag any termination that coincides with jury duty, voting, protected workers' compensation claim, OSHA complaint, or other public-policy complaint. Consult legal counsel.
- **Do not retaliate under implied contract**: Verify that any at-will termination follows established practices; sudden deviations from policy or unexplained termination may trigger implied-contract or good-faith-and-fair-dealing challenges (verify with legal counsel).
- **Ensure non-compete compliance**: If employee has signed non-compete:
  - Physician: Verify it is pre-2025-07-01 or was compliant with 2020–2025 requirements and that hospital is not terminating without cause (which voids enforcement).
  - Non-physician: Verify it is reasonable in scope, duration, and geography; flag for legal review if challenged.
- **Document wage decisions**: For any pay change, promotion, or termination, retain written decision rationale and audit trail (job responsibilities, comparable-role analysis, performance notes, etc.) to defend against pay-equity or discrimination challenges.

**System Validation and Audit Workflows**:

- **Wage compliance audit**: Before processing any wage or classification change, verify that the new wage at least meets $7.25/hour (or $2.13 for tipped employees if applicable). Validate overtime classification (verify FLSA exemption status if non-exempt classification changes).
- **Final-pay calculation audit**: On termination, calculate final pay as all earned wages through termination date, accrued PTO (if required by policy), and vacation (if required by policy). Validate that final pay meets next regular payday or employer policy.
- **Leave balance audit**: Capture and preserve any accrued leave balances in the separation record. Note whether policy requires payout or forfeiture.
- **4-year retention check**: Tag all wage records, separations, and employment changes with a retention expiration date 4 years after the calendar year of the transaction (per IC 22-2-8).

**Open Questions for This Employer**:

- **Final-Pay Timing**: Does the employer have a written final-pay policy? For voluntary resignation, IC 22-2-5-2 permits next regular payday; for termination, verify whether the employer pays immediately, on next payday, or per contract. Ensure policy is consistently applied and documented.
- **Promotion and Pay-Change Policy**: Does the employer have a written policy for promotion approval, pay-change process, and documentation? Ensure the platform captures effective date, new rate, job title, and decision date.
- **WARN Compliance**: Is the employer subject to federal WARN Act (100+ employees within 75 miles)? If yes, the platform must trigger 60-day notice requirements for mass layoffs (50+ workers in 30-day period) and coordinate with state unemployment separation notice.
- **Non-Compete Agreements**: Does the employer use non-compete, non-solicitation, or confidentiality agreements? If so, review all agreements for enforceability under Indiana common law and IC 25-22.5-5.5. Separate physician agreements from non-physician agreements; flag physician agreements for compliance with 2025 amendments effective July 1, 2025.
- **E-Verify Status**: Is the employer a state contractor or does it have significant public contracts/funding? If so, verify E-Verify compliance per IC 22-5-1.7 and flag any workers without verified work authorization.
- **Data Breach Protocol**: Does the employer have an employee data-breach notification protocol? If personal information (SSN, driver's license, financial account, medical record, or biometric data) is exposed, the platform should trigger "expedient" notification per IC 24-4.9 and flag for legal review.
- **Federal FMLA Coverage**: Is the employer covered by federal FMLA (50+ employees within 75 miles)? If so, coordinate leave eligibility (qualifying reasons: serious health condition, family member care, military exigency, childbirth/adoption, military caregiver) with role/pay changes and ensure health insurance is maintained during FMLA leave. Note: Indiana has no state-level paid FMLA or state paid-leave mandate.
- **Paid Time Off Policy**: Does the employer have a written PTO or sick-leave policy? If yes, the platform should preserve balances through promotions, document accrual rates per role, and ensure transparent payout or forfeiture rules at termination. If absent, note that Indiana imposes no mandate.
- **Discrimination and Retaliation Safeguards**: Does the employer have internal safeguards against discrimination or retaliation? Ensure the platform logs all pay decisions and role changes with decision dates and rationale to support audit defense. Flag any terminations that coincide with protected conduct (jury duty, OSHA complaints, workers' comp claims, voting).
- **Compliance with Unemployment Insurance**: Ensure all terminations, resignations, and separations are reported to the Indiana Department of Labor via Form UI-14 for unemployment insurance eligibility determination. The platform should automate this workflow and verify receipt.
- **Remote or Multi-State Employees**: If the employer has employees working in other states or remotely, verify which state's law applies. Indiana law applies if the employee is working in Indiana or is an Indiana resident. Multi-state employers must comply with all applicable state laws in states where work is performed.
