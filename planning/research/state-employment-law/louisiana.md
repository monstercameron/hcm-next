# Louisiana Employment Law Research (2026-09-03)

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with statutory exceptions**: Louisiana recognizes at-will employment by default; however, public policy exceptions (illegal retaliation, protected activity, illegal discrimination) and some written contracts may limit this right.
- **No state minimum wage; federal FLSA applies**: Louisiana has no separate minimum wage statute; federal minimum ($7.25/hour) applies to all covered employees.
- **Final pay requirement: 15 days or next payday**: La. R.S. 23:631 requires final wages (including accrued leave, if applicable) to be paid within 15 days of termination or on the next regular payday, whichever is sooner; La. R.S. 23:632 imposes penalties for violations.
- **Pay frequency governed by contract; no state mandate**: Employers may set pay frequency (weekly, biweekly, etc.); no state law requires a specific schedule, but agreed frequency must be honored (La. R.S. 23:633).
- **Noncompete enforceability limited**: La. R.S. 23:921 caps noncompete agreements at 2 years in duration; agreements must be reasonable in scope and territory; 2024 amendments added physician-specific provisions (verify current amendments).
- **Louisiana Employment Discrimination Law (La. R.S. 23:301 et seq.)**: Prohibits discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age (40+), disability, and other protected bases; enforced by the Louisiana Workforce Commission (verify current coverage and exceptions).
- **Database Security Breach Notification (La. R.S. 51:3071 et seq.)**: Requires notification to affected individuals "without unreasonable delay" if personal information is breached; no specific day count specified.
- **Drug testing permitted with limitations**: La. R.S. 23:1017.1 et seq. permits drug testing, but employers must post required notices, follow procedures, and may not test or retest without cause (verify current case law on permit/license industries).
- **E-Verify mandate for public contracts**: La. R.S. 42:1451 and R.S. 38:2212.10 require E-Verify verification for public-contract employees and certain state-funded positions.
- **No mini-WARN; federal WARN Act applies**: Louisiana has no state-level mini-WARN act; federal WARN (60 days for mass layoffs of 50+ workers) applies to covered employers.
- **New Orleans local rules may apply**: New Orleans has enacted local fair-employment ordinances (verify current scope: hourly minimum-wage increase, paid leave policies, discrimination protections beyond state).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Louisiana is an at-will employment state under common law and La. R.S. 23:1004. Unless an employment contract specifies a term or conditions of employment, either party may terminate the relationship at any time, for any reason, or for no reason—**except** when termination violates public policy (e.g., retaliation for jury duty, voting, workers' compensation claims, protected-class discrimination under La. R.S. 23:301 et seq., or reporting illegal activity).

No state law requires written notice to an employee at hire regarding at-will status, wage theft prevention, or scheduled pay-rate changes. Employers should document any at-will agreements and changes for compliance; federal FLSA notice rules apply to wage and hour matters.

When an employee is separated, employers must file a Separation Notice (DE 9 form or equivalent) with the Louisiana Workforce Commission for unemployment insurance; this is administrative, not a worker notification. No statutory notice period to the worker is required beyond at-will principles or employment-contract terms.

**Employer obligations for promotions and pay changes**: Document the effective date, new wage/rate, and whether the change is at-will or subject to contract terms. No state-mandated notice period before the change takes effect; however, best practice is to notify the employee in writing and retain the notice in the personnel file.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: Louisiana has no statutory minimum wage; federal FLSA minimum ($7.25/hour) applies. Employers must pay whichever is higher: state minimum (none) or federal ($7.25/hour). For practical purposes, $7.25/hour is the floor.

**Overtime**: Federal FLSA rules apply. No state-specific deviation: overtime is 1.5× regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Executive, administrative, professional, and outside-sales exemptions follow FLSA definitions (29 U.S.C. § 213).

**Pay Frequency**: La. R.S. 23:633 does not mandate a specific pay frequency. Employers may set their own schedule (weekly, biweekly, monthly, etc.) provided they honor the agreed frequency and comply with La. R.S. 23:631 (final pay timing).

**Final Paycheck on Termination or Resignation**: **La. R.S. 23:631** requires all earned wages, including accrued paid time off (if the employment contract or employer policy provides for it), to be paid within **15 days of termination** or on the **next regular payday, whichever is sooner**. Failure to comply triggers penalties under La. R.S. 23:632 (penalties, including interest and potential damages). Employers must calculate and pay overtime through the final day worked.

**Deductions and Pay Statements**: La. R.S. 23:634 prohibits forfeiture of wages. La. R.S. 23:635 limits permissible deductions to those required by law, court order, or authorized in writing by the employee (e.g., union dues, tax withholding, court-ordered child support). No Louisiana-specific pay-statement content requirement found; federal FLSA record-keeping applies. Deductions for "breakage," uniform damage, or cash-register shortages are prohibited unless authorized and documented (verify case law on exceptions).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Louisiana state law restricts inquiries about salary history or past compensation. Federal EEOC guidance discourages such inquiries in some contexts, but Louisiana has not enacted a categorical ban.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Louisiana state law requires salary ranges in job postings. Federal rules (FLSA) do not mandate pay transparency in recruitment; this remains optional.

**Equal Pay Law**: Louisiana recognizes an equal pay principle but **no specific equal pay statute for private employers exists** (verify: Louisiana may recognize federal Equal Pay Act claims). Discrimination based on sex in compensation is covered under La. R.S. 23:301 et seq. (Employment Discrimination Law); La. R.S. 23:332 prohibits sex-based discrimination, and La. R.S. 23:341 addresses pregnancy discrimination. The Louisiana Workforce Commission (LWC) enforces these statutes. Exceptions may exist for seniority, merit systems, or factors other than sex.

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Louisiana state law requires employers to report pay equity data. Federal EEO-1 reporting applies to employers with 100+ employees.

**Protected Classes**: La. R.S. 23:301 et seq. prohibits discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age (40+), and disability. La. R.S. 23:332 specifically addresses sex-based discrimination; La. R.S. 23:341 addresses pregnancy, childbirth, and related conditions. Title VII, ADA, ADEA, and other federal laws also apply. Louisiana recognizes these statutes alongside federal law; enforcement is through the Louisiana Workforce Commission and the federal EEOC.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

**No state-mandated paid leave**: Louisiana has **no state law requiring paid sick leave, paid family leave, or paid time off** for private-sector employees. Federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid per 12 months for qualifying reasons: serious health condition, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver, childbirth/adoption) applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles). Employers must maintain health-insurance benefits during FMLA leave.

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional; no state mandate. Employers may set policies and communicate them clearly at hire. If an employee is promoted or has a base-pay change, existing leave balances (if any) are typically preserved; no state rule specifies carryover or payout rules. Verify employer policy and any written contract.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: If an employee accrues paid time off and is terminated, La. R.S. 23:631 (final pay) may require payout, depending on whether the employment contract or employer policy grants accrued leave as earned wages. Verify the employer's leave policy and contract language.

**New Orleans local paid-leave requirements**: New Orleans has enacted a paid-leave ordinance (verify: as of 2026, minimum 5 days paid time off per year for businesses with 10+ employees in the city); this applies to employees working in New Orleans, regardless of employer location (verify current scope and phase-in).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: **No specific state retention period found**; federal FLSA requires 3 years (29 U.S.C. § 211). Best practice: retain payroll records and supporting documents for at least 3 years. Louisiana civil code may impose additional obligations for certain record types (verify statutory or contractual retention requirements).

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**: Louisiana does not have a specific "personnel file" statute granting employees an automatic right to access their records. However, La. R.S. 1:55 (privacy rights) and common-law privacy principles may apply. Best practice: allow employees to review their own personnel files upon reasonable request; withhold information that is confidential, privileged, or relates to third-party evaluations (verify current case law).

**Record Format**: No state requirement for paper vs. electronic. Employers may maintain records digitally provided they remain accessible, authentic, and meet federal FLSA requirements.

**Separation Records**: Employers should retain separation notices, final pay records, and Unemployment Insurance Separation Notices as part of payroll and personnel retention cycles.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Louisiana has **no comprehensive state data privacy statute** equivalent to CCPA or GDPR. However, **La. R.S. 51:3071 et seq.** imposes **Data Security Breach Notification** requirements.

**Breach Notification**: When an employer (or vendor) that collects, transmits, or maintains unencrypted digital records of personal information (e.g., driver's license number, Social Security number, credit card number, financial account information, health records) experiences a breach, the employer must notify affected individuals **"without unreasonable delay"** unless law enforcement determines notification would compromise a criminal investigation. No specific day count is specified; "expedient" or "without unreasonable delay" is the standard. Notification should include the nature of the breach, types of personal information involved, and steps the individual should take.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Louisiana-specific statute regulates employee biometric collection (e.g., fingerprints for background checks) or electronic monitoring (e.g., keystroke logging, location tracking). Federal law (Title I of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act, ECPA) and Louisiana common law on privacy tort and invasive-torts apply (verify case law on reasonable expectation of privacy).

**Employee Record Confidentiality**: Louisiana does not impose state-specific employee data residency or cross-border processing restrictions; federal law and ordinary commercial data-protection practices apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Louisiana has **no ban-the-box law**. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history on applications, during interviews, or via background check at any time in the hiring process.

**Background Checks**: Permitted. Employers commonly use criminal-history checks through the Louisiana State Police or the FBI (NCIC). No state law restricts timing or scope of criminal record inquiries for most private employers. verify: Certain industries (e.g., healthcare, child care) may have additional restrictions or mandates.

**Drug Testing**: **La. R.S. 23:1017.1 et seq.** permits pre-employment and workplace drug testing. Employers using drug testing must (1) post required notices in the workplace, (2) follow procedures (e.g., chain of custody, confirmation testing), and (3) not discriminate based on protected status. Testing after a workplace injury or for reasonable suspicion is permitted. Testing negative does not preclude discipline for other reasons. **Certain professional licenses** (e.g., drivers, healthcare workers) may be required or restricted by industry law; verify applicability.

**E-Verify**: **La. R.S. 42:1451 and R.S. 38:2212.10** require **E-Verify verification for public-contract employees** and employees funded by state money. Private employers are not statutorily required to use E-Verify, but many do for compliance. Employers who use E-Verify must register with USCIS and must follow procedures (verify current enrollment and compliance).

**Negligent Hiring**: Louisiana recognizes negligent-hiring liability; use of background checks and careful vetting mitigates risk.

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**WARN Act**: The federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires covered employers (100+ employees) to provide **60 days' written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers in a 30-day period. Applies nationwide, including Louisiana. Failure to notify carries penalties. Louisiana has **no state-specific mini-WARN act**.

**Severance Pay**: No state law mandates severance. Severance is a matter of contract or employer discretion.

**Notice to Employee on Termination**: No state-specific notice period required; at-will employment permits immediate termination. However, employers should follow any notice period specified in an employment contract or internal policy.

**Noncompete and Nonsolicitation Agreements**: **La. R.S. 23:921** permits noncompete agreements but imposes **significant enforceability limits**. Key restrictions:

- **Duration cap: 2 years maximum** (any longer term is void)
- **Reasonableness standard**: Agreements must be reasonable in scope and geographic territory
- **Legitimate business interest**: Employer must demonstrate protectable interest (trade secrets, customer relationships, etc.)
- **Physician amendments (2024)**: Recent amendments added specific provisions for physician noncompetes; verify current physician-specific rules regarding parish-level restrictions and enforceability

**Nonsolicitation agreements** are also subject to reasonableness standards. Overly broad or indefinite agreements are unenforceable. **verify**: Current Louisiana case law on enforceability and what constitutes "reasonable" scope and duration.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Louisiana's Employment Security Law (La. R.S. 23:1471 et seq.) defines "employee" and "employment" for unemployment-insurance purposes. The **ABC test** is applied: (A) the worker is free from control; (B) the work is outside the employer's usual business; and (C) the worker is engaged in an independently established trade or business. A single "failed" prong may render the worker an employee. Misclassification of employees as independent contractors triggers wage-reporting and tax liabilities and exposes employers to audit.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Louisiana-specific rules govern remote workers or multi-state employees. Federal FLSA applies to wages; Louisiana income-tax withholding is required if the employee is working in Louisiana (verify with Louisiana Department of Revenue). Multi-state employers must comply with all applicable state laws in states where work is performed.

**Reciprocity**: Louisiana recognizes only Louisiana law for employment disputes involving Louisiana-based work. Out-of-state employment contracts may invoke choice-of-law provisions, but if work is performed in Louisiana, Louisiana law applies to many statutory protections.

## 11. Implications for HCM Next (P1A/P1B)

When the platform processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in Louisiana:

**For Promotions and Base-Pay Changes:**

- **Document the effective date and new compensation** in the employee record
- **Record at-will status** in the worker profile (no contract-term field required for most Louisiana workers unless an exception applies)
- **Ensure final-pay calculation complies with La. R.S. 23:631**: Triggered when a termination occurs, not at promotion/pay change, but prepare the system to calculate within 15 days or the next regular payday
- **Track accrued paid time off (if applicable)** and whether it is owed at termination under the employer's policy
- **Document pay frequency** and ensure the promotion/pay-change effective date aligns with the next pay cycle (no specific state requirement, but best practice)
- **Retain all wage and promotion records** for at least 3 years (federal FLSA); recommend 4 years for audit trail
- **Flag noncompete enforceability** if the employer uses noncompete agreements: verify 2-year cap, reasonableness, and physician-specific amendments if applicable
- **Track New Orleans applicability**: If the employee works in New Orleans, confirm paid-leave compliance (minimum 5 days/year for 10+ employee businesses; verify current scope and effective date)

**For Terminations:**

- **Calculate final pay** (all earned wages including overtime through last day, plus accrued leave if applicable) within 15 days of termination or on the next regular payday (La. R.S. 23:631)
- **Check for La. R.S. 23:632 violation risks** and flag if the employer may be in breach
- **File Unemployment Insurance Separation Notice** with LWC (administrative requirement)
- **Implement WARN Act workflows** if employer has 100+ employees and termination affects 50+ workers in 30 days
- **No paid-leave mandate** for private-sector employees, but preserve any accrued leave owed under the employment contract or policy

**For Discrimination and Protected Activity:**

- **Implement workflows** that flag terminations or pay reductions based on protected class (La. R.S. 23:301 et seq.: race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age 40+, disability, pregnancy)
- **Track retaliation risks** (e.g., termination within 90 days of jury duty, workers' compensation claim, or protected complaint)

**Data Security and Breach Notification:**

- **If personal information is exposed** (SSN, driver's license, financial account), trigger notification workflow within "without unreasonable delay" standard (recommend 2–3 days for safety)

**Drug Testing and E-Verify:**

- **If the employer requires drug testing**, ensure procedures are documented and notices are posted (La. R.S. 23:1017.1 et seq.)
- **If the employee works on public contracts or is funded by state money**, ensure E-Verify verification is documented (La. R.S. 42:1451)

**Open Questions:**

- Does the employer have a written policy on final-pay timing (15 days, next payday, or longer)?
- Does the employer have a noncompete/nonsolicitation policy? If so, are agreements reviewed for La. R.S. 23:921 compliance (2-year cap, reasonableness)?
- Does any employee have a written employment contract specifying a term or termination notice period (limiting at-will status)?
- Does the employer require drug testing? If so, are procedures documented and notices posted?
- Does the employer use E-Verify? If so, is registration current and compliance verified?
- Does any employee work in New Orleans? If so, is the employer compliant with New Orleans paid-leave ordinance?
- Does the employer have a paid-leave or accrued-time policy? If so, are final-pay calculations explicit (payout vs. forfeiture)?
