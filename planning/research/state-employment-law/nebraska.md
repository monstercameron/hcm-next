# Nebraska Employment Law Research

**State:** Nebraska | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with discrimination exceptions**: Nebraska recognizes at-will employment by default; employers may terminate at any time except on grounds of illegal discrimination (race, color, religion, sex, disability, marital status, national origin, age, or union activity).
- **Minimum wage: $15/hour (2026), with annual increases**: Effective January 1, 2026, minimum wage is $15/hour per Initiative 433 (LB 258); increases 1.75% annually starting January 1, 2027, calculated and published by October 15 each year. Tipped workers minimum $2.13/hour plus tips equaling minimum wage.
- **Paid sick leave mandate (Initiative 436) effective October 1, 2025**: Employers with 11+ employees must provide paid sick leave: accrual of 1 hour per 30 hours worked (after 80 hours employment); small businesses (11–19 employees) permit up to 40 hours/year usage, larger employers 56 hours/year. Accrued time carries over annually unless employers pay out unused time and provide fresh time.
- **Final pay timing: next payday or within 2 weeks of termination, whichever is sooner**: Unpaid wages (including accrued vacation/PTO) become due immediately on separation under Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1230. Accrued PTO is considered wages.
- **Overtime governed by federal FLSA**: Nebraska imposes no state-specific overtime rule; federal 1.5× over 40 hours applies.
- **Equal Pay Act § 48-1219**: Prohibits sex-based wage discrimination for equal work requiring equal skill, effort, responsibility, and similar working conditions. Prima facie burden-shifting framework applies.
- **Data breach notification required without unreasonable delay**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-803 requires notification of affected parties "as soon as possible and without unreasonable delay" if personal information is breached; law enforcement delay permitted only if investigation would be impeded.
- **Nebraska WARN Act (LB 921) requires 90-day notice for mass layoffs of 25+ employees**: State mini-WARN applies to separations of 25 or more employees on the same day, for the same reason, expected to last 7+ days; federal WARN Act (60 days for 50+ in 30-day period) applies to employers with 100+ employees.
- **E-Verify mandate for public contractors**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 4-114 requires public contractors to verify work eligibility via E-Verify; private employers are not federally mandated.
- **Ban-the-box applies to public employers only**: Public employers cannot conduct criminal-history checks until after determining applicant meets minimum employment qualifications. Private employers may inquire at any time.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Nebraska is an at-will employment state. Unless a written employment contract specifies a term, either party may terminate the relationship at any time, for any reason, or for no reason, **except** termination cannot be based on illegal discrimination (race, color, religion, sex, disability, marital status, national origin, age—if employer has 25+ employees—or union activity or retaliation for wage disclosure).

**No state law requires written notice to an employee at hire** regarding at-will status, wage-theft prevention, or compensation changes. Employers should document any at-will agreements and wage-rate changes for internal compliance; no state-specific form or timing is mandated.

**Notice of payday changes**: If an employer alters regular paydays, 30 days' written notice must be given to each employee before the change takes effect (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1230).

**Accrued paid time is wages**: Any accrued vacation, paid time off, or earned paid sick leave is considered wages and must be paid in full on termination or resignation. The right to payment depends on whether the employee has an absolute contractual right to take such time.

**No statutory severance requirement**: Severance is discretionary and a matter of employment contract or employer policy.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: Effective January 1, 2026, Nebraska's minimum wage is **$15.00 per hour** (Initiative 433, codified in Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1203). Starting January 1, 2027, the minimum wage increases annually by 1.75%, calculated and published by the Nebraska Department of Labor no later than October 15 of each year.

**Special wage rates**:

- **Tipped employees**: Minimum $2.13/hour plus gratuities; sum of wages and tips must equal applicable minimum wage.
- **Youth minimum wage** (ages 14–15): $13.50/hour.
- **Training wage**: $13.50/hour through December 31, 2026.

**Overtime**: Federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) governs. Nebraska imposes no state-specific deviation: overtime is 1.5× regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek. Executive, administrative, professional, and outside-sales exemptions follow FLSA definitions.

**Pay Frequency**: No state law mandates a specific pay frequency (weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, monthly). Employers may set their own schedule provided wages are paid in full and on regular designated days. **Thirty days' written notice is required before changing regular paydays** (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1230).

**Final Paycheck on Termination**: When an employer (other than a political subdivision) separates an employee from payroll, unpaid wages (including accrued vacation/PTO if legally owed) become due on **the next regular payday or within two weeks of the date of termination, whichever is sooner** (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1230).

**Wage Statements**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1230 requires employers to deliver or make available to each employee on each regular payday a wage statement showing, at minimum: (1) identity of the employer, (2) hours for which the employee was paid, (3) wages earned by the employee, and (4) deductions made. Exempt employees are exempt from hour-documentation requirements unless the employer has a policy of paying them overtime, bonuses, or hour-based compensation.

**Deductions and Wage Reductions**: Federal FLSA governs permissible deductions (only those required by law, court order, or authorized by employee in writing are allowed). No state-specific prohibitions on voluntary deductions (beyond federal law) found. Written authorization from the employee is required before any voluntary deduction is withheld; notice of intent to reduce pay rates must be communicated to the employee before such rates take effect.

**Pay-Frequency Flexibility and Notice Timing**: Employers have no state-mandated minimum pay frequency (weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly are all permitted) provided wages are paid in full on regular designated paydays. However, **Thirty days' written notice is mandatory before any change to regular paydays takes effect** (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1230). This notice must be delivered to the employee in advance of the effective date.

**Wage Payment Penalties**: Employers who violate wage-payment requirements (Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 48-1231 to 48-1234) are liable for unpaid wages plus a civil penalty. The exact penalty structure appears in §§ 48-1231 (attorney's fees and court costs for wage claims) and 48-1232 (additional wage damages for willful violations) (verify: specific penalty percentages and recovery mechanisms). Prevailing employees in wage disputes may recover attorney's fees and court costs.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Nebraska state law restricts inquiries about salary history. Employers may ask prospective employees about prior compensation.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Nebraska state law requires salary ranges in job postings or on request. Federal rules do not mandate pay transparency in recruitment; this remains optional.

**Equal Pay Law**: Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 48-1219 to 48-1227 (Equal Pay Act) prohibit wage discrimination based on sex. A plaintiff must establish a prima facie case by showing (1) he/she was paid less than a person of the opposite sex employed in the same establishment, (2) for equal work on jobs requiring equal skill, effort, and responsibility, (3) performed under similar working conditions. If a prima facie case is proven, the burden shifts to the employer to show an affirmative defense (seniority system, merit system, production system, or factor other than sex). Employers are protected against retaliation for employees invoking this statute (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1221).

**Wage Transparency and Disclosure**: Employers cannot apply different standards of compensation based on race, color, religion, sex, disability, marital status, or national origin unless justified by a bona fide seniority, merit, or production system. Additionally, an employer must not discriminate against an employee who has inquired about, discussed, or disclosed wages, benefits, or other compensation (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1114, part of Fair Employment Practice Act).

**Protected Classes**: Title VII (race, color, religion, sex, national origin), ADA (disability), ADEA (age if employer has 25+ employees), and Nebraska Fair Employment Practice Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 48-1101 to 48-1127) apply. Protected characteristics include sex, disability, physical or mental limitations, pregnancy, childbirth, related medical conditions, marital status, and protective hairstyles (braids, locks, twists). Covered employers must make reasonable accommodations for qualified individuals' known limitations unless undue hardship would result.

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Nebraska state law requires employers to report pay equity data to state agencies. Federal EEO-1 reporting applies to employers with 100+ employees.

**Age Discrimination**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1001 (Part 10 of the Fair Employment Practice Act) prohibits discrimination based on age in hiring, promotion, compensation, termination, and other terms and conditions of employment. Applies to employers with **20 or more employees**. The Act protects workers of all ages but is often asserted by employees age 40 and older. No explicit age range is specified in state law; federal ADEA (age 40+, employers 20+ employees) provides parallel protection and operates concurrently.

**Retaliation Protection**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1114 (part of Fair Employment Practice Act) explicitly prohibits employers from retaliating against employees who inquire about, discuss, or disclose wages, benefits, or other terms and conditions of employment. Retaliation includes discharge, discipline, harassment, demotion, or any adverse action because an employee engaged in protected wage-disclosure activity. Employees have the right to discuss compensation without employer interference or threat.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

**Paid Sick Leave (Nebraska Healthy Families and Workplaces Act, Initiative 436, effective October 1, 2025)**

Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 48-3801 to 48-3811 establish paid sick leave requirements. Applies to employers with **11 or more employees**; LB 415 (2025) exempts employers with 10 or fewer employees, temporary workers, and employees under age 16.

- **Accrual**: Employees begin accruing paid sick time after 80 hours of employment at a rate of **1 hour per 30 hours worked**. Accrual starts on hire or on the first day of employment, whichever is later.
- **Annual usage caps and employer size tiers**:
  - Small businesses (11–19 employees): not required to permit use of more than 40 hours/year.
  - Larger employers (20+): not required to permit use of more than 56 hours/year.
  - Employers may provide higher limits; statutory minimums floor the requirement.
- **Carryover and payout**: Accrued paid sick time **shall carry over to the following year** unless the employer implements a "use-it-or-lose-it" policy combined with payout of unused time at year-end and provision of fresh time at year start. If a payout-and-refresh cycle is used, employees must receive notice at the start of each year.
- **Transfer and re-hire**: When an employee transfers within the same employer, all accrued unused paid sick time transfers. If an employee is rehired within 12 months after separation, previously accrued unused paid sick time "shall be reinstated" (verify: whether reinstatement applies to all former employees or only those meeting specific criteria).
- **Compensation rate**: Paid sick time must be compensated at the same hourly rate and with the same benefits (including health insurance) as the employee typically earns, with a minimum of Nebraska minimum wage. Commission-based or piece-rate employees receive compensation calculated as an average weekly rate converted to an hourly amount based on a standard 40-hour workweek.
- **Notice requirement**: Employers must provide written notice to employees—at hire or by September 15, 2025, whichever is later—specifying: (1) that employees are entitled to paid sick time beginning October 1, 2025; (2) the amount of paid sick time employees accrue and may use; (3) the terms and conditions for using paid sick time; (4) that retaliation, discrimination, or discharge for requesting or using paid sick time is prohibited.
- **Prohibited retaliation**: Employers may not interfere with, restrain, or deny use of paid sick time, nor retaliate against employees who exercise their right to use paid sick time or who file a complaint or participate in a proceeding related to paid sick leave rights.

**Federal FMLA**: The Family and Medical Leave Act (29 U.S.C. §§ 2601–2654) provides 12 weeks unpaid leave per 12 months for qualifying reasons (serious health condition, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver leave, childbirth/adoption). Applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75 miles. Employers must maintain health insurance during leave.

**No state-mandated paid family or medical leave for private employees**: Beyond FMLA and paid sick leave, Nebraska imposes no requirement for paid leave. Holidays and PTO are entirely optional.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: If an employee is promoted or receives a base-pay change, existing accrued leave balances (vacation, PTO, paid sick leave, if any) are typically preserved unless forfeited by employment contract. The Healthy Families and Workplaces Act does not require forfeiture on role/pay changes; paid sick time accrual continues.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: Employers must retain payroll records for **no fewer than 3 years** for tax-reporting purposes (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 77-2753). Federal FLSA requires 3 years; this aligns.

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**:

- **Public school employees** (teachers, administrators, full-time staff) have statutory access to their personnel files upon request and the right to attach written responses to file items, except letters of recommendation (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 79-8,109).
- **Private-sector employees**: No statutory personnel-file access law. However, employers must maintain work records open to inspection by the labor commissioner (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-612).

**Employer Records (Labor Commissioner Access)**: Each employer must keep true and accurate work records as required by the Commissioner of Labor. Such records are open to inspection and copying by the Commissioner or authorized representatives at any reasonable time.

**Record Format**: No state requirement for paper vs. electronic. Employers may maintain records digitally provided they remain accessible and authentic.

**Separation Records**: Employers should retain separation notices, final-pay records, and related documentation as part of the payroll retention cycle.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Nebraska Data Privacy Act (Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 87-1101 to 87-1130, effective January 1, 2025, Laws 2024, LB 1074) is a limited consumer privacy law modeled on CCPA/CPRA but does **not comprehensively regulate employee data** in the way that California's CCPA does. The Act applies to "controllers" processing personal information of Nebraska residents for commercial purposes. Employee data collected and processed for employment purposes (payroll, tax compliance, benefits administration, background checks) is generally outside the scope of the Data Privacy Act (verify: specific exemptions for employment data or B2B processing). Employers should not assume the Act imposes CCPA-like obligations for employee personal information; however, data breaches affecting employee personal information still trigger Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-803 breach-notification requirements.

**Workplace Privacy Act**: Neb. Rev. Stat. §§ 48-3501 to 48-3511 restrict employer access to employee Internet accounts, personal social media accounts, and personal devices without employee consent. Employers may not demand access to personal accounts or require employees to disclose credentials.

**Data Breach Notification** (Financial Data Protection and Consumer Notification of Data Security Breach Act, Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-803):

- When an individual or commercial entity conducts business in Nebraska and owns or licenses computerized data including personal information about a Nebraska resident, a breach of the security of the system triggers notification obligations.
- **Timing**: Notice must be made "as soon as possible and without unreasonable delay" consistent with legitimate law-enforcement needs and measures necessary to determine breach scope and restore data-system integrity.
- **Exceptions**: If law enforcement determines that notification would compromise a criminal investigation, notice may be delayed; it must be made in good faith and without unreasonable delay once the investigation no longer requires secrecy.
- **Definition of breach**: Unauthorized acquisition of unencrypted computerized data compromising security, confidentiality, or integrity of personal information.
- **Third-party obligations**: Entities maintaining personal information they do not own/license must notify the owner/licensee of breaches affecting Nebraska residents.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Nebraska-specific statute regulates biometric collection (e.g., fingerprints for background checks) or electronic monitoring (keystroke logging, location tracking). Federal law (Title I of Electronic Communications Privacy Act) and Nebraska common law on privacy tort apply.

**Data Residency and Cross-Border Processing**: No state-specific constraint; federal law applies.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Applies to **public employers only**. Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-202 and related statutes require public employers to determine whether an applicant meets the minimum employment qualifications **before** conducting criminal-history record checks. Private employers are not subject to this restriction and may inquire about criminal history at any stage of hiring.

**Background Checks**: Permitted. Employers commonly obtain criminal-history checks via the Nebraska Crime Information Center (NCIC) with fingerprints or name-based consent.

**Drug and Alcohol Testing**: Nebraska Revised Statutes §§ 48-1901 to 48-1910 address drug and alcohol testing in the workplace. Section 48-1901 establishes legislative intent "to help in the treatment and elimination of drug and alcohol use and abuse in the workplace while protecting the employee's rights." Critically, **the statute does not require employers to conduct drug or alcohol testing**, nor does it prescribe specific procedures, timing, or notice requirements for testing. Employers retain discretion to implement or abstain from testing policies. No state law prohibits pre-employment or workplace drug testing. Employers may establish drug-testing policies and drug-free workplace policies. Testing must comply with federal law (e.g., DOT-regulated industries, federal contractors) but faces no additional Nebraska-specific procedural mandate (verify: whether §§ 48-1902 to 48-1910 contain employee-protection provisions for tested employees).

**E-Verify**: **Required for public contractors only** (Neb. Rev. Stat. § 4-114). Every contract between a public employer and public contractor must contain a provision requiring the contractor to use E-Verify (or equivalent federal work-authorization verification system) for employees physically performing services in Nebraska. This requirement does not apply to contracts awarded by public employers prior to October 1, 2009.

**Private employers**: Not federally mandated to use E-Verify by Nebraska state law (verify: federal E-Verify mandates for certain federal contractors or specific industries may apply).

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**Federal WARN Act**: The Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires covered employers (100+ employees) to provide **60 days' written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers within a 30-day period. Applies nationwide, including Nebraska.

**Nebraska Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (LB 921)**:

- **Scope**: More expansive than federal WARN. Applies to **mass layoff events** affecting 25 or more full-time employees.
- **Timing**: Employers planning a business closing or mass layoff must serve **written notice to affected employees (or their representatives) and to the Nebraska Department of Labor** and may not order such action until the end of a **90-day period** beginning after notice is served.
- **Advantage**: Applies regardless of whether the federal WARN Act threshold (100+ employees, 50+ workers affected) is met, providing broader protection.

**Severance Pay**: No state law mandates severance. Severance is a matter of contract or employer discretion.

**Notice to Employee on Termination**: No state-specific notice period required; at-will employment permits immediate termination. However, employers should follow any notice period specified in an employment contract or policy.

**Final Paycheck Timing** (covered in § 3): Next regular payday or within 2 weeks of termination, whichever is sooner.

**Noncompete and Nonsolicitation Agreements**:

- **Statutory framework**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 87-404 applies **only to franchise-relationship noncompetes** (Franchise Practices Act context) and is not the governing law for general employment noncompetes.
- **Enforceability under common law**: Nebraska enforces noncompete agreements under common-law principles, applying a **strict reasonableness test without blue-pencilling authority** (the court cannot modify an overly broad covenant to make it enforceable; instead, the covenant is simply unenforceable). Covenants are enforceable if they are reasonable in scope, duration, and geography, and reasonably protect a legitimate business interest (e.g., trade secrets, confidential information, substantial relationships with prospective or existing customers).
- **Customer nonsolicits**: Nebraska law limits nonsolicitation covenants to customers **with whom the employee actually dealt** during employment (verify: _H&R Block v. Cardoso_ and _Gaver v. Schneider's O.K. Tire_, Nebraska Supreme Court cases establishing that customer restrictions cannot extend to all customers of the business, only those with whom the employee had direct contact). Courts reject overbroad customer lists as unreasonable restraints.
- **Reformation not available**: Unlike some jurisdictions, Nebraska does not permit courts to "blue-pencil" or reform overly broad noncompetes to make them enforceable. An agreement deemed unreasonable in its full scope is unenforceable in its entirety (verify: this approach differs from states allowing judicial modification).
- **Burden and proof**: Employers asserting noncompete enforceability bear the burden of proving the agreement is reasonable in scope, duration, and geography, and that it protects a legitimate business interest. Employee receives deference in disputes.

### Local ordinances (Omaha and Lincoln)

**Omaha**: No city-level employment law restrictions beyond state and federal law found. Omaha ordinances do not establish their own minimum wage, paid leave, pay-transparency, or ban-the-box requirements for private employers.

**Lincoln**: No city-level employment law restrictions beyond state and federal law found. Lincoln ordinances do not establish their own minimum wage, paid leave, pay-transparency, or ban-the-box requirements for private employers.

Both cities defer to state law for employment regulation; employers should monitor state statutory changes rather than city ordinances for employment-law compliance.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**:

- **Tests**: Nebraska applies the traditional common-law test for contractor status (similar to IRS test) under the Contractor Registration Act (CRA) and Employee Classification Act (ECA). For unemployment insurance purposes, a stricter "ABC" test may apply.
- **Presumptions for construction and delivery**:
  - An individual performing construction labor services is **presumed an employee** (not contractor) for ECA purposes unless the individual meets criteria in Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-604(5), was registered as a contractor prior to commencing work, and has been assigned a combined tax rate or is exempt from unemployment insurance.
  - Similarly, individuals performing delivery services are **presumed employees** unless meeting § 48-604(5) criteria or exempt from UI coverage.
- **Misclassification liability**: Classifying an employee as a contractor may trigger wage-reporting and tax liabilities and expose employers to audit.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employment**:

- **Services in multiple states**: When services are customarily performed in more than one state, those services can be deemed performed in any one state in which the worker performs services, has residence, or the employer maintains a place of business, if the employer makes an election with employee acquiescence, approved by the state's unemployment compensation agency.
- **No comprehensive remote-work statute**: Nebraska does not impose specific remote-work employment law beyond unemployment insurance coordination rules.
- **Choice of law**: If work is performed in Nebraska, Nebraska law applies to many statutory protections (minimum wage, paid sick leave, final pay timing, etc.).

### Statutory leave and jury/voting/military duty (supplement to section 5)

**Jury Duty**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 25-1640 provides that an employer shall not penalize, threaten, or coerce an employee for service on jury duty. Employees may not be discharged, threatened, or otherwise disciplined for attending court or jury service. No requirement for paid jury duty leave; state law prohibits only retaliation.

**Voting Leave**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 32-922 requires employers to allow employees **two hours** to vote during an election. Employees may take time off at the beginning or end of the workday to vote in any statewide or federal election; employers must permit this time without penalty or loss of wages.

**Military Service**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 55-160 (Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act § 4301 et seq. USERRA applies uniformly) requires employers to grant unpaid leave for military service and reemploy veterans returning from active duty. Employees serving in the uniformed services (Army, Navy, Air Force, Marines, Coast Guard, National Guard, Reserves) have the right to reemployment with restoration of seniority and benefits after service ends. Health insurance and benefits continuation during military leave follow federal USERRA rules.

**Absence of Mandated Leaves Not Covered**:

- **No paid family/medical leave beyond FMLA**: Beyond the federal Family and Medical Leave Act (29 U.S.C. §§ 2601–2654) and paid sick leave (Initiative 436), Nebraska does not mandate paid leave for births, adoptions, serious health conditions of family members, or care obligations.
- **No salary-history ban**: Employers may inquire about and consider prior compensation when making offers or determining pay.
- **No pay-transparency law**: No requirement to disclose salary ranges in job postings or to applicants, or to provide comparative pay data on request.
- **No personnel-file access for private employees**: Private-sector employees have no statutory right to access their personnel files (public-school employees have limited access rights under Neb. Rev. Stat. § 79-8,109).
- **No mini-WARN beyond state WARN (25+ threshold)**: Federal WARN Act (60 days for 50+ workers in 30-day period, employers with 100+ employees) applies; Nebraska's state WARN (90 days for 25+ employees, any employer size) provides broader coverage, but no additional notice requirement below these thresholds.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

When the platform processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in Nebraska:

**Wage Compliance**:

- **Ensure minimum wage compliance**: Track annual minimum-wage increases (1.75% annually starting January 1, 2027); publish updated rates no later than October 15 each year in the system.
- **No notice required for wage increases**: Pay increases may be implemented immediately.
- **30-day notice for payday changes**: If altering regular paydays, provide 30 days' written notice to the employee before the change.

**Paid Sick Leave** (if employer has 11+ employees):

- **Accrual preservation on promotion/pay change**: Accrued paid sick time is not forfeited on promotion or base-pay change; accrual continues at 1 hour per 30 hours worked.
- **Carryover enforcement**: Enforce accrued paid sick leave carryover to next year, or implement a pay-out-and-refresh cycle if employer policy allows.
- **Usage tracking**: Enforce annual usage caps (40 hours for small businesses, 56 hours for larger employers).
- **Notification**: Ensure employees receive written notice of paid sick leave entitlements if not already provided by September 15, 2025.

**Equal Pay and Compensation**:

- **Capture job-classification and compensation data**: Neb. Rev. Stat. § 48-1219 requires equal pay for equal work; document job descriptions, skill requirements, effort, responsibility, and working conditions for all roles.
- **Flag wage-equity disparities**: If a promotion or pay change appears to create a wage disparity based on sex for comparable work, escalate for legal review.
- **Wage disclosure protection**: Employees have the right to discuss and disclose wages without retaliation; ensure no policies prohibit wage discussions.

**Final Pay and Accrued Time on Separation**:

- **Calculate final pay due**: All earned wages, overtime, and accrued vacation/PTO (if legally owed) become due on the next regular payday or within 2 weeks of termination, whichever is sooner.
- **Accrued paid sick leave**: If employer policy requires payout of unused paid sick leave on separation, include it in final-pay calculation.
- **Document wage records**: Retain final-pay records for 3 years minimum.

**Termination and Mass Separation**:

- **Single terminations**: No statutory notice required beyond employment contract or policy (at-will employment applies).
- **Mass layoffs (25+ employees)**: If 25 or more full-time employees are separated on the same day, for the same reason, expected to last 7+ days, comply with Nebraska WARN Act: provide written notice to affected employees/representatives and Department of Labor, with a 90-day waiting period before action takes effect.
- **Larger mass layoffs (50+, 100+ employees)**: If federal WARN Act applies (100+ employer employees, 50+ separations in 30-day period), provide 60 days' notice; coordinate with state WARN notice.

**Record Retention**:

- **Retain payroll and wage records**: Maintain all wage, hour, and separation records for minimum 3 years.
- **Retain separation documentation**: Maintain final-pay records, separation notices, and related documentation.

**Data Security and Breach Notification**:

- **If personal information is breached**: Trigger notification workflow to affected parties "as soon as possible and without unreasonable delay" (under-3-day protocol recommended); log notification dates for compliance verification.

**Noncompete Documentation**:

- If the employer uses noncompete agreements, attach to employment record and flag for legal review; Nebraska enforceability depends on reasonableness and is subject to potential reformation by courts.

**Open Questions for HCM Configuration**:

- Does the employer have 11+ employees (triggering paid sick leave mandate)?
- Does the employer have 25+ or 50+ employees (triggering state or federal WARN requirements)?
- Does the employer use noncompete agreements? If so, what is the scope, duration, and geographic limitation? (Verify enforceability with legal counsel.)
- What is the employer's final-pay timing policy (immediately, next payday, or other)? Ensure compliance with the "next payday or 2 weeks" rule.
- Does the employer have an existing paid sick leave policy (accrual rate, carryover, payout method)? If not, implement one by October 1, 2025.
- Does the employer track accrued PTO/vacation as wages? If so, ensure proper calculation and payout on separation.
- Does the employer use E-Verify for public contracts? If so, verify contractor eligibility on record.
