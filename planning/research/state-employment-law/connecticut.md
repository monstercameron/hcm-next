# Connecticut Employment Law Research

**State:** Connecticut | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Employment-at-will with exceptions**: Connecticut recognizes wrongful discharge for termination violating clear public policy (filed wage claims, workers' comp claims, jury duty, religious freedom); implied employment contracts may also create obligations.
- **Pay transparency mandate**: As of 2026, job postings must disclose wage range AND general benefits description; wage range must be provided to applicants on request or before offer.
- **Salary history ban**: Employers may not inquire about or direct third parties to inquire about prospective employees' wage/salary history unless voluntarily disclosed by the candidate.
- **Paid sick leave expansion**: As of January 1, 2025, employers with 25+ employees must provide up to 40 hours paid sick leave annually; as of January 1, 2027, this expands to employers with 1+ employees.
- **Connecticut FMLA (16 weeks)**: Private employers with 75+ employees must provide 16 weeks unpaid leave in a 24-month period for qualifying family/medical reasons; 12-month tenure and 1,000 work-hours required for eligibility.
- **Restrictive covenants tightened (October 1, 2026)**: Non-competes require less restrictive alternatives first (non-disclosure, non-solicitation, trade secrets law); must be reasonable in duration, geography, and scope; 10+ business days for employee legal review required.
- **Wage theft protections**: As of January 1, 2027, general contractors are jointly/severally liable for subcontractor wage theft on construction contracts.
- **Mass layoff severance**: Employers conducting mass layoffs must pay severance at 2 months' pay per year of employment; federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for 100+ employees) also applies.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Connecticut is an employment-at-will state: either party may terminate the relationship without cause (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-1).

**Exceptions to at-will status** (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-51q and common law):

- Termination for filing a workers' compensation claim, unemployment claim, or wage enforcement claim.
- Termination for exercising federal or state constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religious freedom).
- Termination for jury duty, voting, or military service.
- Termination in violation of public policy (e.g., whistleblowing, refusing illegal act).
- **Implied employment contract**: Oral or written promises of job security, detailed employment practices, or course of dealing may create enforceable contract duties beyond at-will terms.

**Required written notices at hire and on change**:

- At time of hiring, employer must advise employees in writing of the rate of remuneration, hours of employment, wage payment schedules, and make available (in writing or posted) all employment practices and policies regarding wages, vacation, sick leave, benefits, and policy changes (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-71f).
- On any change of pay or role, employers should provide written notice of new terms to align with § 31-71f disclosure requirements; no specific statutory notice period mandated for pay changes except final pay timing (see § 3 below).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- Connecticut minimum wage is $16.35 per hour as of January 1, 2025 (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-58).
- Effective January 1, 2026, the minimum wage increases to $16.94 per hour, adjusted annually by the Connecticut Labor Commissioner based on the prior calendar year's change in the federal Employment Cost Index (ECI) (PA 19-4; announced by October 15 each year).
- Special rates for specific sectors ("Restaurant and Hotel Workers," "Mercantile Trade (Retail)") are indexed to the same ECI-based adjustment mechanism.
- No subminimum wage allowed for minors or any class of worker.

**Overtime**:

- Employees must be paid 1.5x regular rate for hours worked over 40 per week (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-58).
- No daily overtime beyond 8 hours or 7th-day overtime unless agreed upon.
- Exempt categories include agricultural workers, executives, professionals, outside salespeople, and domestic workers.

**Pay frequency**:

- Wages must be paid weekly on a designated payday (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-71b).
- Payment via cash, check, or direct deposit (with written employee consent).

**Final pay on termination**:

- **Laid-off employees**: Full wages and accrued leave due on next regular payday.
- **Discharged employees**: Full wages and accrued leave due "the next business day" (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-71c).
- **Resigned employees**: Full wages due on next regular payday unless employee does not provide notice; verify current case law on notice requirements.

**Permitted deductions**:

- Employers cannot withhold wages except as required by law, with written employee authorization on an approved form, or for court-ordered garnishments, tax withholdings, medical/retirement plans (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-71e).
- No deductions for "tools of trade" or similar employer-imposed costs.

**Pay statement requirements**:

- Employers must maintain records for three years documenting employee name, address, occupation, daily/weekly hours (rounded to 15-minute increments), wage information, and deductions (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-49).
- No specific law mandating itemized pay stubs; federal FLSA applies.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure in postings and on request**:

- Public Act 21-30 (effective October 1, 2021) requires employers to disclose the "wage range" anticipated for a position to applicants upon the earlier of request or before making an offer (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-74g).
- Public Act 26-12 (signed 2026, verify effective date; likely 2026 or 2027) expanded this to require both wage range AND general benefits description (health insurance, retirement, fringe, paid leave, other compensation) in ALL public and internal job postings.

**Salary history ban**:

- Employers may not inquire about or direct third parties to inquire about a prospective employee's wage/salary history unless the candidate has voluntarily disclosed it (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-40z, enacted by PA 18-8). (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-40z)

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state-level pay equity reporting requirement; federal EEO-1 or similar applies if applicable to the employer.

**Equal pay statute**:

- Connecticut's "equal pay for equal work" law prohibits sex-based wage discrimination (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-75): employers cannot pay employees of one sex less than employees of the opposite sex for equal work performed under similar working conditions and requiring equal skill, effort, and responsibility (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-40z).
- Employer defense: the wage differential is based on seniority, merit, production quality, or system other than sex.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**:

- As of January 1, 2025, employers with 25+ employees must provide up to 40 hours paid sick time annually (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-57y).
- As of January 1, 2027, this expands to employers with 1+ employees (verify final phase-in schedule).
- Employees can use accrued paid sick leave for their own serious health condition, child's/spouse's/parent's serious health condition, closure of workplace due to public emergency, or domestic violence/sexual assault-related needs.
- Employers may require certification from health care provider if employee uses more than 3 consecutive days for one reason.

**Connecticut Family and Medical Leave Act (CTFMLA)**:

- Employers with 75+ employees in Connecticut must provide up to 16 weeks unpaid leave in any 24-month period (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 31-51kk to 31-51qq).
- Eligibility: 12+ months tenure (non-consecutive) and 1,000 work-hours in prior 12 months.
- Qualifying reasons: birth/adoption, child care within first year, serious health condition of employee or family member, spouse/child/parent military service, organ donation.
- Job restoration: employee must be restored to same or equivalent position upon return.

**Connecticut Paid Family and Medical Leave (CT PFML)**:

- Connecticut administers a paid family and medical leave insurance program (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 31-49e et seq.) through the Connecticut Paid Leave Authority (established by PA 19-29).
- **Eligibility and contributions**: The program covers all employers with 1+ Connecticut employees. Employees contribute 0.5% of wages (up to the annually adjusted Social Security taxable wage base; for 2026, maximum annual contribution is approximately $922.50 on wages up to $184,500).
- **Wage replacement and duration**: Eligible employees receive wage replacement benefits for up to 12 weeks (or more in some cases) of partially paid leave for qualifying family and medical reasons, plus 12 days of PFML for family violence-related matters.
- **Benefit calculation**: Two-tier wage replacement system: 95% of weekly earnings up to the state minimum wage, and 60% of weekly earnings above that threshold, capped at 60 times the state minimum wage (approximately $1,016.40 weekly for 2026).
- **Contribution and benefit history**: Contributions began January 1, 2021; benefits began January 1, 2022. The program is income-replacement only and does not provide job protection (job protection is provided by CTFMLA, above).
- **Eligibility requirements for benefits**: Employees must have earned at least $2,325 in the highest-earning quarter of the first four of the five most recently completed quarters (base-period calculation).

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- If an employee takes paid sick leave or FMLA leave and returns to a different pay or role, the platform must not reduce the employee's wages below what is contractually due or required by law; verify case law on whether reinstatement requires original salary if promotion occurred during leave.
- Paid sick leave accrual continues during unpaid FMLA leave.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- Employers must maintain payroll records for 3 years documenting employee name, address, occupation, hours worked (rounded to 15-min increments), wages, and deductions (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-49).
- Medical records must be retained separately from personnel files for at least 3 years following termination (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-48h).
- General personnel records may be kept for longer; no maximum retention period specified in statute.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- Employees may inspect their personnel and medical records within a reasonable time after written request (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-48h).
- Inspection occurs during regular business hours at or near the employee's worksite.
- Employees may obtain copies of all or part of their file within reasonable time; employers may charge a reasonable fee related to copying costs.
- **Limit**: No more than 2 inspections per calendar year without employer consent (verify whether copying requests count separately).

**Disputed information**:

- If an employee disagrees with information in the personnel file, removal or correction may be agreed upon. If not agreed, the employee may submit a written statement of their position, which must be maintained as part of the file and accompany any disclosure to third parties.

**Record format**:

- No Connecticut statute specifies electronic vs. paper format; employers may maintain records electronically if retention and access requirements are met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Connecticut Data Privacy Act (CTDPA)**:

- Public Act 22-15 (effective May 10, 2022; Conn. Gen. Stat. § 1-620, Chapter 743jj) is a comprehensive consumer privacy law that applies to controllers and processors of personal data.
- **Sensitive data** includes genetic or biometric data (fingerprint, voice print, retina/iris image, or other unique physical representation); controllers must obtain consumer consent to process sensitive data.
- CTDPA defines "consumer" broadly; applicability to employee data is evolving; **verify** whether employee data falls under CTDPA or whether only customer/applicant data is covered.

**Employee privacy—electronic monitoring**:

- Illegal for employers to operate electronic surveillance devices or systems to monitor employees in areas designed for health/personal comfort (restrooms, locker rooms, lounges) or for safeguarding possessions (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-48a).
- No statutory ban on email/internet monitoring of work accounts or general workplace video monitoring (subject to reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine).

**Breach notification**:

- Connecticut breach notification law (Conn. Gen. Stat. §§ 36a-692 to 36a-701) requires notification "without unreasonable delay" if personally identifiable information is accessed, acquired, or used by unauthorized person; verify whether employee records trigger this or only consumer data.

**Data residency or processing constraints**:

- No Connecticut law mandates data residency for employee records; employers may process/store in-state or out-of-state so long as data security and privacy laws are met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- For **state employment**: Connecticut prohibits the state from disqualifying persons based solely on prior conviction of a crime (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 46a-80); arrests without conviction or erased convictions cannot be used or disseminated in connection with employment applications (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 54-142a).
- For **private sector**: No statewide ban-the-box law for private employers; federal EEOC guidance applies (verify if any recent legislation).
- Some municipalities have enacted local ban-the-box ordinances; employers in those jurisdictions must comply locally.

**Background checks**:

- No Connecticut statute restricts private employers from conducting background checks; federal FCRA applies.
- If an employer uses a third-party consumer reporting agency, FCRA notice, dispute, and adverse-action procedures apply.

**Drug testing**:

- No state-level statute regulating private-sector drug testing; federal law (pre-employment, reasonable suspicion, post-accident) governs; federal DOT rules apply to transportation.
- Employers should verify local ordinances in jurisdiction where employees work.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No Connecticut state mandate for private-sector E-Verify use; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice and severance (Connecticut "mini-WARN")**:

- Connecticut requires employers conducting a "mass layoff" to pay severance at the rate of 2 months' pay for each year of employment (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-51u).
- **Mass layoff definition**: Permanent layoff of 75+ employees at one worksite within a 3-month period (verify exact threshold and period).
- Employers must provide information packet to each laid-off employee "immediately" upon layoff, regardless of reason for separation (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-51t).
- Federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for employers with 100+ employees and mass layoff/plant closing affecting 50+ employees) also applies.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- Connecticut enforces reasonable non-compete agreements by common law and statute (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-49h, amended by PA 26-12 effective October 1, 2026).
- **Enforceability criteria** (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-49h):
  - Employer must provide employee at least 10 business days, or more if reasonable, to seek legal advice before signing.
  - Agreement must be reasonable in duration, geographical scope, and type of employment/business prohibited.
  - Employer must have legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential info, customer relationships, substantial relationship with prospective or existing clients).
  - **New requirement (PA 26-12, effective 10/1/26)**: Covenant must employ less restrictive alternatives first (non-disclosure, non-solicitation, trade secrets law protection) and is no more restrictive than necessary.
- Courts may modify unreasonable restrictions to make them enforceable (blue-pencil doctrine).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- Non-solicitation agreements (restriction on soliciting employees or customers) are not "non-competes" if they do not restrict work for more than 1 year and are no more restrictive than necessary in duration, geography, type of work, and employer scope (Conn. Gen. Stat. § 31-49h).
- Enforced under same reasonableness standard as non-competes.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification (ABC test)**:

- Connecticut applies the ABC test to determine contractor vs. employee status for unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, and wage-and-hour purposes (used by Joint Enforcement Commission on Employee Misclassification).
- All three factors must be met:
  - **(A)** Freedom from direction/control in performing services, both contractually and in fact.
  - **(B)** Service performed outside usual course of employer's business or outside all employer's locations.
  - **(C)** Individual customarily engaged in independently established trade/occupation/profession of the same nature.
- Misclassification is subject to enforcement by Connecticut Department of Labor, Department of Revenue Services, Attorney General, Workers' Compensation Commission, and Chief State's Attorney (Joint Enforcement Commission).

**Remote workers**:

- No Connecticut statute specifically addresses remote work classification or requirements; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee is remote but works for Connecticut employer or performs work in Connecticut, Connecticut law applies.

**Multi-state employment (reciprocity)**:

- No reciprocity doctrine in Connecticut statute. If employee works across state lines, Connecticut applies its laws to work performed in the state and may be applied concurrently with other states' laws (verify choice-of-law case law).
- Platform should flag when a workforce change affects employees in multiple states to ensure compliance with each state's requirements.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

**Required typed obligations and timing constraints**:

1. **Pay transparency**: Before extending an offer or posting a job, platform must prompt for wage range and benefits description; system must timestamp when range was disclosed to applicant.
2. **At-will exceptions**: Flag terminations for public-policy violations (workers' comp claim, jury duty, union activity, wage theft report) and block/require approval.
3. **Paid sick leave accrual**: Model 40-hour annual entitlement (staggered per phase-in: 25+ employees from 1/1/25, all from 1/1/27); track usage for leave-balance enforcement.
4. **CTFMLA eligibility**: Gate 16-week leave for employees in Connecticut with 75+ employer headcount, 12+ months tenure, and 1,000+ prior-year hours; track leave usage in 24-month rolling window.
5. **Final pay timing**: On discharge, platform must calculate and trigger payment by next business day; on layoff, next regular payday.
6. **Severance on mass layoff**: If 75+ employees laid off within 3 months, trigger severance calculation (2 months per year of service) and generate notice packet.
7. **Non-compete/non-solicit**: Store date when non-compete was signed, date employee received 10+ business days for legal review, and reasonableness attributes (duration, geography, scope); flag any restrictions post-10/1/26 that may violate PA 26-12.
8. **Wage theft notice (construction)**: If employer is general contractor on public works, track subcontractor payments and flag joint liability as of 1/1/27.

**Field restrictions and validations**:

- Salary history inquiry: Block form field or flag if user attempts to request salary history from external candidates.
- Wage range in posting: Require wage range + benefits description before posting job (post-PA 26-12).
- Overtime classification: Default non-exempt; flag exempt categories (executive, professional, outside sales, agricultural, domestic) and require documentation.
- Paid sick leave: Disable manual override of accrual calculation (statutory entitlement).

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether CTDPA "sensitive data" consent applies to employee biometric data (fingerprints for timekeeping) or only customer/applicant data.
- Clarify whether implied employment contract doctrine has been applied in recent CT case law and what factual patterns trigger it.
- Confirm whether pay-change notice has any statutory waiting period or is informational only.
- Verify whether reinstatement after CTFMLA leave must occur at the exact same salary if employee is promoted during leave.
- Confirm threshold for "mass layoff" (is it 75 or a different number) and 3-month measurement period.
