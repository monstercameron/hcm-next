# Pennsylvania Employment Law Research

**State:** Pennsylvania | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **Employment-at-will with public-policy exceptions**: Pennsylvania recognizes at-will employment but prohibits termination for exercising constitutional rights, filing wage/workers' compensation claims, jury duty, military service, and public-policy violations (e.g., whistleblowing).
- **Wage Payment Law with WPCL (43 P.S. § 260 et seq.)**: Employers must pay all wages by next regular payday on termination (any reason); liquidated damages of 25% plus attorney fees for wage withholding; advance notice required for pay-rate changes (§ 260.4).
- **Minimum wage $7.25/hour (federal floor) with 1.5x overtime for hours over 40/week** under 43 P.S. § 333.101 et seq.; no state increase above federal rate; tipped minimum $2.83/hour; no subminimum for exempt classes beyond federal FLSA.
- **Limited equal pay statute (43 P.S. § 336.1)**: Protects sex only (no race, age, or other classes); requires equal work with equal skill, effort, responsibility, and similar working conditions; merit/seniority/quality-of-production systems permitted.
- \*\*Human Relations Act (43 P.S. § 951 et seq., 4+ employees): Applies to 9 protected classes (race, color, creed, ancestry, age, sex, national origin, disability); June 2023 regulations expanded definitions to include sexual orientation within sex and comprehensive religious-creed observance (16 Pa. Code § 41.201–41.207 effective Aug 17, 2023).
- **Philadelphia Wage Equity Ordinance (§ 9-1131, upheld Feb 2020)**: Bans inquiries into salary history; applies to all external hires in Philadelphia; salary history may not be used to set wages. Enforced Sep 1, 2020.
- **Philadelphia paid sick leave (§ 9-4101 et seq.)**: 40 hours/year accrual (1 hour per 40 hours worked); applies to all private employers; carryover required unless frontloading policy provided at hire.
- \*\*Pittsburgh paid sick leave (eff. Jan 1, 2026): Employers with 15+ employees provide 72 hours/year; fewer than 15 provide 48 hours/year; accrual 1 hour per 30 hours worked; carryover mandatory.
- **No statewide paid sick leave**: Only Philadelphia and Pittsburgh ordinances; Allegheny County has rules; no state mandate.
- **No statewide salary-history ban**: Only Philadelphia and Executive Order (state agencies only, 2018); no state legislative ban (verify pending bills).
- **Non-competes enforceable under common law (reasonableness standard); Fair Contracting for Health Care Practitioners Act (Act 74, eff. Jan 1, 2025): Prohibits non-competes for healthcare practitioners lasting >1 year or covering dismissals**.
- **No mini-WARN act**: Federal WARN (60 days) applies if 100+ employees; Pennsylvania has no state-level mass-layoff notice requirement.
- **Criminal History Restrictions (18 Pa.C.S. § 9125, Philadelphia/Allegheny ban-the-box)**: Cannot inquire about criminal history in initial application; written notice required if rejection based on criminal record; local ordinances bar questions pre-offer.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Pennsylvania recognizes at-will employment: employers and employees may terminate without cause absent contractual limitation or public-policy exception.

**Exceptions to at-will termination**:

- Termination for filing wage theft or workers' compensation claims (WPCL retaliation).
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religious freedom).
- Termination for jury duty, voting (42 Pa Cons Stat § 4563), military service, or witnessing.
- Termination for public-policy violations (e.g., refusing illegal act, whistleblowing on public employers under 43 P.S. § 1421).
- Termination based on protected-class status under Pennsylvania Human Relations Act (race, color, creed, ancestry, age, sex, national origin, disability); June 2023 regulations expand sex to include sexual orientation.

**Required notices at hire and on change**:

- 43 P.S. § 260.4 requires written notice _at hire_ and _in advance_ of any change to wage rate, pay frequency, deductions, or fringe benefits.
- Failure to provide advance notice triggers WPCL wage-theft penalties (25% liquidated damages, attorney fees, 3-year statute of limitations; federal statute of limitations does not cap PA WPCL claims).
- No specific statutory offer-of-employment form; written documentation of role, rate, and frequency recommended.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (43 P.S. § 333.101 et seq.; 34 Pa. Code § 231.101a):

- $7.25/hour (federal floor; no state increase).
- Tipped employees: $2.83/hour direct wage; employer must supplement to $7.25 if tips do not reach threshold.
- No subminimum for minors, trainees, or learners.

**Overtime** (34 Pa. Code § 231.41–231.43):

- Time-and-a-half (1.5x regular rate) for all hours over 40/week (workweek = 7 consecutive days).
- Regular rate includes all compensation except gifts, discretionary bonuses, vacation, sick leave, jury duty pay, or holiday pay (§ 231.43).
- Exemptions follow federal FLSA (executive, administrative, professional, outside sales).
- No daily overtime (8/day) trigger; only weekly 40-hour threshold applies.

**Pay frequency and timing** (43 P.S. § 260.3):

- Employers must establish regular paydays and pay all wages due on those days.
- Common law: twice per month typical; no statutory minimum frequency except "regular" paydays.
- No statutory waiting-period requirement; all earned wages due by next regular payday.

**Final pay on termination** (43 P.S. § 260.3):

- All wages earned must be paid no later than next regular payday following termination (discharge, resignation, layoff, or suspension).
- Applies to all earnings including accrued bonuses, commissions, and fringe benefits due.
- If dispute about final wages exists, employer must pay undisputed amount without condition (no offset for disputed items).

**Permitted deductions** (43 P.S. § 260 et seq.; 34 Pa. Code § 9.1–9.2):

- Only deductions required by law (tax, garnishment, court order, ERISA welfare plans) or with written authorization.
- No deductions for employer-required tools, uniforms, equipment, or costs unless written consent given and resulting wage does not fall below minimum.
- WPCL violation triggers 25% liquidated damages on withheld wages, plus attorney fees and costs (3-year statute of limitations; federal FLSA limitations do not cap WPCL).

**Pay statement content** (34 Pa. Code § 231.36):

- Employers must provide written statement showing hours worked, wage rates, gross wages, and deductions; retain 3 years.
- No statutory itemization detail requirement, but records must track hours, rate, deductions, and net pay.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage range disclosure**:

- No statewide requirement; Philadelphia Wage Equity Ordinance (§ 9-1131) applies to all employers hiring in Philadelphia (effective Sep 1, 2020); no wage-range posting mandate (verify if Philadelphia enacted range law post-2024).

**Salary history ban** (Philadelphia § 9-1131):

- Employers in Philadelphia cannot inquire about applicants' wage/salary history or direct third parties to inquire.
- Candidates may voluntarily disclose without employer prompting.
- Ban does not apply to internal promotions or transfers.
- Retaliation (refusing to interview, hire, or employ for refusal to disclose) prohibited.
- No statewide ban; state agencies follow 2018 Executive Order for internal hires only (verify current state policy).

**Equal pay statute** (43 P.S. § 336.1; 34 Pa. Code § 9.11 et seq.):

- Unlawful to pay employees at lower rates based on **sex** for equal work on jobs requiring equal skill, effort, responsibility, and similar working conditions.
- Protected class: sex only (does not include age, race, national origin, or disability—those covered under Human Relations Act discrimination, not equal pay).
- Exceptions: seniority system, merit system, or quality/quantity-of-production system.
- No treble damages; recovery includes unpaid wages plus reasonable attorney fees.

**Protected classes under PHRA** (43 P.S. § 951 et seq.; 16 Pa. Code § 41.201–41.207, June 2023):

- Nine protected classes: race, color, religious creed, ancestry, age, sex (includes sexual orientation as of June 2023), national origin, non-job-related handicap/disability.
- Religious creed includes all aspects of religious observance, practice, or belief (per June 2023 regulations).
- Applies to employers with 4+ employees.

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state pay-equity audit requirement; federal EEO-1 filing applies if applicable (100+ employees nationwide).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Statewide mandate**:

- No statewide paid sick leave; only Philadelphia (§ 9-4101), Pittsburgh (eff. Jan 1, 2026), and Allegheny County ordinances apply.

**Philadelphia Paid Sick Leave** (§ 9-4101 et seq.):

- All private employers must provide up to 40 hours/year.
- Accrual: 1 hour per 40 hours worked.
- Permitted uses: employee's own or family member's physical/mental health diagnosis, care, treatment; domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking; child's school activities; public health emergency quarantine.
- Carryover: Unused hours carry forward annually unless written policy limits carryover (verify max cap); frontloading (providing 40 hours at year start) satisfies accrual requirement.

**Pittsburgh Paid Sick Leave** (eff. Jan 1, 2026):

- Employers with 15+ employees: 72 hours/year; fewer than 15: 48 hours/year.
- Accrual: 1 hour per 30 hours worked (faster than Philadelphia).
- Carryover mandatory except when frontloading the full annual hours at year start.

**No state family/medical leave mandate**:

- No Pennsylvania state FMLA equivalent. Federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid for covered employers) applies if 50+ employees within 75 miles.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- On promotion within same employer, accrued sick leave carries forward to promoted role.
- Termination triggers payout of accrued balance unless written policy limits compensation (policy must be provided at hire).
- Unpaid leave (e.g., unpaid suspension) does not suspend sick-leave accrual (verify case law).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll record retention** (43 P.S. § 260 et seq.; 34 Pa. Code § 231.31):

- Employers must retain payroll records (hours, wages, deductions) for 3 years.
- Wage statements showing hours, rates, gross/net pay required.

**Personnel file access** (Pennsylvania Personnel Files Act, 43 P.S. § 1321–1324):

- Employees have right to inspect and copy their own personnel file used for employment decisions (qualification, promotion, compensation, termination, discipline).
- Access at reasonable times during regular business hours at the office where records are maintained.
- Employer may require employee to inspect on free time; employee may designate agent to inspect.
- Employer may charge reasonable copying fee.
- No statutory timeframe mandate (verify whether "reasonable time" has case-law definition).

**Record format**:

- No statute mandates electronic vs. paper format; electronic records acceptable if retention and access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Breach Notification, Biometric/Monitoring, Social Media

**Data breach notification** (Pennsylvania Breach of Personal Information Notification Act, 73 P.S. § 2301 et seq., amended May 2, 2023):

- Applies to personal information (name + SSN, credit card, medical information, health insurance, online account credentials).
- Notification to affected individuals in most expedient time without unreasonable delay; government entities (agencies, counties, municipalities, schools) must notify within 7 business days.
- State agencies must also notify governor's Office of Administration within 3 business days.
- HIPAA-covered entities deemed in compliance if compliant with HIPAA privacy and security standards.

**Workplace monitoring**:

- No state statute restricting email/internet monitoring on work devices; reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies.
- Video monitoring permitted absent reasonable privacy expectation (e.g., restrooms, locker rooms).

**Social media**:

- No Pennsylvania statute protecting access to personal social media accounts or requiring disclosure (federal law governs; state common law may apply).

**Data residency**:

- No Pennsylvania statute mandating employee data residency; employers may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided security/privacy laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box** (Local ordinances; Criminal History Record Information Act, 18 Pa.C.S. § 9125):

- Philadelphia and Allegheny County ordinances: Employers cannot inquire about criminal record during initial employment application.
- CHRIA (18 Pa.C.S. § 9125) limits _use_ of criminal history: felony/misdemeanor convictions may be considered only to the extent they relate to job suitability; written notice required if rejection based on criminal history.
- Employers in jurisdictions without ordinances may inquire; CHRIA notice requirement applies statewide.

**Background checks**:

- No state statute restricting private-sector background checks; federal FCRA applies. Employers using third-party agencies must provide FCRA notice, dispute rights, and adverse-action procedures.

**Drug testing**:

- No state-level statute regulating private-sector drug testing; federal law (pre-employment, reasonable suspicion, post-accident) and DOT rules apply.

**E-Verify mandate** (Public Works Employment Verification Act, 43 P.S. § 167.1 et seq., amended Act 141 of 2022, eff. July 22, 2023):

- Mandate applies only to public works contractors/subcontractors (public works = construction/demolition/repair funded entirely/partially by public body, >$25,000 cost).
- Contractors must verify employment eligibility via E-Verify within 5 business days of hire for all new employees on public works projects.
- No mandate for general private-sector employers; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice**:

- No Pennsylvania state mini-WARN act; federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for 100+ employees) applies if threshold met.
- Verify whether Pennsylvania Department of Labor & Industry imposes supplemental notice requirements for state-funded employers.

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance except federal WARN Act. Contractually negotiated or discretionary otherwise.

**Non-compete enforceability** (Common law + Fair Contracting for Health Care Practitioners Act, Act 74, eff. Jan 1, 2025):

- Governed by common law reasonableness standard: must protect legitimate employer interests (trade secrets, confidential info, customer relationships), impose no undue hardship, and not injure public.
- Courts uphold durations of 6 months to 2 years depending on industry/role.
- PA courts apply "blue penciling" (judicial modification of overly broad agreements).
- **Act 74 (health care practitioners only)**: Non-competes for MDs, DOs, CRNAs, CNPs, and PAs are void if >1 year or if employee was dismissed by employer (including terminations for cause). Existing agreements (pre-Jan 1, 2025) remain enforceable under prior law.
- Non-compete covenants for non-healthcare workers remain enforceable under common law (verify pending legislative changes post-2026).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness standard (verify case-law scope).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification** (Construction Workplace Misclassification Act, 43 P.S. § 933.1 et seq. [Act 72]; PMWA § 333.107 wage-hour rules):

- Construction industry (all commercial/residential work): Three-part test required to classify as independent contractor:
  - Written contract to perform services.
  - Free from control/direction over work performance.
  - Customarily engaged in independently established trade or business.
  - Must maintain liability insurance of $50,000+.
- Failure to meet all three prongs = employee classification.
- Federal/state income tax withholding failure not considered in determining contractor status.
- Retaliation prohibited.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine. If employee works across state lines, each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Platform should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable state rules.

**Remote workers**:

- No Pennsylvania statute specifically addressing remote-work classification. Standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee physically works in Pennsylvania (even remotely for out-of-state employer), Pennsylvania law applies to that work.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling: Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

**Mandatory obligations and field restrictions**:

1. **Pay-Rate Change Notice** (43 P.S. § 260.4): On pay decrease, generate automated notice _in advance_ of effective date with new rate, frequency, and effective date. Pay _increases_ need not be pre-announced (system default).

2. **Salary History Inquiry Block (Philadelphia § 9-1131)**: Disable any form field attempting to request prior salary/wages from external candidates hired in Philadelphia. Log any such queries as compliance risk.

3. **Equal Pay Audit Flag** (43 P.S. § 336.1, _sex only_): On promotion + base-pay change, flag if new rate for female employee differs materially from peer male rates for "equal work" (same skill, effort, responsibility, working conditions, same employer/location). Require manager justification or HR/legal approval to override. (Verify whether PA courts require strict geographic proximity or employer-only comparisons.)

4. **WPCL Compliance** (43 P.S. § 260): Document all pay rate changes and provide written notice in advance; failure triggers wage-theft penalties (25% liquidated damages, attorney fees, 3-year SoL). Track notices for audit trail.

5. **Accrued Sick Leave Payout** (Philadelphia § 9-4101, Pittsburgh eff. Jan 1, 2026): On termination, payout accrued balance unless written policy limits compensation (must be provided at hire). On promotion within same employer, carry forward balance to promoted role.

6. **Final Pay Timing** (43 P.S. § 260.3): Schedule final paycheck by _next regular payday_ following termination; include all wages due (including accrued paid time off per local ordinance if applicable).

7. **PHRA Protected Class Flag** (43 P.S. § 951, 4+ employees, nine protected classes): On promotion, flag if new rate differs materially from peer rates for other protected-class employees performing "substantially similar work" (skill, effort, responsibility). Require justification; document salary-setting rationale.

8. **Philadelphia vs. Other Jurisdictions**: Apply Philadelphia wage-equity and paid-sick-leave rules _only_ to employees working in Philadelphia. Apply Pittsburgh paid-sick-leave rules (48–72 hours/year) only to Pittsburgh-based employees. Statewide employees follow no state mandate (only local).

9. **Military Leave (51 Pa.C.S. § 7301)**: If employee called to active military duty, grant leave; reinstatement rights apply upon return within 90 days of discharge.

10. **Jury Duty Protection (42 Pa Cons Stat § 4563)**: Employers with 15+ employees (or 40+ manufacturing) may not penalize employees for jury service; no pay-during-service required but no retaliation for participation.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether "substantially similar work" equal-pay comparisons require same job title, same department, same work location, or company-wide peers.
- Confirm whether sick-leave accrual continues during unpaid leave or layoff period.
- Clarify legal definition of "reasonable time" for personnel-file inspection requests.
- Verify whether Pennsylvania HB 1275 (or other pending bills) impose wage-range posting requirements statewide (2025–2026).
- Confirm non-compete blue-pencil doctrine: can PA courts modify overly broad covenants or must they void entirely (verify recent case law post-2024)?
- Verify whether Allegheny County paid-sick-leave rules differ from Pittsburgh and whether both apply to same employees.
