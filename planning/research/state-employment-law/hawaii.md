# Hawaii Employment Law Research

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Minimum wage and scheduled increases**: Hawaii minimum wage is $16.00/hour (2026), increasing to $18.00/hour on January 1, 2028 (HRS §387-2); no subminimum allowed for minors or any class of worker.
- **Overtime requirement**: Employees must be paid 1.5x regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek (HRS §387-3); no daily overtime trigger.
- **At-will employment with statutory exceptions**: Hawaii is at-will, but employers cannot discharge or discriminate based on protected class (race, sex, sexual orientation, age, religion, color, ancestry, disability, marital status), filing workers' comp or wage claim, or performing jury duty (HRS §378-2, §378-32).
- **Final pay timing**: At discharge, all earned wages due at time of discharge or no later than next working day; semi-monthly pay required (HRS §388-2, §388-3).
- **Pay transparency mandate (SB 1057, effective 1/1/24)**: Employers with 50+ employees must disclose reasonably expected wage/hourly rate in all job postings (internal and external).
- **Hawaii Family Leave Law (HFLL)**: Employers with 100+ employees must provide up to 4 weeks unpaid leave per calendar year for qualifying family/medical reasons (birth, adoption, child/spouse/parent care, reciprocal beneficiary); 6+ consecutive months tenure required; unpaid but can substitute accrued paid leave (verify integration with FMLA).
- **Temporary Disability Insurance (TDI)**: Non-work-related illness/injury/pregnancy covered; eligibility: 14 weeks employment, 20+ hours/week, $400+ earned in prior 52 weeks; provides partial wage replacement; available within 14 days of separation (HRS ch. 392).
- **Mini-WARN (HRS §394B)**: Employers with 50+ employees must provide 60 days' notice of divestiture, closing, partial closing, or relocation; severance required; failure to provide notice = back pay/benefits for up to 60 days.
- **Non-compete enforceability**: Restrictive covenants enforceable under common law if reasonable in duration, geography, and scope and protect legitimate business interests (trade secrets, customer contacts, confidential info); technology workers excluded (Act 158, 2015); post-employment covenants void unless showing clear protective purpose (Prudential Locations v. Gagnon, 2022); non-solicitation requires "active initiation of contact" to prove violation.
- **Wage theft penalties**: Violations of HRS §388 subject to administrative penalty of $500+ per violation, plus back pay, damages (amount of unpaid wages + 6% interest), and attorney's fees (HRS §388-10).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Hawaii is an employment-at-will state: either party may terminate the employment relationship without stated cause, advance notice, or severance (common law).

**Statutory exceptions to at-will status** (HRS §378-2, §378-32):

- Discrimination based on protected class: race, sex, sexual orientation, age, religion, color, ancestry, disability, or marital status is unlawful (HRS §378-2).
- Wrongful discharge for filing workers' compensation claim, wage claim, or filing for wage earner bankruptcy protection (HRS §378-32).
- Discharge or discrimination for jury duty, voting, or military service (HRS §378-32, §378-33).
- Discharge or discrimination for pursuing wage enforcement through complaint or legal action.
- Violation of public policy (e.g., whistleblowing, refusing illegal act); verify scope under Hawaii case law.

**Required written notices at hire and on change**:

- At time of hiring, employer should provide written notice of rate of pay, hours of work, pay frequency, and wage policies (best practice; no explicit statute, but HRS §388-2 requires semi-monthly pay).
- On change of pay or role, employer should provide written notice of new terms (informational; no statutory notice period specified except final pay timing on discharge).

**Wrongful discharge complaint**: Employee may file complaint with Hawaii Department of Labor & Industrial Relations within 30 days of alleged unlawful discharge or discrimination (HRS §378-33).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (HRS §387-2):

- $16.00/hour effective January 1, 2026.
- $18.00/hour effective January 1, 2028.
- Applies to all employees; no subminimum for minors or any class of worker.

**Overtime** (HRS §387-3):

- Employees must be paid 1.5x regular rate for hours worked over 40 in a workweek.
- No daily overtime (hours over 8 in a single day do not trigger overtime).
- Exempt categories: agricultural workers, executives, professionals, outside salespeople, domestic workers (verify exact exemption list).

**Tipped employees**:

- Tip credit allowed if combined cash wages + tips = minimum wage + $7.00/hour; verify exact calculation for 2026.
- Minimum direct cash wage for tipped employees must be $14.75/hour (2026, verify for current year).

**Pay frequency** (HRS §388-2):

- Wages must be paid at least semi-monthly (twice per month) on designated paydays.
- Payment by cash, check, or direct deposit (with written employee consent).

**Final pay on termination** (HRS §388-3):

- **Discharge**: All earned wages and accrued leave due at time of discharge; if circumstances prevent immediate payment, due no later than next working day.
- **Resignation**: All earned wages due on next regular payday (verify whether resignation requires advance notice for final pay timing).
- **Laid off**: All earned wages and accrued leave due on next regular payday or within 30 days, whichever is earlier (verify exact timing).

**Permitted deductions** (HRS §388-5):

- Employers may not deduct wages except as required by law, court order (garnishment), or written authorization on approved form (tax withholding, authorized retirement/benefit plans).
- Deductions for cash shortages, damages, uniforms, or tools of trade are prohibited.

**Pay statement requirements**:

- Employer must provide itemized pay stub showing gross pay, deductions, net pay, and hours worked each payday (verify statutory requirement vs. best practice).
- Records must be maintained for at least 6 years (HRS §388-4).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure in postings and on request** (SB 1057, effective 1/1/24):

- Employers with 50+ employees in Hawaii must disclose in all job advertisements (internal and external, online and print) the "reasonably expected actual wage rate" or hourly wage/salary range.
- Applies to all job posting methods and locations.
- Exemptions: internal promotions/transfers, positions with employers under 50 employees, public sector covered by collective bargaining (verify exact exemptions).
- Enforcement: Hawaii Civil Rights Commission (HCRC) investigates complaints; violations may result in corrective action, penalties, or damages.

**Salary history inquiry restriction**:

- Verify whether Hawaii has a specific statutory ban on inquiring into prospective employee's salary history; SB 1057 does not explicitly ban history inquiries but is part of broader pay equity framework.

**Equal pay statute** (HRS §378-71, verify exact section):

- Hawaii law prohibits wage discrimination based on protected class (sex, race, age, etc.) for "substantially similar work" (SB 1057 expanded from "equal work").
- Employer defense: wage differential based on seniority, merit system, quality of production, or factor other than protected class (verify exact defenses).

**Pay data reporting**:

- No Hawaii-specific pay equity reporting requirement; federal EEO-1 applies if applicable to employer.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, TDI, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**:

- No state mandate for private employers to provide paid sick leave.
- Employers may offer voluntarily; if offered, terms and accrual must comply with agreement.

**Hawaii Family Leave Law (HFLL)** (HRS §378-71a, verify exact section):

- Employers with 100+ employees must provide up to 4 weeks unpaid leave in any calendar year for qualifying family/medical reasons.
- **Qualifying reasons**: Birth of child, adoption, care of child/spouse/reciprocal beneficiary/parent with serious health condition, care of sibling/parent-in-law/grandparent/grandparent-in-law with serious health condition (verify complete list).
- **Eligibility**: 6+ consecutive months of employment with employer; verify if non-consecutive months count.
- **Job restoration**: Employee must be restored to same or equivalent position upon return.
- **Substitution of paid leave**: Employer may allow (or require, verify) employee to substitute accrued paid leave (vacation, sick leave) for unpaid leave.

**Federal FMLA**:

- 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave per 12-month period for covered employers (50+ employees) and eligible employees (12+ months tenure, 1,250 hours prior 12 months).
- Hawaii employers with 50-99 employees covered by federal FMLA only; 100+ may have both HFLL and FMLA obligations.

**Temporary Disability Insurance (TDI)** (HRS ch. 392):

- **Coverage**: Non-work-related illness, injury, or pregnancy; employers required to provide wage-replacement coverage.
- **Eligibility**: 14 weeks employment (non-consecutive, multiple employers permitted), 20+ hours/week, $400+ earned in prior 52 weeks; benefits available within 14 days of separation.
- **Benefit amount**: Partial wage replacement (approximately 50% of weekly wage, up to statutory maximum; verify exact percentage and cap for 2026).
- **Cost sharing**: Employer pays entire cost or shares equally with employee (employee share capped at 0.5% of weekly wages).
- **Excluded employees**: Federal government employees, some domestic workers, insurance agents/real estate sales (commission-only), workers under 18 in newspaper delivery, family employees, student nurses, interns (verify complete exclusion list).

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- If employee takes HFLL, TDI, or FMLA leave and returns to different pay or role, platform must ensure reinstatement to same or equivalent position; verify whether salary must match pre-leave amount if employee promoted during leave.
- TDI and FMLA leave are separate entitlements and do not run concurrently unless employer policy permits.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll records retention** (HRS §388-4):

- Employers must maintain payroll records for at least 6 years showing: employee name, address, occupation, rate(s) of pay and basis (hourly, salary, piece, commission, etc.), date of hire/termination, hours worked (rounded to nearest 15 minutes).

**Personnel records retention and access**:

- **Employment referral records**: 1 year (verify whether this applies to all personnel records or only recruitment-related).
- **Medical records**: Retain separately from personnel file for at least 3 years following termination (verify if state law mandates this or if OSHA/ADA federal law applies).
- **General personnel records**: No maximum retention period specified; employers may retain indefinitely; verify best practice and legal hold obligations.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- Hawaii law is not explicit on employee right to access own personnel file; **verify** whether state statute grants this right or if FMLA/ADA federal rights apply.
- Government employees (state) have access rights under HRS §89-16.5 (exclusive representatives and union access); private sector access rights unclear.

**Record format**:

- No Hawaii statute restricts format (paper vs. electronic); employers may maintain electronically if retention and security requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Hawaii privacy law and employee data**:

- Hawaii Revised Statutes Chapter 487N addresses data breach notification and personal information security (primarily consumer data; **verify** whether employee data specifically covered).
- No comprehensive Hawaiian state employee privacy statute comparable to state consumer privacy laws; federal law applies (FCRA, ADA, HIPAA where applicable).

**Biometric data** (HRS ch. 487N, effective 7/1/26):

- Biometric data (fingerprint, voice print, retina/iris scan, or other unique physical representation) treated as sensitive personal information in breach notification; breaches must be treated with same urgency as Social Security number breaches (effective 7/1/26).
- No standalone biometric consent statute; FCRA and common-law consent principles apply.
- Employers may collect biometric data for timekeeping/identification if disclosed to employees; **verify** whether explicit written consent required under Hawaii law or whether notice + opt-out accepted.

**Employee monitoring**:

- No Hawaii statute explicitly banning workplace video/audio monitoring; employers must disclose monitoring and obtain written consent for use of recording devices in workplace (verify administrative rule authority).
- Recording employees without consent may violate wiretapping or expectation-of-privacy principles; **verify** scope.

**Breach notification** (HRS §487N-1 et seq.):

- Employers must notify affected individuals "without unreasonable delay" if personal information is accessed, acquired, or used by unauthorized person; verify whether employee records trigger this.
- Notification must describe breach, date, types of personal information affected, steps taken to mitigate, and contact for more information.

**Data residency or processing constraints**:

- No Hawaii law mandates data residency for payroll or personnel records; employers may process/store out-of-state if security/privacy laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- Hawaii does not have an explicit statewide ban-the-box statute for private employers; **verify** whether HRS §378 or local ordinances impose restrictions.
- Government/state employment: State employer may not disqualify based solely on prior conviction; arrests without conviction or erased convictions cannot be used/disseminated (verify exact state provisions).
- Some Hawaii municipalities (e.g., Honolulu) may have local ban-the-box ordinances; employers in those jurisdictions must comply locally.

**Background checks**:

- Employers using third-party consumer reporting agencies must comply with federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA): provide written notice, obtain written consent, and follow adverse-action procedures.
- Hawaii consumer reporting statute supplements FCRA (verify specific state requirements beyond federal).
- Criminal convictions: No state-specific lookback limit; convictions may be reported indefinitely under FCRA §605(a)(5); arrests/non-conviction info may not be reported if over 7 years old.

**Drug testing**:

- No Hawaii statute regulates private-sector drug testing; federal law governs (DOT rules for transportation).
- Employers should verify local ordinances in jurisdictions where employees work.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No Hawaii state mandate for private-sector E-Verify; federal I-9 requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice and severance (Hawaii Dislocated Workers Act, HRS §394B)**:

- Employers with 50+ employees in preceding 12 months must provide 60 days' advance written notice of divestiture, partial closing, closing, or relocation to affected employees and Hawaii Department of Labor & Industrial Relations.
- **Severance requirement**: Employers must pay severance; verify exact calculation (lump-sum vs. period pay, formula based on tenure).
- **Notice content**: Verify what must be disclosed (reason, effective date, support services, WARN Act language if applicable).
- **Failure to provide notice**: Employer liable for back pay and benefits for missing days (up to 60 days).
- **Federal WARN Act** (60 days' notice for employers 100+ employees, mass layoff 50+ affected, plant closing 50+ affected) also applies; state law does not preempt federal.

**Severance agreement**: If offered, must be in writing; verify whether Hawaii law imposes any restrictions on severance condition (e.g., non-disparagement, release of claims).

**Non-compete enforceability** (common law, no comprehensive statute):

- Restrictive covenants are enforceable in Hawaii if reasonable in duration, geographic scope, and type of work/business restricted.
- **Legitimate business interests**: Protection of trade secrets, customer contacts/lists, confidential information, substantial relationships with prospective/existing clients.
- **Technology worker exception** (Act 158, 2015): Non-competes with employees of technology businesses are void; **verify** exact definition of "technology business" and "employee."
- **Post-employment covenants** (Prudential Locations v. Gagnon, 2022 Hawaii Supreme Court): Void unless employer can show very clear business protective purpose (e.g., guarding close customer contacts or proprietary/confidential information); verify whether pre-employment covenants subject to same standard.
- **No statutory waiting period or notice requirement** for presenting non-compete at hire (contrast with Connecticut's 10-business-day rule).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- Non-solicitation of employees or customers is enforceable under same reasonableness standard as non-competes.
- **Proof of violation**: Requires showing employee engaged in "active initiation of contact" with customer/employee of former employer; passive response to customer inquiry may not suffice.

**Non-disclosure and trade secret protection**:

- Trade secret theft and misappropriation claims enforceable under common law and Hawaii Uniform Trade Secrets Act (verify statute number).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification (ABC test)**:

- Hawaii applies ABC test for unemployment insurance, workers' compensation, and wage-and-hour classification (enforced by Hawaii Department of Labor & Industrial Relations and Joint Enforcement Commission on Employee Misclassification).
- All three factors must be met:
  - **(A)** Freedom from direction/control in performing services, both contractually and in fact.
  - **(B)** Service performed outside usual course of employer's business or outside all employer's locations.
  - **(C)** Individual customarily engaged in independently established trade/occupation/profession of same nature.
- Misclassification enforcement: Department of Labor, Attorney General, Director of Taxation, Workers' Compensation Division.

**Remote workers**:

- No Hawaii statute specifically addresses remote work; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee is remote but performs work in Hawaii or works for Hawaii employer, Hawaii law applies.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No explicit reciprocity doctrine in Hawaii; if employee works across state lines, each state may apply its laws to work performed in its state.
- Platform should flag when workforce change affects employees in multiple states to ensure compliance with each state's rules.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

**Required typed obligations and timing constraints**:

1. **Minimum wage compliance**: Track employee location; apply Hawaii $16/hr minimum (2026) or $18/hr (2028) when employee located in Hawaii; flag if wage change would drop below minimum.

2. **Overtime accrual**: Model 40-hour-per-week threshold; calculate 1.5x for hours over 40; no daily overtime trigger.

3. **Pay transparency**: Before posting job in Hawaii, require entry of reasonably expected wage/salary range; gate posting if 50+ employee count and range missing; timestamp disclosure.

4. **Final pay on discharge**: Trigger payment obligation at time of discharge (next working day latest); calculate accrued leave (verify which types of leave accrue and vest); same-day or next-day payment is legal obligation, not discretionary.

5. **Semi-monthly pay requirement**: Flag if pay frequency set to less frequent than semi-monthly; enforce at least two pay periods per month.

6. **HFLL eligibility**: Gate 4-week leave entitlement to employees in Hawaii with 100+ employer headcount, 6+ consecutive months tenure; track calendar-year usage (not rolling 12-month).

7. **TDI coverage**: Model eligibility (14 weeks employment, 20+ hours/week, $400+ earned in prior 52 weeks); available within 14 days of separation; if employer provides TDI, track benefit calculation and cost-sharing (capped at 0.5% employee contribution).

8. **Mini-WARN trigger**: If 50+ employees laid off in a transaction, flag 60-day notice requirement, severance calculation obligation, and reporting to Hawaii Department of Labor & Industrial Relations.

9. **Wage payment timing**: Flag as non-compliance if employer delays final pay beyond discharge day (or next working day if immediate payment impossible); track actual payment date.

10. **Non-compete/non-solicit**: Store contract date, business protective purpose (trade secret, customer list, confidential info), duration, geography, scope; flag technology worker exemption if applicable; post-employment covenants require clear protective purpose.

11. **Payroll record retention**: Retain payroll records for 6 years; personnel referral records 1 year (verify other categories).

12. **Pay statement delivery**: Provide itemized pay stub each payday showing gross, deductions, net, hours worked (rounded to 15-min increments).

13. **Wage theft penalties**: On underpayment or deduction violation, calculate penalties ($500+ per violation plus back pay, 6% interest, attorney's fees); notify of liability.

**Field restrictions and validations**:

- **Wage field**: Warn if entry drops below $16.00/hr (2026) or $18.00/hr (2028).
- **Pay frequency**: Enforce semi-monthly minimum; block weekly if not explicitly authorized.
- **Termination workflow**: Require final pay date and amount; block discharge without calculating final pay.
- **Job posting**: Require wage range entry before publishing if employer has 50+ employees.
- **Non-compete clause**: Require business protective purpose statement and review flag for tech-worker check.
- **Termination reason**: Prompt for protected-class flags (discharge for workers' comp claim, wage claim filing, jury duty) and block if detected without legal review.

- Clarify interaction of HFLL (calendar-year) with FMLA (12-month rolling); can employee take 4 weeks HFLL + 12 weeks FMLA in overlapping period?
- Verify whether reinstatement after HFLL/FMLA/TDI leave must occur at exact same salary if employee promoted/demoted during leave.
