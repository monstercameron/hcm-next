# West Virginia Employment Law Research (2026-09-03)

**State:** West Virginia | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment dominates**: West Virginia recognizes at-will employment by default; either party may terminate without cause unless a written contract specifies otherwise (no explicit statute, common-law default).
- **Minimum wage for 6+ employees**: $8.75/hour for employers with six or more non-exempt employees (W. Va. Code § 21-5C-2, effective 2016); employers with fewer than six employees follow federal FLSA minimum of $7.25/hour.
- **Overtime triggered at 40 hours/week**: Non-exempt employees earn 1.5× regular rate for all hours over 40 in a workweek (§ 21-5C-3); no daily overtime trigger.
- **Semi-monthly pay requirement**: Employers must pay wages at least twice per month with no more than 19 days between paydays (§ 21-5-3); final pay must be due on or before the next regular payday (§ 21-5-4).
- **Pay-rate-change notice is critical**: Employers must notify employees in writing "prior to the time of such changes" for any pay-rate or wage-arrangement change; Labor Department guidance states at least 1 full pay period advance notice is expected (§ 21-5-9(4)); failure to provide advance notice triggers mandatory reimbursement of reduction amount.
- **Liquidated damages for wage theft**: Employers who fail to pay wages as required are liable to the employee for 2× the unpaid amount in liquidated damages (§ 21-5-4(e)).
- **Records retention: 2 years minimum**: Employer must retain employee name, address, rate of pay, hours, payroll deductions, and pay-period amounts for at least 2 years (§ 21-5C-5).
- **Equal pay protection (sex-based)**: W. Va. Code § 21-5B prohibits sex-based wage discrimination for work of comparable character requiring comparable skills; affirmative defenses include seniority systems, merit systems, and factors other than sex.
- **Human Rights Act protections**: W. Va. Code § 16B-17-1 et seq. (recodified 2024 from former § 5-11) prohibits employment discrimination based on race, color, religion, sex, national origin, ancestry, age, blindness, or disability; applies to employers with 12+ employees.
- **No state paid sick or family leave mandate**: Federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid) applies to covered employers; West Virginia imposes no state-level paid-leave requirement for private-sector employees.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

West Virginia is an at-will employment state under common law. No specific statute codifies at-will employment, but it is the default presumption. Absent a written contract specifying a term of employment, either employer or employee may terminate the relationship at any time, for any reason, or for no reason, **except** termination cannot be based on:

- Illegal discrimination (race, color, sex, national origin, religion, age 40+, disability, genetic information under Title VII, ADEA, ADA, GINA, or West Virginia Human Rights Act § 16B-17)
- Public policy (jury duty, witness duty, voting, military service, reporting safety violations)
- Retaliation (reporting wage violations, unsafe working conditions, or filing workers' compensation claims)

**Written Notice Requirements at Hire and on Change**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5-9(1) requires employers to notify employees in writing **at the time of hiring** of the rate of pay, day, hour, and place of payment.

W. Va. Code § 21-5-9(2) requires employers to notify employees in writing or via posted notice of any changes to pay arrangements **prior to the time of such changes**.

W. Va. Code § 21-5-9(4) specifies that when an employer changes an employee's rate of pay, pay period, place or method of payment, time of payment, or any other term of employment, the employer shall furnish written notice to the affected employee **at least 1 full pay period prior to the effective date of the change**. Violation results in reimbursement to the employee of the reduction amount on the initial paycheck.

**No At-Will Statute Requiring Posting**: West Virginia law does not require employers to post an at-will employment notice, though best practice is to include at-will status in the offer letter or employee handbook.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**:

- Employers with **six or more non-exempt employees**: $8.75/hour (W. Va. Code § 21-5C-2, effective January 1, 2016).
- Employers with **fewer than six employees**: Federal FLSA minimum of $7.25/hour applies.
- Tipped employees: Tipped minimum wage follows federal law ($2.13/hour base); employer must ensure tips plus base wage meet or exceed the applicable minimum ($8.75 for employers with 6+, $7.25 for smaller employers).

**Overtime**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5C-3 requires all non-exempt employees to receive overtime at **1.5× the regular rate** for all hours worked **over 40 in a workweek**. Overtime cannot be waived by agreement. No daily overtime trigger exists (unlike some states). Tipped employees' overtime must be calculated based on the full minimum wage, not the reduced tipped wage.

**Pay Frequency**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5-3 mandates that employers pay wages **at least semi-monthly** (twice per month) with no more than 19 calendar days between paydays. Employers may not adopt longer cycles (e.g., monthly pay for non-exempt workers). Dates must be fixed and regular.

**Final Paycheck on Termination or Resignation**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5-4 requires all earned wages to be paid in full to a separated employee **on or before the next regular payday** on which wages would otherwise be due and payable. For employees laid off, final wages are due on the next regular payday. This includes all regular wages through the termination date, overtime, accrued vacation (if employer policy mandates payout), and any bonuses or incentive compensation earned.

**Liquidated Damages for Non-Payment**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5-4(e) imposes strict liability: if an employer fails to pay an employee wages as required, the employer is liable to the employee for **two times (2×) the unpaid amount** in liquidated damages, plus potential additional penalties.

**Permissible Deductions**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5-3(a) permits deductions only for:

- Taxes (federal, state, local income tax)
- Social Security and Medicare (FICA)
- Court-ordered deductions (child support, wage garnishment)
- Written employee authorization (health-insurance contributions, 401(k), union dues, voluntary deductions)

Prohibited deductions include uniforms, tools, equipment, cash shortages, damage, breakage, parking, meals, and training clawbacks unless explicitly authorized in writing and not reducing pay below minimum wage.

**Pay Statements**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5-9(3) requires employers to make available to employees in writing or via posted notice employment practices and policies regarding vacation pay, sick leave, and comparable matters. Federal FLSA pay-stub standards apply (gross, deductions, net).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: West Virginia has **no statute banning salary-history inquiries**. Employers are permitted to ask applicants about prior compensation during the hiring process.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: West Virginia has **no state law requiring salary ranges in job postings** or on request. Federal law does not mandate this; it remains employer discretion.

**Equal Pay Law (Sex-Based)**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5B-3 prohibits employers from discriminating between the sexes in the payment of wages for work of comparable character requiring comparable skills. Payment must not be based on the employee's sex; wage differentials are permissible only if based on:

- Seniority or merit systems (not discriminatory on sex basis)
- Factors other than sex (e.g., education, licenses, experience level, geographic location)

No employee may be reduced in wages to eliminate existing wage discrimination. Violations are enforceable through civil action; remedies include back pay, liquidated damages, and attorney fees.

**Protected Classes Under Human Rights Act**:

W. Va. Code § 16B-17-1 et seq. (recodified 2024 from former Chapter 5, Article 11) protects employees from discrimination and retaliation based on:

- Race and color
- Religion
- Sex (including pregnancy and sexual harassment; verify current case law on gender identity)
- National origin and ancestry
- Age (typically 40 and over, consistent with federal ADEA)
- Disability (physical or mental, with reasonable accommodation requirement)
- Blindness

The statute applies to employers with **12 or more employees**. Enforced by the West Virginia Human Rights Commission (now administratively placed under the Office of Inspector General under the Department of Health as of 2024 reorganization).

**Pay Data Reporting**: West Virginia has no state-level requirement for employers to report pay-equity data to the state. Federal EEO-1 reporting applies to employers with 100+ employees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

**No State Paid Sick Leave or Paid Family Leave Mandate**: West Virginia does **not** require private-sector employers to provide paid sick leave or paid family leave. Employers may provide these benefits voluntarily, but they are not statutorily mandated.

**Federal Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)**:

The federal FMLA (29 U.S.C. § 2601 et seq.) applies to employers in West Virginia with 50 or more employees within 75 miles. Eligible employees receive up to 12 weeks unpaid, job-protected leave in a 12-month period for serious health conditions, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver leave, or childbirth/adoption. Employers must maintain health-insurance benefits during FMLA leave.

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional under West Virginia law. Employers are **not required** to observe or pay for any specific holiday. If an employer provides holiday pay or PTO, that pay must comply with wage-and-hour rules and cannot be deducted without authorization.

**Vacation and Accrual Payout**: Accrued vacation is governed by employment contract or employer policy, not by state statute. **West Virginia does not mandate payout of accrued vacation on termination** (unlike California or Illinois). Employers may forfeit unused vacation if the employment contract or handbook explicitly permits forfeiture and the policy is in writing and communicated to the employee before hire. However, if an employer voluntarily provides vacation and the contract states "vacation is paid on termination," that promise is enforceable as a contract term.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**:

When an employee is promoted or experiences a base-pay change:

- Existing leave balances (if any) should be **preserved** in the employee record unless the employment contract specifies forfeiture.
- Any promised vacation payout on termination remains enforceable at promotion.
- Accrual rates may be adjusted with advance written notice (ideally 30 days before effective date).
- FMLA leave eligibility (if employer is covered) continues through role/pay changes.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**:

W. Va. Code § 21-5C-5 requires employers to retain for **at least two years**:

- Employee name and address
- Rate of pay
- Hours of employment
- Payroll deductions
- Amount paid per pay period

Records must be kept at the place of employment and made available to the West Virginia Commissioner of Labor or authorized representative for inspection.

**Employee Access to Personnel Files**:

**Correction (2026-09-03 contradiction closure):** W. Va. Code § 21-3-22 is part of the state's public-improvement construction-safety program (Article 3, workplace-safety enforcement); it is not a personnel-records statute and does not grant a private-sector personnel-file inspection right. West Virginia has **no statute requiring private employers to give employees access to their personnel files**; only public-sector employees have file-access rights, under agency-specific administrative rule (e.g., W. Va. Code St. R. § 143-1-19), not a labor-code provision that reaches private employment. Absent a written employer policy or collective bargaining agreement granting access, a private-sector West Virginia employee has no statutory right to inspect or copy their personnel file.

**Record Format**: West Virginia law does not restrict records to paper format. Employers may maintain records digitally, provided they remain accessible, authentic, and non-alterable. Digital records must be retained for the full retention period (2 years).

**Separation Records**: Separation notices (e.g., for unemployment-insurance purposes) should be retained as part of the 2-year payroll-retention cycle.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: West Virginia has no comprehensive state data-privacy statute equivalent to CCPA or GDPR. However, breach notification is required.

**Breach Notification**:

W. Va. Code § 46A-2A-102 requires any individual or entity that owns or licenses computerized data containing personal information to notify affected residents "without unreasonable delay" following discovery or notification of a breach that exposes unencrypted and unredacted personal information (Social Security number, driver's license number, or financial account number) and causes or reasonably is believed to cause identity theft or fraud. If the breach affects more than 1,000 West Virginia residents, notification to the three major credit-reporting agencies is also required.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: West Virginia has no specific statute regulating employee biometric collection (fingerprints, iris scans, facial recognition) or electronic monitoring (keystroke logging, location tracking, email monitoring). Federal law (Electronic Communications Privacy Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2511) and West Virginia common law on privacy tort apply. Best practice: Obtain written consent for biometric collection and monitoring.

**Employee Data Confidentiality**: West Virginia imposes no employee data residency or cross-border processing restrictions; ordinary commercial data-protection practices and federal law apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: West Virginia has **no ban-the-box law**. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history on applications, during interviews, or via background check at any time in the hiring process.

**Background Checks**: Permitted without state-specific restrictions beyond the federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA). Employers may obtain criminal history, civil records, education, identity, and employment-verification checks with written consent. The FCRA applies if a third-party consumer reporting agency is used.

**Drug and Alcohol Testing**:

W. Va. Code § 21-3E (Safer Workplace Act, effective July 7, 2017) authorizes employers to test employees and job applicants for drugs and alcohol as a condition of employment or hiring, provided:

- A written drug-free workplace program exists (notice, education, procedural requirements)
- Testing is conducted by certified labs and samples are collected under reasonable and sanitary conditions
- Observers of urine-sample collection are of the same sex as the employee
- Pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, random, post-accident, and return-to-work testing are all permitted

Employers may not discriminate against registered medical-marijuana users solely based on that status.

**E-Verify**: West Virginia has **no state-mandated E-Verify enrollment requirement** for private employers. Federal E-Verify is required for federal contractors and certain federal-fund recipients. W. Va. Code § 21-1B-4 requires employers to maintain I-9 documentation and records of legal employment status; verification must occur prior to hire or before entering into an employment contract. Records must be retained for at least 2 years after separation (note: this may conflict with federal I-9 retention requirements; verify with federal guidance).

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Non-Competes

**Federal WARN Act**: The federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires **60 days' written advance notice** for mass layoffs from covered employers meeting the federal employer-size and layoff-size thresholds (see us-federal.md; do not restate the figures here). West Virginia has **no supplementary state mini-WARN law**.

**Severance Pay**: West Virginia imposes **no mandatory severance requirement**. Severance is entirely a matter of employment contract, written policy, or employer discretion. If offered, severance is subject to wage-and-hour rules (timely payment, no non-authorized deductions).

**Notice to Employee on Termination**: West Virginia law imposes **no statutory advance notice period** for at-will termination. Employers may separate workers immediately unless a written contract specifies notice or the employer handbook creates an enforceable implied contract.

**Non-Compete and Non-Solicit Agreements**:

West Virginia permits non-compete and non-solicit covenants under **common law** (no comprehensive statute governs them for most professions). Enforceability is determined case-by-case based on reasonableness of:

- **Scope**: Must protect a legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential information, customer goodwill, customer relationships).
- **Geography**: Limited to the area where the business operates or where the employee worked.
- **Duration**: 1 year is typically presumed reasonable; 2–3 years often enforceable depending on industry and business justification; 5+ years are scrutinized (verify current case law).
- **Consideration**: Must be supported by employment at hire (consideration is the job itself) or independent consideration (promotion, bonus, raise, stock options) if imposed after hire.

West Virginia courts may "blue pencil" (reform) overly broad covenants to make them enforceable or strike them entirely. Verify current case law as of 2026-09-03 for enforceability thresholds.

**Physician Non-Competes**:

W. Va. Code § 47-11E-2 (Physicians Freedom of Practice Act, 2017) limits non-compete covenants for physicians to:

- **Duration**: One (1) year from termination of employment
- **Geographic Scope**: Thirty (30) road miles from the physician's primary place of practice with the employer

A non-compete covenant is **void and unenforceable upon termination of employment by the employer**. The statute does not limit covenants prohibiting the physician from taking patient lists, records, or employer property, or provisions requiring repayment of relocation/practice-establishment remuneration.

**Separation Notice for Unemployment Insurance**:

Employers must file a separation notice (e.g., UK-ES form) with the West Virginia Department of Unemployment Insurance within the required timeframe (typically within 10 days of separation). Include the separation reason (voluntary resignation, reduction in force, misconduct, etc.), which affects the employee's unemployment-insurance eligibility.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: West Virginia employment law (unemployment-insurance law) defines "employee" based on the true nature of the working relationship, **not** on contract labels or parties' intent. Factors include control over work methods/schedules, provision of tools/equipment, integration into the business, permanence of relationship, and whether the worker sets own rates/terms. Misclassification triggers unemployment-insurance, wage, and tax liabilities.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: West Virginia has no specific rules governing remote workers or multi-state employment. Employers must comply with all applicable state laws where work is performed. West Virginia income-tax withholding applies to employees working in West Virginia; multi-state employers must ensure compliance with wage-and-hour, non-compete, and discrimination laws of each jurisdiction.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

When HCM Next processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in West Virginia:

### Promotion + Base-Pay Change Workflow

**At-Will Status and Contract Terms**:

- Record at-will status (default for most West Virginia workers); flag if a written employment agreement exists specifying term, severance, notice, or non-compete.
- Document the effective date of promotion and pay change, ideally aligned with a regular payroll date.

**Pay-Rate-Change Notice (Critical)**:

- **Enforce § 21-5-9(4)**: Notify the employee in writing of any pay-rate or wage-arrangement change **at least 1 full pay period prior to the effective date**. Failure triggers mandatory reimbursement of the reduction amount to the employee.
- Document the notification date and content in the employment record.
- If pay frequency is changing, ensure notice is given at least 1 pay cycle in advance (ideally 30 days).

**Pay Frequency Compliance**:

- **Enforce § 21-5-3 semi-monthly requirement**: Ensure the new pay schedule pays wages at least twice per calendar month with no more than 19 days between paydays. Flag as **non-compliant** if employer intends monthly pay for non-exempt workers.
- Confirm wages are paid on or before each scheduled payday.

**Overtime and Wage Calculation**:

- Compute overtime at **1.5× regular rate** for all hours over 40 in a workweek (§ 21-5C-3).
- Include any overtime and bonuses in the wage calculation for the promotion or raise.

**Equal Pay and Wage Equity (§ 21-5B)**:

- Capture comparable roles, skills, responsibilities, and pay for employees of the same sex in similar positions.
- When a promotion involves a pay raise, audit whether the raise aligns with historical patterns for that role and gender.
- Maintain an **audit trail** of pay-change justifications (e.g., "promotion to lead role," "market adjustment," "performance-based").
- Flag if the employer has multiple job levels; build a **pay-band matrix** by role and experience to ensure systematic, non-discriminatory pay decisions.

**Human Rights Act Compliance (§ 16B-17)**:

- If the employer has 12+ employees, verify compliance with protected-class discrimination prohibitions (race, color, sex, national origin, religion, age, disability, blindness, ancestry).
- Ensure pay changes do not discriminate based on protected status.

**Record Retention (§ 21-5C-5)**:

- Retain all wage and separation records for **at least 2 years**: promotion date, old pay, new pay, effective date, overtime calculations, final-pay records, and all supporting documentation.
- Implement automated archival in the platform backend to mark records as "archive-eligible" after 2 years.

**Leave Balance Handling**:

- If the employee has accrued PTO, vacation, or sick leave, record the balance at the time of promotion in the employee profile.
- **Preserve the balance** unless the employment contract explicitly permits forfeiture.
- If the employer mandates vacation payout on separation (per policy or contract), calculate payout and include in final pay.
- Flag if the employer has no written leave policy, since this creates compliance ambiguity at separation.

### Manager Change Workflow

**No Special Legal Requirements**: West Virginia law does not impose specific restrictions on manager changes. When an employee's manager changes:

- Update the employee's direct-report relationship and organizational hierarchy.
- Ensure the employee is notified of the new manager and any changes to reporting expectations.
- Carry forward all leave balances, accrued compensation, and employment terms to the new manager's team; no reset or forfeiture is permitted.
- Ensure the new manager is briefed on the employee's compensation, non-compete, and employment-agreement terms.
- Do not use a manager change as a pretext for discrimination or retaliation; such actions may expose the employer to wrongful-termination claims.

### Separation Workflow

**At-Will Termination**:

- **Document termination rationale** (performance, reduction in force, misconduct, voluntary resignation) for internal compliance and to defend against wrongful-termination or discrimination claims.
- **No advance written notice is required** unless the employment contract specifies a notice period or federal WARN applies.
- **Immediate separation is permissible** for at-will employees absent contractual obligation.

**Final Pay (§ 21-5-4)**:

- **Compute final wages** (all earned wages through termination date):
  - Regular wages for hours worked
  - Overtime at 1.5× for hours over 40 in final workweek
  - Accrued vacation payout (if employer policy mandates; not required by law but check handbook)
  - Any earned bonus or incentive compensation
- **Ensure payment on or before the next regular payday** per § 21-5-4. If employer delays beyond next regular payday, flag as **potential violation** and liable for 2× damages.
- **Flag any non-compliant deductions** (uniforms, shortages, damage, etc.); only tax-mandated or written-authorized deductions are permitted.

**WARN Act Compliance**:

- If the employer and the layoff meet the federal WARN thresholds (see us-federal.md), **federal WARN applies**.
- Provide **60 days' written advance notice** to affected employees, unions, and state/local workforce agencies.
- No West Virginia supplementary state mini-WARN requirement exists.

**Non-Compete and Non-Solicit Enforcement**:

- If the employer uses non-compete or non-solicit agreements, attach the agreement to the employment record and **flag for legal review before separation**.
- For non-physicians: verify reasonableness of scope, geography, and duration against current West Virginia case law before enforcement.
- For physicians (§ 47-11E-2): non-competes are **void and unenforceable upon termination by employer**; limit enforcement to 1 year/30 miles if employee terminates voluntarily.
- **Consult employment counsel** before enforcing a non-compete; overly broad agreements may be struck or reformed by courts.

**Separation Notice and Unemployment Insurance**:

- File a separation notice with the West Virginia Department of Unemployment Insurance within 10 days of separation.
- Include separation reason (voluntary resignation, reduction in force, misconduct, etc.).
- Keep notice on file with employment record for at least 2 years.

**Severance Pay (if offered)**:

- If employer provides severance, calculate and pay according to company policy or offer (e.g., "1 week per year of service").
- Ensure severance is paid timely (on or before next regular payday) and that no non-authorized deductions are applied.
- Document severance in a separation letter or severance agreement; flag any conditions (non-disparagement, non-solicit, non-compete waiver, ADEA release).

### Termination Data Model (P1A/P1B Fields and Constraints)

**Employee Base Data**:

- **Employee.AtWillStatus**: Boolean (default `true` for West Virginia); mark `false` if written employment contract specifies term or notice period.
- **Employee.EmploymentContractAttachment**: File or link to written employment agreement (if exists).
- **Employee.ContractEffectiveDate**: Date contract took effect.
- **Employee.ContractTermLength**: Duration of contract (e.g., "1 year," "indefinite term").

**Pay Change Data**:

- **PayChange.NoticeDate**: Date pay change was communicated to employee; must precede effective date by **at least 1 full pay period** per § 21-5-9(4).
- **PayChange.ChangeReason**: Enum (Promotion, Raise, Reclassification, Adjustment, Market Correction).
- **PayChange.OldRate** and **PayChange.NewRate**: Previous and new wages/salary.
- **PayChange.PayFrequencyCode**: Enum (Weekly, BiWeekly, SemiMonthly, Monthly); **enforce § 21-5-3 minimum of semi-monthly** (flag if Monthly selected).
- **PayChange.NewPayCycleStartDate**: First date of new pay schedule.
- **PayChange.OvertimeEligible**: Boolean; `true` for non-exempt employees (most of workforce).
- **PayChange.NoticeCompliance**: Boolean; set to `false` if notice is less than 1 full pay period; trigger compliance alert.

**Termination and Final Pay Data**:

- **Termination.TerminationDate**: Date employment ended.
- **Termination.TerminationReason**: Enum (Voluntary Resignation, Reduction in Force, Performance Termination, Misconduct, Retirement, Other); affects unemployment-insurance eligibility.
- **Termination.NoticeProvidedDate**: Date written notice (if any) was provided.
- **Termination.FinalPayDueDate**: Next regular payday (per § 21-5-4); flag if FinalPayAmount is not paid by this date.
- **Termination.FinalPayAmount**: Gross amount (before taxes):
  - Earned regular wages through TerminationDate
  - Unpaid overtime at 1.5× (hours over 40 in final workweek)
  - Accrued vacation payout (if employer policy mandates)
  - Any earned bonus or incentive compensation
- **Termination.Deductions**: Array of deductions applied; verify each is law-required or written-authorized; flag any non-compliant deductions.
- **Termination.NetFinalPayAmount**: FinalPayAmount minus authorized deductions.
- **Termination.PaymentMethod**: Enum (Check, Direct Deposit, Other).
- **Termination.LeaveBalanceForfeited**: Boolean; if true, record which types and on what legal basis (if any).

**Leave and Accrual Data**:

- **Employee.LeavePolicy**: Link to company handbook or written policy governing vacation, sick leave, PTO accrual, carryover, forfeiture.
- **Employee.AccruedVacationBalance**: Hours or days accrued as of promotion or termination date.
- **Employee.VacationPayoutOnTermination**: Boolean; does employer policy mandate vacation payout at separation?
- **PayChange.LeaveBalanceCarryforward**: Flag to ensure leave balances are preserved and updated following promotion or pay change.

**Equal Pay and Wage Equity Data (§ 21-5B)**:

- **PayChange.ComparableEmployees**: List of employees in same or similar role with same sex, showing their pay, experience, and job level; used for equal-pay audit.
- **PayChange.PayAdjustmentJustification**: Text field capturing business reason for pay change (e.g., "promotion to lead role," "market adjustment," "performance-based"); supports equal-pay defense.
- **PayChange.EqualPayAuditRequired**: Boolean; set to true if pay change is to a new or existing comparable role for employee of same sex; trigger HR review to confirm pay parity.

**Non-Compete and Restrictive Covenant Data**:

- **Employee.NonCompeteAttachment**: File link to signed non-compete agreement (if exists).
- **NonCompete.RestrictionScope**: Text (e.g., "Cannot work for competitors in software development within West Virginia").
- **NonCompete.RestrictionDuration**: Duration (e.g., "1 year from termination date").
- **NonCompete.RestrictionTerritory**: Geographic area (e.g., "West Virginia," "30-mile radius of primary work location").
- **NonCompete.PhysicianStatus**: Boolean; set to true if employee is a physician; if true, non-compete is **void upon termination by employer** (§ 47-11E-2).
- **NonCompete.FlagForLegalReview**: Boolean; set to true upon separation if non-compete exists; require legal counsel sign-off before enforcement.
- **NonCompete.EnforcementStatus**: Enum (Not Enforced, Under Review, Enforced, Waived).

**WARN and Mass-Separation Data**:

- **Termination.WARNApplicable**: Boolean; set to true if the employer and the layoff meet the federal WARN thresholds (see us-federal.md).
- **Termination.WARNNoticeIssuedDate**: Date 60-day WARN notice was issued (if applicable).
- **Termination.WARNNoticeRecipients**: List of recipients (employees, unions, state/local workforce agencies).
- **Termination.StateNoticeRequired**: Boolean; if yes, file separation notice with West Virginia Department of Unemployment Insurance within 10 days.

### Open Questions for Employer

1. Does the employer have a written semi-monthly pay schedule, and is it communicated to all employees?
2. What is the employer's policy on final pay: payment on termination day, next regular payday, or other?
3. Does the employer offer PTO/vacation, and if so, is accrued leave paid out on termination?
4. Is the employer subject to federal WARN (see us-federal.md for the thresholds)? If so, how is 60-day notice coordinated?
5. Does the employer use non-compete or non-solicit agreements? Have they been reviewed for West Virginia enforceability under current case law?
6. Has the employer reviewed pay equity by gender/race for comparable roles (§ 21-5B compliance)?
7. Does the employer have a written drug-free workplace policy compliant with the Safer Workplace Act (§ 21-3E)?
8. Does the employer maintain I-9 and employment-verification records per § 21-1B-4, and what is the retention protocol?
