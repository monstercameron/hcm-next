# Missouri Employment Law Research

**State:** Missouri | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment**: Missouri presumes at-will status; either party may terminate at any time for any lawful reason absent a written contract specifying otherwise.
- **Minimum wage**: $13.75/hour effective 2025 (RSMo § 290.502); increases to $15.00/hour January 1, 2026; no further annual indexing (HB 567 effective August 28, 2025 removed CPI adjustments).
- **Overtime**: Federal FLSA applies (1.5× over 40 hours/week); RSMo § 290.505 matches federal standard; amusement/recreation businesses excepted at 52 hours/week.
- **Pay frequency**: Semi-monthly (every 16 days) required for corporations; monthly permitted for executives, professionals, and commission-based employees (RSMo § 290.080).
- **Final pay on discharge**: Due immediately on day of discharge (RSMo § 290.110); if delayed 7+ days after written request, employer liable for continuing wages (capped 60 days) at original rate plus damages.
- **Pay-rate reduction**: 30-day written notice required before reduction effective; failure triggers $50 penalty per employee (RSMo § 290.100).
- **Equal pay**: RSMo § 290.410 prohibits sex-based wage discrimination for same quantity/quality of work; variations allowed for seniority, skill, shift, hours, or other non-sex factors applied in good faith.
- **No state paid-leave mandate**: Paid sick leave (Proposition A) repealed August 28, 2025 by HB 567; federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid, 50+ employees, 75-mile radius) applies to covered employers.
- **Human Rights Act**: RSMo Chapter 213 prohibits discrimination on race, color, religion, national origin, ancestry, sex, age (40+), disability (6+ employees, 180-day filing deadline with Missouri Commission on Human Rights).
- **Service letter statute**: RSMo § 290.140 (corporations 7+ employees, 90+ days tenure): employer must issue letter within 45 days of written request stating tenure, job duties/performance, and termination reason; failure to issue triggers damages; punitive damages if no letter issued at all.
- **Non-solicit safe harbor**: RSMo § 431.202 presumes reasonable any employee non-solicitation covenant ≤1 year protecting trade secrets, customer/supplier relationships, goodwill, or loyalty; durations >1 year evaluated case-by-case; non-competes governed by common-law reasonableness (no statutory safe harbor).
- **E-Verify for public contractors**: RSMo § 285.530 requires all public employers and contractors on state contracts >$5,000 to enroll in E-Verify and verify all employees hired post-enrollment; affirmative defense available if enrolled and compliant.
- **Data breach notification**: RSMo § 407.1500 requires notification "without unreasonable delay" when personal information breach occurs; no specific day count specified; applies to any entity collecting/maintaining Missouri resident data.
- **No mini-WARN law**: Federal WARN Act (60-day notice, 100+ employees, 50+ affected in 30 days) applies; no state add-on requirements.
- **No salary-history ban**: No Missouri law restricts inquiries about prior wages.
- **No pay-transparency mandate**: No state requirement for salary ranges in job postings or on applicant request.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

**At-Will Presumption**: Missouri law presumes employment is at-will absent a written contract specifying a definite term or probationary period. Either employer or employee may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any lawful reason, or for no reason at all, without cause or prior notice. The burden is on the employee to prove an oral or implied contract stating otherwise.

**Exceptions to At-Will**: Termination is prohibited when:

- Based on illegal discrimination (race, color, religion, national origin, ancestry, sex, age 40+, disability, per RSMo Chapter 213).
- Retaliatory termination for reporting statutory violations, jury duty, military service, or filing workers' compensation claims.
- Violation of a written employment contract or collective bargaining agreement specifying terms or cause.
- Violation of public policy (e.g., refusal to violate law, as established by Missouri Supreme Court case law).

**Written Notices at Hire and on Change**: No Missouri state law requires written notice to an employee at hire regarding at-will status, wage theft prevention, rights, duties, or any other employment term. Federal FLSA rules (29 U.S.C. § 206–214) govern wages and hours for covered employers; no Missouri-specific hire, onboarding, or notice form is mandated by state law.

**Pay-Rate Change Notices**: Advance written notice is required **only** when reducing wages: at least 30 days before reduction is to take effect (RSMo § 290.100). Wage increases do not require advance notice. Notice may be posted in a conspicuous workplace location or mailed individually.

**Separation Documentation**: When an employee is separated, the employer must file unemployment insurance documentation with the Missouri Department of Labor (DOL-8 Separation Notice) for administrative purposes. No statutory advance notice period to the worker is required for at-will terminations; however, employers should comply with any notice specified in an employment contract or employer policy. If the employer has 100+ employees and the termination is part of a mass layoff, federal WARN Act requirements (60-day notice) apply.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: RSMo § 290.502. Effective January 1, 2025, $13.75/hour. Effective January 1, 2026, $15.00/hour (the permanent rate unless future legislation changes it). HB 567 (signed mid-July 2025, effective August 28, 2025) repealed the Consumer Price Index (CPI) adjustment provision of Proposition A; no further automatic annual increases. Only future statutory changes or federal minimum wage increases will alter the state minimum. Applies to all covered employers in Missouri; no exemptions for small businesses.

**Overtime**: RSMo § 290.505 adopts the federal FLSA standard: 1.5× the employee's regular rate of wages for all hours over 40 in a workweek. Exempt categories (executive, administrative, professional, outside-sales) follow federal FLSA definitions (29 U.S.C. § 213). Amusement and recreation businesses may use a 52-hour weekly threshold under 29 U.S.C. § 213(a)(3). Overtime is calculated on a weekly basis; daily overtime (e.g., >8 hours/day) does not trigger overtime unless the week exceeds 40 hours. No "day of rest" or special weekend-hour multipliers apply under Missouri law.

**Pay Frequency**: RSMo § 290.080. Corporations and railroad operators doing business in Missouri must pay wages and salaries of employees **at least semi-monthly** (minimum every 16 calendar days after the close of each payroll period). Exceptions: Executives, administrative and professional employees (as defined under FLSA exemptions), salespeople, and commission-based employees may be paid **monthly** at the employer's discretion instead. Employers must furnish a **wage statement** at least once each calendar month showing the total amount of all deductions for the period, either attached to the wage payment voucher or sent separately. Violation of pay-frequency or statement requirements is a misdemeanor carrying a fine of $50–$500 per offense.

**Final Paycheck on Discharge or Resignation**: RSMo § 290.110. All unpaid wages of a discharged employee become "due and payable on the day of the discharge or refusal to longer employ." The statute applies to both involuntary discharge and voluntary quit. If an employee requests in writing that wages be sent to a specific station or office where the company maintains a regular agent, the employer must send payment (money or valid check) to that location. If payment does not arrive within **7 days** of the written request, the statute imposes a **continuing-wage penalty**: wages continue to accrue at the discharged employee's regular rate from the discharge date, **capped at 60 days maximum**. Exception: Commission-based employees (account collectors, merchandise-oversight roles) whose final amount requires an audit are excluded from the immediate-pay requirement. The statute does not mandate payment of accrued unused vacation, personal days, or sick time unless the employer's policy or employment contract explicitly provides for payout.

**Wage Theft Liability**: RSMo § 290.527. Any employer paying an employee less than the minimum wage or failing to pay overtime as required is liable to the affected employee for: (1) the full amount of unpaid wages or overtime, plus (2) an additional **treble-damages amount equal to twice the unpaid wages** as liquidated damages, plus (3) attorney's fees and costs. This treble-damages provision creates strong incentives for compliance and applies regardless of the employer's intent or good faith.

**Deductions and Pay Statements**: Federal FLSA rules govern permissible deductions; only deductions required by law (taxes, court orders), by valid written authorization, or by collective bargaining agreement are lawful. Overpayments may be recouped in the next paycheck if it does not reduce pay below the minimum wage. No Missouri-specific pay-statement content requirement beyond federal FLSA record-keeping (name, address, pay period, hours worked, rate, gross/net pay, deductions).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Missouri law restricts inquiries about prior salary history or restricts the use of salary history in pay-setting decisions. Employers may ask applicants and employees about prior wages at any stage of hiring or promotion. This contrasts with states like California, which ban salary-history inquiries; Missouri places no such restriction.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Missouri law requires employers to disclose salary ranges in job postings, applications, or in response to applicant or employee inquiries. Federal FLSA does not mandate pay transparency in recruitment. Thus, pay ranges are entirely optional and left to employer discretion.

**Equal Pay Law**: RSMo § 290.410 (Equal Pay for Equal Work Act, enacted 1963). Prohibits employers from paying any female employee "at wage rates less than the wage rates paid to male employees in the same establishment for the same quantity and quality of the same classification of work." This statute creates a **presumption of unlawful pay discrimination** when the same quantity and quality of work is shown. However, the employer may defend by proving pay differences are based on (1) seniority or length of service, (2) ability or skill differences, (3) differences in duties or responsibilities, (4) shift or time of day worked, (5) hours of work, (6) weight-lifting restrictions, or (7) "other reasonable differentiation" applied in good faith. The statute applies to all employers and all classifications of work; no minimum employer size. **Verify**: enforcement mechanism (private lawsuit vs. state agency), statute of limitations, and whether males may sue under the statute for lower pay than females (one-directional vs. bidirectional).

**Pay Equity Data Reporting**: No Missouri state law requires employers to report pay-equity data by race, gender, or other category to state or federal agencies. Federal EEO-1 reporting (EEOC) applies to employers with 100+ employees; this is a federal requirement, not a Missouri state mandate.

**Protected Classes and Human Rights Act**: RSMo Chapter 213 (Missouri Human Rights Act, MHRA) is the primary state employment-discrimination statute. Prohibits discrimination in employment based on:

- Race, color, religion, national origin, ancestry
- Sex (including pregnancy, childbirth, related medical conditions)
- Disability (physical or mental impairment substantially limiting a major life activity)
- Age (40 years and older)

**Coverage**: Applies to employers with **6 or more employees** working in Missouri. Public and private employers covered equally.

**Enforcement**: Enforced by the **Missouri Commission on Human Rights (MCHR)**, part of the Missouri Department of Labor and Industrial Relations.

**Filing Deadline**: An aggrieved employee or applicant must file a written complaint with MCHR within **180 days of the date of the alleged unlawful practice** (or the date the practice became known). Missing this deadline bars the complaint unless the employer failed to post required notices.

**Covered Conduct**: MHRA prohibits discrimination in all aspects of employment: hiring, firing, compensation, promotion, job assignment, training opportunities, and terms/conditions of employment. Retaliation for filing a complaint or participating in a proceeding is also unlawful.

**Remedies**: If MCHR finds reasonable cause, it may attempt conciliation. If unsuccessful, the matter may proceed to a full hearing before an administrative law judge, with potential awards for back pay, front pay, compensatory damages (emotional distress), and attorney's fees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Mandated Leave

**Paid Sick Leave Status**: **Proposition A (November 2024)** mandated earned paid sick leave (accrual of 1 hour per 30 hours worked, minimum 5 days/year, beginning May 1, 2025). However, **HB 567** (signed by Governor Kehoe in mid-July 2025, effective August 28, 2025) **completely repeals** the paid sick leave requirement. Employers are obligated to provide paid sick time only for the **window May 1, 2025 through August 27, 2025** (a 17-week period). After August 28, 2025, no Missouri state law requires paid sick leave. **Verify**: whether HB 567 requires employers to pay out accrued balances to employees on August 28, 2025, or whether accrued time vests as paid and unused time is forfeited.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave**: No Missouri state mandate for private-sector employees. Some employers offer voluntary plans; not required by law.

**Federal FMLA** (Family and Medical Leave Act): Applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75-mile radius, worked 1 year, 1,250+ hours). Provides up to **12 weeks unpaid leave per 12-month period** for qualifying reasons: serious health condition of employee or family member, childbirth/adoption, military exigency, military caregiver leave. Employers must maintain health-insurance benefits during FMLA leave and restore the employee to the same or equivalent position upon return. State employees (Missouri state government) have additional paid leave policies not applicable to private sector.

**Jury Duty and Military Service**: No specific Missouri statute on paid jury duty or military leave pay; federal law and common practice generally govern. Employers may not retaliate against employees for jury service or military service.

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional; no Missouri state mandate for holiday pay or paid time off (PTO). Employers set policies, including accrual rates, carryover rules, and payout-on-separation rules. Employers should communicate policies clearly at hire and on any changes.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: If an employee is promoted or receives a base-pay change, existing leave balances (PTO, holidays, paid sick time accrued under employer policy prior to August 28, 2025) are typically preserved under the "use-it-or-lose-it" or accrual rules of the employer policy. No state statute specifies mandatory carryover or payout upon promotion. Verify employer policy, employment contract, and CBA (if applicable) for specific rules on leave preservation and payout during role transitions.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: Employers must retain payroll records and documents related to wages for at least 3 years under federal FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 211). No Missouri statute specifies a longer retention period. The 3-year federal rule applies.

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**: No comprehensive Missouri personnel-file access statute. Employees may access their own records under common law and employer policy on request; the Missouri Sunshine Law (RSMo ch. 610) governs public bodies only and gives private-sector employees no access right; no timeline is codified. Private employers are not required to disclose internal evaluations or reference letters if confidentiality is preserved.

**Record Format**: No state requirement for paper vs. electronic. Employers may maintain records digitally, provided they remain accessible and authentic.

**Separation Records**: Retain separation notices and final pay documentation as part of payroll records (3-year federal minimum).

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Missouri has no comprehensive state data-privacy statute equivalent to California's CCPA, Colorado's CPA, or GDPR. RSMo § 407.1500 (data-breach notification) is the primary state-level privacy obligation affecting employers. Private data practices are otherwise governed by federal law (FCRA for background checks, HIPAA for health data, etc.), common law (tort of invasion of privacy), and ordinary commercial data-protection standards.

**Breach Notification**: RSMo § 407.1500 is Missouri's data-breach notification law. Key provisions:

- **Trigger**: A "breach of security" is defined as unauthorized access to and unauthorized acquisition of personal information maintained in computerized form that compromises the security, confidentiality, or integrity of the information.
- **Personal Information Covered**: Social Security number, driver's license/passport number, credit/debit card number, financial account information, date of birth, and any other information permitting identity theft or fraud.
- **Notification Requirement**: The person owning or licensing personal information of a Missouri resident, or conducting business in Missouri with data on residents, must notify affected individuals "without unreasonable delay" of the breach.
- **Timing Standard**: "Without unreasonable delay" is the statutory phrase; no specific number of days (e.g., 30 or 60) is mandated. Best practice: notify within 3 business days to avoid disputes over reasonableness.
- **Scope**: Applies to any entity (in-state or out-of-state) that collects or maintains data on Missouri residents.
- **Exception**: Notification is not required if, after investigation in consultation with law enforcement, the entity reasonably determines that identity-theft or fraud risk is not likely to occur as a result of the breach.
- **No Private Right of Action**: The statute does not explicitly grant individuals a private right of sue; enforcement appears to be by the Attorney General, but employers should still comply to avoid regulatory action.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Missouri-specific statute regulates employee biometric collection (fingerprints, iris scans, facial recognition for security or timekeeping), genetic testing, or electronic surveillance (keystroke logging, GPS tracking, location monitoring). Federal law (Title I of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act, 18 U.S.C. § 2511, which generally permits employers to monitor business systems) and Missouri common law on tort of invasion of privacy apply. Employers should obtain written consent before collecting sensitive biometric data and disclose monitoring practices in employee handbooks.

**Employee Data Residency and Processing**: No Missouri state-specific constraints on data location, cloud storage, or cross-border processing. Federal law (HIPAA for health data, FERPA for education records if applicable) and ordinary commercial data-protection practices (encryption, access controls) apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Missouri has **no ban-the-box law**. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history at any stage of hiring—on the application form, during interviews, or via background-check reports—without delay or restriction. This contrasts with states like California, Illinois, and others that delay criminal-history inquiries until a later stage of the hiring process. **Verify**: no recent legislative changes as of 2026-09-03.

**Background Checks**: Permitted and common. Employers may conduct criminal-history checks via:

- The Missouri State Highway Patrol's Criminal Justice Information Services Division (MACHS) with fingerprints or name-based search.
- Third-party consumer-reporting agencies (CRAs) subject to the Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA, 15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.), which requires written disclosure and authorization.
- Employment-screening firms.

Employers should disclose background-check practices in job postings or at the first contact with applicants and obtain written consent before running checks. Any adverse decision based on a background check must include notice and an opportunity to dispute under FCRA.

**Drug Testing**: No Missouri state law prohibits pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, random, or post-accident drug testing. Federal laws (Americans with Disabilities Act, FLSA, HIPAA) apply to the extent that testing does not constitute disability discrimination, violate employee privacy, or misclassify drug-use disorders as disabilities. Safety-sensitive positions (transportation, healthcare) may be tested per Department of Transportation and OSHA standards.

**E-Verify Mandate**: RSMo § 285.530 (Missouri's mandatory E-Verify law, part of the state's work-authorization compliance framework).

**Covered Employers**:

- **All public employers** (state, local, municipal agencies) must enroll and actively participate in federal E-Verify.
- **Private contractors on state contracts >$5,000** for services must also enroll and participate.
- **Private employers not on state contracts** are not statutorily required to use E-Verify, but federal I-9 reverification rules apply to all employers.

**Requirements**:

- Enrolled employers must verify employment eligibility of **every employee hired after enrollment** using E-Verify.
- Employers must retain a copy of the dated E-Verify verification report (Form I-797 or electronic receipt).
- Enrolled employers have an **affirmative defense** against federal work-authorization violations (safe harbor).

**Penalties**: Non-compliance by public employers or state contractors may trigger audit, contract termination, and loss of state contracts. No specific dollar penalty mentioned in the statute.

## 9. Separation: WARN Act, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**WARN Act**: Federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires covered employers (100+ employees at a single site of employment or aggregated across multiple sites) to provide **60 days' advance written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50 or more employees in a 30-day period. Notice must be given to affected workers, labor unions, the state Rapid Response Coordinator, and the chief elected official of the municipality where the closure/layoff occurs. Applies nationwide including Missouri. **Missouri has no state mini-WARN law**; the federal WARN Act is the only notification requirement. Failure to comply may result in back-pay liability and civil penalties.

**Severance Pay**: No Missouri state law mandates severance pay upon termination. Severance is entirely a matter of contract (individual employment agreement, offer letter) or employer discretion/policy. Employers who offer severance should document the terms (eligibility, calculation, payment timing, release-of-claims requirement) to avoid disputes.

**Notice Period for Termination**: No Missouri state law requires advance notice period before terminating an at-will employee. Immediate termination is permissible. However, employers should follow any notice period specified in an employment contract, offer letter, or employer handbook. Union employees are covered by collective bargaining agreements (CBAs), which often mandate notice or progressive discipline.

**Service Letter Statute**: RSMo § 290.140 (Dismissal Letter; corporations 7+ employees, tenure 90+ days).

- **Eligibility**: Applies to corporations doing business in Missouri with **7 or more employees** if the employee has been in service **at least 90 days**.
- **Request Process**: Employee must request the letter in writing by **certified mail** to the superintendent, manager, or registered agent of the company, with specific reference to RSMo § 290.140.
- **Timeline**: Employer must issue the letter within **45 days after receipt of the request**.
- **Content**: The letter must state: (1) the length of time the employee was employed, (2) the nature and character of service rendered (duties performed and whether employee performed them adequately/completed job requirements), and (3) the reason the employee was terminated or voluntarily quit.
- **Penalties for Non-Compliance**: (a) If the employer issues a letter but does so late (after 45 days), the employer is liable for compensatory damages. (b) If the employer completely fails to issue a letter after request, the employer may be liable for **nominal damages and punitive damages**. (c) Employees may sue in civil court; no state administrative process required.

**Non-Solicitation Agreements**: RSMo § 431.202 creates a statutory **"safe harbor"** for non-solicitation covenants. An employee non-solicitation covenant of **≤1 year** is **presumed reasonable** when designed to protect:

- Confidential or trade-secret business information,
- Customer or supplier relationships,
- Goodwill, or
- Company loyalty.

**Effect of Safe Harbor**: Any non-solicitation agreement meeting these criteria is enforceable; the burden shifts to the employee to prove it is unreasonable. Covenants exceeding 1 year duration are **not presumed reasonable** but may still be enforced if the employer proves reasonableness based on the specific facts and circumstances.

**Modification by Courts**: Missouri courts (per Whelan Security Co. v. Kennebrew and related precedent) may modify an unreasonable restriction—for example, by narrowing geographic scope or shortening duration—to bring it within reasonable limits and enforce it as modified (blue-pencil doctrine).

**Non-Compete Agreements**: **Not covered by RSMo § 431.202's statutory safe harbor.** Non-compete agreements between employers and employees are governed by **common-law reasonableness**, evaluated case-by-case. Enforceability depends on:

1. **Legitimate Protectable Interest**: Trade secrets, confidential business information, customer relationships, substantial relationships of personal trust.
2. **Reasonable Duration**: Courts scrutinize duration; overly long (e.g., 5+ years) agreements may be unreasonable.
3. **Reasonable Geographic Scope**: Restrictions must be geographically limited to areas where the business actually operates or has legitimate interests.
4. **Reasonable Scope of Prohibited Work**: Restrictions must be narrowly tailored to the legitimate business interest (not a blanket ban on all work in the industry).

**Enforcement Variability**: Missouri courts scrutinize non-competes closely and disfavor them. Overly broad agreements are frequently held unenforceable. **Verify**: current Missouri Supreme Court precedent on enforceability standards and any recent legislative changes.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Missouri's Department of Labor and Industrial Relations (MDLIR) applies a **modified IRS 20-factor test** for purposes of unemployment insurance (UI) tax and wage-hour compliance. Key factors evaluated:

1. **Right of Control** (primary factor): If the employer retains the right to control **how, when, and where** the work is performed, the worker is presumed an employee.
2. **Integration into Business**: If the work is integrated into the business operations, the worker is more likely an employee.
3. **Tools and Equipment**: Employer provision of tools and workspace suggests employee status.
4. **Payment Method**: Regular payment of wages (hourly, salary) suggests employee; project-based or fee-based payment suggests contractor.
5. **Duration**: Ongoing or indefinite engagement suggests employee; short-term project suggests contractor.
6. **Skill Level**: Specialized independent skill may suggest contractor; routine tasks suggest employee.

**Key Principle**: The worker's title (e.g., "independent contractor," "consultant") is not determinative. **The true underlying relationship governs.** If an employer exercises significant control over the worker's performance and the worker is economically dependent on the employer, misclassification as a contractor exposes the employer to:

- Back-wage claims and unpaid minimum wage/overtime liability.
- UI tax audits and penalties (back UI taxes plus penalties and interest).
- Potential wage-theft liability (RSMo § 290.527) including treble damages.
- ACA compliance issues (classification affects health-insurance mandate).

**Best Practice**: Document the reasoning for classification (contract, task-based nature, specialized skills, independent business operations) and review periodically. If classification is questionable, treat as employee (safer approach).

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Missouri-specific rules govern remote work arrangements or allocation of multi-state compensation. Federal **FLSA applies to wages** regardless of work location (if the employer is a covered employer under FLSA). **Missouri state income-tax withholding** is required if the employee works in Missouri (including remotely from a home office in Missouri). Multi-state employers must:

- Comply with wage-hour laws in **every state** where work is performed (e.g., if an employee works partly in Missouri and partly in Illinois, both states' minimum wage, overtime, and pay-frequency rules must be met; the higher/more restrictive standard applies).
- Withhold state income tax from all wages earned in Missouri.
- File state wage reports and comply with each state's record-keeping and payroll requirements.

**Choice of Law and Reciprocity**: Missouri recognizes **Missouri law** for employment disputes arising from Missouri-based work. Out-of-state employment contracts may contain choice-of-law clauses specifying another state's law; however, **if work is performed in Missouri, Missouri statutory protections apply regardless of choice of law**. For example, a contract specifying "New York law" would not override Missouri's minimum wage, pay-frequency, or final-pay requirements if the employee works in Missouri. **Verify**: whether Missouri courts give effect to out-of-state choice-of-law provisions in employment contracts or apply Missouri law as mandatory/non-waivable.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

When HCM Next processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in Missouri:

**Promotion Workflow**:

1. **At-Will Status and Contract Check**: Record or confirm at-will status in the employee's profile unless a written employment agreement specifies a term (e.g., "2-year executive contract") or probationary period.

2. **Compensation Review & Equal Pay Audit (RSMo § 290.410)**:
   - **Capture comparables**: Before approving promotion and new pay rate, identify employees of different sex performing the same quantity/quality of same classification of work.
   - **Document pay justification**: Record the basis for the new rate (e.g., "promotion from Analyst to Senior Analyst; market rate for role is $X; employee's tenure 3 years exceeds standard which justifies top-of-range $Y"). This documentation is crucial if an equal-pay complaint is filed.
   - **Red flag**: If promoting an employee to a rate lower than a peer of opposite sex in a comparable role, verify non-sex basis (e.g., seniority, skill, hours) and document clearly.
   - **Example**: Promoting a female employee from position A ($50k) to position B; check if males in position B earn more for the same quantity/quality of work. If yes, sex-based discrimination risk unless employer proves seniority, ability, skill, or other factor. Document the factor.

3. **Leave Balance Preservation**: If the promoted employee has accrued PTO, personal days, or paid sick leave (accrued prior to August 28, 2025, when state mandate ended), preserve those balances unless the employer's policy explicitly permits forfeiture. Document the carryover in the promotion record.

4. **Service Letter Readiness**: If the employer is a corporation with 7+ employees and the promoted employee has been employed 90+ days, flag in HCM Next that if the employee separates within 1 year of promotion, a service letter request must be honored within 45 days (RSMo § 290.140). Retain a copy of any service-letter request and issuance.

5. **Restrictive Covenant Review**: If the promoted employee has signed a non-solicitation or non-compete agreement, review enforceability under Missouri law:
   - Non-solicitation ≤1 year: presumed reasonable (§ 431.202); flag if it may inhibit the promoted role.
   - Non-compete: governed by common law; flag if overly broad and potentially unenforceable.
   - If employee is being promoted into a sales role and has a broad non-compete, review conflict and flag for legal review.

**Base-Pay Change Workflow (Increase or Decrease)**:

1. **Notice Requirement for Reductions (RSMo § 290.100 — MANDATORY)**:
   - **Rule**: Any reduction in base pay requires **written notice 30 days before the reduction takes effect**.
   - **HCM Next Implementation**: Build a mandatory workflow that: (a) flags if a pay change is a reduction, (b) enforces a 30-day minimum notice period before the change is effective, (c) generates the required written notice to the employee (by mail or posted in workplace).
   - **Penalty for Violation**: $50 per employee, per violation (civil liability).
   - **Example**: If an employee's salary is reduced from $60k to $55k on January 15, the notice must be given by December 16 at the latest. If notice is given on January 1 (only 14 days), the employer is in violation.
   - **No Notice for Increases**: Wage increases do not require advance notice; they may be effective immediately. However, employers often provide notice as a retention/morale gesture.

2. **Equal Pay Audit (RSMo § 290.410)**:
   - **Pre-Change Review**: Before approving any base-pay change, audit the employee's new rate against comparables (employees of opposite sex in the same classification of work, same establishment).
   - **Document Justification**: If the new rate is higher than a comparable of opposite sex, this is low-risk. If lower, verify non-sex basis (e.g., "employee is new to role vs. peer who has 5 years tenure in role; seniority difference justifies pay difference"). Document in the change record.
   - **Risk Scenario**: Employee A (female) is paid $70k in role X. Employee B (male) is paid $75k in role X for the same quantity/quality of work. If Employee A's pay is reduced or frozen while Employee B receives a raise, this may trigger equal-pay liability unless seniority or other factor is documented.

3. **Pay Frequency Compliance (RSMo § 290.080)**:
   - Ensure the new pay rate aligns with the employer's semi-monthly (≤16 days) or monthly pay schedule.
   - If the employee is transitioning to a new pay schedule (e.g., weekly to biweekly), ensure compliance with § 290.080 and document the change.

4. **Final Pay Impact (RSMo § 290.110)**:
   - If the pay change is part of a termination (e.g., employee is laid off after receiving a pay cut), calculate final pay correctly: all unpaid wages at the employee's last rate, due immediately on discharge day.
   - If the rate change occurs mid-pay-period and the employee is then terminated, reconcile wages for both rates.

5. **Wage Statement and Transparency**:
   - After the pay change is effective, ensure the next wage statement (monthly minimum per § 290.080) clearly shows the new rate and all deductions.
   - Deductions must be authorized in writing and must not reduce pay below minimum wage ($15/hour effective 2026).

6. **Wage Theft Liability (RSMo § 290.527)**:
   - **Critical**: Any underpayment (below the minimum wage or owed overtime) triggers **treble damages**: unpaid wages + 2× unpaid wages as liquidated damages, plus attorney's fees.
   - **Example**: If an employee should have earned $3,000 in overtime but was paid only $2,000, the employer owes $3,000 + ($1,000 × 2) = $5,000 plus fees.
   - Ensure pay calculations are accurate before pushing any change live.

7. **Human Rights Act Compliance (RSMo Chapter 213)**:
   - **Timing**: Before approving a base-pay change (increase or decrease), verify the reason is **not pretextual discrimination**.
   - **Protected Classes**: Do not base pay changes on race, color, religion, national origin, ancestry, sex, age (40+), or disability.
   - **Example Red Flag**: If a female employee is given a pay cut after reporting a pay disparity issue or a disabled employee's pay is reduced after requesting an accommodation, this may trigger retaliation liability.
   - **Document Legitimate Reason**: Market adjustment, performance review score, role change, tenure milestone, or business need. Document in the change record to defend against discrimination claims.

8. **Open Issues to Address**:
   - **Rounding Practices**: How does the employer handle rounding of wages (e.g., does partial hours round to nearest quarter, half, or full hour)? Ensure compliance with FLSA and state law.
   - **Retroactive Adjustments**: If a pay change is applied retroactively (e.g., employee was underpaid for 2 weeks, employer corrects in paycheck 3), ensure the correction is complete and documented.
   - **Off-Cycle Payrolls**: If the employer issues off-cycle checks to correct errors, ensure they don't miss other affected pay periods or employees.

**Manager Change**:

- **No special regulatory obligations** specific to manager changes. Terminating the prior manager or assigning a new manager is a routine business decision.
- **Discrimination Check**: Ensure the change is not based on unlawful discrimination (RSMo Chapter 213). Document business reason (org restructure, performance, retirement, etc.).
- **Contractual Obligations**: If the prior manager has an employment contract, verify no breach of contract terms (notice period, severance, cause definition).

**Termination Workflow**:

1. **Final Paycheck Timing (RSMo § 290.110 — MANDATORY IMMEDIATE PAYMENT)**:
   - **Rule**: All unpaid wages are due and payable **on the day of discharge** (termination date).
   - **Exception 1**: If the employee requests in writing that payment be sent to a specific address (by certified mail), payment must be received within **7 calendar days** of the request. If not, wages continue to accrue at the employee's regular rate as a penalty, capped at 60 days total.
   - **Exception 2**: Commission-based employees whose pay requires an audit are excluded from the "day of discharge" requirement; they are paid when audited.
   - **No Vacation Payout Mandate**: Unused vacation and PTO are not required to be paid unless the employer's policy or employment contract mandates payout.
   - **HCM Next Implementation**: Enforce a final-pay workflow: (a) flag termination date, (b) calculate all unpaid wages/overtime to that date, (c) generate final paycheck before or on termination date, (d) document date paid to defend against § 290.110 claims.
   - **Example**: Employee terminated Friday, January 13. Final pay for hours through January 13 must be paid Friday or by the following Friday at the latest. If delayed and employee requests payment mailed to address on Monday, payment must arrive by the following Monday (7 days).

2. **Service Letter (RSMo § 290.140 — CORPORATION 7+ EMPLOYEES, 90+ DAYS TENURE)**:
   - **Applicability Check**: Is the employer a corporation (vs. LLC, partnership)? Does it have 7+ employees? Was the employee employed 90+ days?
   - **Compliance Workflow**: (a) Retain a copy of any written service-letter request from the employee (by certified mail). (b) Issue the letter within 45 days of the request stating: (i) length of employment, (ii) nature/character of work performed and whether employee met job requirements, (iii) reason for termination or voluntary quit. (c) File the letter in the employment record and flag the 45-day deadline in HCM Next.
   - **Penalties**: Late issuance = compensatory damages; failure to issue at all = nominal + punitive damages. Employees may sue in civil court.
   - **Content Tip**: The service letter should be factual and neutral ("Employee X was employed as [title] from [date] to [date]; job duties included [description]; employee was terminated due to [reason]"). Avoid subjective criticism or defamatory statements.

3. **Unemployment Insurance Separation Notice**:
   - **Requirement**: File form DOL-8 (Separation Notice) or successor form with the Missouri Department of Labor within 48 hours of termination (verify current deadline).
   - **HCM Next Integration**: Automate the generation and filing of the separation notice on termination. Capture reason for separation (quit, discharge, layoff, other) to ensure accurate UI record.

4. **WARN Act Notice (Federal — 100+ EMPLOYEES, 50+ AFFECTED IN 30 DAYS)**:
   - **Applicability**: If the employer has 100+ employees and the termination is part of a mass reduction affecting 50+ employees in a 30-day period (or a plant closing), federal WARN Act applies.
   - **Requirement**: Issue **60 days' advance written notice** to: (a) affected employees (individually or by posting), (b) unions representing affected workers, (c) State Rapid Response Coordinator, (d) chief elected official of the municipality where the layoff occurs.
   - **HCM Next Implementation**: Flag layoffs 100+ employees ago; trigger WARN notice workflow 60+ days before the layoff effective date. Document notice issuance and distribution.
   - **Penalty for Non-Compliance**: Back-pay liability (60 days of wages + benefits) for affected employees.

5. **Data Breach Notification (RSMo § 407.1500 — PERSONAL INFORMATION EXPOSURE)**:
   - **Trigger**: If the employee's personal information (SSN, driver's license, passport, financial account, date of birth) is exposed or accessed without authorization during separation (e.g., personnel file left unattended, email forwarded to wrong recipient), the employer must notify the employee.
   - **Timing**: "Without unreasonable delay" (best practice: within 3 business days).
   - **Content**: Explain what information was exposed, when, what the employer is doing to secure data, and what the employee should do (fraud alerts, credit monitoring).
   - **Example**: If a hard-copy personnel file with employee SSN is lost during office move, notify the employee within 3 days.

6. **Non-Solicit/Non-Compete Enforcement**:
   - **Restrictive Covenant Check**: If the employee has signed a non-solicitation agreement (presumed reasonable if ≤1 year per § 431.202) or non-compete (common-law reasonableness), review whether the employee's next employment violates it.
   - **Flag for Enforcement**: If the employee is moving to a direct competitor or soliciting clients/employees, flag in HCM Next and consider legal action if the restriction is enforceable and the employee's conduct breaches it.
   - **Blue-Pencil Doctrine**: Missouri courts may modify overly broad restrictions; ensure any enforcement action is based on a clearly reasonable restriction.
   - **Example**: Employee A signed a non-solicitation agreement preventing solicitation of customers for 2 years. Employee A leaves and immediately solicits a major client. This is likely enforceable; flag and consult legal counsel on injunctive relief.

7. **Record Retention and Documentation**:
   - **Final Pay Records**: Retain for 3 years minimum (federal FLSA requirement).
   - **Service Letter Request & Issuance**: Retain copies of the request and issued letter.
   - **Separation Notice**: Retain UI separation notice/filing confirmation.
   - **WARN Notice (if issued)**: Retain proof of distribution.
   - **Termination Documentation**: Performance reviews, warnings, cause letter (if for-cause termination), and any signed separation/release agreement.

**Open Questions for the Employer (Section 11 Verification Checklist)**:

- **At-Will or Contract Employees**: How many employees have written employment contracts specifying a term or cause-for-termination? Flag these; they are not at-will.
- **Final Pay Policy**: Is there a written policy specifying final-pay timing (same day, next regular payday, 7 days, etc.)? Ensure it complies with § 290.110 (due immediately).
- **Pay Change Procedures**: Are promotions or pay changes subject to board approval, HR review, or other special procedures? Document approval timelines to ensure 30-day notice is feasible for reductions.
- **Restrictive Covenants**: Does the employer use non-solicitation or non-compete agreements? If so, are they reviewed annually for enforceability under Missouri law? If overly broad, update language.
- **E-Verify Status**: Is the employer a public employer or contractor on state contracts >$5,000? If so, is E-Verify registration current and all required employees verified?
- **Equal Pay Procedures**: Does the employer have a written procedure for auditing pay changes against comparables before they are approved? This is crucial for RSMo § 290.410 compliance.
- **Service Letter Preparedness**: Is the employer a corporation with 7+ employees? If so, does the employer have a template service letter and a process to respond to requests within 45 days?
- **WARN Act Coverage**: Does the employer have 100+ employees? If so, is there a process to identify mass layoffs 60+ days in advance and issue required notice?
