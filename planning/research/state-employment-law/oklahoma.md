# Oklahoma Employment Law Research

**State:** Oklahoma | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with public-policy exceptions**: Oklahoma recognizes at-will termination except for discharge related to workers' compensation claims, whistleblowing, jury duty, military service, refusal to commit illegal acts, and anti-discrimination (OADA applies to all employers with 1+ employee; federal FLSA applies for some protections).
- **Semimonthly pay required**: 40 O.S. § 165.2 mandates at least twice-monthly payday (exempt employees and public entities may be paid monthly); max 11 days between end of pay period and payday; max 3 days after payday to comply.
- **Final pay at next regular payday**: 40 O.S. § 165.3 requires wages upon termination paid by next designated payday; 2% daily liquidated damages (capped at unpaid amount) if willfully withheld without bona fide disagreement.
- **Federal minimum wage $7.25 (State Question 832 rejected June 2026)**: 40 O.S. § 197.2 adopts federal floor; Oklahoma has no state overtime; FLSA applies; SQ 832 ($15/hour by 2029) was rejected by voters; coverage threshold is 10+ employees and $100k+ gross annual sales.
- **Broad anti-discrimination (all employers)**: 25 O.S. § 1301 et seq. covers race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, age, genetic information; applies to employers with 1+ employee (no size threshold as under OADA 2011 exclusivity amendment).
- **Non-competes void; non-solicitation enforceable**: 15 O.S. § 219A voids "pure" non-competes; § 219A permits non-solicitation of established customers (strict compliance required); § 219B permits non-solicitation of employees/contractors.
- **Medical marijuana: no adverse action for license; safety-sensitive zero-tolerance effective Nov 1, 2026**: 63 O.S. § 427.8 (HB 3127, signed Apr 17, 2026) prohibits discipline solely for license; expanded testing authority; safety-sensitive roles face zero-tolerance.
- **No paid sick leave mandate; no mini-WARN; no salary-history ban; E-Verify voluntary for private employers**: 40 O.S. § 160 prohibits any municipal/local sick leave mandates; federal WARN Act applies; 25 O.S. § 1313 mandates E-Verify only for public employers and contractors on public contracts.
- **Drug testing with written policy required**: 40 O.S. §§ 551-563 permits pre-employment, transfer, post-accident, reasonable-suspicion, random, and return-to-duty testing; written policy and 10-day notice to employees required; employers may discipline refusal to test.
- **Jury duty protections and workers' comp retaliation**: 38 O.S. § 34 prohibits termination/adverse action for jury service; 85A O.S. § 7 prohibits retaliation for filing workers' comp claim; damages up to $100k plus attorney fees; district court exclusive jurisdiction.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Oklahoma is an at-will employment state: employers and employees may terminate the relationship at any time without cause or notice.

**Public-policy exceptions** to at-will status exist when discharge violates a clear mandate of public policy articulated by constitutional, statutory, or decisional law:

- Termination for filing or pursuing workers' compensation claim or occupational disease benefits (85A O.S. § 7).
- Termination for exercising occupational safety and health rights (40 O.S. § 551 et seq., OSHA equivalents).
- Termination for asserting rights under federal or state wage/hour laws (40 O.S. § 165 et seq.).
- Termination for whistleblowing or reporting illegal conduct to law enforcement.
- Termination for jury duty, military service, or voting (38 O.S. § 34; 40 O.S. § 500).
- Termination for refusal to commit illegal act.
- Termination in violation of implied employment contract (implied by handbook, oral promises, or course of conduct).
- Termination for exercising protected civil rights (free speech, religion, lawful off-duty conduct).

**Implied contract**: Courts recognize implied employment contracts based on written handbooks, oral promises, and course of conduct; such contracts may limit at-will status and require cause for termination.

**Required notices at hire and on change**: 40 O.S. § 165.2 requires employers to notify employees in writing of regular payday and any changes to payday in advance of the effective date.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (40 O.S. § 197.2):

- Oklahoma adopts federal minimum wage of $7.25/hour; no separate state minimum.
- Coverage threshold: employers with 10+ employees and gross annual sales >$100,000.
- Tipped employees: employers may credit up to $3.63/hour (50% of federal minimum) toward minimum wage if tips make up difference.
- State Question 832 (2026) rejected; would have raised wage to $12 (2027), $13.50 (2028), $15 (2029).
- No state overtime standard; federal FLSA applies (1.5x for hours over 40/week).

**Pay frequency** (§ 165.2):

- Employers must pay all employees at least twice each calendar month (semimonthly).
- Exception: exempt employees and state/county employees may be paid once monthly.
- Max 11 days may elapse between end of pay period and regular payday; employer has 3 days after payday to comply.
- Payday must be regular and consistent.

**Final pay on termination** (§ 165.3):

- Upon discharge: all wages (fixed and definite) due at next regular designated payday (or by certified mail if requested).
- Upon resignation: all wages due at next regular designated payday.
- Employer failure to pay: employee entitled to liquidated damages of 2% of unpaid wages per day after day earned, if willfully withheld without bona fide disagreement (capped at unpaid amount).
- Offset and bona fide disagreement permitted.

**Permitted deductions** (§ 165.3, § 165.4):

- Deductions authorized by law (taxes, garnishments, court orders) or in writing by employee for lawful purposes (union dues, insurance, retirement, charitable).
- No deduction for tools, uniforms, or employer-required costs unless written consent and deduction does not reduce wages below minimum.
- No deduction from final wages for lost/damaged items if employee not at fault.

**Pay statement content**: No state-mandated itemized format; federal FLSA requirements and § 165.4 record-keeping obligations apply (should document hours, wage rate, deductions, net pay).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**:

- No state mandate for job-posting salary ranges or disclosure at hire.
- No prohibition on inquiring about prior salary/wages; employers may request wage history.

**Equal pay statute** (40 O.S. § 198.1):

- Prohibits paying employees of one sex lower rates than opposite sex for comparable work (comparable skill, effort, responsibility, working conditions).
- Exceptions: seniority systems, merit systems, production-quality systems, or differential based on factor other than sex.
- Commissioner of Labor enforces; employer may not reduce other employees' wages to comply.

**OADA protected classes** (25 O.S. § 1301 et seq.):

- Applies to all employers (no size threshold as of 2011 amendment).
- Covers race, color, religion, sex, national origin, disability, age, genetic information.
- Retaliation prohibited for asserting discrimination claim.

**No state privacy law, salary-history ban, pay-data reporting mandate, or pay-transparency law** specific to private employers.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**:

- No state mandate for paid or unpaid sick leave.
- 40 O.S. § 160 prohibits municipalities from establishing mandatory minimum vacation or sick leave.
- Voluntary employer benefit only; federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees and conditions met.

**Paid family/medical leave (PFML)**:

- Oklahoma does not mandate PFML; no state-managed fund or contribution requirement.
- Employers may voluntarily offer PFML; federal FMLA applies if eligible.

**Jury duty leave** (38 O.S. § 34):

- Employee may not be terminated, removed, or subject to adverse employment action for jury service.
- Employee may not be required to use vacation, annual, or sick leave for jury duty (employer not required to pay, but employee may use paid leave).
- Violation is misdemeanor; penalty up to $5,000.

**Military service leave**: No specific statute; federal Uniformed Services Employment and Reemployment Rights Act (USERRA) applies.

**Domestic abuse leave**: No state statute separate from federal FMLA; some limited protections under workers' comp context (verify).

**Interaction with promotion/base-pay change**:

- If employee on paid leave is promoted, employer must not reduce wage below pre-leave level upon return.
- No statutory requirement to apply promotion wage retroactively to leave period (verify case law).
- Leave accrual during paid leave policy-dependent (verify if employer policy dictates cessation or continuation).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention** (40 O.S. § 165.4):

- Employers must retain true and accurate records of hours worked and wages paid for at least 1 year.
- Records subject to inspection by labor commissioner or authorized representatives.
- Labor commissioner has authority to demand records; retention obligation applies to all payroll records.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic right to inspect or copy personnel records.
- Public/state employees may review files during normal business hours (verify exact scope).
- No state-mandated access statute for private employers; common law and employer policy control.

**Record content and format**:

- No state mandate for electronic vs. paper format.
- Employers should document hours worked, wage rate, deductions, gross/net pay per § 165.4 and FLSA requirements.
- No state-mandated pay-stub format beyond federal FLSA minimums.

**Data breach notification** (24 O.S. §§ 161-166):

- Any business holding Oklahoma residents' computerized personal information must notify without unreasonable delay after discovering breach.
- SB 626 (effective Jan 1, 2026) requires notification in most expedient manner (verify exact deadline); AG notification if 1,000+ residents affected.
- Penalties: AG or DA may seek civil penalties up to $150,000 per breach or series of similar breaches.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Employee data privacy**:

- No state-specific employee data privacy law analogous to CCPA; general data breach notification (§ 161 et seq.) applies.
- No state law banning workplace email/internet monitoring or video monitoring of work areas.
- Reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies (common law).

**Tobacco use** (40 O.S. § 500 et seq., Oklahoma Employee Privacy Act — lawful off-duty tobacco use):

- Employer may not discriminate against or discipline employee for lawful off-duty tobacco use.
- No requirement for employer accommodation of tobacco use on-site.

**Medical marijuana** (63 O.S. § 427.8, HB 3127, effective Nov 1, 2026):

- Employers cannot discipline, fire, or refuse to hire solely because employee holds Oklahoma medical marijuana patient license.
- However, HB 3127 grants expanded testing authority: employers with compliant written drug policy may take action on positive marijuana tests (not just safety-sensitive roles).
- Safety-sensitive positions (defined by HB 3127) subject to mandatory zero-tolerance policy; definition of safety-sensitive expanded from prior law.

**Data residency and processing**:

- No state law mandating data residency for employee records; may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided breach notification and security laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box**:

- No statewide ban-the-box law for private employers in Oklahoma.
- Criminal history may be inquired at any point of application process.

**Background checks**:

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions.
- Third-party background check providers must comply with FCRA notice, dispute, adverse-action procedures.

**Drug testing** (40 O.S. §§ 551-563, Standards for Workplace Drug and Alcohol Testing Act):

- Employers may (but not required to) conduct drug testing for pre-employment, transfer/reassignment, post-accident, reasonable-suspicion, random, and return-to-duty/follow-up circumstances.
- Employers must adopt written drug/alcohol policy prior to requiring testing; must provide 10-day notice to employees and copy to each applicant upon conditional offer.
- Policy must state disciplinary actions for refusal; employers may discipline or discharge for refusal to test per § 562.
- No specific employee-class exemptions (verify for safety-sensitive vs. non-safety-sensitive distinction post-HB 3127).

**E-Verify mandate** (25 O.S. § 1313, HB 1804):

- Mandatory only for public employers and their contractors/subcontractors performing physical services on public contracts.
- Voluntary for private employers.
- Public employer using Status Verification System is exempt from liability for work-eligibility verification of employees hired after July 1, 2008.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mass layoff notice**:

- No state mini-WARN law; Oklahoma relies solely on federal WARN Act.
- Federal WARN Act applies to employers with 100+ employees; requires 60 days' advance notice before plant closing or mass layoff (25+ employees or 25%+ of workforce over 3 months).

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance; severance is contractual and requires explicit agreement.

**Non-compete enforceability** (15 O.S. §§ 218-219B):

- "Pure" non-compete agreements are void and unenforceable (§ 219A); former employees may work in same industry/business.
- Non-competes enforceable in connection with sale of business (§ 218) or partnership dissolution (§ 219).
- Partners may agree not to carry on similar business within specified county and contiguous counties where partnership transacted business.

**Non-solicitation enforceability** (§ 219A, § 219B):

- Non-solicitation of employer's established customers: permissible (§ 219A; strict compliance required by Oklahoma courts).
- Non-solicitation of employer's employees or independent contractors: permissible (§ 219B).
- Courts require strict adherence to statute; vague or overly broad non-solicitation likely unenforceable.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Oklahoma courts apply common-law "right to control" test: if employer exercises control over manner/means of work, relationship is employment, not independent contractor.
- Oklahoma uses 20-factor IRS Revenue Ruling 87-41 test; federal IRS, state unemployment insurance, and workers' comp commission each have separate tests; results may differ.
- Worker's Compensation Commission examines skill, materials supply, place of work, and degree of control (greater control = employee).
- On and after Nov 1, 2012, independent contractors must maintain liability insurance/bond and major medical or workers' comp insurance; failure is prima facie misclassification evidence.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute addressing remote-work classification.
- Standard employment law applies regardless of work location; if work performed in Oklahoma (remote or on-site), Oklahoma law applies.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Best practice: specify in writing that Oklahoma law governs for Oklahoma-based work.

**Firearms in vehicles** (21 O.S. § 1289.7a):

- Employers cannot prohibit employees from transporting and storing firearms/ammunition in locked vehicles in workplace parking lots.
- No employer liability for occurrences resulting from stored firearms in locked vehicle on parking lot (unless employer commits criminal act with firearms).

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints**:

1. **Pay frequency/payday notification** (§ 165.2): On promotion or base-pay change, flag if payday changes. If payday remains regular (≤11 days from end of period, ≤3 days after payday), no additional notice required unless employer policy mandates advance notice. Document change date.

2. **Base-pay change timing and notice** (§ 165.2, § 165.3): Wage increase may be implemented immediately; wage decrease should be preceded by written advance notice (best practice: 1+ pay period prior). Capture written acknowledgment in system; flag if change reduces pay below minimum wage.

3. **Minimum wage compliance**: On promotion or base-pay change, verify new wage meets $7.25/hour floor (or any higher federal floor if multistate). Flag if promotion results in subminimum wage.

4. **Final pay on separation during/after promotion**: If termination follows promotion, ensure promoted-rate wages from effective date forward are paid by next regular payday per § 165.3; wages not paid face 2% daily liquidated damages (capped at unpaid amount). Document final pay calculation and payday.

5. **Equal pay statute compliance** (40 O.S. § 198.1): On base-pay increase, flag if promoted wage for one gender differs substantially from peer rates for comparable work within same employer; document business justification (seniority, merit, production, other non-sex factor) or require HR approval.

6. **Anti-discrimination (OADA)** (25 O.S. § 1301 et seq.): On promotion, flag if promotion/pay change triggered by protected-class status or if disparate impact on protected class. Capture manager justification and ensure neutral criteria applied.

7. **Workers' compensation interaction** (85A O.S. § 7): If employee returns from workers' comp leave and is promoted, employer must not reduce wage below pre-leave level as form of retaliation. Document promotion timing and base-pay calculation.

8. **Medical marijuana** (63 O.S. § 427.8, HB 3127): If promoted employee holds medical marijuana license, cannot take adverse action (including pay reduction) solely for license status. HB 3127 (eff. Nov 1, 2026) permits action on positive test if written policy exists and role safety-sensitive; ensure policy clearly distinguishes license status from test result.

9. **Non-compete enforceability check** (15 O.S. § 219A, § 219B): On promotion to customer-facing or high-access role, if employee subject to non-compete, flag as unenforceable per § 219A; document non-solicitation clause (if present) and whether it meets strict-compliance standard (established customers only, reasonable duration/scope).

10. **Final pay on resignation** (§ 165.3): If promoted employee resigns, final pay (including promoted rate from effective date) due by next regular payday; no deferral permitted.

11. **Record retention** (§ 165.4): System must retain promotion records (effective date, wage amount, justification, approval) for minimum 1 year to support labor commissioner inspection rights.

12. **Implied contract review**: On promotion, if handbook or offer letter promises "cause-only termination" or other job security, flag for legal review; determine if implied contract limits at-will status and requires cause for post-promotion termination.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether implied contract for "cause-only termination" (Oklahoma implied-contract case law, e.g. Hinson v. Cameron, 1987 OK 49; verify) can be formed after hire or only at hire.
- Clarify scope of "established customers" under § 219A non-solicitation (specific names, customer list, defined territory).
- Verify whether workers' comp retaliation extends to all employment actions (including promotion deferral or pay increase denial).
- Confirm whether SQ 832 rejection (June 2026) affects any pending legislation on minimum wage (2026-2027 session).
- Verify whether HB 3127 "safety-sensitive" definition (eff. Nov 1, 2026) includes healthcare, transportation, security, or other specific categories.
- Confirm whether wage-setting criteria must be documented in writing to establish "merit system" or "other factor other than sex" defense under § 198.1.
