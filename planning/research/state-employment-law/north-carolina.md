# North Carolina Employment Law Research

**State:** North Carolina | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **At-will employment with REDA protections**: North Carolina is at-will; Retaliatory Employment Discrimination Act (§ 95-240 et seq.) protects employees from discharge, demotion, or retaliation for filing workers' compensation claims, OSHA complaints, wage-and-hour complaints, and other legally protected activities.
- **Minimum wage**: $7.25/hour (federal minimum); no state override.
- **Overtime**: Time-and-a-half for hours over 40/week (§ 95-25.4); federal FLSA applies.
- **Pay frequency**: Daily, weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly (§ 95-25.6); bonuses/commissions may be paid annually if prescribed in advance.
- **Final pay timing**: On termination, "the next regular payday" for wages earned in the period of discharge; bonuses/commissions subject to separate rules.
- **No state paid sick leave or paid family/medical leave mandate**: Federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees; no state requirement.
- **School involvement leave (§ 95-28.3)**: 4 hours/calendar year for parent involvement at child's school; retaliation prohibited.
- **Domestic violence leave (§ 50B-5.5)**: Reasonable time off to obtain protective order; employer cannot discharge/demote/deny promotion.
- **No statewide salary-history ban**: North Carolina has no statute restricting private-employer salary-history inquiries; only Executive Order No. 93 (2019, not 2024) bars _state government_ agencies from requesting or relying on applicant salary history. Private employers may still ask.
- **E-Verify mandate**: Private employers with 25+ employees must use E-Verify for new hires (verified within 3 business days; HB 36, effective July 1, 2013).
- **Non-competes (§ 75-4)**: Must be written, signed, reasonable in time/territory; enforced narrowly against drafter.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

North Carolina follows at-will employment doctrine: employers and employees may terminate without cause or notice.

**Public-policy exceptions** (REDA, § 95-240 et seq.):

- Termination for filing or threatening workers' compensation claim.
- Termination for filing or threatening wage-and-hour or OSHA complaint.
- Termination for refusing to perform illegal act.
- Termination for jury duty, military service, or voting.
- Termination related to sickle cell trait (§ 95-28.1) or genetic testing (§ 95-28.1A).
- Termination related to lawful off-duty use of lawful products (§ 95-28.2; applies to 3+ employee employers).
- Termination for requesting or using school involvement leave (§ 95-28.3) or domestic violence leave (§ 50B-5.5).

**Written notice at hire or change** (§ 95-25.13): Employer must provide written notice of pay rate and paydays at hire; 24 hours' notice required before reduction in wages or wage benefits (amended 2021; verify exact timing).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (§ 95-25.3): $7.25/hour (federal rate; no state override).

**Overtime** (§ 95-25.4): Time-and-a-half for all hours over 40 in a workweek (federal FLSA applies); no daily overtime threshold in NC.

**Pay frequency** (§ 95-25.6):

- Employers must designate regular paydays: daily, weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly.
- Wages accruing must be paid on regular payday.
- Bonuses, commissions, or other forms of calculation may be paid as infrequently as annually if prescribed in advance.

**Final pay timing** (§ 95-25.6, administrative rules):

- On termination: All wages earned due on "the next regular payday" for the pay period in which employment is discontinued.
- Bonuses and commissions: Subject to separate terms if prescribed in advance.
- If employee requests mailed final check, employer must mail at employer's expense; employer cannot withhold final check for failure to pick up at worksite.

**Permitted deductions** (§ 95-25.8): Deductions allowed only if required by law (tax, garnishment) or authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes. Deduction must not reduce wages below applicable minimum wage.

**Pay statement content**: No state-mandated itemized pay-stub format; federal FLSA requirements apply.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary history ban**: No statewide private-sector ban.

- North Carolina has NO statewide law prohibiting private employers from asking about salary history.
- State agencies ONLY: Executive Order 93 (2019) restricts state government employers from requesting or relying on salary history.
- Private employers may inquire about prior wages; no state law restricts this practice.

**Pay transparency mandate**: No state-wide requirement for salary range disclosure in job postings.

**Equal Employment Practices Act** (§ 143-422.2):

- Applies to employers with 15+ regularly employed employees.
- Prohibits discrimination based on race, religion, color, national origin, age, sex, disability.
- **Policy only; no private right of action** (state agency enforcement).

**Equal pay**: No separate equal-pay statute; federal Equal Pay Act (FLSA § 206(d)) applies.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave**: No state mandate for private employers; employer discretion.

**Paid family and medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate; federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid, 50+ employees) is the statutory floor.

**School involvement leave** (§ 95-28.3):

- 4 hours per calendar year.
- Purpose: Parent/guardian attendance or involvement at child's school.
- Timing: Mutually agreed upon; employer may require 48 hours' notice.
- Employer may require written verification from school.
- Retaliation prohibited; private right of action.

**Domestic violence leave** (§ 50B-5.5):

- Reasonable time off to obtain or attempt to obtain relief for domestic violence.
- Permitted uses: File/attend protective order proceeding, consult attorney, meet law enforcement.
- Timing: Employee must follow usual time-off policy unless emergency prevents notice.
- Employer may require documentation of emergency or reason for need.
- Retaliation prohibited; employer cannot discharge, demote, deny promotion, or discipline solely for taking leave.

**Interaction with promotions**: On promotion during leave, employer must not reduce post-leave wage below pre-promotion level.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention** (§ 95-25.15):

- Employers must maintain true and accurate records of hours worked, wages, ages (for minors), and other conditions/practices essential to enforcement.
- Minor employment certificates must be retained for 2 years after termination.
- Specific retention period for general payroll records: subject to commissioner regulation (verify exact period; federal FLSA = 3 years minimum for wage claims).

**Employee access to personnel files**: No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic right to inspect or copy personnel records (applies to public/state employees only).

**Record format**: No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; either acceptable if retention/access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data breach notification** (§ 75-65, Identity Theft Protection Act):

- Applies to any business owning or licensing personal information.
- Timeline: Notify affected individuals "without unreasonable delay" after discovery of breach (no numeric deadline; interpreted as days to weeks, not months).
- Notify North Carolina Attorney General Consumer Protection Division of every qualifying breach.
- Definition: Personal information = first name/initial + last name + one sensitive element (SSN, account number, driver license, passport, financial/medical/biometric data, unique identifier) if not encrypted/redacted.
- Safe harbor: No notification if data encrypted and encryption key not compromised.

**Workplace monitoring and privacy**: No state statute restricting email/internet monitoring or video surveillance; common law reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies.

**Data residency**: No state law mandating data residency for employee records; out-of-state storage permitted if breach notification and security laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box for private employers**: No state statute; only Executive Order 158 (state agencies, not private sector).

**Background checks**: Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions for private employers (third-party providers must follow FCRA notice, dispute, adverse-action procedures).

**Drug testing** (§ 95-230 et seq., Controlled Substance Examination Regulation):

- No state law prohibiting private-sector drug testing.
- Employers have broad discretion: pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, random.
- All drug-testing procedures must comply with § 95-230 et seq. reliability and sample-collection standards.
- Confirmation required: Screening tests producing positive result must be confirmed by gas chromatography/mass spectrometry or equivalent method.
- Testing must use reliable, minimally invasive examination/screening; samples collected under reasonable, sanitary conditions preserving dignity.

**E-Verify mandate** (HB 36, effective July 1, 2013):

- **Applies to private employers with 25+ employees** (phased in: 500+ by Oct 1, 2012; 100+ by Jan 1, 2013; 25+ by July 1, 2013).
- New hires must be verified within 3 business days of start date; applies to newly hired employees working in North Carolina regardless of employer HQ location.
- Exemptions: Employers with 24 or fewer employees; individuals with employment term less than 9 months in calendar year.
- Enforcement: First-time violators must file sworn affidavit of compliance within 7 days of notice; failure to file triggers $10,000 civil penalty per violation.
- Failure to comply may result in loss of business license and other civil remedies.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mini-WARN act**: No state law; federal WARN Act (100+ employees, 60 days' notice for plant closing or mass layoff affecting 50+ employees or 25%+ of workforce) is the sole requirement.

**Severance**: No state mandate; contractual and voluntary only.

**Non-compete enforceability** (§ 75-4):

- **Required: Written agreement, signed by restrained party** (contract-formation requirement is strict).
- Enforceability test: (1) In writing, (2) signed, (3) based on valuable consideration (employment itself may suffice), (4) reasonable in time and territory, (5) not against public policy.
- Enforced narrowly against drafter; single defective element (missing signature, indefinite scope, unreasonable duration) defeats enforcement entirely.
- Valid legitimate business interests: trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information, goodwill.
- **No specific time/territory standard provided in statute; reasonableness determined case-by-case** by courts; restrictive interpretation favors employee.
- Blue-pencil doctrine: Courts generally do not modify overly broad provisions; unenforceability stands as written.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**: No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness test (time, scope, geography, legitimate business interest).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification** (common law "right to control" test):

- North Carolina courts apply common law test with eight factors; no single factor determinative.
- Key factors: (1) Control/direction by hiring entity; (2) Integral to hiring entity's business; (3) Permanency of relationship; (4) Contractor's investment in facilities/equipment; (5) Opportunities for profit and loss; (6) Initiative, judgment, foresight in market competition; (7) Degree of independent business operation; (8) Totality of circumstances.
- Misclassification enforcement by NCDOL, Revenue Department, Attorney General; remedies include back wages, reclassification, penalties.

**Remote workers**: No specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location. If work performed in North Carolina (remote or on-site), NC law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**: No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints for promotion and base-pay changes**:

1. **Minimum wage compliance**: On promotion or base-pay change, verify wage meets $7.25/hour state minimum. Flag if promotion results in wage below minimum.

2. **Wage change notice and timing** (§ 95-25.13): For wage decrease, provide written 24 hours' notice before effective date (verify exact timing; 2021 amendment; capture written acknowledgment).

3. **Pay frequency verification** (§ 95-25.6): On promotion, verify payday remains regular (daily through monthly). If payday changes, flag per employer policy.

4. **Final pay on termination** (§ 95-25.6): If termination occurs after promotion, ensure all wages at promoted rate due on "next regular payday" for discharge period.

5. **School involvement leave interaction** (§ 95-28.3): If employee uses 4-hour leave and receives promotion, do not reduce post-leave wage below pre-promotion level. Leave accrual continues unaffected by promotion.

6. **Domestic violence leave interaction** (§ 50B-5.5): If employee takes DV leave during promotion period, do not discharge, demote, or deny promotion; maintain wage from newly promoted position.

7. **REDA retaliation check** (§ 95-240 et seq.): If employee recently filed wage-and-hour, OSHA, or workers' compensation complaint, flag promotion timing to ensure retaliation cannot be inferred (timing shortly before complaint is suspicious).

8. **Sickle cell trait/genetic testing protection** (§ 95-28.1, § 95-28.1A): If promotion involves inquiry into genetic information, verify compliance (no adverse action based on trait or genetic testing history).

9. **Lawful products protection** (§ 95-28.2, applies 3+ employees): Promotion cannot be denied or conditioned on employee's lawful off-duty use of lawful products (tobacco, alcohol, etc.).

10. **Salary history**: No statewide private-sector restriction exists (corrected 2026-09-03 — there is no "2024 ban"; only Executive Order 93 (2019) binds state agencies). For a private-sector Human Capital Management Suite tenant, salary history may lawfully be used; base compensation-fairness controls on internal policy, not a state mandate, unless the employer is itself a NC state agency.

11. **Records retention** (§ 95-25.15): Retain promotion documents (wage change, effective date, justification, business rationale) per employer record policy (verify whether 3 years or employer-set); support labor commissioner inspection.

12. **Common law contract review**: If handbook or offer letter promises "cause-only termination" or similar job security, flag for legal review to determine if implied contract limits at-will status post-promotion (verify case law).

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify exact timing requirement for wage-decrease notice (§ 95-25.13 amendment 2021; "24 hours" vs. "one pay period").
- Confirm whether "reasonable time off" for domestic violence leave (§ 50B-5.5) has a statutory cap or is entirely discretionary per employer policy.
- Clarify whether "prescribed in advance" for annual bonus/commission payment (§ 95-25.6) requires calendar-year advance notice or offer-letter specification.
- Verify whether implied at-will-override contracts (handbooks, verbal promises) survive a promotion or only apply at hire.
- Determine whether § 95-25.13 notice requirement applies to all wage reductions or only to periodic rate decreases (commission, incentive, bonus changes).
- Verify status of proposed mandatory paid sick leave (SB 622, effective 1/1/2026 verify); if enacted, clarify accrual floor and promotion interaction.
