# Kansas Employment Law Research

**State:** Kansas | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment is default**: Kansas recognizes at-will employment unless a written employment contract specifies otherwise; no statutory contract-term requirement at hire or change.
- **Minimum wage and overtime**: Kansas statutory minimum ($7.25/hour, K.S.A. 44-1202) equals federal FLSA floor. Overtime under federal FLSA (40 hours/week at 1.5×) applies; Kansas employers not covered by FLSA must pay overtime after 46 hours (K.S.A. 44-1204) if applicable (verify coverage triggers).
- **Wage Payment Act governs pay frequency and final pay**: K.S.A. 44-313 to 44-327 require regular pay intervals (at least semimonthly) and timely final payment on separation; notice of pay-rate changes required before work is performed (K.S.A. 44-320, K.A.R. 49-20-1).
- **No paid sick leave or family leave mandate**: No state-level paid sick leave or PFMLA equivalent; federal FMLA applies to covered employers (50+ within 75 miles, 12 weeks unpaid).
- **Noncompetes remain common law; non-solicits are now partly statutory**: Non-compete agreements are enforced solely under common-law reasonableness (_Weber v. Tillman_) — K.S.A. 50-163 does not reach them. 2025 SB 241 (eff. 2025-07-01) amended K.S.A. 50-163 to presume enforceable two categories of non-solicitation covenant (employee non-solicits protecting confidential/trade-secret information or ≤2 years; customer non-solicits limited to material-contact customers and ≤2 years) and to require reformation of overbroad ones.
- **Anti-discrimination**: Kansas Act Against Discrimination (K.S.A. 44-1001 et seq., 4+ employees); age discrimination (K.S.A. 44-1111, 44-1112).
- **No E-Verify mandate**: Federal IRCA applies; Kansas has not enacted state E-Verify requirement.
- **No mini-WARN act**: Federal WARN Act applies (60 days for mass layoffs affecting 50+ in 30 days); Kansas has no state-level mass-separation notice requirement.
- **Data breach notification**: K.S.A. 50-7a01 requires "without unreasonable delay" notification (typically 3–5 days) when personal information is accessed without authorization.
- **No state privacy statute**: No comprehensive employee data privacy law equivalent to CCPA; federal law applies.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Kansas is an at-will employment state. K.S.A. 44-202 codifies at-will status: either party may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any lawful reason or no reason, except where a written contract or statute specifies otherwise. Exceptions to at-will include: termination in violation of public policy (retaliation for jury service, voting, military duty, or whistleblowing); breach of an implied covenant of good faith and fair dealing (rare); or termination violating statutory protections (discrimination, workers' comp retaliation, wage-and-hour violations).

**Notice Requirements at Hire**: No Kansas statute mandates written notice of at-will status at hire. However, K.S.A. 44-320 requires notice of pay rate and any changes before the employee performs work under the new rate. K.A.R. 49-20-1 clarifies that written notice of pay-rate reductions must be provided before work is performed.

**Pay-Rate Change Notification**: K.S.A. 44-320 requires employers to notify employees of any change in pay rate. K.A.R. 49-20-1 specifies that notice of pay-rate reductions must be written and given before the employee performs work under the reduced rate. Same-day or minimal notice is common practice; verify with Kansas Department of Labor for "reasonable notice" timing.

**Separation Notice**: No state law mandates advance notice before termination (except federal WARN Act if 100+ employees and triggering event occurs). Employers should comply with any notice period in an employment contract, collective bargaining agreement, or handbook policy.

**Public Policy Exceptions to At-Will**: Retaliation for jury duty (K.S.A. 43-173), voting (K.S.A. 25-418), military duty (K.S.A. 48-517), or workers' compensation claims (K.S.A. 44-501 et seq., common law) voids at-will terminations. Whistleblower retaliation for public policy matters (K.S.A. 75-2973, public employees) may apply; verify private-sector coverage.

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions, and Records

**Minimum Wage**: K.S.A. 44-1202 sets Kansas minimum wage at $7.25/hour, equal to the federal FLSA minimum (29 U.S.C. § 206). No state-specific rate above federal minimum; employers must pay the higher of state and federal (currently equal). Tip credit (if allowed federally) is not expanded under Kansas law; federal 29 U.S.C. § 203(m) applies.

**Overtime — FLSA-Covered Employers**: Federal FLSA 1.5× regular rate applies for hours over 40 per workweek. FLSA exemptions (executive, administrative, professional, outside-sales, computer, some highly compensated) follow federal thresholds (29 U.S.C. §§ 213, 541–549).

**Overtime — Non-FLSA Employers**: K.S.A. 44-1204 provides that employers not covered by FLSA must pay overtime at 1.5× regular rate for hours exceeding 46 per workweek (verify: applicability and coverage triggers). This differs from the federal 40-hour threshold. **Verify** current Kansas Department of Labor guidance on which employers are exempt from FLSA and thus subject to K.S.A. 44-1204.

**Pay Frequency**: K.S.A. 44-314 mandates payment of wages at least semimonthly (twice per calendar month, or every two weeks for weekly-pay employers, depending on practice). More frequent pay is permitted. Collective bargaining agreements may specify different intervals. Salaries must align with this schedule.

**Final Paycheck on Termination/Resignation**: K.S.A. 44-315 requires all earned, accrued wages to be paid by the next regular payday following termination or resignation. If an employment contract specifies immediate payment (e.g., same-day final pay), that requirement supersedes the "next payday" rule. Accrued vacation/PTO is paid out per employer policy or contract; Kansas does not mandate payout unless promised in writing.

**Deductions from Wages**: K.S.A. 44-319 permits deductions only where:

- Required by federal, state, or local law (taxes, garnishments, child support);
- Authorized in writing by the employee; or
- Required by court order.
  Illegal deductions: those that reduce gross wages below minimum wage; fines or penalties for poor work, tardiness, or breakage; uniforms, tools, or equipment costs (employer must absorb); kickbacks; or unauthorized reductions. Employers must maintain deduction records and provide employee notification of deduction policies at hire.

**Pay Statement Content**: No Kansas statute mandates specific pay-stub format or fields. Federal FLSA requires employers to document hours worked and wages paid for compliance audits. Best practice: include gross wages, deductions (with explanations), net pay, pay period dates, and YTD totals.

**Wage Payment Act Penalties**: K.S.A. 44-322a and 44-323 provide penalties for wage violations: employers who fail to pay wages owed or violate K.S.A. 44-313 to 44-327 may be liable for unpaid wages plus damages and court costs. Criminal penalties apply for willful violations (K.S.A. 44-323).

**Record Retention and Notice**: K.S.A. 44-320 requires written notice of pay rate at hire and before any reduction takes effect (per K.A.R. 49-20-1). Employers must retain payroll records for at least 3 years per federal FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 211); Kansas does not impose a longer retention requirement, but best practice is 4 years.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Kansas state law restricts inquiries about salary history. Federal EEOC guidance discourages such inquiries to reduce wage inequality, but Kansas has not enacted a ban. Employers may ask about prior compensation during recruitment.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Kansas state law requires salary ranges in job postings or on request. Pay transparency is optional and not mandated.

**Equal Pay and Anti-Discrimination**: Kansas Act Against Discrimination (K.S.A. 44-1001 et seq.) applies to employers with 4 or more employees. It prohibits discrimination in hiring, pay, benefits, or terms of employment based on race, color, religion, sex (including gender identity and sexual orientation — **verify** current scope), national origin, ancestry, or marital status. Equal pay for equal work is implied under K.S.A. 44-1002 (discrimination in wages is unlawful). Federal Equal Pay Act (29 U.S.C. § 206(d)) and Title VII also apply concurrently.

**Age Discrimination**: K.S.A. 44-1111 (Kansas Age Discrimination in Employment Act) prohibits age discrimination for employees 40 and older (mirrors federal ADEA, 29 U.S.C. § 623). K.S.A. 44-1112 provides similar remedies and burden-of-proof rules as ADEA.

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Kansas state mandate for pay-equity analysis or EEO-1 reporting; federal EEO-1 applies to employers with 100+ employees. Kansas does not require internal pay-equity audits or disclosure.

**Remedies**: Violations under K.S.A. 44-1001 et seq. may result in administrative complaints to the Kansas Human Rights Commission, or private litigation for damages, attorney fees, and injunctive relief. No private right of action is explicitly codified but implied in enforcement language.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, Voting, Jury Duty, Military

**Paid Sick Leave**: No Kansas state law mandates paid sick leave for private-sector employees. Employers may voluntarily provide paid sick leave; if they do, the policy binds them to its terms and must comply with any promises in writing. Accrual, usage, and carryover are employer discretion absent a written policy.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave (PFMLA)**: No Kansas state mandate for paid family leave or paid medical leave (distinct from federal FMLA). Employers may voluntarily provide; federal FMLA applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles) and provides 12 weeks unpaid leave per 12 months.

**Federal FMLA**: Applies to employers with 50+ employees within 75-mile radius. Covers: serious health condition (own or family member), childbirth/adoption/foster care, military exigency leave, military caregiver leave. Up to 12 weeks unpaid per 12 months; health insurance must be maintained during leave. Reinstatement to same or equivalent position required upon return.

**Jury Duty**: K.S.A. 43-173 prohibits employers from discharging or threatening employees for jury service. Employers may require proof of service. Pay during jury duty is employer discretion (no mandate); many employers pay, some do not.

**Voting**: K.S.A. 25-418 allows employees up to 2 hours to vote in elections. The employee may choose voting time; employer may specify timing to minimize business disruption. Paid or unpaid is employer discretion (no mandate, but practice varies).

**Military Leave**: K.S.A. 48-517 provides military leave for Kansas National Guard members called to active duty. Reemployment rights and pay during leave vary by duration and federal USERRA (38 U.S.C. § 4301 et seq.) requirements. Verify specific military-leave entitlements with Kansas Department of Military Affairs or private legal counsel.

**Holidays and Paid Time Off (PTO)**: Entirely optional. No Kansas state mandate for holidays, vacation, or personal days. Employers set policy; if written policy or contract promises carry-over, employers must honor it. Unused PTO at separation is paid out only if the policy or contract requires it (not mandatory statewide). Verify employer handbook.

**Leave and Pay/Role Changes**: When an employee is promoted or receives a base-pay increase, accrued leave balances are typically preserved under state law unless the employer's written policy explicitly provides for forfeiture (rare). Payout at separation follows the employer's documented policy or contract. Handbook should specify accrual, carry-over, and payout rules.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: Federal FLSA requires retention for at least 3 years (29 U.S.C. § 211). Kansas does not impose a longer retention period; verify current Kansas Department of Labor guidance. Best practice: retain for 4 years to account for potential federal audits and statute-of-limitations extensions.

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**: Kansas does not have a comprehensive state statute granting employee access to personnel records (unlike some states). Common law and federal law apply: employees generally have the right to inspect their own records in the employer's possession, but the employer may withhold confidential information (reference letters, third-party evaluations, internal investigations) unless otherwise required by law.

**Record Format**: No Kansas requirement for paper vs. electronic. Digital records are acceptable if they remain accessible and authentic.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometrics, Monitoring, Breach Notification

**State Data Privacy Law**: Kansas has no comprehensive employee data privacy statute (equivalent to CCPA or GDPR). General common-law privacy torts and federal law (FCRA, HIPAA, GLBA where applicable) apply. Data collection and retention practices follow federal standards and employer policy.

**Breach Notification**: K.S.A. 50-7a01 et seq. (Kansas Breach Notification Law) requires notification to affected individuals "without unreasonable delay" when the employer (or an entity it engages) accesses or acquires personal information (defined as unencrypted or unredacted data identifying an individual, including SSN, driver's license, financial account numbers, credit card numbers, or passport number) without authorization. Notification must be:

- In writing or, if not feasible, by telephone, email, or broadcast media;
- In the most expedient manner possible, and without unreasonable delay (no specific day count codified, but 3–5 days is standard practice for "expedient");
- May be delayed if law enforcement advises that notification would interfere with a criminal investigation (K.S.A. 50-7a03).
  Employers must notify the Kansas Attorney General if the breach affects 250 or more Kansas residents (K.S.A. 50-7a04).

**Consumer Reports and Background Checks**: Kansas does not prohibit background checks or criminal-history inquiries. Federal FCRA (15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.) governs adverse-action notice, dispute procedures, and limits on inquiries. Employers must provide FCRA-compliant disclosure and consent before obtaining consumer reports.

**Biometric Data and Electronic Monitoring**: No Kansas-specific statute regulates collection of biometric data (fingerprints, facial recognition, iris scans, voice recognition) or electronic monitoring (keystroke logging, GPS location tracking, surveillance cameras). Common-law privacy torts and federal ADA (regarding disability-related monitoring) apply. Employers have broad discretion to monitor employee communications and work-related activity; however, excessive monitoring or highly invasive monitoring in private areas may trigger tort liability or violate ADA if disability-related.

**Data Residency and Processing Constraints**: No Kansas state-specific requirements on where employee data is stored or which entities may process it. Federal law (FCRA for third-party background checks, HIPAA for health information, GLBA for financial data) applies. FERPA (Family Educational Rights and Privacy Act) does not apply to employment data.

**Notice and Consent**: No Kansas statute mandates written notice of data collection or employee access-to-personnel-file rights (but see Section 6 on common-law access). Employers should provide clear privacy policies and obtain written consent for sensitive data collection (e.g., health, background checks, genetic testing under GINA).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Kansas has **no ban-the-box law** at the state level. Employers may inquire about criminal history on applications, during interviews, or at any stage of the hiring process. No delay or restriction on criminal-history questions is mandated by state law. However, federal EEOC guidance and Title VII liability concern recommend delaying detailed criminal inquiries until later in hiring to reduce adverse impact on protected classes; best practice is to avoid up-front questions and use background checks after a preliminary interest in the candidate.

**Background Checks and FCRA Compliance**: Permitted and common. Employers use:

- Criminal-history checks via the Kansas Bureau of Investigation (KBI) or third-party screening vendors.
- Credit checks (limited by FCRA and restricted for certain positions).
- Sex-offender registry checks (permitted, especially for positions involving children or vulnerable populations).
  Federal FCRA (15 U.S.C. § 1681 et seq.) governs all consumer reports:
- Employers must provide clear written disclosure and obtain written authorization before obtaining a report.
- If a report will be used adversely (denial of hire, demotion, termination), employers must provide the candidate a pre-adverse action notice and copy of the report, allowing time to dispute inaccuracies.
- Post-adverse-action notice must explain the candidate's right to dispute.
  No Kansas state law imposes additional restrictions; federal FCRA is the floor.

**Drug Testing**: No Kansas statute prohibits pre-employment drug testing. Employers may test applicants and employees for drug use:

- Federal ADA (42 U.S.C. § 12114) prohibits testing for the presence of illegal drugs, but does not prohibit testing for alcohol or lawful substances.
- Drug testing does not trigger disability-discrimination concerns unless used to screen out individuals with disabilities (e.g., using prescription-medication detection as proxy for disability).
- Workers' compensation: K.S.A. 44-501(b)(1)(D) creates a rebuttable presumption of benefits if an employee is injured while under the influence of illegal drugs (employers cannot deny benefits solely on this basis; they must prove negligence or willful misconduct).
  No state-specific restrictions on timing, frequency, or procedures; federal law applies.

**E-Verify**: **No Kansas state mandate**. Federal Immigration Reform and Control Act (IRCA, 8 U.S.C. § 1324a) requires all employers to verify work authorization via Form I-9 (federal work-eligibility verification). E-Verify (a federal system) is voluntary and used by many employers to streamline verification; it is not required in Kansas unless:

- A specific federal contractor or federal-grant recipient agreement requires it (vary by agency).
- Local city or county ordinance mandates it (rare; verify with local HR departments).
  Employers that voluntarily use E-Verify must comply with federal E-Verify procedures and nondiscrimination rules (42 U.S.C. § 1324b).

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompete/Nonsolicitation

**WARN Act — Mass Separation Notice**: Federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (29 U.S.C. § 2102) applies to covered employers (100+ employees in any state). Requires 60 days' written notice before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50+ employees in a 30-day period at a single site or multi-site affected area. **Kansas has no separate state mass-separation (mini-WARN) notice requirement.**

**Severance Pay**: No Kansas state law mandates severance. Severance is voluntary and a matter of contract, employer policy, or discretion. If promised in writing (handbook, employment agreement, or severance plan), severance terms are enforceable; absent a written commitment, no severance obligation exists.

**Notice Period for Termination**: No Kansas statute mandates employer notice or warning before termination (at-will employment default). Employers should comply with any notice period in an employment contract, collective bargaining agreement, or handbook. However, federal WARN Act (60 days for mass layoffs) overrides at-will if triggered.

**Noncompete Agreements**: Enforced under Kansas common law (not a comprehensive statutory regime). The leading case, _Weber v. Tillman_, establishes that noncompete agreements are enforceable if reasonable in:

- **Duration**: typically 1–3 years from termination (longer terms scrutinized);
- **Geographic scope**: limited to areas where the employer actually operates or has legitimate business interests;
- **Line of business**: restricted to the employer's actual or prospective business.

Restrictions must protect legitimate business interests: trade secrets, confidential business information, customer relationships, or goodwill. Overly broad provisions are unenforceable or may be rewritten by courts (blue-pencil doctrine). Non-solicit clauses (barring solicitation of customers or employees) receive similar scrutiny.

**2025 Legislative Change — confirmed (2026-09-03 contradiction closure)**: SB 241 (2025 Kan. Sess. Laws ch. 74), signed April 9, 2025 and effective July 1, 2025, amends K.S.A. 50-163 (the Restraint of Trade Act) to create a **conclusive presumption of enforceability** for two categories of non-solicitation covenant: (1) employee non-solicits that protect confidential trade-secret, business, or customer/supplier information, or that run 2 years or less post-employment; and (2) customer non-solicits limited to material-contact customers that run 2 years or less post-employment. The Act also mandates judicial reformation (not invalidation) of an overbroad covenant that falls outside those safe harbors. **SB 241 does not reach traditional non-compete agreements** — the amended § 50-163 continues to exclude covenants not to compete from its presumption; non-compete enforceability in Kansas remains governed entirely by common law (_Weber v. Tillman_ reasonableness), unchanged by SB 241. Cite K.S.A. 50-163 (as amended by SB 241) for non-solicitation covenants only; cite common law for non-competes.

**Nonsolicitation Agreements**: Similarly enforced under common law. Non-solicitation of customers and employees (during employment and for a specified post-employment period) is generally enforceable if the period (typically 1–2 years) and scope (defined customer list or employee base) are reasonable and protect legitimate interests. Customer non-solicits receive stronger judicial support than employee non-solicits.

**Garden Leave / Payment for Restrictive Covenants**: Kansas does not mandate payment to employees for the burden of noncompete or nonsolicitation restrictions. However, courts may weigh lack of consideration as a factor in enforceability; mutual covenants (e.g., promise of continued employment) strengthen enforceability.

**Enforcement**: Breach of a noncompete or nonsolicitation covenant may result in injunctive relief (restraining the employee) and/or damages. Employers must prove a legitimate business interest and reasonableness of terms. Employees may challenge the covenant as overly broad or lacking consideration.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Kansas recognizes the **ABC test** for unemployment insurance purposes (K.S.A. § 44-701 et seq.) similar to other states. Control over work methods, provision of tools, integration into the business, and permanence determine status. Misclassification exposes employers to wage-and-hour audits and unemployment insurance liabilities.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Kansas-specific remote-work law. Federal FLSA applies to wages. State income-tax withholding is required if the employee works in Kansas; verify with Kansas Department of Revenue. Multi-state employers must comply with all applicable state laws where work is performed.

**Reciprocity**: Kansas recognizes only Kansas law for employment disputes involving Kansas-based work. Federal law supersedes on issues like minimum wage, overtime, and discrimination.

## 11. Typed Obligations and HCM Next Implementation

When the platform processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker in Kansas, the following obligations, notices, and constraints must be modeled:

**Hiring and Onboarding**:

- Record at-will employment status (unless a written contract specifies otherwise).
- Document pay rate and pay frequency at hire (K.S.A. 44-314: semimonthly minimum).
- Provide written notice of pay rate at hire (K.S.A. 44-320).
- If background checks are used, provide FCRA disclosure and obtain written consent.
- If noncompete/nonsolicitation will be required, provide a copy and mark for legal review.

**Promotion or Base-Pay Increase**:

- **Notice trigger**: Provide written notice of pay-rate increase before or at the time of change (K.S.A. 44-320); same-day notice is common practice but advance notice is safer.
- **Leave preservation**: Preserve accrued leave balances unless the written policy explicitly permits forfeiture (rare). Document leave balances at time of promotion.
- **Pay rate timing**: Implement the new rate at the next scheduled payday per K.S.A. 44-314 or sooner if the contract specifies.
- **Documentation**: Record the promotion date, new role, new pay rate, effective date, and leave balance in the employee's personnel file.
- **Anti-discrimination check**: Verify that the pay increase does not discriminate based on protected class (K.S.A. 44-1001 et seq., federal Title VII, ADEA for 40+).

**Base-Pay Decrease or Role Change**:

- **Mandatory written notice**: K.S.A. 44-320 and K.A.R. 49-20-1 require written notice of pay-rate reductions BEFORE the employee performs work under the reduced rate. Delay or failure exposes employer to wage-and-hour liability.
- **Timing**: Implement reductions only after notice is provided and acknowledged.
- **Leave impact**: Verify whether leave balances are affected (rare unless policy specifies).
- **Documentation**: Record the date notice was given, the new pay rate, the effective date, and employee acknowledgment.

**Termination or Resignation**:

- **Final pay calculation** (K.S.A. 44-315):
  - Include all earned wages, overtime, and applicable bonuses or commissions.
  - Include accrued leave (vacation, PTO) payout IF the written policy or contract requires it (not mandatory statewide; verify employer policy).
  - Exclude unearned bonuses or future incentives.
  - Pay on the next regular payday (or earlier if contract requires same-day final pay).
- **Anti-retaliation check**: Verify termination does not retaliate for jury duty (K.S.A. 43-173), voting (K.S.A. 25-418), military duty (K.S.A. 48-517), or workers' compensation claim (K.S.A. 44-501 et seq.).
- **WARN compliance**: If the employer has 100+ employees and termination is part of a plant closing or mass layoff (50+ in 30 days), trigger 60-day WARN notice workflow (29 U.S.C. § 2102).
- **Noncompete/nonsolicitation enforcement**: If a signed covenant exists, classify it first: non-competes are assessed under common-law reasonableness (_Weber v. Tillman_); non-solicits are assessed under K.S.A. 50-163 as amended by SB 241 (2025), which presumes the covenant enforceable if it fits one of the statute's safe harbors. Flag for legal review either way. Document in separation file.
- **Data breach check**: If personal information (SSN, financial data, etc.) was exposed during separation, trigger K.S.A. 50-7a01 breach notification (without unreasonable delay, typically 3–5 days). Notify Kansas AG if 250+ residents affected.

**Leave Coordination**:

- **FMLA employers** (50+ employees within 75 miles): Track FMLA eligibility (12 months employed, 1,250 hours worked). Coordinate promotions and pay changes with leave eligibility and accrual.
- **No paid leave mandate**: Kansas has no paid sick leave or PFMLA requirement. Employer policy controls accrual and payout.
- **Voting and jury duty**: Allow up to 2 hours for voting (K.S.A. 25-418) and jury service (K.S.A. 43-173) without discharge. Pay is employer discretion (common practice: paid, but not mandated).
- **Military leave**: Employees called to active duty under K.S.A. 48-517 receive reemployment rights per USERRA (38 U.S.C. § 4301 et seq.).

**Anti-Discrimination**:

- **Protected classes**: Apply federal Title VII, ADA, ADEA (40+), and GINA. Also apply K.S.A. 44-1001 et seq. (Kansas Act Against Discrimination, 4+ employees): race, color, religion, sex (including gender identity — **verify** current scope), national origin, ancestry, marital status.
- **Age**: K.S.A. 44-1111 (40+) mirrors federal ADEA. Verify pay comparisons across age groups.
- **Pay equity**: No mandatory audit, but equal pay is implied under K.S.A. 44-1002 and federal Equal Pay Act.

**Record Retention**:

- Retain payroll records (wage sheets, deductions, hours) for at least 3 years per federal FLSA (29 U.S.C. § 211); best practice 4 years.
- Retain personnel files for at least 1 year after termination (federal and Kansas practice).
- Retain I-9 and background-check records per FCRA and federal requirements.

**Employer Intake Questionnaire**:

1. **At-will status**: Do any employees have written employment contracts specifying term or conditions? If yes, load contract terms into the system.
2. **Pay frequency**: What is the current pay schedule (weekly, biweekly, semimonthly, monthly)? Ensure it meets K.S.A. 44-314 semimonthly minimum.
3. **PTO and leave policy**: What are the accrual, carry-over, and payout rules for vacation, sick leave, PTO? Document in system.
4. **FMLA coverage**: Does the employer have 50+ employees within 75 miles? If yes, enable FMLA tracking and coordination.
5. **Noncompete use**: Does the employer use noncompete or nonsolicitation agreements? If yes, provide copies for legal review and flag for post-termination enforcement.
6. **WARN coverage**: Does the employer have 100+ employees? If yes, enable WARN notice workflow for mass separations.
7. **Handbook policies**: Are there written policies on severance, notice periods, final-pay timing, or leave carryover? Load into system.
8. **Wage and hour**: Does the employer have employees not covered by FLSA? If yes, apply K.S.A. 44-1204 (overtime after 46 hours/week) instead of federal 40-hour threshold.
9. **Discrimination concerns**: Ensure system flags pay, promotion, and termination decisions for gender, age, and other protected-class analysis.
10. **Data security**: Does the employer collect or store employee personal data (SSN, financial info)? If yes, implement K.S.A. 50-7a01 breach-notification workflow and notify Kansas AG if 250+ residents affected.
