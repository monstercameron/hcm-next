# Illinois Employment Law Research

**State:** Illinois | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Pay transparency and Equal Pay Act (820 ILCS 112):** Effective Jan 1, 2025 (PA 103-0539), employers with 15+ employees must include pay scale in job postings, cannot screen applicants by salary history, must disclose pay range before offer, and extend equal pay protections to race-based discrimination; Equal Pay Registration Certificate required for 100+ employees.
- **Wage payment and final pay (820 ILCS 115):** Wages must be paid at least semimonthly on designated paydays; final pay (including accrued vacation) due immediately on termination or upon employee's written notice of resignation; deductions permitted only if legally required or written-authorized by employee.
- **Pay rate change notice (820 ILCS 115/3):** Employer must provide written notice of any change in pay rate, basis, payday, or wage deductions at least one pay period in advance (or as agreed).
- **Pay stub amendments (2025):** Enhanced pay stub requirements effective 1/1/2025 include: hours worked, rate of pay, gross wages, deductions (itemized), net pay, pay period dates, employer name/address; retain for 3 years.
- **Paid Leave for All Workers Act (820 ILCS 192):** Employees earn minimum 40 hours paid leave annually (1 hour per 40 hours worked), accrual begins Jan 1, 2024 or hire date (whichever later); Chicago and Cook County have more stringent local ordinances; paid leave cannot be forfeited on role change.
- **Separation notice (Illinois WARN, 820 ILCS 65):** Mass layoffs affecting 50+ employees in 30 days require 60-day notice to affected workers, state, and local officials; coal mining and electric generation operations require 2-year notice.
- **Non-compete and non-solicit thresholds (820 ILCS 90):** Non-competes void unless employee earns $75,000+ annually (increasing to $80,000 in 2027, $85,000 in 2032, $90,000 in 2037); non-solicits require $45,000+ threshold (increasing to $47,500, $50,000, $52,500); no blanket ban exists; statute maintains earnings-threshold model with specific exemptions (construction workers, unionized employees, mental health professionals serving veterans/first responders as of Jan 1, 2025).
- **Personnel file access (820 ILCS 40):** Employees have right to inspect and copy personnel records within 7 business days of written request (effective 2026-01-01).
- **Biometric data (BIPA, 740 ILCS 14):** Strict consent and disclosure requirements for biometric identifiers (fingerprints, face recognition, iris scans); written release required before collection; $1,000-$5,000 per violation per person (negligent: $1,000 or actual damages, whichever greater; intentional/reckless: $5,000 or actual damages, whichever greater); note: multiple instances of same collection method from same person = single violation.
- **AI in hiring (775 ILCS 5, 2025 amendment):** Employers cannot use predictive data analytics that proxies race via zip code in employment decisions; violation = civil rights violation.
- **Ban-the-box (820 ILCS 75):** Employers cannot inquire into criminal history before conditional job offer; background checks permitted after offer only if convictions directly relate to job duties.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Required Notices

Illinois is at-will employment with no state restriction on at-will doctrine. However, § 2810.5 (Wage Theft Protection Act) does not apply in Illinois; instead, § 115/3 requires written notice of **any change** in pay rate, basis, payday, or deductions **at least one pay period before** the change takes effect (or as agreed between employer and employee).

At hiring, employers are not required to provide a written notice summarizing wage terms under state law (unlike California's § 2810.5 model); however, best practice is to issue a written offer or employment agreement.

On **separation**, notice timing depends on whether termination is without cause (at-will) or constructive:

- **Termination without cause:** No notice required under state law; federal FMLA (50+ employees) requires 30 days if applicable.
- **Resignation:** Employee may provide notice of intent to resign; final pay must be paid immediately if employee provides written notice of termination, or within the next regular payday (no later than 15 days) if employee is laid off.

**800 ILCS 290/145 (Day and Temporary Labor Services Act):** Temporary workers and day laborers must be notified in writing of:

- Rate of pay (hourly, daily, or other)
- Overtime rate
- Expected hours of work
- Payday
- Deductions
- Pay frequency

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions, and Pay Statements

**Minimum wage:** Illinois § 105 (Minimum Wage Law) requires $15.00/hour effective Jan 1, 2025; indexed annually thereafter. Chicago minimum wage is $16.60/hour effective July 1, 2025. Cook County has local minimums in unincorporated areas; verify Cook County rates as they exceed state minimum.

**Overtime:** No state overtime rule beyond federal FLSA; federal 1.5x for 40+ hours/week applies.

**Pay frequency:** § 115/4 requires wages be paid **at least semimonthly** on designated paydays. Payday designation must be communicated to employees.

**Final pay:** § 115/5 requires all earned wages (including accrued vacation per § 115/2) be paid:

- **Immediately** on discharge (firing)
- **Immediately** if employee provides written notice of intent to resign (effective same day)
- **No later than 15 days** after last day of work if employee is laid off or resigns without written notice (or as otherwise agreed)

Failure to pay final wages within these timelines creates liability for unpaid wages plus 5% monthly penalties (accruing since 2021 amendments), administrative fees ($500–$1,250), and potential willful violation criminal charges.

**Deductions:** § 115/7 permits deductions only if:

- Legally required (taxes, garnishment, etc.)
- **Written authorization by employee** (in separate writing; not in employment contract alone per case law)

**Pay statement content:** § 115/9 and PA 103-0539 amendments (effective 2025-01-01) require itemized pay stub showing:

- Gross wages
- Hours worked (if hourly)
- Rate of pay
- Deductions (itemized)
- Net pay
- Pay period dates
- Employer name and address

**Pay statement timing:** No specific state requirement for electronic vs. paper; federal rules apply if applicable.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity: Salary Range, History Ban, Pay Data Reporting, Equal Pay

**Salary history ban (PA 103-0539, effective Jan 1, 2025):** 820 ILCS 112 prohibits employers from:

- Screening applicants based on current or prior wages or salary histories
- Requesting or requiring wage/salary history as condition of employment
- Seeking wage history from prior employers (limited exceptions for public records and internal promotions)

**Pay scale disclosure (PA 103-0539, 15+ employee requirement):** Employers with 15+ employees must:

- **Include pay scale and benefits** in every specific job posting
- **Disclose pay scale and benefits** to applicant prior to any offer or compensation discussion
- Satisfy via hyperlink to publicly available information
- Penalties: $500–$10,000 per violation (higher for subsequent offenses and active postings)

**Internal promotions:** PA 103-0539 requires promotion opportunities be posted to internal staff within 14 days of external posting.

**Equal Pay Act (820 ILCS 112):** Prohibits sex-based wage discrimination; **PA 103-0539 extends protection to race-based discrimination** (pay cannot differ based on race for substantially similar work requiring comparable skill, effort, and responsibility). Employee may not be retaliated against for requesting wage information or discussing wages.

**Equal Pay Registration Certificate:** Employers with 100+ employees must obtain Equal Pay Registration Certificate from Department of Labor. Recertify every 2 years; $150 filing fee; must submit wage records and compliance statements verifying equal pay compliance.

**Pay data reporting:** No state equivalent to federal EEO-6 or mandatory pay equity audit; federal EEO-1 applies if employer has 100+ employees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Paid Leave for All Workers, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid Leave for All Workers Act (820 ILCS 192, effective Jan 1, 2024):**

Employees working in Illinois earn **minimum 40 hours of paid leave per 12-month period** (or pro rata if shorter tenure), accrued at rate of 1 hour per 40 hours worked. Accrual begins on:

- Commencement of employment with employer, OR
- January 1, 2024 (whichever is later)

**Use restrictions:** Employee can use paid leave on **90th day** of employment. Employer can designate when paid leave is used (per 12-month period or calendar year).

**Carry-over:** Paid leave does not forfeit on role change, demotion, or separation. Accrued but unused paid leave must be paid out at employee's regular rate on termination.

**Chicago Paid Leave and Paid Sick and Safe Leave Ordinance (effective 2024-07-01):** Chicago requires 40 hours paid leave (general) + 40 hours paid sick and safe leave annually for employees of employers with 50+ employees in Chicago.

**Cook County Paid Leave Ordinance (effective 2024-01-01):** Cook County mandates minimum 40 hours paid leave annually for employees working in unincorporated Cook County.

**Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA):** Federal FMLA applies if employer has 50+ employees within 75 miles; 12 weeks unpaid job-protected leave per year for own serious health condition, family care, or new child (no state CFRA equivalent; Illinois does not provide paid FMLA substitute).

**Pregnancy disability leave:** No separate state requirement beyond FMLA; employer must accommodate per disability law and ADA as applicable.

**Paid leave interaction with pay change:** PA § 192 requires paid leave accrual balance to be preserved on role change, demotion, or termination; no forfeiture allowed. Pay changes do not reduce accrued balance.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Format, Employee Inspection Rights

**Personnel File Access (820 ILCS 40, amended by P.A. 104-158, effective 2026-01-01):** Employees have right to inspect and copy personnel records (performance evaluations, training records, disciplinary records, grievances) during reasonable business hours, at employee's request and employer's normal business location.

**Timing:** Employer must provide access and copies within **7 business days** of written request.

**Inspection scope:** Employer may redact medical records, attorney-client privileged records, and closed investigation files (pending or concluded) if redaction does not impair employee's understanding of personnel record. 2025 amendments clarified law enforcement personnel file scope to include "all records related to a law enforcement officer's performance, discipline, training, employment history, and any investigation."

**Payroll record retention:** § 115/14 requires payroll records (wage, hours, deductions) retained for **at least 5 years**. Pay stubs and records must be available for employee inspection.

**Personnel file retention:** No specific statutory period for personnel files; however, wage claims have 3-year statute of limitations (5 years for willful violations). Recommend retaining for 5+ years post-separation.

**Record format:** No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; copies can be provided electronically if employee consents.

**Penalties for non-compliance:** Violations of § 40 inspection rights may result in damages and attorney's fees (verify specific penalty amounts).

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Biometric Information Privacy Act (BIPA, 740 ILCS 14):**

Illinois strictly regulates collection, use, and storage of biometric identifiers (fingerprints, face scan, iris scan, voice patterns, keystroke patterns used for identification). Key requirements:

- **Written release required:** Employer must obtain **separate written consent** from employee before collecting biometric data; consent cannot be condition of employment unless biometric is necessary for business purpose.
- **Notice requirements:** Employer must inform employee of purpose for collection, duration of retention, and third parties accessing the data.
- **Storage and destruction:** Biometric data must be stored securely; destroyed within **3 years** of employee termination or expiration of need (whichever is sooner) OR when employee requests deletion (or 10 business days' notice).
- **Penalties:** $1,000–$5,000 per violation for collection without consent or improper storage; $1,000–$5,000 per person per day for breach of security; private right of action allows employees to sue.
- **Exceptions:** Police and corrections personnel; background check vendors (limited); legal proceedings.

**Personal Information Protection Act (815 ILCS 530):** Employers handling employee personal information must:

- Maintain reasonable security measures
- Notify employees of data breaches within reasonable time (no specific statutory timeline; best practice is 30–60 days)
- Notify affected individuals if breach involves unencrypted/unredacted personally identifiable information (names, SSN, financial account numbers, etc.)

**Employee data privacy (general):** No state-specific employee privacy statute beyond BIPA and breach notification; FCRA and state privacy laws (PIPA) do not broadly exempt employee data but employee records are addressed separately by common law and employment statutes.

**Monitoring and surveillance:** No state law prohibiting workplace monitoring; however, BIPA restricts biometric monitoring specifically. Employers should disclose monitoring practices and obtain consent where required by law.

**Data residency:** No state requirement for data residency in Illinois or US; however, employers must comply with federal data protection rules (GDPR-like requirements if applicable to EU employees).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Job Opportunities for Qualified Applicants Act (Ban-the-Box, 820 ILCS 75):**

Employers cannot ask about or consider applicants' criminal history before **conditional job offer**. Specifics:

- **Pre-offer prohibition:** Job applications, postings, and interviews cannot ask about arrests, convictions, or pending charges.
- **Post-offer evaluation:** After conditional offer, employer may conduct background check and consider convictions only if directly related and harmful to job duties. Employer must provide copy of background report to applicant and **opportunity to dispute** accuracy.
- **Exceptions:** Certain high-security or sensitive roles (law enforcement, childcare, healthcare) may have exemptions.

**Background checks (general):** Must comply with federal FCRA; obtain written consent and provide FCRA disclosures. State law does not impose additional restrictions beyond FCRA and ban-the-box.

**Drug testing:** No state prohibition on pre-employment or post-offer drug testing; employer testing is permitted if employer has written drug-free workplace policy. Testing must be reasonable and conducted by licensed lab.

**E-Verify mandate:** No state mandate for E-Verify use; federal I-9 verification rules apply. Illinois does not require employers to participate in E-Verify.

## 9. Separation: Illinois WARN (Mini-WARN), Severance, Notice, Non-Compete and Non-Solicit

**Illinois Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act (WARN, 820 ILCS 65):**

Employers must provide **60-day written notice** of mass layoff, plant closing, or relocation to:

- All affected employees (method ensuring receipt)
- Illinois Department of Commerce and Economic Opportunity (DCEO)
- Chief elected official of each city/county where layoff occurs

**Covered events:**

- **Plant closing:** Permanent/temporary shutdown of facility/site if 50+ employees lose jobs in 30 days (excluding part-time employees working fewer than 20 hours/week or employed fewer than 6 months)
- **Mass layoff:** 50+ employees in 30 days
- **Relocation:** Moving facility operations

**Special requirement (coal/utility):** Owners of coal mining operations or investor-owned electric generating plants must provide **2-year notice** before closure.

**Penalties for non-compliance:** Employer liable to each affected employee for unpaid wages up to 60 days of pay (or half the period of employment, whichever is less) plus benefits.

**Severance:** No state mandate for severance; employer may offer as separation agreement but not required by law. If offered, terms must be honored and may not condition severance on non-compete or non-disparagement unless enforceable under state law.

**Non-Compete Agreements (820 ILCS 90, Freedom to Work Act):**

Employers may enforce non-competes only if:

- **Employee earns $75,000+** annually (threshold increases: $80,000 on 1/1/2027, $85,000 on 1/1/2032, $90,000 on 1/1/2037)
- Covenant is ancillary to valid employment relationship
- Covenant is no greater than necessary for legitimate business interest
- Covenant does not impose undue hardship on employee
- Covenant is not injurious to public

**Categorical voids:**

- All collective bargaining agreement members
- All construction workers (except management, engineering, design, sales, ownership)
- Mental health professionals serving veterans/first responders (effective Jan 1, 2025)

**COVID-19 exception:** Employer cannot enforce non-compete against employee terminated/furloughed due to pandemic unless employer pays salary equivalent to base pay during restriction period, minus other earnings.

**Blanket ban status:** No blanket prohibition on non-competes exists. Illinois statute maintains earnings-threshold model with specific exemptions; no pending blanket-ban legislation has passed.

**Non-Solicit Agreements (820 ILCS 90):**

Employers may enforce non-solicits only if:

- **Employee earns $45,000+** annually (threshold increases: $47,500 on 1/1/2027, $50,000 on 1/1/2032, $52,500 on 1/1/2037)
- Same other requirements as non-competes (legitimate business interest, no undue hardship, not injurious to public)

**Notice requirement:** Employer must advise employee to consult attorney and provide **14 calendar days** for document review before non-compete/non-solicit is binding.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor test:** Illinois does not codify a single test (unlike California's AB 5 ABC test). Common law _right of control_ test applies: if hiring entity has right to control manner and means of work performance, worker is employee. Burden on hiring entity to prove contractor status.

**Domestic workers (820 ILCS 192):** "Employee" definition under Paid Leave Act includes domestic workers (household employees); same paid leave rules apply.

**Temporary/Day Labor (800 ILCS 290/145):** Day laborers and temporary workers must receive written notice of rate of pay, overtime rate, hours, payday, and deductions. Employer cannot charge workers for equipment or tools unless agreed in writing.

**Remote workers:** No special remote-work classification rule. Remote Illinois employees are subject to same minimum wage, overtime, pay frequency, and paid leave rules as on-site employees. Employer with Illinois employees must apply Illinois rules regardless of employer headquarters or remote nature of work.

**Multi-state reciprocity:** Illinois does not defer to another state's wage/hour laws if employee works in Illinois (even part-time). HCM Next must apply Illinois rules to any Illinois employee regardless of employer location or classification in other states.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

**Must-model obligations for promotion and base-pay change:**

1. **Pay rate change notice (§ 115/3):** Upon pay raise, cut, or basis change, system must flag 1-pay-period advance-notice requirement. Track completion and record notice delivery date. On promotion with pay change, ensure notice issued before effective date or within agreed timeline.

2. **Pay scale disclosure (PA 103-0539, 15+ employees):** System must store and surface current pay scale per position. On promotion to new role, system must ensure pay scale is available for employee access. On job posting export, include pay scale automatically (or alert if missing).

3. **Equal Pay compliance (PA 103-0539):** On promotion, system must allow entry of job level, skill, effort, responsibility to support equal pay documentation. Flag if promoted employee's new salary differs significantly from same-level incumbents (race/sex-agnostic comparison); require manager explanation/approval.

4. **Paid leave accrual preservation (820 ILCS 192):** On demotion or role change, system must preserve accrued paid leave balance; no forfeiture allowed. Display accrual balance before and after role change. On termination, calculate and include payout of unused paid leave in final pay calculation.

5. **Final pay calculation (§ 115/5):** On termination, system must calculate final pay as: gross wages + accrued vacation + accrued paid leave (paid at regular rate) + any other compensation owed. Flag if termination effective date is < 15 days away (triggers immediate or expedited final pay timeline). Alert employer of legal deadline (immediate if written notice; 15 days if not).

6. **Pay stub accuracy (§ 115/9):** System must generate pay stub on each pay cycle showing gross, deductions (itemized), net, hours (if hourly), rate of pay, pay period dates, employer name/address. Retain copies for 5 years.

7. **Non-compete/non-solicit voidance review (820 ILCS 90):** On any employee with non-compete or non-solicit clause, system must:
   - Check earning threshold ($75,000 for non-compete, $45,000 for non-solicit; adjust for inflation per statute)
   - Flag if covenant is void (below threshold, construction worker, mental health professional as of Jan 1, 2025)
   - Record 14-day review period before agreement becomes binding

8. **Biometric data consent (BIPA, 740 ILCS 14):** If system collects biometric data (fingerprint clock, facial recognition, etc.) for time-tracking or access control, obtain separate written consent before collection. Document purpose, retention period (max 3 years or employee request), and secure storage. Audit deletion on termination (10 business days max).

9. **Personnel file access requests (820 ILCS 40):** On employee request, system must flag 7-business-day deadline to provide inspection/copies. Track request receipt, redactions (if applicable), delivery date, and employee acknowledgment.

10. **Paid leave interaction with promotion:** On promotion with increased hours or responsibilities, system must recalculate paid leave accrual rate if threshold changes (e.g., part-time to full-time). Preserve existing accrual; apply new rate prospectively.
