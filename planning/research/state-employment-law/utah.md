# Utah Employment Law Research

**State:** Utah | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for Human Capital Management Suite

- **At-will employment**: Utah recognizes at-will employment doctrine; either party may terminate without cause. Public-policy exceptions exist for refusal to commit illegal acts, whistleblowing, jury duty, workers' compensation claims, and discrimination.
- **Minimum wage $7.25/hour**: Utah tracks federal FLSA floor; no state increase; no tipped-credit sub-minimum (tipped employees must earn $7.25/hour combined).
- **No state overtime law**: Overtime entirely governed by federal FLSA (40-hour weekly threshold, 1.5x pay); no state daily or alternative thresholds.
- **Pay frequency**: Semimonthly or more frequent required (§ 34-28-3); salaried employees may be paid monthly (on/before 7th of following month).
- **Final pay on termination**: Involuntary discharge within 24 hours; voluntary resignation on next regular payday (§ 34-28-5). Willful withholding triggers 60-day penalty accumulation.
- **Antidiscrimination**: 15+ employees required (§ 34A-5-101); protected classes include race, color, religion, sex, age 40+, national origin, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity (2015 SB 296), pregnancy; reasonable accommodations required.
- **Non-competes 1-year max**: Post-employment non-competes capped at 1 year from termination date (§ 34-51-102); exceeding 1 year is void (effective May 10, 2016).
- **No state paid sick leave mandate, no pay-transparency law, no salary-history ban, no personnel-file access statute for private employees, no mini-WARN act**.
- **E-Verify for 150+ employees** (§ 13-47-101); no penalty for non-compliance.
- **Breach notification** required without unreasonable delay if misuse of personal information reasonably likely (§ 13-44-101).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Utah recognizes at-will employment: either employer or employee may terminate without cause, notice, or specified reason.

**Public-policy exceptions** limit at-will termination when discharge violates clear and substantial public policy:

- Termination for refusing to commit illegal act or violating law.
- Termination for whistleblowing (reporting unlawful conduct in good faith; § 34-01-20 for private employees, § 67-21 for public employees).
- Termination for filing workers' compensation claim or applying for benefits (§ 34A-2-114).
- Termination for jury duty (§ 78B-1-116), military service (§ 39-3-1), or voting (§ 20A-3a-105).
- Termination based on protected-class discrimination (§ 34A-5-101).

Scope of public policy is "far narrower" than general public policy; must derive from express legislative or constitutional standards (Peterson v. Browning, 1992 Utah Supreme Court).

**Written notice requirements**: On hire, employer must inform employee of regular payday (§ 34-28-3). Advance notice of wage/payday changes required before implementation.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (§ 34-40-101):

- $7.25/hour statewide (federal floor; no state increase since 2009).
- No tipped-credit sub-minimum; tipped employees must earn $7.25/hour when tips included.
- No local minimum wage override.

**Overtime**: No state law. Federal FLSA governs: time and one-half for hours exceeding 40 in a workweek. No daily or consecutive-hours threshold. Paid holidays/vacation do not count toward 40-hour threshold.

**Pay frequency** (§ 34-28-3):

- Regular paydays required; semimonthly (twice/month) or more frequent.
- For salaried employees hired on yearly salary basis, monthly payment permitted (on/before 7th of following month).
- Wages earned during pay period due within 10 days after close of period.
- Direct deposit and stored-value card authorized if employee consents.

**Final pay on termination** (§ 34-28-5):

- **Involuntary discharge**: All earned, unpaid wages due within 24 hours of termination. Methods: hand delivery, direct deposit (transaction initiated within 24 hours), or mail (postmarked within 24 hours).
- **Voluntary resignation**: Wages due on next regular payday.
- **Penalty for willful withholding**: Wages continue to accrue at same rate for up to 60 days (max liability: 60 days additional wages).

**Permitted deductions** (§ 34-28-4):

- Deductions only if required by law (taxes, garnishments) or authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes (union dues, insurance, retirement, charitable).
- No deduction for tools, uniforms, costs unless employee consents in writing and does not reduce below minimum wage.
- Wage deductions must not be punitive.

**Pay statement content**: No state-mandated itemized format; employers should document hours worked, rate, deductions, gross/net pay per wage records requirement (§ 34-28-10).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**: No state mandate for job-posting salary ranges or wage disclosure at hire. Employers may inquire about prior salary/wages (exception: Salt Lake City public employers prohibited from asking).

**Salary history ban**: No state ban. Employers free to request and consider wage history (Salt Lake City local restriction on public employers only).

**Equal Pay for Substantially Equal Work** (§ 34A-5-106(1)(a)):

- Applies to all employers in Utah (15+ employee threshold for antidiscrimination).
- Prohibits payment of differing wages to employees having substantially equal experience, responsibilities, and skill for particular job.
- Exception: lawful seniority systems, merit systems, production-quality systems, or factor other than sex.
- Employer may not reduce other wages to comply.
- Enforcement through Human Rights Commission; private right of action for back pay and liquidated damages.

**Pregnancy accommodation as pay equity** (§ 34A-5-106(6)):

- Employer must provide reasonable accommodation for pregnancy, childbirth, breastfeeding, or related conditions upon request.
- Employer not required to accommodate if undue hardship demonstrated.
- Written notice of employee rights must be posted or included in handbook.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Paid sick leave**: No state mandate for private-sector paid or unpaid sick leave. Employers who voluntarily offer may set terms; employees cannot be penalized for using earned leave.

**Paid family/medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate. Employers may voluntarily offer. Federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees, 12-month tenure, 1,250 hours worked in past 12 months. Public-sector employees (state level) eligible for up to one week paid safe leave (effective Jan 1, 2025) and paid parental leave (effective July 1, 2026).

**Jury duty** (§ 78B-1-116): Employer may not discharge, threaten, or retaliate against employee for jury service. Employee may not be required to use vacation/sick leave for jury duty time.

**Voting leave** (§ 20A-3a-105): Employer must allow up to two hours absence on election day (between polls opening/closing). If requested at beginning/end of shift, employer shall grant. No wage deduction permitted.

**Military leave** (§ 39-3-1 et seq.): Members of U.S. armed forces reserves called to active duty may take up to five years unpaid leave. State employees in National Guard entitled to full pay for up to 15 days/year for annual encampment, rifle competition, or reserve training.

**Breastfeeding/lactation**: Federal PUMP Act applies (reasonable break time and private space for expressing milk, one year postpartum); no additional state mandate.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**: If employee uses voluntarily offered paid leave and is promoted/receives base-pay increase, wage must not decrease from pre-leave level upon return (best practice; state law silent).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and wage records** (§ 34-28-10):

- Employers must retain true and accurate records of time worked, hours worked, wages paid, and wage rates for at least **one year after entry** of the record.
- Construction-licensed employers must retain copies of pay statements for **three years** after giving copy to employee.
- Records subject to inspection by Department of Labor, Antidiscrimination and Labor Division agents at reasonable times.

**Employee access to personnel files**: No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic right to inspect or copy personnel records. Only public employees have statutory access rights (Utah Code Title 67, Chapter 18, under Government Records Access and Management Act - GRAMA); private employers not required to provide access.

**Record content and format**: No state-mandated electronic vs. paper requirement; either acceptable. No state-mandated pay-stub format. Employers should retain wage records showing names, addresses, dates of birth, hours worked, rates, deductions, gross/net pay.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Breach Notification** (§ 13-44-101 et seq., amended 2024):

- Applies to any person/business owning computerized data with personal information of Utah residents.
- Notification required without unreasonable delay if investigation reveals that misuse of personal information for identity theft or fraud has occurred or is reasonably likely.
- Notification methods: written mail to most recent address, electronic, telephone, or publication if infeasible.
- If 500+ Utah residents affected, notify Attorney General and Utah Cyber Center (effective May 1, 2024).
- If 1,000+ residents affected, also notify nationwide consumer reporting agencies.
- Encryption safe harbor: No notification required if data encrypted and encryption key not compromised.
- Penalties: Up to $5,000 per violation.

**Employee monitoring**: No state statute restricting workplace email/internet/video monitoring. Reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies under common law.

**Internet Employment Privacy Act** (§ 34-48-101 et seq.):

- Employer may not request or require employee/applicant to disclose username and password for personal online accounts (social media, email, etc.).
- Employer may not take adverse action or penalize refusal to disclose such information.
- Exceptions: employer-provided devices/accounts; investigation of unauthorized transfer of employer proprietary/confidential data; disciplinary action for same; investigation based on specific information about activity on personal account that may violate law/policy.
- Penalty: up to $500 per violation per affected individual.

**Utah Consumer Privacy Act** (§ 13-61-101, 2023):

- Employee data processed/maintained in course of employment or role is exempt from the act.
- Applies to "consumers" (individuals not acting in employment/commercial context).

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal history inquiry)**:

- Applies to **public employers only** (Utah has no statewide ban-the-box law for private employers).
- Private employers exempt; may inquire about criminal history at any stage.

**Background checks (private employers)**:

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions.
- Employers may request background checks at any stage.

**Drug testing** (§ 34-38-1 et seq., Utah Drug and Alcohol Testing Act):

- No comprehensive state mandate; employers have broad discretion.
- Pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, and random testing all permitted (except those handling high-level nuclear waste must test applicants/employees).
- Written policy required if testing implemented; must be distributed to employees and available to prospective employees.
- Testing may be required for investigation of individual employee impairment, investigation of workplace accidents/theft, or maintenance of workplace safety.

**E-Verify mandate** (§ 13-47-101, Private Employer Verification Act):

- Applies to employers with **150+ employees** (threshold increased May 4, 2022 from prior 15+ employees).
- Employer must register with and use E-Verify or similar status-verification system for new hires.
- No penalty for non-compliance; voluntary "Verify Utah" registration available.
- Employers may register regardless of size.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mass layoff notice (state level)**:

- **Utah has no mini-WARN act**. Federal WARN Act applies only: requires 60 days' notice for employers with 100+ employees; plant closing affecting 50+ employees or mass layoff affecting 500+ or 50-499 where 33%+ of workforce.

**Severance**: No state mandate; severance is contractual and requires explicit agreement.

**Non-compete agreements** (§ 34-51-102, effective May 10, 2016):

- Post-employment non-competes capped at **1 year from termination date**.
- Agreements exceeding 1 year are **void** and unenforceable.
- Exceptions: non-competes entered into as part of sale of business or sale of substantial assets may exceed 1 year if reasonably necessary to protect legitimate business interests (§ 34-51-202).
- Mental health professionals: if employer has waitlist longer than two months when agreement signed/employment ends, non-compete void regardless of duration (HB 186, 2025).
- Restrictions must be reasonable in duration, geography, and scope to protect legitimate business interests (trade secrets, customer relationships, confidential information).

**Non-solicitation agreements**:

- Common law applies; enforceable if reasonable in duration, geography, scope with legitimate business interest.
- No specific statute; generally less restrictive than non-competes.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Utah applies common-law test (not ABC test); factors include: integration into business, nature of services, control/direction, hiring/supervising assistants, set working times, and others.
- No presumption of employee status; employers have discretion if facts support contractor status.
- Misclassification enforced by Department of Labor, Department of Revenue, Attorney General; penalties include back wages and reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If work performed in Utah (remote or on-site), Utah law applies to that work.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Best practice: specify in writing that Utah law governs employment relationship for multi-state workers.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints**:

1. **Payday consistency** (§ 34-28-3): On promotion or base-pay change, verify payday remains regular and consistent. If payday changes, communicate advance notice to employee. Capture effective date in system.

2. **Base-pay increase implementation** (§ 34-28-3): Wage increase may be implemented immediately (no statutory advance notice required). For wage decrease, best practice: written advance notice (1+ pay period) and employee acknowledgment; capture in system.

3. **Minimum wage compliance** (§ 34-40-101): On promotion or base-pay change, verify wage meets $7.25/hour. Flag if promotion results in subminimum wage.

4. **Final pay on separation during/after promotion** (§ 34-28-5): If termination occurs post-promotion, ensure all wages earned at promoted rate from effective date forward are paid within 24 hours (involuntary) or next payday (voluntary). Do not forgive accrued final-pay penalty if promotion occurred.

5. **Equal Pay compliance** (§ 34A-5-106(1)(a)): On promotion or base-pay change within same establishment, flag if new wage for one sex differs materially from peer rates for comparable work; require manager justification or HR approval for outlier increases. Document non-discriminatory basis.

6. **Wage record retention** (§ 34-28-10): System must retain promotion records (wage change, effective date, justification) for minimum **1 year after employment ends**; construction-licensed employers must retain pay statements for **3 years**; support Department of Labor inspection rights.

7. **Non-compete/non-solicit review** (§ 34-51-102): On promotion, check if employee subject to non-compete. If entered before May 10, 2016, verify enforceability; if after May 10, 2016, cap at 1 year from promotion date is implicit (termination occurs later). If non-compete, document cap and void status if exceeded. If non-solicit exists, verify reasonableness; document in system.

8. **Antidiscrimination review** (§ 34A-5-101): On promotion, verify wage/terms not discriminatory based on protected class (race, color, religion, sex, national origin, age 40+, disability, sexual orientation, gender identity, pregnancy). Document non-discriminatory basis.

9. **Whistleblower protection** (§ 34-01-20 private employees, § 67-21 public): If promotion follows any protected whistleblower activity (good-faith report of unlawful conduct), flag for legal review to ensure retaliation risk is mitigated.

10. **Workers' compensation retaliation** (§ 34A-2-114): If employee recently filed workers' comp claim or applied for benefits, promotion and wage change must not appear retaliatory; document business justification. Employer prohibited from discharging, threatening, or disciplining employee for workers' comp claim.

11. **Final pay risk on resignation post-promotion** (§ 34-28-5): If employee resigns post-promotion, final pay at promoted wage due by next regular payday; system must track effective date to ensure correct final-pay wage rate and 60-day penalty accrual if employer willfully withholds.

12. **Pregnancy accommodation** (§ 34A-5-106(6)): If promotion involves role/schedule change, ensure no discriminatory impact on employee's ability to seek pregnancy-related accommodation. Document accommodation provided if requested.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether wage "substantially equal work" for Equal Pay Act comparisons (§ 34A-5-106) includes multi-site employers or same establishment only.
- Clarify whether non-compete 1-year cap applies prospectively only or retroactively to agreements entered before May 10, 2016.
- Confirm whether implied contract for "cause-only termination" in handbook can override at-will status after promotion or only at hire.
- Verify scope of Medical Marijuana cardholders' protections: employer right to prohibit working while impaired vs. discrimination for cardholder status.
- Clarify whether overtime threshold under federal FLSA (40 hours/week) is the only threshold or if paid leave accrual counts toward threshold.
