# Tennessee Employment Law Research

**State:** Tennessee | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with statutory exceptions**: Tennessee recognizes at-will employment as the default; exceptions include discharge for whistleblowing (Public Protection Act § 50-1-304), jury duty, military service, voting, workers' compensation claims, or union affiliation (right-to-work state).
- **Minimum wage**: $7.25/hour (federal minimum); no state override; federal FLSA applies to overtime (40+ hours/week).
- **Pay frequency**: At least monthly; if semi-monthly (2+ times/month), all wages earned prior to the 1st of the month due by the 20th of the following month. Biweekly, weekly, or daily also permitted (§ 50-2-103).
- **Final pay timing**: On termination, all wages due by next regular payday or 21 days after discharge/resignation, whichever is later (§ 50-2-103).
- **Employment discrimination**: Human Rights Act (§ 4-21-101 et seq.) covers 8+ employee employers; prohibited bases are race, creed, color, religion, sex, age, national origin; enforcement transferred to Attorney General's Division of Civil Rights Enforcement as of 2025.
- **Mandatory E-Verify**: Private employers with 35+ full-time equivalent employees must verify new hires via E-Verify as of January 1, 2023 (§ 50-1-703).
- **Pregnancy accommodation**: Employers with 15+ employees must provide reasonable accommodation for pregnancy-related medical needs and permit 4 months leave for adoption/pregnancy/childbirth (§ 4-21-408, effective October 1, 2020).
- **Plant closure notice**: Employers with 50–99 employees must notify affected employees and the state when reducing 50+ employees due to closing or relocation >50 miles (§ 50-1-601 et seq.; no penalty, notification-only).
- **No state-mandated paid sick or family leave**: Federal FMLA (50+ employees, unpaid) is the statutory floor; Tennessee preempts local ordinances that impose wage/benefit mandates (§ 50-2-112).
- **Non-competes**: Enforced under common law reasonableness test (time, scope, geography, legitimate business interest). Healthcare providers subject to statutory cap: 2 years maximum, 10-mile radius or county restriction (§ 63-1-148).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Tennessee follows at-will employment doctrine: employers and employees may terminate without cause or notice, except where statute or contract provides otherwise.

**Public policy exceptions to at-will** (§ 50-1-304 Public Protection Act):

- Discharge for refusing to participate in or remaining silent about illegal activities (federal or state criminal/civil law or public health/safety regulation).
- Discharge for jury duty (§ 22-4-106), military service (§ 8-33-109), or voting.
- Discharge for filing workers' compensation claim (§ 50-1-101 et seq.).
- Discharge based on union affiliation or refusal to join union (right-to-work; § 50-1-201).
- Discharge for lawful use of tobacco products or other lawful products off-duty, provided employee complies with workplace policies (§ 50-1-304(e)).

**Implied contract exception**: Handbooks or employment agreements containing clear, affirmative language guaranteeing discharge only for cause or progressive discipline create enforceable implied contracts limiting at-will status (case law; must show genuine intent).

**Notice at hire or change**: No state statute mandating written notice of employment terms at hire, but § 50-2-103 requires notice of regular paydays. No blanket requirement for notice before wage decreases, though § 50-2-103 regulates timing.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (§ 50-2-103): $7.25/hour; federal FLSA applies; no state override.

**Overtime** (federal FLSA applies): Time-and-a-half for all hours over 40 in a workweek; no daily threshold or other state override.

**Pay frequency** (§ 50-2-103):

- Employers must designate regular paydays: daily, weekly, biweekly, semi-monthly, or monthly.
- **Semi-monthly (2+ times per month)**: All wages earned prior to the 1st of any month must be paid by the 20th of the following month.
- **Monthly**: All wages earned prior to the 1st of any month must be paid by the 5th of the following month.
- Employer must post notice of paydays in at least two conspicuous places at worksite.
- Bonuses, commissions, or other forms of compensation may be paid as infrequently as annually if prescribed in advance.

**Final pay on termination** (§ 50-2-103):

- All wages earned must be paid no later than the next regular payday or 21 days following discharge/resignation, whichever occurs last.
- Final pay must include accrued paid time off if employer's written policy provides for payout; conversely, forfeiture is enforceable if policy clearly states so.
- Payment methods: cash, check, direct deposit (if previously authorized), or payroll card permitted.

**Permitted deductions** (§ 50-2-103): Deductions allowed only if required by law (tax, garnishment) or authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes. Deduction must not reduce wages below applicable minimum wage.

**Pay statement content**: No state-mandated itemized format; federal FLSA requirements apply.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary history ban**: No state statute prohibits inquiries into prior wages (federal EPA and Pregnancy Discrimination Act apply).

**Pay transparency in postings**: No state-wide requirement for salary range disclosure in job postings.

**Equal pay statute**: No separate state equal-pay law; federal Equal Pay Act (FLSA § 206(d)) applies.

**Employment discrimination** (Human Rights Act, § 4-21-101 et seq.):

- Applies to employers with 8 or more regularly employed employees.
- Prohibited bases: race, creed, color, religion, sex, age, national origin.
- Protects against discrimination in hiring, compensation, terms/conditions of employment, termination, demotion.
- **Enforcement change (2025)**: Moved from Tennessee Human Rights Commission to Attorney General's Division of Civil Rights Enforcement.
- Worksharing available: federal EEOC intake + state division can preserve rights under both systems; strict filing deadlines apply.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave**: No state mandate for private employers; employer discretion.

**Paid family and medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate; federal FMLA (12 weeks unpaid, 50+ employees, 12-month period) is the statutory floor.

**School involvement leave**: No state statute identified; federal FMLA applies if eligible.

**Domestic violence leave**: No state statute identified; verify case law and federal protections.

**Pregnancy-related leave** (§ 4-21-408, Pregnant Workers Fairness Act, effective October 1, 2020):

- Applies to employers with 15 or more employees.
- Reasonable accommodation required for medical needs arising from pregnancy, childbirth, or related conditions.
- Employee may take up to 4 months leave for adoption, pregnancy, childbirth, or nursing infant if employed full-time for at least 12 consecutive months.
- Specific accommodations include flexible/frequent breaks, modified schedule, etc.
- Employer cannot require leave if another accommodation suffices; retaliation prohibited.

**Military service leave** (§ 8-33-109):

- State and private employees entitled to 20 days paid leave per year for active duty (Tennessee service or federal service).
- No loss of pay, regular leave, vacation, efficiency rating, or other benefits.
- Federal USERRA (38 U.S.C. § 4301 et seq.) also applies; more favorable provision controls.

**Jury duty** (§ 22-4-106):

- Employers with 5+ employees must pay usual compensation for jury duty.
- Employer may deduct jury service fee.
- Retaliation prohibited; no termination solely for jury duty.

**Interaction with promotions**: On promotion during leave, do not reduce post-leave wage below pre-promotion level if promotion occurs during protected leave period.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- § 50-2-103 requires true and accurate records of hours, wages, ages (minors), and other conditions essential to enforcement.
- No specific retention period mandated by state; federal FLSA = 3 years minimum for wage claims (verify whether state-imposed retention differs).
- Minor employment certificates retained for 2 years after termination.

**Employee access to personnel files**: No state statute grants private-sector employees automatic right to inspect or copy personnel records (applies to public employees only).

**Record format**: No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; either acceptable if retention/access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data breach notification** (§ 47-18-2107, Release of Personal Consumer Information):

- Timeline: Notify affected individuals immediately, but no later than 45 days from discovery or notification of breach.
- Definition: Breach = unauthorized acquisition of computerized data materially compromising security/confidentiality/integrity of personal information.
- Safe harbor: Encrypted data is not a breach if encryption key not compromised.
- Scope: Applies to any person/business owning, licensing, or maintaining computerized personal information of Tennessee residents.
- Law enforcement delay allowed if notification would impede investigation.

**Workplace monitoring and privacy**: No state statute restricting email/internet monitoring or video surveillance; common law reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies (minimal protection).

**Employee Online Privacy Act** (§ 50-1-1001 et seq., effective January 1, 2015):

- Employers prohibited from requesting or requiring employees/applicants to disclose passwords for personal Internet accounts.
- Employers not liable for failure to monitor personal accounts.
- Employers may comply with federal law (SEC self-regulatory organizations, law enforcement, investigations into officer conduct) or state duties requiring monitoring.

**Data residency**: No state law mandating data residency for employee records; out-of-state storage permitted if breach notification and security laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box for private employers**: No state statute; federal standards apply.

**Background checks**: Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions on third-party providers (notice, dispute, adverse-action procedures required).

**Drug testing** (§ 50-9-101 et seq., Drug-Free Workplace Program):

- Voluntary program; employers not required to participate.
- If implemented per statute (notice, education, testing procedures), employer may require drug/alcohol tests.
- Positive result may trigger termination and loss of workers' compensation eligibility.
- No state prohibition on pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, or random testing.

**E-Verify mandate** (§ 50-1-703, Tennessee Lawful Employment Act):

- **Applies to private employers with 35+ full-time equivalent employees**, as of January 1, 2023.
- New hires must be verified within 3 business days of start date.
- Applies to all new hires working in Tennessee regardless of employer HQ location (counts employees under same FEIN).
- Exemptions: Employers <35 FTE; employment term <9 months in calendar year.
- Enforcement: First-time violators must file sworn affidavit of compliance within 7 days of notice; failure triggers $10,000 civil penalty per violation.
- Failure to comply may result in loss of business license and other civil remedies.
- Smaller employers (<35 FTE) may request copy of qualifying documents (I-9 documents) as alternative.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mini-WARN act** (§ 50-1-601 et seq., Plant Closings and Reduction in Operations Act):

- Applies to employers with 50–99 employees (federal WARN Act covers 100+, creating gap).
- Triggered by reduction of 50+ employees over any 3-month period due to closing, modernization, or relocation >50 miles.
- Notification-only: Employer must notify affected employees and telephone state Department of Labor and Workforce Development Commissioner.
- No set notice period; no penalty or private right of action.
- Unlike federal WARN Act, no exception for unforeseeable business circumstances.

**Severance**: No state mandate; contractual and voluntary only.

**Non-compete enforceability** (common law + § 63-1-148):

- Non-healthcare providers: Enforced under common law reasonableness test (time, scope, geography, legitimate business interest). Must be written, signed, based on valuable consideration. Narrowly construed against drafter; single defect defeats enforcement.
- Healthcare providers (physicians except emergency specialists, chiropractors, podiatrists, dentists, optometrists, psychologists): § 63-1-148 presumptively reasonable if (1) written, (2) 2 years or less duration, (3) confined to 10-mile radius or county of primary practice location (or narrower). Rebuttable presumption of reasonableness.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**: No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness test (time, scope, geography, legitimate business interest).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**: Common law "right to control" test with eight factors; no single factor determinative.

- Key factors: Control/direction; integral to business; permanency; contractor's equipment investment; profit/loss opportunity; independent judgment; independent business operation; totality of circumstances.
- Enforcement by NCDOL, Revenue Department, Attorney General; remedies include back wages, reclassification, penalties.

**Remote workers**: No specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of work location. If work performed in Tennessee (remote or on-site), Tennessee law applies.

**Multi-state employment**: No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints for promotion and base-pay changes**:

1. **Minimum wage compliance** (§ 50-2-103): Verify wage meets $7.25/hour. Flag if promotion results in wage below minimum.

2. **Pay frequency verification** (§ 50-2-103): On promotion, verify payday remains regular (daily through monthly). If payday changes, flag per employer policy.

3. **Final pay interaction**: If termination occurs after promotion, ensure all wages at promoted rate due on next regular payday or within 21 days of discharge.

4. **Pregnancy accommodation interaction** (§ 4-21-408, 15+ employees): If employee is pregnant/postpartum and requests reasonable accommodation, do not condition promotion on accommodation refusal or denial. Maintain wage from newly promoted position during any approved leave.

5. **Whistleblower retaliation check** (§ 50-1-304): If employee recently reported illegal activity, flag promotion timing and ensure timing does not appear retaliatory and cannot be inferred as discharge threat.

6. **E-Verify compliance verification** (§ 50-1-703, 35+ FTE employers): On promotion, confirm employee's I-9/E-Verify status remains valid; flag if verification window approaching renewal (3-year/1-year reverification may be needed).

7. **Jury duty/military protection** (§ 22-4-106, § 8-33-109): Do not deny promotion or reduce wage based on prior jury duty or military service leave. Maintain service-accrued benefits.

8. **Right-to-work compliance** (§ 50-1-201): Promotion cannot be denied or conditioned on employee's union affiliation or refusal to join/affiliate.

9. **Tobacco/lawful product protection** (§ 50-1-304(e)): Promotion cannot be denied or conditioned on employee's lawful off-duty use of tobacco or other lawful products (provided employee complies with workplace policies).

10. **Records retention** (§ 50-2-103): Retain promotion documents (wage change, effective date, justification) per employer record policy (verify whether 3 years or employer-set); support labor commissioner inspection.

11. **Handbook contract review**: If handbook or offer letter promises "cause-only termination," flag for legal review to determine if implied contract limits at-will status post-promotion.

12. **At-will affirmation**: If no handbook promise, document promotion as continuing at-will employment; employer retains right to terminate without cause.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether § 50-2-103 notice requirement applies to all wage reductions or only to periodic rate decreases.
- Clarify what constitutes "prescribed in advance" for annual bonus/commission payment (calendar-year notice vs. offer-letter specification).
- Determine status of implied at-will-override contracts (handbooks, verbal promises) after promotion or only at hire.
- Verify whether 2026 paid sick leave bill (if enacted) would affect promotion interaction and accrual floor.
- Confirm whether Pregnant Workers Fairness Act (§ 4-21-408) 4-month leave is post-childbirth, post-due-date, or customizable.
