# Wyoming Employment Law Research

**State:** Wyoming | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with limited exceptions**: Wyoming recognizes at-will employment; public policy exceptions exist for workers' compensation claims, jury duty, whistleblowing, military service, voting, and discrimination under WFEPA; implied contract exception applies if handbook creates binding disciplinary procedures.
- **Minimum wage $7.25/hour**: Wyoming tracks federal FLSA floor (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-202); no state increase; tipped employees $2.13/hour with wage guarantee to reach $7.25/hour.
- **No state overtime mandate**: Wyoming defers to federal FLSA 40-hour weekly threshold; exception for public works (8-hour daily trigger for laborers).
- **Semimonthly pay frequency**: Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101 requires semimonthly payment for industrial/factory operations; itemized pay stub showing all deductions; other employers may set own schedules.
- **Final pay on termination**: Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-104 requires all earned wages paid by next regular payday or by employer's usual payment schedule; no statutory final-pay acceleration; 18% statutory interest + attorney fees if pay is wrongfully withheld.
- **Non-competes nearly void (SF 107, effective 2025-07-01)**: Wyo. Stat. § 6-3-501 et seq. voids most post-employment non-competes except for executives/managerial staff, sale of business, trade secret protection, and recovery of training/relocation expenses.
- **Protected classes under WFEPA**: Wyo. Stat. § 27-9-101 et seq. prohibits discrimination based on race, color, creed, sex, national origin, ancestry, age 40+, disability, pregnancy; applies to all employers (1+ employee).
- **Equal pay mandate**: Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-302 requires equal pay for comparable work requiring equal skill, effort, responsibility, and working conditions within same establishment.
- **No state paid sick leave mandate, no PFML mandate, no mini-WARN act, E-Verify optional (public sector only), no salary-history ban, no pay-transparency posting mandate, no personnel-file access statute for private sector**.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Wyoming follows at-will employment: either party may terminate without cause or notice. **Public policy exceptions** limit at-will termination:

- Termination for filing workers' compensation claim or applying for benefits (Wyo. Stat. § 27-14-101 et seq.; retaliatory discharge tort exists).
- Termination for jury duty, military service, or voting (Wyo. Stat. § 1-11-401; § 22-2-111).
- Termination for whistleblowing (reporting unlawful conduct to law enforcement or governmental body; common law).
- Termination for refusal to perform illegal act.
- Termination based on protected class discrimination (WFEPA, § 27-9-101 et seq.).
- Termination for asserting FLSA or state wage rights.

**Implied contract exception**: Employee handbook, personnel policies, or letters of employment stating disciplinary procedures or listing causes for termination may create binding implied contract if not disclaimed; requires **clear, conspicuous, and prominent** at-will disclaimer to preserve at-will status (Trabing v. Kinko's, Inc., 2002).

**Covenant of good faith and fair dealing**: Implied in every employment contract; limits extreme or arbitrary employer conduct.

**Written notice requirements**: Employers should inform employees at hire of regular payday (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101). No statutory notice requirement for pay rate changes; best practice is advance notice.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-202):

- Statewide minimum: $7.25/hour (federal FLSA floor; no state increase since 2001).
- Tipped employees: $2.13/hour if tips customarily exceed $30/month; employer must ensure total compensation (wage + tips) meets $7.25/hour.

**Overtime**: No state mandate beyond federal FLSA; 1.5x for hours exceeding 40 in workweek. Exception: Public works projects (laborers, workmen, mechanics) trigger 1.5x for 8+ daily hours or 40+ weekly hours (verify statute).

**Pay frequency** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101):

- Industrial/factory operations, mines, refineries, oil/gas production: semimonthly required (on or before 1st for first half of month; on or before 15th for second half).
- Other employers: no state mandate; may establish own schedule.
- Itemized pay statement (detachable check stub) required showing all deductions.

**Final pay on termination** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-104):

- All earned wages due by next regular payday or by employer's usual practice; no statutory acceleration (e.g., "within 5 business days").
- Applies to voluntary resignation, involuntary discharge, and layoff.
- Offset right: Employer may deduct sums owed by employee to employer if properly documented.
- No statute on accrued vacation/leave forfeiture; common law applies (verify employer policy enforceability).

**Permitted deductions** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101(b)):

- Deductions allowed if required by law (taxes, garnishments, court orders) or authorized in writing by employee for lawful purposes.
- All deductions must be itemized on pay stub.

**Penalties for violation**: Misdemeanor, $500–$750 per violation; prevailing employee in suit for withheld wages entitled to 18% statutory interest and reasonable attorney fees (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-104).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**: No state mandate for job-posting salary ranges; employers may request prior wage history.

**Salary history ban**: No state prohibition.

**Pay equity**: Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-302 prohibits wage discrimination based on gender for equal work within same establishment. Exceptions: seniority, merit, production-quality systems, factor other than sex.

**Enforcement**: Wyoming Department of Workforce Services; private right of action for back pay and liquidated damages.

**Protected classes** (WFEPA, Wyo. Stat. § 27-9-101 et seq.):

- Applies to all employers (1+ employee).
- Race, color, creed, sex, national origin, ancestry, age 40+, disability, pregnancy.
- Sexual orientation and gender identity **not** protected under state law (verify local ordinance coverage).
- Retaliation prohibited.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, PFML, Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave**: No state mandate. Employers who voluntarily offer sick leave may set terms; employees cannot be penalized for using earned leave.

**Paid family/medical leave (PFML)**: No state mandate. Federal FMLA applies if 50+ employees, 12-month tenure, 1,250 hours worked in past 12 months.

**Jury duty** (Wyo. Stat. § 1-11-401): Employer may not discharge or threaten employee for jury service. Jury duty leave is **unpaid**; jurors receive $30/full or part day, +$20/day after 5 days at court discretion (Wyo. Stat. § 1-11-103).

**Voting** (Wyo. Stat. § 22-2-111): Employer must provide one (1) hour **paid** leave to vote if employee lacks three (3) or more consecutive off-duty hours while polls open.

**Military service**: No state statute (verify; federal USERRA applies).

**Breastfeeding/lactation**: Federal PUMP Act applies; no additional state mandate.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**: If employee uses voluntarily offered paid leave and is promoted/receives base-pay increase, wages must not decrease upon return (best practice; state law silent).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and wage records** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101(b)):

- Employers must maintain true and accurate records of hours worked, wages paid, and all deductions.
- Retention period: At least 2 years (or longer if required by federal law).
- Records subject to inspection by Department of Workforce Services at reasonable times.

**Employee access to personnel files**: No state statute mandates access for private-sector employees; public employees have limited access under public records law (Wyo. Stat. § 16-4-101 et seq., with exemptions).

**Record format**: No state mandate for electronic vs. paper; either acceptable.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Breach Notification, Monitoring

**Data breach notification** (Wyo. Stat. § 40-12-501):

- Applies to any entity owning computerized personal information of Wyoming residents.
- Notification required in most expedient time possible without unreasonable delay (45 days industry standard, verify).
- Must notify affected Wyoming residents; if 1,000+ residents affected, notify Wyoming Attorney General.
- Personal information: SSN, driver's license, state ID, or account/credit card number + security code.

**Employee monitoring**: No state statute restricting email/internet/video monitoring; reasonable-expectation-of-privacy doctrine applies under common law.

**Data residency**: No state law mandating in-state storage/processing.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box** (criminal history inquiry):

- Applies to **public employers only**; private employers exempt.

**Background checks (private sector)**:

- Federal FCRA applies; no additional state restrictions.

**Drug testing**:

- No comprehensive state statute; employers have broad discretion.
- Pre-employment, reasonable-suspicion, post-accident, random testing permitted.
- If test required as condition of employment, employer must pay (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-403, verify).
- Written policy recommended.

**E-Verify mandate**: Public employers and state contractors only; private employers optional.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit

**Mass layoff notice**:

- Federal WARN Act applies (100+ full-time employees; 60 days' notice for 25+ affected).
- No Wyoming mini-WARN act; Wyoming is not among seven states with state-level layoff statutes.

**Severance**: No state mandate; contractual if explicitly agreed.

**Non-compete agreements** (Wyo. Stat. § 6-3-501 et seq.; SF 107 effective 2025-07-01):

- Post-employment non-competes **void and unenforceable** except:
  - Executive or managerial personnel and professional staff directly supporting them (definition verify; narrow interpretation expected).
  - Sale of business or sale of substantial assets.
  - Protection of trade secrets (as defined in Wyo. Stat. § 6-3-501(a)(xi)).
  - Recovery of training and relocation expenses (verify scope).
- Existing non-competes signed before 2025-07-01 may remain enforceable under prior law; new agreements signed on/after 2025-07-01 subject to SF 107.
- Geographic/duration/scope must be reasonable if carve-out applies.

**Non-solicitation agreements**: Common law applies; enforceable if reasonable in duration, geography, scope protecting legitimate business interest (customer relationships, trade secrets, confidential information).

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Wyoming applies modified ABC test (Prongs A and C only, per Wyo. Stat. § 27-3-104):
  - **Prong A**: Free from control/direction; contractor determines means and manner of work.
  - **Prong C**: Independently established in that line of work.
- Mere contract designation insufficient; facts must support contractor status.
- Misclassification enforced by Department of Labor; penalties include back wages and reclassification.

**Remote workers**:

- No specific statute; standard employment law applies regardless of location.
- If work performed in Wyoming, Wyoming law applies.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Best practice: specify in writing that Wyoming law governs (or law of work location).

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B: Promotion + Base-Pay Change Modeling

**Mandatory obligations and constraints**:

1. **Pay-frequency verification** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101): On promotion or base-pay change, confirm payday remains regular/consistent. Communicating payday changes in advance recommended (not statutorily required but best practice).

2. **Base-pay increase implementation** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101): Wage increase may be implemented immediately; no statutory advance notice required. For wage decrease, advance written notice (1+ pay period) and employee acknowledgment recommended.

3. **Minimum wage compliance**: On promotion or base-pay change, verify wage meets $7.25/hour (or $2.13 for tipped employees with guarantee to $7.25). Flag subminimum wage errors.

4. **Final pay timing post-promotion** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-104): If termination occurs post-promotion, all wages earned at promoted rate from effective date forward due by next regular payday or employer's usual payment schedule. No statutory acceleration.

5. **Equal pay flag** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-302): On promotion or base-pay change within same establishment, system should flag if new wage differs materially from peer rates for comparable work; require manager justification or HR approval for outlier increases.

6. **Wage record retention** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101): System must retain promotion records (wage change, effective date, business justification) for minimum 2 years to support Department of Workforce Services inspection and defense against wage claims.

7. **Non-compete voidness check** (SF 107, effective 2025-07-01): On promotion, check if employee subject to non-compete. If non-executive (and not in sale/trade-secret carve-out), mark as **void and unenforceable** under SF 107. Document in system that non-compete cannot be enforced post-promotion.

8. **Handbook implied contract** (Trabing v. Kinko's, 2002): If promotion involves change to termination procedures or at-will status, verify handbook has **clear, conspicuous** at-will disclaimer to preserve at-will employment. If employee handbook lists causes for termination and procedures are bypassed, employer risks implied contract breach claim.

9. **Workers' compensation retaliation** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-14-101 et seq.; common law tort): If employee recently filed workers' comp claim or applied for benefits, promotion and wage change must not appear retaliatory. Document business justification (merit review date, performance data, budget cycles, etc.).

10. **WFEPA compliance** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-9-101 et seq.): On promotion, verify wage/terms not discriminatory based on protected class (race, color, creed, sex, national origin, ancestry, age 40+, disability, pregnancy). Document non-discriminatory basis (performance, market rate, seniority, etc.).

11. **Itemized pay stub** (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-101(b)): After promotion, ensure pay stub clearly shows new wage rate, effective date, and all deductions itemized. Failure to provide invokes misdemeanor penalty.

12. **Jury duty/voting leave interaction**: If promoted employee is called for jury duty or voting, confirm employer honors unpaid jury duty leave (or paid voting leave per § 22-2-111) without retaliation. Promotion timing should not interfere with statutory leave rights.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify scope of "executive or managerial personnel" exception to non-compete void under SF 107; whether mid-level managers, department heads, or only C-suite qualify.
- Clarify whether wage decrease requires affirmative employee consent or advance notice; whether oral vs. written notice sufficient under § 27-4-101.
- Confirm whether "same establishment" for equal pay comparisons (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-302) means single worksite or multi-site employer.
- Verify public-works overtime trigger (8-hour daily) statute citation and applicability to private-sector promotions.
- Confirm drug-testing employer cost obligation (Wyo. Stat. § 27-4-403) and applicability to post-promotion screening.
