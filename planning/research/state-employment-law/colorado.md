# Colorado Employment Law — HCM Next Research

**Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Scope:** Private-sector employment in Colorado

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Job postings and promotions** must include hourly or salary range, benefits description, and application-window close date; employers must notify Colorado employees of job opportunities before selection and then notify colleagues of who was hired/promoted within 30 calendar days (EPEWA, CRS § 8-5-101 et seq., amended Jan 1, 2024).
- **Base-pay changes** cannot use applicant's salary history; employers cannot enforce pay-secrecy policies; equal-pay rules (CRS § 8-5-101 et seq.) prohibit paying employees of one sex less than another sex for substantially similar work.
- **Manager changes** that constitute promotions or job opportunities trigger the notification and posting requirements above; a manager transfer of the same employee may not; verify scope.
- **Termination** requires immediate final-pay delivery if involuntary, or payment on next regular payday if resignation; accrued vacation must be paid out within 14 days of written demand (no "use-it-or-lose-it" policies allowed); separate unemployment-insurance notice must be provided with reason for termination (SB 22-234).
- **Paid leave** obligations: 48 hours paid sick leave per year (Healthy Families and Workplaces Act, CRS § 8-3-1 et seq.), plus 12 weeks paid family/medical leave if eligible under Colorado FAMLI (family members, newborn, or personal medical condition; employer or private plan required).
- **Payroll records** and personnel files must be retained for at least 3 years; employees have a right to inspect and copy their own personnel file annually.
- **Non-competes** are void unless the employee earns at or above the "highly compensated worker" threshold AND the restriction is no broader than necessary to protect trade secrets (CRS § 8-2-113, amended Aug 6, 2025).
- **Biometric data** (fingerprints, iris, voice, face recognition) requires informed, written consent, a written retention/deletion policy, and destruction by the earlier of: original purpose fulfilled, 24 months after last interaction, or 45 days after determining retention is no longer necessary (HB 24-1130, effective July 1, 2025).

## 2. Employment Relationship

Colorado is an at-will employment state (CRS § 8-4-100). In the absence of a contract to the contrary, neither employer nor employee is required to give notice or advance notice of termination or resignation, and neither is required to give a reason.

**Exception: Separation Notice.** As of May 25, 2022, Colorado employers must provide a written separation notice to all employees whose employment is terminated, regardless of the reason, informing them of unemployment-insurance eligibility and the reason for termination (SB 22-234, amending CRS § 8-90-107(2)). The notice must be delivered at or before the time of separation.

**No mandatory hire notice.** No state rule requires a written offer letter or at-hire notice beyond federal requirements (I-9, tax withholding). Employers should document employment terms to avoid disputes.

## 3. Wages

**Minimum wage:** $15.16 per hour statewide as of January 1, 2026 (CRS § 8-6-102); local jurisdictions (Denver, Boulder, Edgewater) set higher floors. Denver: $19.29/hour (2026). Tipped employees must be paid at least $12.14/hour, with tips supplementing to the state minimum.

**Overtime:** Colorado's COMPS Order #40 (7 CCR 1103-1) requires time-and-a-half pay after 12 hours worked in a workday OR 12 consecutive hours. When Colorado and federal (FLSA) rules differ, the employee-favorable standard applies. Hours worked in separate workweeks cannot be averaged.

**Pay frequency:** Employers shall pay wages at least monthly (CRS § 8-4-102). No statutory prohibition on biweekly or twice-monthly, but monthly is the standard minimum.

**Final pay on termination (involuntary):** Compensation for labor earned, vested, determinable, and unpaid at discharge is due immediately (CRS § 8-4-109). If payroll department is not operational, wages are due within 6 hours of the next business day's start. If wages remain unclaimed after 60 days, employer must mail to employee's last-known address. Willful violation triples wages; automatic penalties can double.

**Final pay on resignation:** Wages become due on the next regular payday (CRS § 8-4-109).

**Accrued vacation/PTO:** Must be paid out upon separation (paid or unpaid); "use-it-or-lose-it" policies are void (Nieto v. Clark's Market, 2021 CO 27). Employer must pay within 14 days of written demand or face double wages or 10 days' average daily pay, whichever is greater (Colorado Supreme Court ruling).

**Pay statement requirements:** Employers must provide an itemized pay statement at each wage payment showing gross pay, deductions (with reason for each), net pay, pay period, and rate(s). Statements may be electronic (CRS § 8-4-103). Records must be retained for 3 years.

**Deductions:** Only legally required deductions (taxes, FICA, unemployment insurance, wage garnishments, court orders) and authorized voluntary deductions (health insurance, 401(k), etc.) are permitted; unlawful deductions make the entire pay subject to wage-claim penalties (CRS § 8-4-105).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Equal Pay for Equal Work Act (EPEWA, CRS § 8-5-101 et seq.):** Employers shall not pay employees of one sex a wage rate less than the wage rate paid to employees of a different sex for substantially similar work. Applies to all employers with at least one Colorado employee.

**Job posting requirements (effective Jan 1, 2024):** All job postings and notices of job opportunities (including promotions, transfers, temporary assignments) must include:

- The hourly or salary compensation OR the range of compensation
- General description of benefits and other compensation
- Anticipated application-window close date (CRS § 8-5-102)
  Failure to comply: $500–$10,000 per violation; over 2,800 complaints received as of June 2026 (verify current enforcement).

**Post-selection notice:** Within 30 calendar days after the selected candidate begins in the position, employer must make reasonable efforts to notify at minimum the employees with whom the selected candidate works regularly (CRS § 8-5-102).

**Salary history ban:** Employers cannot ask about or rely on a prospective employee's salary history to set pay, and cannot discriminate against applicants who refuse to disclose history (CRS § 8-5-103).

**Pay secrecy ban:** Employers cannot adopt or enforce policies preventing employees from discussing or disclosing their own wages or the wages of coworkers (CRS § 8-5-103); such policies themselves violate EPEWA.

**Career progression disclosure:** Employers must provide notice to employees of opportunities for promotion, advancement, and salary/wage progression (verify specific requirements with CDLE INFO guidance).

**Record retention:** Employers must maintain job descriptions, wage rate history, and selection records for each employee for duration of employment and 3 years thereafter.

## 5. Leave and Time

**Paid sick leave (Healthy Families and Workplaces Act, CRS § 8-3-1 et seq., effective July 15, 2020):** All employers must provide at least 1 hour of accrued paid leave per 30 hours worked, up to 48 hours per year. Expanded as of August 7, 2023 to include bereavement and weather/power/water closures. Employees cannot be disciplined, terminated, or have hours reduced for using protected sick leave. Accrual begins on day one of employment.

**Paid Family and Medical Leave Insurance (FAMLI, CRS § 8-13.5-101 et seq.):** Effective January 1, 2024, all Colorado employers must provide paid family and medical leave to eligible employees through either the state-run plan or a private plan with equal or greater benefits. Up to 12 weeks paid leave for birth, adoption, employee illness, family member illness, bereavement, or qualifying need; additional 4 weeks for pregnancy complications; up to 12 additional weeks for NICU-hospitalized newborns (effective Jan 1, 2026). Eligibility requires $2,500 earned wages in the prior 4 calendar quarters. No waiting period. Employee must work 180 days for job-protection eligibility. Employer must continue health-insurance contributions during leave. Premiums split employee/employer (50/50 default, employer can cover fully).

**Health benefits continuation:** Employer must continue paying normal share of health benefits when an employee is on FAMLI leave.

**Interaction with pay/role change:** A promotion or base-pay change does not typically affect accrued sick-leave or FAMLI eligibility, but verify whether the new role or compensation level triggers plan changes.

## 6. Records and Access

**Payroll record retention:** Employers must retain records of wage payments, deductions, pay statements, and hours worked for at least 3 years (CRS § 8-4-103; 4 years recommended for tax records). Electronic retention is permitted if all statement information is captured.

**Personnel file retention:** Employment agreements, job descriptions, wage-rate history, performance reviews, and disciplinary records should be retained for duration of employment plus 3 years (Colorado Wage Act) to 5 years (recommended for potential CADA discrimination claims and statute of limitations).

**Employee access:** Colorado law requires employers to permit employees to inspect and obtain a copy of any part of their own personnel file annually, without unreasonable delay (CRS § 8-2-137). verify specific procedures and cost rules.

**Selection and promotion records:** EPEWA requires retention of job descriptions, wage rate history, and selection records for each position and candidate.

**Record format:** No state rule mandating paper vs. electronic, but electronic records must meet the same accuracy and availability standards.

## 7. Privacy and Data

**Colorado Privacy Act (CPA, CRS § 6-1-1301 et seq.):** The CPA does not apply to personal data collected for employment purposes (e.g., payroll, personnel records, job applicant data). Consumers are explicitly excluded if acting in a "commercial or employment context, as a job applicant, or as a beneficiary of someone acting in an employment context." Therefore, most employee data (salary, performance, discipline, etc.) is exempt from CPA access/deletion rights.

**Biometric data exception (HB 24-1130, effective July 1, 2025):** Biometric identifiers (fingerprint, iris, voice, face, hand geometry) and derived biometric data ARE subject to privacy rules even in the employment context. Controllers must:

- Provide clear, affirmative, informed, written consent before collection.
- Cannot make consent a mandatory condition of employment for routine work unless for enumerated purposes (secure-facility access, hardware/software access, identity verification, etc.).
- Adopt a written biometric policy with retention schedule, deletion guidelines, and security-incident response protocol.
- Permanently destroy biometric data by the earliest of: original purpose fulfilled, 24 months after last interaction, or 45 days after determining retention is unnecessary.
- Non-compliance incurs damages and injunctive relief.

**Monitoring and surveillance:** No state rule specifically restricting non-biometric electronic monitoring (email, GPS, keystroke logging), but CADA protects against discrimination based on monitoring results.

**Breach notification:** No Colorado-specific employee data breach law; federal FCRA and state consumer-privacy breach standards apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background

**Ban the box (Chance to Compete Act, CRS § 8-2-130, effective Sept 1, 2019):** Private employers cannot ask about criminal history on the initial written or electronic application; cannot advertise that they won't hire with criminal records. Background checks may be conducted at any time, but the criminal-history question must be deferred. Applies to all employers regardless of size since Sept 1, 2021. Denver and Boulder have stricter rules: conditional job offer required before background check.

**Background check requirements:** No Colorado-specific pre-screening license or training; federal FCRA and Fair Credit Reporting Act rules apply.

**Drug testing:** No state rule prohibiting private-employer drug testing; federal requirements apply.

**E-verify:** No state mandate; federal I-9 and Form I-9 verification apply.

**Salary history inquiry:** Prohibited under EPEWA (see Section 4).

## 9. Separation

**At-will termination:** No advance notice required (CRS § 8-4-100). Employer can terminate for any lawful reason or no reason.

**Separation notice (reason):** Must provide written notice including reason for termination and unemployment-insurance eligibility statement (SB 22-234).

**Final pay timing:** Immediate for involuntary termination (within 6 hours of next business day if payroll office closed); next regular payday for resignation.

**Vacation/PTO payout:** Mandatory; must pay within 14 days of written demand or face double wages or 10 days' average pay (whichever greater).

**WARN Act (federal only):** No Colorado mini-WARN act. Federal WARN Act applies: employers with 100+ employees must give 60 days' written notice of plant closure or mass layoff to affected workers, CDLE Dislocated Worker Unit, and local government. Penalties: back pay/benefits for each day of insufficient notice + up to $500/day for failure to notify government agencies.

**Non-competes (CRS § 8-2-113, amended Aug 6, 2025):** Generally void. Enforceable only if: (1) employee earns annualized cash compensation at or above the "highly compensated worker" threshold set by state (verify current figure; typically $75,000–$100,000), AND (2) restriction is no broader than necessary to protect legitimate trade secrets. Physician non-competes are categorically void. Sale-of-business covenant is always enforceable.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State

**Independent contractor test (CRS § 8-6-101 et seq.):** Colorado uses a modified ABC test requiring BOTH:

- **(A) Control:** Worker is free from control and direction in the performance of service.
- **(C) Business relationship:** Worker is customarily engaged in an independent trade, occupation, profession, or business related to the service performed.

(Note: Colorado does not require the standard ABC **(B)** prong—that the work be outside the hiring entity's core business.) Burden of proof is on the employer. A properly drafted written contract is necessary but insufficient. Nine statutory factors indicate contractor status; employer must demonstrate majority are present.

**Remote worker / multi-state:** If a Colorado employee works remotely in another state, Colorado law applies to the at-hire relationship and wage/overtime rules unless the employee's primary work location is in a state with stricter standards (verify conflict-of-laws guidance). Payroll records must comply with the employee's actual workstate.

**Out-of-state employer hiring Colorado worker:** If the employer lacks Colorado presence but hires a Colorado resident, Colorado wage, anti-discrimination, and FAMLI laws apply to that employee.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B

**P1A (Promotion):**

- Model job-posting and selection-notice obligations as a timed requirement: posting must include salary range and close date; selection notice must be sent within 30 calendar days (CRS § 8-5-102).
- Record the promotion as part of wage-rate history for 3+ years; store job description.
- If promoted to manager or into a new pay band, trigger a new pay-statement cycle and verify no salary-history reliance occurred.
- Model non-compete enforceability check: is the promotee above the highly-compensated threshold? If not, non-competes are void.
- Verify whether manager change triggers posting/notice or is a lateral reassignment.

**P1B (Base-Pay Change):**

- Prohibit using applicant's prior salary to set new pay; enforce pay-secrecy ban on employer policies.
- All pay changes must be documented in a new pay statement; wages must include the new rate on the next regular payday or within the pay frequency (CRS § 8-4-102).
- If raising pay, verify no equal-pay violation for other employees in similar roles (CRS § 8-5-101).
- Model pay-change recording to support EPEWA equal-pay compliance (wage-rate history, position, gender of all similarly situated workers).
- Termination or layoff scenario: verify final pay (immediate if involuntary) and vacation payout (14-day deadline after written demand) are calculated at the most recent rate.

**Open questions:**

- Verify the current "highly compensated worker" threshold for non-compete enforceability (SB 25-083 effective Aug 6, 2025; likely $75,000+ but confirm exact amount).
- Confirm whether manager lateral reassignment (same pay, same level) requires posting/notice under EPEWA or if "job opportunity" is limited to promotion, demotion, or pay change.
- Clarify FAMLI interaction with unpaid leave (e.g., can FAMLI paid leave run concurrently with unpaid leave from another policy?).
- Confirm whether severance must be included in final pay or can be a separate disbursement post-termination.
- Verify COMPS Order #40 overtime calculation rules for shift changes (e.g., does a mid-week role change reset the 12-hour threshold?).
