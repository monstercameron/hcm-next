# Georgia Employment Law Research (2026-09-03)

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment dominates**: Georgia recognizes at-will employment by default except where illegal discrimination occurs; no written employment contract required at hire or on change.
- **Minimum wage defers to federal**: Georgia's statutory minimum ($5.15/hour) is superseded by federal FLSA ($7.25/hour), which applies to all covered employers.
- **Final pay and overtime governed by FLSA**: No state-specific final paycheck timing; federal FLSA rules on overtime (1.5x over 40 hours) and timely wage payment apply.
- **E-Verify mandate for 10+ private employees**: O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6(d) requires private employers with more than 10 employees to verify work eligibility via E-Verify.
- **Pay record retention 4 years**: Payroll records must be retained for no fewer than 4 years after the calendar year in which remuneration was paid.
- **Equal pay protection exists**: O.C.G.A. § 34-1-2 prohibits sex-based wage discrimination for comparable work; enforced by Georgia Commission on Equal Opportunity.
- **WARN Act and mass-separation rules**: Federal WARN Act (60 days notice for layoffs/closures) applies; Georgia additionally requires Mass Separation Notice to GDOL within 48 hours when 25+ workers separated same day for same reason for 7+ days.
- **No state paid-leave mandate for private sector**: FMLA (federal, 12 weeks unpaid) applies to covered employers; no state-mandated paid sick leave or paid family leave for private employees.
- **Noncompetes enforceable**: Georgia permits noncompete and nonsolicitation covenants; enforceability determined by reasonableness of scope, duration, and geographic area (verify current case law).
- **Data breach notification required**: O.C.G.A. § 10-1-912 requires notification "in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay" when personal information is breached.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Written Notices

Georgia is an at-will employment state under common law and statutory recognitions. Unless a written contract specifies a term of employment, either party may terminate the employment relationship at any time, for any reason, or for no reason, **except** termination cannot be based on illegal discrimination (e.g., race, sex, disability under Title VII of the Civil Rights Act or the ADA).

No state law requires written notice to an employee at hire regarding at-will status, wage theft prevention, or pay-rate changes—these are governed by federal law (FLSA 29 U.S.C. § 206–214) and general contract principles. Employers should document any at-will agreements and wage-rate changes for internal compliance; no state-specific form or timing is mandated.

When an employee is separated, employers must file a Separation Notice (DOL-800 form) with the Georgia Department of Labor for unemployment insurance purposes, but this is an administrative requirement, not a worker notification. No statutory notice period to the worker is required beyond at-will principles (federal WARN Act applies only to mass layoffs).

## 3. Wages: Minimum, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, and Deductions

**Minimum Wage**: Georgia's statutory minimum is O.C.G.A. § 34-4-3 ($5.15/hour), but the federal FLSA minimum ($7.25/hour) supersedes it; employers must pay whichever is higher. For practical purposes, the federal floor applies.

**Overtime**: Federal FLSA rules apply. No state-specific deviation: overtime is 1.5× regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek; executive, administrative, professional, and outside-sales exemptions follow FLSA definitions (29 U.S.C. § 213).

**Pay Frequency**: No state rule; federal FLSA requires payment of all earned wages, but does not dictate frequency. Employers may set their own pay schedule (weekly, biweekly, monthly, etc.) provided wages are paid in full and timely.

**Final Paycheck on Termination or Resignation**: No state-specific timing; federal FLSA and Georgia at-will principles require that all earned wages be paid in full. Absent a state law, the next regular payday or immediately (depending on employment contract or practice) governs. No Georgia statute mandates severance pay; it is a matter of contract.

**Deductions and Pay Statements**: Federal FLSA governs permissible deductions (only those required by law, court order, or authorized by employee in writing are allowed). No Georgia-specific pay-statement content requirement found; federal FLSA record-keeping applies.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary History Bans**: No Georgia state law restricts inquiries about salary history. Federal EEOC guidance discourages such inquiries, but Georgia has not enacted a categorical ban.

**Pay Range Disclosure**: No Georgia state law requires salary ranges in job postings. Federal rules (FLSA) do not mandate pay transparency in recruitment; this remains optional.

**Equal Pay Law**: O.C.G.A. § 34-1-2 (Equal Pay for Equal Work Act) prohibits employers from paying employees of one sex at a lower rate than the opposite sex for comparable work requiring "the same or essentially the same knowledge, skills, efforts and responsibilities" (verify exact scope and case law on what constitutes "comparable work"). Enforced by the Georgia Commission on Equal Opportunity (GCEO). Exceptions may exist for seniority, merit, production systems, or factors other than sex; consult GCEO for guidance.

**Pay Data Reporting**: No Georgia state law requires employers to report pay equity data. Federal EEO-1 reporting applies to employers with 100+ employees.

**Protected Classes**: Title VII, ADA, Age Discrimination in Employment Act (ADEA), and other federal laws apply. Georgia enforces these through GCEO and aligns with federal standards; no state-specific expansion of protected classes found.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

No Georgia state law mandates paid sick leave or paid family leave for private-sector employees.

**Federal FMLA** (12 weeks unpaid per 12 months for qualifying reasons: serious health condition, family member care, military exigency, military caregiver, childbirth/adoption) applies to covered employers (50+ employees within 75 miles). Employers must maintain health-insurance benefits during FMLA leave.

**Georgia State Employees** receive 15 days/year sick leave and up to 12 weeks family leave, but these are state-employee policies, not private-sector mandates. verify: Private employers in Georgia are not obligated to provide paid sick leave unless the employment contract or employer policy specifies it.

**Holidays and PTO**: Entirely optional; no state mandate. Employers may set policies and communicate them clearly at hire or change.

**Interaction with Pay/Role Changes**: If an employee is promoted or has a base-pay change, existing leave balances (if any) are typically preserved; no state rule specifies carryover or payout rules. Verify employer policy and contract.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll Record Retention**: Employers must retain payroll records and documents related to remuneration for **at least 4 years** after the calendar year in which the remuneration was paid (O.C.G.A. § 34-7-2; also federal FLSA 29 U.S.C. § 211 requires 3 years). The more stringent 4-year rule applies.

**Personnel Files and Employee Access**: Georgia law (drawn from Open Records Act principles O.C.G.A. § 50-18-70) permits employees to access their own personnel records. Employers must allow review; specific timelines are not codified but "reasonably prompt" is expected. Some information (e.g., reference letters, internal evaluations under certain conditions) may be withheld if disclosure would violate privacy or breach third-party confidentiality.

**Record Format**: No state requirement for paper vs. electronic. Employers may maintain records digitally provided they remain accessible and authentic (federal FLSA and state law allow electronic records).

**Separation Records**: Employers should retain separation notices and final pay records as part of the 4-year payroll retention cycle.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, Breach Notification

**Data Privacy Law**: Georgia has no comprehensive state data privacy statute equivalent to CCPA or GDPR, but O.C.G.A. § 10-1-912 imposes **data-breach notification** requirements.

**Breach Notification**: When an employer (or vendor) that collects, transmits, or maintains unencrypted digital records of personal information (e.g., driver's license number, social security number, credit card number, financial account information) experiences a breach, the employer must notify affected individuals "**in the most expedient time possible and without unreasonable delay**" unless law enforcement determines notification would compromise a criminal investigation. No specific day count is specified; "expedient" is the standard.

**Biometric Data and Monitoring**: No Georgia-specific statute regulates employee biometric collection (e.g., fingerprints for background checks) or electronic monitoring (e.g., keystroke logging, location tracking). Federal law (Title I of the Electronic Communications Privacy Act) and Georgia common law on privacy tort apply.

**Employee Record Confidentiality**: Georgia does not impose state-specific employee data residency or cross-border processing restrictions; federal law and ordinary commercial data-protection practices apply.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-Box**: Georgia has **no ban-the-box law**. Employers are permitted to inquire about criminal history on applications, during interviews, or via background check at any time in the hiring process. verify: No recent legislative changes found as of 2026-09-03.

**Background Checks**: Permitted. Employers commonly use criminal-history checks via the Georgia Crime Information Center (GCIC) with fingerprints or name-based consent. No state law restricts timing or scope of criminal record inquiries for most private employers.

**Drug Testing**: No state law prohibits pre-employment drug testing. Federal rules (Americans with Disabilities Act, FLSA) apply to the extent that testing is not disability discrimination.

**E-Verify**: **Required for private employers with more than 10 employees** (O.C.G.A. § 36-60-6(d), Georgia Security and Immigration Compliance Act). Employers must verify work eligibility through E-Verify, operated by U.S. Citizenship and Immigration Services. Public employers and their contractors have been required since 2007 (GDOL Rules Chapter 300-10-1). Failure to comply may expose employers to audit and penalties.

**Negligent Hiring**: Georgia recognizes negligent-hiring liability; use of background checks and careful vetting mitigates risk.

## 9. Separation: WARN, Severance, Notice, and Noncompetes

**WARN Act**: The federal **Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act** (29 U.S.C. § 2102) requires covered employers (100+ employees) to provide **60 days' written notice** before plant closings or mass layoffs affecting 50+ workers in a 30-day period. Applies nationwide, including Georgia. Failure to notify carries penalties.

**Georgia Mass Separation Notice**: In addition to WARN, Georgia requires employers to file a **Mass Separation Notice (DOL-402A form)** with the Georgia Department of Labor **within 48 hours** when 25 or more workers are separated on the same day, for the same reason, and the separation is permanent or indefinite or expected to last 7+ days (GDOL rules). This is an administrative requirement separate from WARN.

**Severance Pay**: No state law mandates severance. Severance is a matter of contract or employer discretion.

**Notice to Employee on Termination**: No state-specific notice period required; at-will employment permits immediate termination. However, employers should follow any notice period specified in an employment contract or policy.

**Noncompete and Nonsolicitation Agreements**: Georgia permits noncompete and nonsolicitation covenants. Enforceability is determined by Georgia case law: agreements must be (1) reasonable in scope (restricted to legitimate business interests), (2) reasonable in geographic area, and (3) reasonable in duration. Georgia courts scrutinize these closely; overly broad agreements may be unenforceable. **verify**: Consult current Georgia case law (e.g., state Supreme Court rulings on enforceability) before relying on these; enforcement varies by fact pattern.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State Rules

**Contractor vs. Employee Classification**: Georgia's Employment Security Law (O.C.G.A. § 34-9-2) defines "employee" and "employment" for unemployment-insurance purposes. Neither contract labels nor the parties' belief controls; liability is based on the **true underlying nature of the working relationship**. Factors include control over work methods, provision of tools, integration into the business, and permanence of the relationship. Misclassification of employees as independent contractors triggers wage-reporting and tax liabilities and may expose employers to audit.

**Remote Work and Multi-State Employees**: No Georgia-specific rules govern remote workers or employees working partly in Georgia. Federal FLSA applies to wages; state income-tax withholding is required if the employee is working in Georgia (verify with Georgia Department of Revenue). Multi-state employers must comply with all applicable state laws in states where work is performed.

**Reciprocity**: Georgia recognizes only Georgia law for employment disputes involving Georgia-based work. Out-of-state employment contracts may invoke choice-of-law provisions, but if work is performed in Georgia, Georgia law applies to many statutory protections.

## 11. Implications for HCM Next (P1A/P1B)

When the platform processes a promotion, base-pay change, manager change, or termination for a worker based in Georgia:

- **Record the at-will status** in the worker profile and any termination event; no contract-term field is required for most Georgia workers.
- **Model final-pay calculation** as FLSA-compliant (all earned wages, overtime at 1.5×, final pay on next regular payday or sooner).
- **Retain all wage and separation records** for 4 years; enforce this in data-retention policies.
- **Check E-Verify compliance** if the employer has 10+ employees; flag if employer is not registered or if individual's work authorization is not verified.
- **Capture equal-pay comparables** (comparable roles, skills, responsibilities) for audit trails if wage-equity disputes arise; O.C.G.A. § 34-1-2 protection is automatic but requires documentation.
- **Implement WARN and mass-separation workflows**: Prompt notification to GDOL within 48 hours for mass separations; log 60-day WARN notices for covered reductions.
- **No paid-leave mandate** for promotion/pay change, but if employee has accrued PTO or sick leave, preserve balances unless contract specifies forfeiture (verify employer policy).
- **Data-breach notification workflows**: If personal information is exposed, trigger "expedient" notification (under-3-day protocol recommended to avoid "unreasonable delay" disputes).
- **Noncompete documentation**: If the employer uses noncompete agreements, ensure they are attached to the employment record and flagged for legal review (Georgia enforceability is fact-dependent).

**Open questions**:

- Does the employer have a written policy on final-pay timing (immediately, next regular payday, or longer)?
- Is the employer subject to federal WARN (100+ employees)? If so, coordinate with 60-day notice requirement.
- Does the employer use noncompete agreements? If so, review for enforceability under Georgia case law.
- Does the employer have E-Verify registration, and is it current?
- Is the employer covered by FMLA (50+ employees within 75 miles)? If so, coordinate leave eligibility with role/pay changes.
