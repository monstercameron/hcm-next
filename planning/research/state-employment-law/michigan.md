# Michigan Employment Law Research

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Employment-at-will with public policy exceptions**: Michigan presumes all employment is at-will; exceptions protect employees discharged in violation of law, for refusing illegal acts, for workers' compensation claims, whistleblowing, or jury duty.
- **Earned Sick Time Act (amended Feb 2025)**: Employers with 11+ employees must provide 72 hours paid sick leave annually (1 hour per 30 hours worked); employers with 10 or fewer must provide 40 hours annually starting October 1, 2025; portable accrual applies.
- **Minimum wage $13.73 (2026)**: Scheduled increase to $14.05 in October 2027, indexed to inflation thereafter; tipped wage is 40% of minimum through February 2027, then phases to 100% by February 2030.
- **Overtime after 40 hours weekly**: Improved Workforce Opportunity Wage Act (MCL 408.934a) requires 1.5x regular rate for all hours over 40 in a workweek; no daily or 7th-day overtime unless agreed.
- **No state family/medical leave law**: Federal FMLA applies; Michigan has no state equivalent (FMLA Equivalent = "No"; federal FLSA/FMLA governs).
- **No mini-WARN or state severance mandate**: Federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for 100+ employees, 50+ affected) only; no Michigan-specific mass layoff severance requirement.
- **Non-compete enforceability**: Governed by MCL 445.774a reasonableness test; courts will enforce agreements reasonable in duration, geography, and scope; can modify unreasonable restrictions (blue-pencil).
- **No statewide pay transparency law or salary history ban**: Employers may ask about prior salary; proposed bills (HB 4406, SB 142) remain pending; state agencies restricted to conditional offer stage only.
- **Ban-the-box applies at 15+ employees**: Employers with 15+ employees must not inquire about criminal history before initial interview; verified by Executive Order; local ordinances (e.g., Detroit, Washtenaw County) may be stricter.
- **Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act (PA 6 of 2023)**: Prohibits discrimination based on sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, race, color, national origin, age, sex, religion, height, weight, familial/marital status.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Michigan presumes all employment is at-will under common law: either party may terminate without cause (verified by Michigan Supreme Court doctrine and related case law).

**Exceptions to at-will status** (public policy and wrongful discharge):

- Termination in retaliation for filing workers' compensation claim or unemployment claim.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, religious freedom, jury duty, voting).
- Termination for whistleblowing under Michigan Whistleblowers' Protection Act (MCL 15.361 et seq.) when employee reports violation or suspected violation of federal/state statute/regulation to public body.
- Termination for refusing to violate law in course of employment.
- Implied contract: Employee handbooks, detailed employment practices, or course of dealing may create enforceable contract duties beyond at-will terms (verify applicability in HCM context).

**Required written notices at hire and on change**:

- No Michigan statute mandates written notice of employment terms at hire comparable to Connecticut; employers should provide written documentation of hours, pay rate, payday schedule, and policies for compliance best practice.
- No statutory notice period required for pay changes; however, employers must comply with final-pay timing rules (see § 3 below) when reducing pay or changing role.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- Michigan minimum wage is $13.73 per hour as of January 1, 2026 (MCL 408.934d).
- October 1, 2027, and thereafter, minimum wage shall be adjusted annually by percentage equal to the prior-year increase in consumer price index (CPI-U).
- **Tipped employees**: Minimum wage for tipped employees is 40% of the minimum wage rate through February 20, 2027. Beginning February 21, 2027, the tipped minimum wage increases annually, reaching 100% of the full minimum wage by February 21, 2030 (MCL 408.934d, as amended by 2025 legislation). Employers must pay difference if tips plus tipped wage fall below full minimum wage.

**Overtime**:

- Employees must be paid 1.5x regular rate for hours worked over 40 per week (Improved Workforce Opportunity Wage Act, MCL 408.934a).
- Overtime is calculated on a weekly basis; no daily or 7th-day overtime mandates unless agreed.
- Exempt categories include executives, professionals, administrative employees, and outside salespeople, subject to federal duties test and salary threshold (currently $684/week, federal standard applies as Michigan tracks federal levels).

**Pay frequency**:

- For monthly pay period, wages earned during the first 15 days must be paid by the first day of each calendar month; wages from 16th through last day must be paid by the 15th (MCL 408.472).
- Alternatively, employers may establish regularly scheduled weekly or biweekly payday (MCL 408.472).
- Payment via cash, check, direct deposit, or payroll debit card (employer may not require one payment method without employee written consent).

**Final pay on termination**:

- On discharge, employer must pay all wages earned and due **immediately** "as soon as the amount can with due diligence be determined" (MCL 408.475).
- In practice, final pay is due on the regularly scheduled payday for the period in which termination occurs if amount cannot be determined before that payday.
- Resignation (employee-initiated): wages due on next regularly scheduled payday unless employee provides notice (verify notice period in case law).

**Permitted deductions**:

- Employers cannot deduct from wages except as required by law, with **full, free, and written employee consent** obtained without intimidation or fear of discharge (MCL 408.477).
- Each deduction for employer benefit requires separate written consent; cumulative deductions must not reduce gross wages below applicable minimum wage.
- Nonprofit organizations may deduct charitable contributions (qualifying under federal law) with initial written consent, without requiring separate consent per paycheck.
- Legally required deductions (taxes, garnishments, workers' comp) and authorized plan deductions (health/retirement) are permitted without additional consent.

**Pay statement requirements**:

- Employers must provide a statement at time of each wage payment including: hours worked, gross wages paid, identification of pay period, and **itemized deductions** (MCL 408.479).
- Employers must maintain records for 3 years (verify: no specific statute mandates electronic vs. paper format).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure in postings and on request**:

- **No Michigan statewide law** requires salary ranges in job postings (federal law does not mandate this).
- Proposed legislation (HB 4406, SB 142) would require salary/wage range disclosure in all job postings for both new hire and internal promotion roles, but bills remain pending as of September 2026.
- **Local requirement**: Ann Arbor city ordinance requires clear wage range in city government job postings (verify scope and applicability to private employers).

**Salary history ban**:

- **No statewide ban**: Michigan prohibits **local governments** from banning salary history inquiries (Executive Order allowing private employer inquiries). Michigan legislature preempted local salary-history bans in 2018.
- **Proposed bill**: SB 145 would create statewide salary history ban, but remains pending.
- **State employer restriction**: Michigan state agencies/departments cannot inquire about salary history until **conditional offer of employment** is made; may not ask current/prior employers or search public records for prior compensation (Executive Directive 2018-4).

**Pay data reporting**:

- No Michigan state-level pay equity or pay-gap reporting requirement.
- Federal EEO-1 (equal employment opportunity) reporting applies if employer meets coverage threshold (100+ employees and federal contractor).

**Equal pay statute and protected classes**:

- Michigan **Elliott-Larsen Civil Rights Act (ELCRA)** (MCL 37.2101 et seq., as amended by PA 6 of 2023) prohibits discrimination "because of religion, race, color, national origin, age, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity or expression, height, weight, familial status, or marital status."
- **Sex-based equal pay**: No explicit "equal pay for equal work" statute, but sex discrimination prohibition covers wage discrimination under ELCRA.
- Definitions: "Sexual orientation" = heterosexuality, homosexuality, bisexuality or history thereof; "Gender identity or expression" = gender-related self-identity or expression whether or not matching assigned sex at birth (PA 6 of 2023).
- Applies to all Michigan employers and employment contexts.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Earned Sick Time Act (ESTA, amended February 21, 2025)**:

- **Employers with 11+ employees**: Must provide paid sick leave accrual of **1 hour per 30 hours worked**, up to **72 hours per year** (effective February 21, 2025).
- **Employers with 10 or fewer employees**: Must provide paid sick leave accrual of 1 hour per 30 hours worked, up to **40 hours per year** (effective October 1, 2025) OR provide 40 hours of paid and 32 hours of unpaid leave under amended SB 15 framework (verify final interpretation: "72 hours" vs. "40 hours paid + 32 hours unpaid" for small employers).
- **Uses**: Employee's own serious health condition, care of family member (child, spouse, parent) with serious health condition, workplace closure due to public emergency, or domestic violence/sexual assault-related needs.
- **Employer requirements**: Accrual is portable (carries over year to year up to 40 hours maximum for large employers; verify carryover cap for small employers). Employers may require reasonable certification (health provider statement) if leave exceeds 3 consecutive days for one reason.
- **Interaction with pay/role changes**: If employee takes ESTA leave and returns to a different pay or role, any pay reduction must be justified by legitimate business reason (not retaliation for leave use); accrual continues during leave.

**Family and Medical Leave Act (FMLA)**:

- **No Michigan state equivalent**: Michigan has no state family/medical leave act; federal FMLA applies exclusively (federal FMLA covers employers with 50+ employees within 75-mile radius; 12+ months tenure; 1,250 hours worked in prior 12 months).
- Qualifying reasons: birth/adoption, newborn/child care, serious health condition of employee or family member, military caregiver, military emergency leave.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- Employees returning from FMLA leave have job restoration rights (same or equivalent position).
- If employer reduces salary during or immediately after FMLA leave, ensure reduction is applied uniformly (not retaliation); document business reason in HCM platform if reduction is mandated by separate policy change.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- Payroll records: Employers must maintain for **3 years** documenting employee name, address, birth date, occupation, total basic rate of pay, total hours worked per pay period, total wages paid, itemized deductions, and fringe benefits (MCL 408.479).
- Personnel records: The Bullard-Plawecki Employee Right to Know Act (MCL 423.501-512) defines "personnel record" as any record kept by employer that identifies employee and affects employment decisions (qualifications, promotion, transfer, compensation, discipline).
- **No maximum retention period** specified in statute; employers may retain longer for litigation defense or internal records management.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- Employees may **inspect** personnel records within reasonable time after written request (MCL 423.503). Inspection occurs during regular business hours at or near employee's worksite.
- Employees may **obtain copies** of all or part of file within reasonable time; employers may charge a reasonable fee for copying costs (MCL 423.504).
- Limit: **No more than 2 inspections per calendar year** without employer consent (verify whether separate copying requests count as separate inspections).

**Disputed information**:

- If employee disagrees with information in personnel file, removal or correction may be agreed upon (MCL 423.505). If not agreed, employee may submit a written statement of their position, which must be maintained as part of file and accompany any disclosure to third parties.
- Disciplinary information: Certain disciplinary records (warning, reprimand, suspension) must be removed from personnel file after a specified period if employee has not violated same rule again (MCL 423.507, verify retention period).

**Record format**:

- No Michigan statute specifies electronic vs. paper format; employers may maintain records electronically if retention and access requirements are met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Internet Privacy Protection Act (IPPA)**:

- Michigan law (MCL 37.271-278, Act 478 of 2012, effective December 28, 2012) prohibits employers from requiring employees or applicants to grant access to, allow observation of, or disclose information that allows access to personal internet accounts (e.g., passwords, usernames, login credentials).
- Employers **cannot**: Require access to social media accounts, personal email, or other personal internet accounts; discharge, discipline, fail to hire, or penalize employees for refusal to grant such access.
- **Exceptions** (MCL 37.275): IPPA does not prohibit employer access to information directly accessible to employer without special access information (e.g., posted public content); access to employer-provided accounts/devices; or legal process/investigation if employer reasonably believes unlawful activity has occurred.

**Employee privacy—electronic monitoring**:

- No comprehensive Michigan statute banning workplace monitoring; employers may monitor work email, internet use, and general workplace activities subject to reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine (common law).
- Employers **cannot** monitor employees in restrooms, locker rooms, or other areas designated for health/personal comfort without statutory violation claim (verify specific statute cross-reference).

**Breach notification**:

- Michigan Identity Theft Protection Act (MCL 445.72, Act 452 of 2004) requires employers/organizations that own or license a database containing personal information to notify residents **"without unreasonable delay"** if unencrypted/unredacted personal information is accessed by unauthorized person.
- Applies to employee records if they contain SSN, financial account numbers, or sensitive identity information.
- Penalties: Up to $250 per failure to notify; aggregate liability capped at $750,000 per breach event.
- No private right of action stated in statute; does not eliminate other legal remedies.

**Data residency or processing constraints**:

- No Michigan law mandates data residency for employee records; employers may process/store in-state or out-of-state so long as data security and privacy laws are met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box (criminal record inquiry)**:

- Michigan law prohibits employers with **15 or more employees** from inquiring about criminal history on employment applications or before initial interview (Executive Directive 2018-4 and related enforcement).
- Criminal history inquiries are permitted **after** initial interview or conditional offer.
- Related protections: Employers shall not request, make, or maintain records of misdemeanor arrests, detentions without conviction, or erased convictions (MCL 780.621).
- **Local ordinances**: Detroit and Washtenaw County have adopted their own ban-the-box ordinances; employers in those jurisdictions must comply with stricter local rules (verify current thresholds and effective dates).

**Background checks**:

- No Michigan statute restricts private employers from conducting background checks; federal Fair Credit Reporting Act (FCRA) applies.
- If using third-party consumer reporting agency, FCRA notice, dispute, and adverse-action procedures required.
- Michigan Clean Slate law (2020, automatic processing began April 11, 2023) automatically seals eligible misdemeanor and felony records after waiting periods; employers cannot use sealed records in hiring decisions.

**Drug testing**:

- **No Michigan state statute regulating private-sector drug testing**: Employers have wide flexibility to test before hiring (pre-employment), after accidents (post-accident), on reasonable suspicion of impairment, or in random selection (especially safety-sensitive roles).
- **Marijuana**: Employees who test positive for THC (including medical marijuana cardholders) have no statutory protection against termination in Michigan (verify: may be subject to ADA accommodations in other states, but not Michigan).
- Public employees: Michigan Civil Service Commission allows drug/alcohol testing for state employees under Regulation 2.07 (reasonable suspicion, post-accident, follow-up, random).

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No Michigan state mandate for private-sector E-Verify use; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply to all employers (verify via ICE/DHS).

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice and severance**:

- **No Michigan "mini-WARN" act**: Michigan has no state-level mass layoff notice or severance requirement; federal WARN Act (60 days' notice for employers with 100+ employees and mass layoff/plant closing affecting 50+ employees) only.
- Severance obligations: No Michigan law requires severance pay; obligations are established by employer written policy, employment contract, or collective bargaining agreement.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- Governed by MCL 445.774a (part of Michigan Antitrust Reform Act), which allows employers to obtain agreements protecting "reasonable competitive business interests" if agreement is reasonable in duration, geography, and type of employment.
- **Reasonableness factors** (courts will enforce if reasonable):
  - **Legitimate business interest**: Customer relationships, trade secrets (secret formulas, techniques, processes with economic value from non-disclosure), confidential business information.
  - **Duration**: 6 months to 2 years generally considered reasonable; beyond 2 years more difficult to enforce (courts will evaluate).
  - **Geography**: Must be limited to areas where employer actually conducts business; nationwide restrictions struck down unless justified.
  - **Scope**: Type of employment/line of business must be narrowly defined.
- **Court remedy**: If agreement found unreasonable, court may **modify and enforce as reasonable** (blue-pencil doctrine); Michigan courts have authority to limit restrictions.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- Enforced under similar reasonableness standard as non-competes (MCL 445.774a).
- Non-solicitation (restricting solicitation of employees or customers) may be enforceable if reasonable in duration (typically 1 year), geography, and scope.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Michigan applies the **ABC test** for contractor vs. employee status (unemployment insurance, workers' comp, wage-and-hour) via Joint Enforcement Commission on Employee Misclassification (JECEM).
- All three factors must be met for contractor status:
  - **(A)** Freedom from direction/control in performing services, both contractually and in fact.
  - **(B)** Service performed outside usual course of employer's business or outside all employer's locations.
  - **(C)** Individual customarily engaged in independently established trade/occupation/profession of the same nature.
- Misclassification subject to enforcement by Michigan Department of Licensing and Regulatory Affairs (LARA), Department of Treasury, Attorney General, Workers' Compensation Agency, and Chief State's Attorney.

**Remote workers**:

- No Michigan statute specifically addresses remote work classification or pay/benefit requirements; standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If remote employee works for Michigan employer or performs work in Michigan, Michigan law applies.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine in Michigan statute; if employee works across state lines, each state's law applies to work performed in that state.
- HCM platform should flag when a workforce change affects employees in multiple states to ensure compliance with each state's requirements.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

**Required typed obligations and timing constraints**:

1. **Minimum wage compliance**: Default non-exempt classification; verify hourly rate against Michigan minimum wage ($13.73 in 2026, indexed annually after October 1, 2027).

2. **Overtime accrual and payment**: Flag any hours over 40 per week for non-exempt employees; calculate 1.5x regular rate; track weekly and pay via regular payroll.

3. **Earned Sick Time accrual**: Model accrual rate of 1 hour per 30 hours worked. Gate entitlement by employer size: 72 hours/year max for 11+ employees (effective 2/21/25); 40 hours/year max for 10 or fewer (effective 10/1/25). Track usage against accrual balance; block payment below statutory minimum when reducing hours during leave.

4. **Tipped employee wage tracking**: For food/beverage service roles, track tipped wage vs. full minimum wage separately; ensure employer makes up difference if tips + tipped wage fall below full minimum. Model tipped wage phase-out (40% through 2/21/27, then increases to 100% by 2/30).

5. **Final pay timing on discharge**: Trigger immediate payment calculation on termination; if amount cannot be determined by close of business, schedule payment for next regular payday with documented reason. Flag any delay beyond that date.

6. **Ban-the-box compliance**: If hiring workflow includes criminal history inquiry, enforce delay until after initial interview for employers with 15+ employees; block pre-interview inquiry for those sizes.

7. **Non-compete/non-solicit storage**: Store execution date, employee name, reasonableness attributes (duration, geography, scope). Flag any non-compete with duration >2 years or nationwide scope for legal review; do not auto-enforce.

8. **Pay change notice**: Provide written notice to employee of any change in compensation, hours, or pay frequency; timestamp delivery and store in employee record.

9. **Personnel file access**: Implement access-tracking for employee file inspections (limit to 2/calendar year). Store all employee requests and copies provided; maintain dispute statements.

10. **Breach notification**: If unencrypted PII (SSN, account numbers, sensitive identity data) is accessed, flag for legal review and track notification timing (must be without unreasonable delay).

**Field restrictions and validations**:

- Salary history inquiry: Block or flag form field that requests prior salary from external candidates (no statewide ban, but best practice; check local ordinances for Ann Arbor, Detroit, Washtenaw County).
- Wage range in posting: No state requirement; suggest implementing if local ordinances apply (Ann Arbor city roles).
- Overtime exemption: Default non-exempt; exempt categories (executive, professional, administrative, outside sales, agricultural, domestic) require documentation of duties test + salary threshold ($684/week minimum, federal standard).
- Paid sick leave: Disable manual override of statutory accrual calculation for employers subject to ESTA.
- Final pay calculation: Auto-calculate all accrued sick leave payout on discharge (verify whether accrued ESTA must be paid out at termination; likely yes, but verify case law on use-it-or-lose-it restrictions).

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether accrued paid sick leave must be paid out (not forfeited) upon termination or resignation in Michigan.
- Clarify small-employer ESTA entitlement: Is it "40 hours paid + 32 hours unpaid" under SB 15, or "40 hours paid" under original ESTA; confirm final effective date (October 1, 2025).
- Confirm whether implied employment contract doctrine applies in Michigan and what factual patterns trigger it (course of dealing, handbook promises, oral assurances).
- Verify whether Michigan has any public-sector pay transparency requirements or salary history restrictions beyond state agencies.
- Clarify whether non-compete agreed after employment begins (post-hire) has different enforceability standard than pre-hire agreements.
- Confirm whether ESTA accrual and usage must be tracked separately per employee or per location/role.
- Verify whether blue-pencil modification of non-competes in Michigan allows courts to extend unreasonable duration downward or only shorten/narrow geographic scope.
