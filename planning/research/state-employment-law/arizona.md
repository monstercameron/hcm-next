# Arizona Employment Law Research

**State:** Arizona | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment**: Arizona recognizes pure at-will employment codified in A.R.S. § 23-1501. Termination requires no advance notice unless a written employment contract specifies otherwise.
- **Minimum wage and geographic variations**: Statewide minimum wage is $15.15/hour (2026), $14.70/hour (2025), adjusted annually for inflation per Proposition 206. Flagstaff: $18.35/hour (no tip credit). Tucson: $15.45/hour (with $3.00 tip credit). Tipped minimum wage statewide: $12.15/hour (2026) with $3.00 tip credit.
- **Final pay timing on termination**: Discharged employee wages due within seven working days or end of next regular pay period (whichever sooner); resigned employee wages due by regular payday for period of termination. Accrued paid sick time need not be paid upon resignation unless employer policy or labor agreement mandates payout.
- **Pay frequency**: Two or more paydays per month, not more than 16 days apart; regular wages paid within five business days after pay period ends.
- **Paid sick leave**: Employees accrue one hour per 30 hours worked; caps: 40 hours/year for employers with 15+ employees, 24 hours/year for employers with <15 employees (A.R.S. § 23-371 et seq.). Usable after 90 days; protected from retaliation.
- **E-Verify mandate**: A.R.S. § 23-214 requires ALL Arizona employers (regardless of size) to verify employment eligibility of all new hires through E-Verify after December 31, 2007. Failure to comply risks business license suspension or permanent revocation.
- **Medical marijuana employment protections**: Employers cannot discriminate against registered cardholders or penalize based on positive test result unless employee was impaired or used marijuana on premises during work hours (A.R.S. § 36-2813).
- **No state privacy law or biometric restrictions**: Arizona has no comprehensive employee data privacy statute or biometric privacy rules. Federal data breach notification applies.
- **Ban-the-box**: No statewide private-sector ban-the-box law. Only 2017 executive order (Executive Order 2017-07) applies to state agency employment applications.
- **Non-compete and non-solicit**: Enforceable under common law if reasonable in time, geography, and scope, tied to legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential information, customer goodwill). Blue-pencil modification available in limited circumstances.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Arizona is a pure at-will employment state under A.R.S. § 23-1501(A)(2): "The employment relationship is severable at the pleasure of either the employee or the employer unless both the employee and the employer have signed a written contract to the contrary setting forth that the employment relationship shall remain in effect for a specified duration of time."

No state statute requires advance notice of termination for at-will employees. The federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act applies to Arizona employers with 100+ full-time employees for mass layoffs (50+ affected workers in a 30-day period).

**Constructive discharge exceptions** (A.R.S. § 23-1502): Employees claiming constructive discharge based on objectively difficult or unpleasant working conditions must provide written notice to the employer and allow 15 calendar days for a written response. If the employer fails to respond or cure the condition, employees may pursue constructive discharge claims. Constructive discharge based on outrageous conduct (sexual assault, threats of violence, continuous discriminatory harassment) bypasses the 15-day notice requirement. Employers must post or communicate these requirements to employees.

**Required notices at hire**: No state statute mandates written wage notices or employment agreements at hire. Federal FLSA posting requirements apply. Recommended practice: provide written notice of pay rate, pay frequency, deductions policy, and paid sick leave rights (which employers must communicate per A.R.S. § 23-373).

**Required notices on pay or role change**: No state statute mandates advance notice of pay cuts, promotions, or role/manager changes. However, pay reductions may trigger paid sick leave accrual recalculations if base rate changes. Substantial role changes could implicate constructive discharge provisions if they alter working conditions materially.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions, and Pay Statements

**Minimum wage** (A.R.S. § 23-363): Effective through the Fair Wages and Healthy Families Act (Proposition 206):

- Statewide 2026: $15.15/hour
- Statewide 2025: $14.70/hour
- Adjusted annually based on Consumer Price Index (CPI-U) change between August of prior two years
- Flagstaff (2026): $18.35/hour; no tip credit allowed; employer must pay full $18.35 in wages
- Tucson (2026): $15.45/hour; $3.00 tip credit allowed, requiring $12.45 in cash wages
- No separate minimum wage for minors

**Tipped minimum wage** (A.R.S. § 23-363(C)): Statewide 2026: $12.15/hour plus tip credit of $3.00 maximum. Tips and gratuities may be counted toward minimum wage if employee receives enough tips to reach minimum wage total. Cash wages plus tips must total at least state minimum.

**Overtime**: Arizona follows federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) only; no state daily or weekly overtime requirement. Overtime due at 1.5x regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek.

**Pay frequency** (A.R.S. § 23-351): Employers must designate two or more paydays per month, not more than 16 days apart. Regular wages must be paid no later than five business days after the pay period ends (or by next regular payday, whichever sooner). Exceptions: school district employees (seven business days); out-of-state centralized payroll for professional/administrative/executive/outside sales employees (may use single monthly payday). Payment methods: currency, check, draft, money order, warrant, direct deposit (with employee consent), or payroll card.

**Final pay on discharge** (A.R.S. § 23-353): Wages due within seven working days or end of the next regular pay period, whichever is sooner. Non-exempt employees' accrued paid sick time must be paid out at regular rate if employer policy or labor agreement requires it.

**Final pay on voluntary resignation** (A.R.S. § 23-353): Wages due by the regular payday for the pay period during which termination occurred. Accrued paid sick time need not be paid unless employer's written policy or labor agreement mandates payout. Upon rehire within 9 months, paid sick leave balance is restored.

**Permitted deductions** (A.R.S. § 23-352): Wage withholding permitted only for good-faith disputes, counterclaims, debts, or recoupment. Deductions must comply with federal law (court-ordered garnishments, tax withholdings, ERISA plan deductions, insurance, uniform/equipment costs if permitted by federal law). Deductions that reduce wages below minimum wage are prohibited.

**Pay statement content**: No detailed state requirement. Employers must maintain records showing daily hours worked, pay rates, earned paid sick time accrual and use per A.R.S. § 23-364. Recommend including gross pay, deductions, net pay, pay period dates, and paid sick time balance on pay stubs.

**Payroll record retention** (A.R.S. § 23-364): Employers must keep payroll records for at least four years. Records must include daily hours worked, wages, overtime pay, earned paid sick time accrual and use, and pay rates. A rebuttable presumption of minimum wage or paid sick time violation arises if records are not maintained. Employees may inspect and copy their own payroll records.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity: Salary Disclosure, History Ban, Pay Data Reporting, and Equal Pay

**No state salary history ban**: Arizona does not prohibit asking about prior salary history during hiring. No state statute bans consideration of salary history in setting pay. Federal Equal Pay Act and Title VII protections apply.

**No state salary range disclosure requirement**: Arizona does not require employers to disclose salary ranges in job postings or on request to applicants or employees. No state analogues to pay transparency laws in other states.

**Pay data reporting**: No state requirement. Federal pay equity reporting requirements (EEO-1, Affirmative Action Plan) apply to covered employers.

**Equal pay statute** (A.R.S. § 23-341): Prohibits pay discrimination based on sex when work requires substantially equal skill, effort, education, experience, and responsibility under similar conditions. Permitted defenses: differences based on seniority, merit, or production-based compensation systems. Violations carry penalties; injured employees may recover unpaid wages and damages.

**Protected class discrimination** (A.R.S. § 41-1461 et seq., Arizona Civil Rights Act): Prohibits pay discrimination based on protected status: race, color, religion, sex, age (40+), national origin, disability, or genetic information. Covers private employers with 15+ employees; sexual harassment covered for any size employer. Cannot pay lower wages to employees of one sex or race for equal work, absent seniority, merit, production quality, or factor other than protected class. Violations subject to civil remedies and attorney fees.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Paid Family and Medical Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave** (A.R.S. § 23-371 et seq., Fair Wages and Healthy Families Act): All employees accrue at least one hour per 30 hours worked. Annual caps depend on employer size:

- 40 hours/year for employers with 15+ employees
- 24 hours/year for employers with fewer than 15 employees
- Time becomes usable after 90 days of employment
- Usable for employee health needs, caring for family, and safety-related absences (domestic violence, sexual violence, stalking, abuse)
- Employers may require documentation for absences of three or more consecutive work days
- Use protected from retaliation; cannot be counted as absence triggering adverse action or discipline
- Carryover of up to 40 hours to following year required (employer may cap total accrual at 120 hours)
- Payout upon discharge required at regular rate if employer policy mandates it; payout upon resignation not required unless policy mandates it

**Voting leave** (A.R.S. § 16-402): Employee may absent themselves from work for voting purposes. If fewer than three consecutive hours exist between opening of polls and work start, OR between work end and closing of polls, employee entitled to absent themselves for amount needed to reach three consecutive hours total (does not exceed 3 hours). Application must be made before election day. Time is paid and cannot trigger adverse action.

**Jury duty leave** (A.R.S. § 21-236): Employers cannot require or request use of annual, vacation, or sick leave for time spent responding to jury summons, participating in jury selection, or serving on jury. Employers cannot refuse to permit jury service or dismiss, penalize, or retaliate against employee for jury service. Seniority cannot be lost. Small employer exception: courts must postpone jurors from employers with 5 or fewer full-time employees if another employee is already serving.

**Military leave** (A.R.S. § 26-168): Employees in National Guard or Armed Forces Reserve have right to leave for competent military orders, camps, maneuvers, drills, and trainings. Leave does not affect vacation rights or seniority. Employee must return to same or higher position commensurate with ability. No loss of efficiency rating for state employees' field training.

**No state paid family and medical leave**: No state statutory requirement. Federal FMLA applies to covered employers (50+ employees in 75-mile radius).

**No state personal or bereavement leave requirement**: Employers are not required to provide personal, bereavement, or other mandated leave by Arizona state law. Federal USERRA provides military service protections.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll record retention** (A.R.S. § 23-364): Four years minimum. Must include daily hours worked, wages and overtime pay, earned paid sick time accrual and use, pay rates. Employees may inspect and copy their own records. Electronic or paper format acceptable; recommend electronic backup to satisfy four-year retention and enable timely access.

**Personnel files**: No state statute mandating personnel file access, retention period, or employee inspection rights for private employers. Federal Title VII and ADEA requirements apply. Recommended retention: three years after hire, separation, or employment action per federal standards.

**Format rules**: No state requirement. Records may be maintained in paper or electronic format.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, and Breach Notification

**No comprehensive employee data privacy law**: Arizona has not enacted an omnibus employee data privacy statute. No baseline state restrictions on employer collection, use, or storage of employee personal information.

**Biometric data**: No Arizona statute restricts employer collection of biometric data (fingerprints, facial recognition, iris scans). Employers may implement biometric timekeeping or access systems without written notice or consent.

**Data breach notification** (A.R.S. § 18-551, § 18-552): Entities must notify Arizona residents of breaches exposing personal information, including biometric data coupled with name, within 45 days. Notification must occur without unreasonable delay. No employee-specific carve-out; same rules apply to both consumer and employee data.

**Monitoring and surveillance**: No state employee monitoring statute. Common law expectations of privacy may apply to personal electronic communications; recommend clear policies on monitoring email, internet use, and location tracking.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify, and Medical Marijuana

**Background checks**: No state limitation on background check scope. Federal FCRA applies. Employers may use third-party screening; must provide disclosures and obtain written consent; must provide adverse action notice and opportunity to respond per FCRA.

**Ban-the-box**: No statewide private-sector ban-the-box law. Only 2017 executive order applies to state agency employment applications.

**Drug testing** (A.R.S. § 23-493.04): Employers permitted but not required to test for drugs and alcohol. If testing is implemented, a written policy must be distributed to all employees subject to testing before any test occurs. Policy must state:

- Employer's drug and alcohol policy
- Employees or groups subject to testing
- Circumstances triggering testing (accident, reasonable suspicion, random basis)
- Substances tested for

Testing after an accident permitted if administered as soon as practicable to employees who may have contributed. Random testing permitted if authorized by written policy. Reasonable suspicion of drug/alcohol use affecting job performance is grounds for testing.

**E-Verify mandate** (A.R.S. § 23-214, Legal Arizona Workers Act): ALL Arizona employers are required to verify employment eligibility of all new hires through E-Verify within a specific timeframe. No exemption for employer size or industry. Verification must be completed after hire, and records maintained for employment duration or minimum three years, whichever is longer.

**Penalties for non-compliance**: Failure to use E-Verify or hiring unauthorized workers results in:

- Business license suspension (minimum 10 days) on first violation
- Business license suspension or permanent revocation on repeat violations
- County Attorney may bring civil suit

**E-Verify defense**: Employers using E-Verify consistently earn a rebuttable presumption that they did not knowingly hire unauthorized workers; strongest legal shield under Arizona law.

**Medical marijuana**: A.R.S. § 36-2813 protects registered medical marijuana cardholders from employment discrimination based on cardholder status or positive drug test result, unless employee was impaired or used marijuana on premises during work hours. Federal law exception: Protections do not apply if following them would cause loss of federal monetary or licensing benefits.

## 9. Separation: Notice Requirements, Severance, Non-Compete, Non-Solicit, and Blacklisting

**At-will termination and notice**: Arizona requires no advance notice for at-will termination (A.R.S. § 23-1501). Exceptions: written employment contracts, federal WARN Act (100+ employees), or constructive discharge procedures (A.R.S. § 23-1502).

**WARN Act (federal)**: Applies to Arizona employers with 100+ full-time employees. 60 calendar days' notice required for plant closings or mass layoffs (50+ affected employees in 30-day period). No Arizona state mini-WARN law.

**Severance**: No Arizona statute requires severance. If offered, severance is a contractual obligation and cannot offset WARN liability unless payments are "voluntary and unconditional" (not already owed under contract or policy).

**Non-compete agreements**: Enforceable under Arizona common law if the restraint:

- Does not exceed what is reasonably necessary to protect employer's business
- Is not unreasonably restrictive of employee's rights
- Does not contravene public policy
- Is reasonable as to time and geographic scope (no statutory limits)

Must be tied to a legitimate business interest: trade secrets, confidential information, customer goodwill, or specialized training. No specific statutory codification; enforceability is fact-intensive and turns on reasonableness of duration, geography, and activity restricted. Blue-pencil modification (judicial reformation to reasonable scope) available in limited circumstances.

**Non-solicit agreements**: Generally more enforceable than non-competes. Must be narrowly tailored to protect legitimate business interests (customer relationships, confidential information). No statutory limits.

**Blacklisting and references** (A.R.S. § 23-1361, § 23-1362): Statute prohibits two or more employers from engaging together to prevent an employee from pursuing a useful occupation ("blacklist").

- **Privileged communications exception**: Communications to government bodies required by law or furnished pursuant to written rules/policies of government agency are privileged; no civil liability.
- **Banking exception**: Banks, credit unions, and financial institutions providing employment references not civilly liable unless information is false and provided with knowledge and malice.
- **Criminal penalties**: Person commits blacklisting (Class 2 misdemeanor) if knowingly exchanges, solicits, or gives out any labor blacklist.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Work, and Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**: Arizona does not apply the ABC Test for general employment classification. Instead, Arizona relies on **common law test**, which examines:

- Control over work methods and schedule
- Financial independence (ability to realize profit/loss)
- Integration into the business
- Provision of tools and equipment
- Duration of the relationship
- Whether services are customary to the employer's business

Misclassification as contractor when the person is an employee can result in liability for unpaid wages, overtime, and penalties. Recommend documenting contractor relationship, scope of work, and independence factors in written agreement.

**Unemployment insurance determinations**: Arizona may apply ABC Test in specific unemployment insurance or wage determination contexts (verify current application).

**Remote worker rules**: No state law restricting remote work or requiring physical presence. Employers may classify roles as remote; employees working remotely remain subject to minimum wage, overtime (federal FLSA), and paid sick leave accrual. No residency or domicile requirements for remote workers.

**Multi-state reciprocity**: When employee works partly in Arizona and partly in another state, Arizona law applies to work performed in Arizona. Interstate employees are covered by employee protection laws of each state where they work. No Arizona statute specifically addresses multi-state reciprocity; apply federal conflict-of-law principles. Recommend determining which state's wage and hour law applies (typically state in which work is performed) and complying with most restrictive rule.

## 11. Implications for HCM Next Platform (P1A/P1B)

**Termination workflows**:

- Model a **7-day working-day deadline** for final wage payment upon discharge, or link to next regular payday if sooner (A.R.S. § 23-353)
- Warn if termination occurs within 90 days of employment (may trigger disputes over paid sick leave accrual)
- Require final paycheck method selection (direct deposit or mail) and date verification
- Clarify whether accrued paid sick time is paid based on employer policy (not required by statute on resignation)

**Pay and base-wage changes**:

- Model constraint: do not reduce base wage retroactively without written employee consent (may support constructive discharge or wage theft claims)
- Flag if pay reduction occurs during or within 30 days of paid sick leave use (may trigger retaliation claim under A.R.S. § 23-373)
- Do not reset or reduce paid sick time accrual if base wage changes; accrue based on hours worked regardless of rate
- Enforce statewide and local minimum wage thresholds (Flagstaff $18.35, Tucson $15.45, statewide $15.15 for 2026)

**Promotions and role changes**:

- Do not apply role change if it substantially increases duties without corresponding wage increase and change is pretext for avoiding paid sick leave payouts
- Ensure manager changes do not trigger unwritten changes to role or duties
- Recommend written notice of material role changes to document no constructive discharge intent

**Paid sick leave integration**:

- Track accrual per employee (1 hour per 30 hours worked) and cap at 40 or 24 hours depending on employer size
- Prevent payout forfeiture on promotion, demotion, or discharge; pay out at regular rate upon discharge if policy requires it
- Allow carryover of up to 40 hours to next year; employer may cap total accrual at 120 hours
- Warn against using paid sick leave "no use" policies; accrual is mandatory
- For resignation, note that accrued paid sick time payout is not required unless employer policy mandates it

**Pay frequency and statements**:

- Enforce minimum two paydays per month, maximum 16 days apart
- Do not permit paycheck delay beyond five business days after pay period end (or next regular payday)
- Recommend including paid sick time balance and accrual YTD on pay stubs (not required but best practice)

**Background check workflow**:

- No ban-the-box restriction for private sector in Arizona; criminal history inquiries permitted at any stage
- If employer implements no ban-the-box policy voluntarily, do not pull background check until conditional offer is extended

**E-Verify workflow**:

- Integrate E-Verify verification for all new hires in Arizona; no exemptions by employer size
- Maintain E-Verify records for employment duration or minimum three years
- Warn if E-Verify not completed; non-compliance risks business license suspension/revocation
- Document E-Verify use to establish rebuttable presumption against knowingly hiring unauthorized workers

**Medical marijuana and drug testing**:

- If employer implements drug testing, distribute written policy before testing begins specifying circumstances and substances
- Clarify that positive test result does not alone justify termination of registered medical marijuana cardholder unless employee was impaired or used at work
- Note federal law exception: employer may refuse if compliance would cause loss of federal benefits

**Contractor vs. employee classification**:

- Implement questionnaire documenting control, financial independence, and business integration factors per common law test
- Flag if classified as contractor but paid on regular hourly schedule similar to an employee (misclassification risk)
- Store classification rationale with engagement letter

**Separation and non-compete**:

- For non-compete enforcement, document reasonable time/geography scope and legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential information, customer goodwill)
- Non-solicit agreements generally more enforceable; tailor narrowly to specific customer relationships or confidential information
- Avoid joint action by multiple employers to exclude applicant (blacklisting violation under A.R.S. § 23-1361)

**Constructive discharge**:

- Require written response within 15 days to employee's written notice of objectively difficult working conditions (A.R.S. § 23-1502)
- Bypass 15-day notice for outrageous conduct (sexual assault, threats, discriminatory harassment)
- Document employer's response and remediation efforts

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether A.R.S. § 23-351 permits employer to pay overtime/exception wages within 16 days if regular wages paid within 5 business days
- Verify whether Arizona employers must restore paid sick leave balance upon rehire within 9 months
- Verify whether accrued paid sick time on resignation must be paid if employee is rehired within 9 months (restoration vs. payout)
- Confirm whether severance payments must be made separately from final wage or may be combined in final paycheck
- Clarify Arizona's application of ABC Test in unemployment insurance determinations
