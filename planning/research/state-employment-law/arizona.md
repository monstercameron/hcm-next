# Arizona Employment Law Research

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment**: Arizona recognizes pure at-will employment with limited statutory exceptions. No advance notice required to terminate unless a written employment contract specifies otherwise (A.R.S. § 23-1501).
- **Final pay timing**: On discharge, final wages due within seven working days or next regular payday (whichever is sooner); on resignation, wages due by regular payday for the pay period (A.R.S. § 23-353). Platform must enforce timing constraints for termination workflows.
- **Minimum wage**: $15.15/hour as of 2026, adjusted annually for inflation. Tipped minimum wage is $12.15/hour with a maximum $3.00 tip credit (A.R.S. § 23-362).
- **Paid sick leave**: Employees accrue one hour per 30 hours worked; capped at 40 hours/year (15+ employees) or 24 hours/year (<15 employees). Usable after 90 days and protected from retaliation (A.R.S. § 23-371 et seq.). Pay or role changes must not reduce accrual or trigger adverse action for use.
- **Pay frequency**: Two or more paydays per month, not more than 16 days apart; wages paid no later than five business days after pay period ends (A.R.S. § 23-351).
- **Payroll record retention**: Four years required; includes daily hours, rates, and paid sick time accrual/use (A.R.S. § 23-364). Employees may inspect and copy their own records.
- **No state privacy law or biometric restrictions**: Arizona has no comprehensive employee data privacy statute or biometric privacy rules; only federal data breach notification applies (ARS 18-551).
- **Ban-the-box**: Effective 2025, employers with four or more workers cannot ask about criminal history on initial application; must wait until after conditional offer. Cannot inquire about arrests not resulting in conviction or sealed/expunged records (A.R.S. § 23-211).
- **Non-compete/non-solicit**: Enforceable if reasonable in time, geography, and scope and tied to legitimate business interest (trade secrets, confidential information, customer goodwill). No statutory codification; common law standard applies.
- **Constructive discharge**: Employer must respond to written notice of objectively difficult working conditions within 15 days, or employee may pursue constructive discharge claim; outrageous conduct (sexual assault, threats, discriminatory harassment) permits bypass of notice requirement (A.R.S. § 23-1502).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Arizona is a pure at-will employment state. A.R.S. § 23-1501(A)(2) states: "The employment relationship is severable at the pleasure of either the employee or the employer unless both the employee and the employer have signed a written contract to the contrary setting forth that the employment relationship shall remain in effect for a specified duration of time."

No state statute requires advance notice of termination in at-will relationships. However, the federal Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification (WARN) Act applies to Arizona employers with 100+ full-time employees for mass layoffs (50+ affected workers).

**Constructive discharge exceptions**: A.R.S. § 23-1502 creates a procedural requirement. Employees claiming constructive discharge based on objectively difficult or unpleasant working conditions must provide written notice to the employer and wait 15 calendar days for a written response. If the employer fails to respond or cure the condition, employees may pursue claims. Constructive discharge based on outrageous conduct (sexual assault, threats of violence, continuous discriminatory harassment) does not require the 15-day notice procedure. Employers must post notice of these requirements or include them in handbooks.

**Required notices at hire**: No state law mandates written wage notices or employment agreements at hire. Federal FLSA posting requirements apply. Best practice is to provide written notice of pay rate, pay frequency, deductions policy, and paid sick leave rights (which employers must communicate per A.R.S. § 23-373).

**Required notices on pay or role change**: No state statute mandates advance notice to employees of pay cuts, promotions, or role/manager changes. However, pay reductions may trigger paid sick leave accrual recalculations if base rate changes. Changes to role or duties could trigger the constructive discharge provisions if they substantially alter working conditions.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions, and Pay Statements

**Minimum wage**: $15.15/hour as of January 1, 2026 (adjusted annually for cost of living). Tipped minimum wage is $12.15/hour; tip credit limited to $3.00 per hour. Cash wage plus tips must total at least state minimum (A.R.S. § 23-362).

**Overtime**: No state daily or weekly overtime requirement. Arizona follows the federal Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA): 1.5x regular rate for hours over 40 in a workweek (A.R.S. § 23-363).

**Pay frequency**: A.R.S. § 23-351 requires two or more paydays per month, not more than 16 days apart. Wages must be paid no later than five business days after the pay period ends, or by the next regular payday (whichever is sooner). Exceptions: school district employees (7 business days), out-of-state centralized payroll (10 days). Payment methods: currency, check, draft, money order, warrant, or direct deposit (with employee consent).

**Final pay on termination**: A.R.S. § 23-353 specifies:

- **Discharged employee**: Wages due within seven working days or the end of the next regular pay period, whichever is sooner. Non-exempt employees accrued paid sick time must be paid out at regular rate.
- **Resigned employee**: Wages due by the regular payday for the pay period during which termination occurred. Upon employee request, may be paid by mail.

**Permitted deductions**: A.R.S. § 23-352 permits wage withholding only for good-faith disputes regarding the amount owed, including counterclaims, debts, or recoupment. Deductions must comply with federal law (court-ordered garnishments, tax withholdings, ERISA plan deductions, insurance, uniform/equipment costs if permitted by federal law). Deductions that reduce wages below minimum wage are prohibited.

**Pay statement content**: No detailed state requirement. Employers must maintain records showing daily hours worked, pay rates, and earned paid sick time accrual and use per A.R.S. § 23-364. Recommend including gross pay, deductions, net pay, pay period dates, and paid sick time balance on pay stubs.

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity: Salary Disclosure, History Ban, Pay Data Reporting, and Equal Pay

**No state salary history ban**: Arizona does not prohibit asking about prior salary history during hiring. Federal Equal Pay Act and Title VII protections apply. SB1548 (status uncertain—verify current status) proposed a ban but does not appear to have passed as of 2026.

**No state salary range disclosure requirement**: Arizona does not require employers to disclose salary ranges in job postings or on request. Federal PUMP Act (2023) applies to federal contractors only.

**Pay data reporting**: No state requirement. Federal pay equity reporting requirements (EEO-1, Affirmative Action Plan) apply to covered employers.

**Equal pay and protected classes**: A.R.S. § 41-1461 et seq. (Arizona Civil Rights Act) prohibits pay discrimination based on protected status: race, color, religion, sex, age (40+), national origin, and disability. Cannot pay lower wages to employees of one sex or race for equal work requiring equal skill, effort, education, experience, and responsibility in the same establishment and similar conditions, unless the differential is justified by seniority, merit, production quality, or a factor other than sex/race. Violations subject to fines of $5,000 (first offense), $1,000 (each subsequent), capped at $10,000.

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Paid Family and Medical Leave, and Other Mandated Leave

**Paid sick leave** (A.R.S. § 23-371 et seq.): All employees accrue at least one hour per 30 hours worked. Annual caps depend on employer size: 40 hours for employers with 15+ employees, 24 hours for employers with <15 employees. Time becomes usable after 90 days of employment. Can be used for employee health needs, caring for family, and safety-related absences (domestic violence, sexual violence, stalking, abuse). Employers may require documentation for absences of three or more consecutive work days. Use of paid sick time is protected from retaliation and cannot be counted as an absence triggering adverse action or discipline.

**No state paid family and medical leave**: No state statutory requirement. Federal FMLA applies to covered employers (50+ employees in 75-mile radius).

**No state personal or bereavement leave requirement**: Employers are not required to provide personal, bereavement, jury duty, voting, or military service leave by Arizona state law. Refer to federal USERRA for military service. Jury duty and voting leave are governed by Arizona civil code (A.R.S. § 21-411 and § 35-393).

**Impact on role/pay changes**: Pay or manager changes must not trigger paid sick leave accrual resets or forfeiture, and must not be used as pretextual retaliation for prior sick leave use.

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, and Format Rules

**Payroll record retention**: A.R.S. § 23-364 requires employers to keep payroll records for at least four years. Records must include:

- Daily hours worked for each day
- Wages and overtime pay
- Earned paid sick time accrual and use
- Pay rates

A rebuttable presumption of minimum wage or paid sick time violation arises if records are not maintained.

**Employee access to own records**: Employees may inspect and copy payroll records pertaining to themselves (A.R.S. § 23-364).

**Personnel files**: No state statute mandating personnel file access or retention period. Federal Title VII and EEOC requirements apply. Recommended retention: three years after hire, separation, or employment action per Title VII and ADEA standards.

**Format rules**: No state requirement. Records may be maintained in paper or electronic format; recommend maintaining electronic payroll records as backup to satisfy the four-year requirement and enable timely access.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data, Biometric Monitoring, and Breach Notification

**No comprehensive employee data privacy law**: Arizona has not enacted an omnibus employee data privacy statute. No baseline restrictions on employer collection, use, or storage of employee personal information.

**Biometric data**: No Arizona statute restricts employer collection of biometric data (fingerprints, facial recognition, iris scans). Employers may implement biometric timekeeping or access systems without written notice or consent.

**Data breach notification** (ARS 18-551 and 18-552): Entities must notify Arizona residents of breaches exposing personal information, including biometric data coupled with name, within 45 days (verify exact timeline). Notification must occur without unreasonable delay. No employee-specific carve-out; same rules apply to both consumer and employee data.

**Monitoring and surveillance**: No state employee monitoring statute. Common law expectations of privacy may apply to personal electronic communications; recommend clear policies on monitoring email, internet use, and location tracking.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Check Limits, Drug Testing, and E-Verify

**Ban-the-box** (A.R.S. § 23-211, effective January 1, 2025): Employers with four or more employees cannot ask about criminal history, arrests, or convictions on an initial job application or during the initial interview. Criminal history inquiry and background checking must occur only after a conditional offer of employment is made. Employers cannot inquire about arrests that did not result in conviction, or about sealed or expunged convictions.

**Background check limits**: No state limitation on background check scope. Federal FCRA applies. Employers may use third-party screening; must provide disclosures and obtain written consent; must provide adverse action notice and opportunity to respond per FCRA. Note: verify whether Arizona has specific time limits on conviction history in background checks.

**Drug testing** (A.R.S. § 23-493.04): Employers are permitted but not required to test for drugs and alcohol. If testing is implemented, a written policy must be distributed to all employees subject to testing before any test occurs. Policy must state:

- Employer's drug and alcohol policy
- Employees or groups subject to testing
- Circumstances triggering testing (accident, reasonable suspicion, random basis)
- Substances tested for

Testing after an accident is permitted if administered as soon as practicable to employees who may have contributed. Random testing of employees or groups is permitted if authorized by written policy. Reasonable suspicion of drug/alcohol use affecting job performance is grounds for testing.

**E-Verify**: No state mandate. Federal I-9 and E-Verify (voluntary) apply.

## 9. Separation: Notice Requirements, Severance, Mini-WARN Acts, Non-Compete, and Non-Solicit

**At-will termination and notice**: Arizona requires no advance notice for at-will termination (A.R.S. § 23-1501). Exceptions: written employment contracts, federal WARN Act, or constructive discharge procedures (A.R.S. § 23-1502).

**WARN Act (federal)**: Applies to employers with 100+ full-time employees. 60 calendar days' notice required for plant closings or mass layoffs (50+ affected employees). No Arizona state mini-WARN law.

**Severance**: No Arizona statute requires severance. If offered, severance is a contractual obligation and cannot offset WARN liability unless payments are "voluntary and unconditional" (not already owed under contract or policy).

**Non-compete agreements**: Enforceable under Arizona common law if the restraint:

- Does not exceed what is reasonably necessary to protect employer's business
- Is not unreasonably restrictive of employee's rights
- Does not contravene public policy
- Is reasonable as to time and geographic scope (no statutory limits)

Must be tied to a legitimate business interest: trade secrets, confidential information, customer goodwill, or specialized training. No specific statutory codification; enforceability is fact-intensive and turns on the reasonableness of duration, geography, and activity restricted.

**Non-solicit agreements**: Generally more enforceable than non-competes in Arizona. Must be narrowly tailored to protect legitimate business interests (customer relationships, confidential information). No statutory limits.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Work, and Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**: Arizona does not apply the ABC Test for general employment classification. Instead, Arizona relies on the **common law test**, which examines:

- Control over work methods and schedule
- Financial independence
- Integration into the business
- Provision of tools and equipment
- Duration of the relationship

Misclassification as a contractor when the person is an employee can result in liability for unpaid wages, overtime, and penalties. Recommend documenting the contractor relationship, scope of work, and independence factors in a written agreement.

**Unemployment insurance determinations**: Arizona may apply a version of the ABC Test in specific unemployment insurance or wage determination contexts (verify current application).

**Remote worker rules**: No state law restricting remote work or requiring physical presence. Employers may classify roles as remote; employees working remotely are still subject to minimum wage, overtime (federal FLSA), and paid sick leave accrual. No residency or domicile requirements for remote workers.

**Multi-state reciprocity**: When an employee works partly in Arizona and partly in another state, Arizona law applies to work performed in Arizona. Interstate employees are covered by the employee protection laws of each state where they work. Recommend determining which state's wage and hour law applies (typically the state in which work is performed) and complying with the most restrictive rule. No Arizona statute specifically addresses multi-state reciprocity; apply federal conflict-of-law principles.

## 11. Implications for HCM Next Platform (P1A/P1B)

**Termination workflows**:

- Model a **7-day business-day deadline** for final wage payment upon discharge, or link to the next regular payday if sooner (A.R.S. § 23-353).
- Reject termination if the employee is on approved leave or within a 15-day constructive discharge notice response period without management acknowledgment (A.R.S. § 23-1502).
- Warn if termination occurs within 90 days of employment (may trigger disputes over paid sick leave accrual).
- Require final paycheck method selection (direct deposit or mail) and date verification.

**Pay/base-wage changes**:

- Model a **constraint**: do not reduce base wage retroactively without written employee consent (wage reduction without consent may support constructive discharge or wage theft claims).
- Flag if pay reduction occurs during, or within 30 days of, paid sick leave use (may trigger retaliation claim under A.R.S. § 23-373).
- Do not reset or reduce paid sick time accrual if base wage changes; accrue based on hours worked regardless of rate.

**Promotions and role changes**:

- Do not apply a role change if it substantially increases duties without corresponding wage increase and the change is a pretext for avoiding paid sick leave payouts (verify this risk under constructive discharge law).
- Ensure manager changes do not trigger unwritten changes to role or duties.
- Recommend written notice of material role changes to document no constructive discharge intent.

**Paid sick leave integration**:

- Track accrual per employee (1 hour per 30 hours worked) and cap at 40 or 24 hours depending on employer size.
- Prevent payout forfeiture on promotion, demotion, or termination; require payout at regular rate upon discharge (A.R.S. § 23-373).
- Warn against using paid sick leave "no use" policies; accrual is mandatory.
- Block termination if employee has used paid sick leave in the prior 90 days without documented employer response to concerns if constructive discharge is alleged.

**Pay frequency and statements**:

- Enforce minimum two paydays per month, maximum 16 days apart.
- Do not permit paycheck delay beyond five business days after pay period end (or next regular payday).
- Recommend including paid sick time balance and accrual YTD on pay stubs (not required but best practice).

**Background check workflow**:

- Do not pull criminal background check until conditional employment offer is extended (post-offer only; violates A.R.S. § 23-211 if done at application).
- Do not disqualify candidates based on arrests not resulting in conviction or sealed/expunged records.
- If an offer is rescinded based on background check, provide FCRA adverse action notice.

**Contractor vs. employee classification**:

- Implement a questionnaire or decision tree documenting control, financial independence, and business integration factors.
- Flag if classified as contractor but paid on an hourly, regular schedule similar to an employee (misclassification risk).
- Store classification rationale with the engagement letter.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether A.R.S. § 23-211 applies to government contractors or only private employers.
- Verify whether Arizona accepts ABC Test in any wage-and-hour context beyond unemployment determination.
- Verify current status of SB1548 (salary history ban) and confirm it has not passed.
- Verify whether "seven working days" in A.R.S. § 23-353 includes weekends or business days only (search results indicate business days, but statute language should be confirmed).
- Confirm whether severance payments, if offered, must be paid separately from final wage or may be combined in final paycheck.
- Clarify whether paid sick time must be paid in full upon resignation or only if the employee was discharged.
