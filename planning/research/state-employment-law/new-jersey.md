# New Jersey Employment Law Research

**State:** New Jersey | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** REVIEWED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **Employment-at-will with exceptions**: New Jersey recognizes at-will employment but prohibits termination for exercising constitutional rights, filing wage/workers' compensation claims, jury duty, military service, or public policy violations.
- **Wage Payment Law with Wage Theft Act (2019)**: N.J.S.A. 34:11-4.1 et seq. governs pay timing; Wage Theft Act extends statute of limitations to 6 years with liquidated damages of 200% of wages due, plus attorney fees and costs.
- **Minimum wage $15.92/hour (6+ employees) and $15.23 (≤5 employees)** as of 2026, with annual CPI adjustments; different rates for agricultural workers ($14.20/hour in 2026).
- **Pay Transparency Act** (N.J.S.A. 34:6B-23, effective June 1, 2025): Employers with 10+ employees must disclose hourly wage or salary range and benefits in all job postings and internal promotion notices.
- **Salary history ban** (N.J.S.A. 34:6B-20, effective Jan 1, 2020): Employers cannot inquire about or require disclosure of prospective employees' prior wages; voluntary disclosure permitted.
- **Earned Sick Leave Law** (N.J.S.A. 34:11D-1): Accrual of 1 hour per 30 hours worked (40 hours/year max); job-protected for health, caregiving, school, and safety needs; no carryover cap specified.
- **Family Leave Act + Temporary Disability Insurance** (N.J.S.A. 34:11B, 43:21-25 et seq.): State-funded paid family leave; July 2026 expansion lowers threshold to 15+ employees (previously excluded small employers).
- **NJ WARN Act (Millville Dallas Airmotive)** (N.J.S.A. 34:21-1 et seq., amended April 2023): 100+ employees nationwide; 90 days' notice for mass layoffs; mandatory severance of 1 week per year of service plus 4 additional weeks if notice inadequate.
- **Diane B. Allen Equal Pay Act** (N.J.S.A. 10:5-12): Unlawful to pay protected-class employees at lower rates for substantially similar work; 21 protected classes including race, sex, sexual orientation, gender identity, disability, age, marital status, genetic information.
- **Ban-the-box (Opportunity to Compete Act)** (N.J.S.A. 34:6B-11): Cannot inquire about criminal record during initial employment application phase unless position requires background check by law.

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

New Jersey recognizes employment-at-will: employers and employees may terminate the relationship without cause absent contractual limitation or public-policy exception.

**Exceptions to at-will termination**:

- Termination for filing wage theft, workers' compensation, or unemployment benefit claims.
- Termination for exercising constitutional rights (free speech, assembly, religious freedom).
- Termination for jury duty, voting, military service, or witnessing.
- Termination in violation of public policy (e.g., refusing illegal act, whistleblowing).
- Implied employment contract: oral or written promises of job security, detailed handbooks, or course of dealing may create enforceable duties.

**Required notices at hire and on pay/role change**:

- N.J.S.A. 34:11-4.1 et seq. requires employers provide written notice of wages, paydays, and pay practices _at hire_ and _in advance_ of any change to wages, deductions, or pay frequency.
- Notice requirements also set baseline for Wage Theft Act compliance (failure to provide notice triggers retaliation/wage-theft penalties).
- No specific statutory offer-of-employment form mandate, but written documentation of role, rate, and frequency recommended for defensibility.

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage** (N.J.S.A. 34:11-56a):

- $15.92/hour (2026) for employers with 6+ employees.
- $15.23/hour (2026) for employers with ≤5 employees.
- $14.20/hour (2026) for agricultural workers.
- Annual adjustment effective January 1, calculated using prior calendar year's CPI-U.
- Tipped employees: $8.87/hour direct wage (2026); employer must supplement to minimum wage if tips do not reach threshold.

**Overtime** (N.J.S.A. 34:11-56a):

- Time-and-a-half (1.5x) for hours over 40/week. No daily overtime (8/day) unless federal law applies.
- Exemptions: agricultural employees, certain professional/executive/administrative employees under federal FLSA.

**Pay frequency and timing** (N.J.S.A. 34:11-4.2):

- At least twice per calendar month on regular pay days designated in advance.
- Exceptions: Bona fide executive/supervisory staff may be paid once per month if paid in full.
- If payday falls on non-work day, payment due on preceding work day.
- End of pay period no more than 10 working days before payday.

**Final pay on termination** (N.J.S.A. 34:11-4.3):

- All wages due must be paid by the next regular payday following termination (whether voluntary or involuntary).
- Applies to all types of termination (discharge, suspension, layoff, resignation).
- For incentive-compensation employees, reasonable approximation acceptable until exact amounts computed.

**Permitted deductions** (N.J.S.A. 34:11-4.4):

- Only deductions required by law (tax, garnishment, court order) or with written authorization for court-ordered support.
- No deductions for tools, uniforms, or employer costs unless written employee consent given and deduction does not reduce wages below minimum.
- Wage Theft Act (2019) criminalizes unlawful deductions; violations trigger 6-year civil claim plus 200% liquidated damages and attorney fees.

**Pay statement content**:

- No specific statutory itemization requirement, but federal FLSA rules apply. Employers should document hours, rate, deductions, and net pay for 3-year record retention (see Records section).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Wage range disclosure** (N.J.S.A. 34:6B-23, effective June 1, 2025):

- Employers with 10+ employees (over 20 calendar weeks) must include in all job postings the hourly wage or salary range (minimum and maximum).
- Also required: general description of benefits and other compensation for the position.
- Applies to new positions, transfers, and promotions.
- Covers all posting formats: websites, print, email, social media, newsletters.
- Violation triggers employee complaint process through NJDOL.

**Salary history ban** (N.J.S.A. 34:6B-20, effective Jan 1, 2020):

- Employers cannot inquire about prospective employees' wage/salary history, nor direct third parties to inquire.
- Candidates may voluntarily disclose without employer prompting.
- Retaliation (refusing to interview, hire, or employ for refusal to disclose) prohibited.

**Equal pay statute** (Diane B. Allen Act, N.J.S.A. 10:5-12):

- Unlawful to pay employees of protected class at lower rate than other employees for substantially similar work.
- 21 protected classes: race, color, national origin, nationality, ancestry, creed, sex, age, marital status, civil union status, domestic partnership status, affectional/sexual orientation, gender identity/expression, disability, armed forces service, genetic information, atypical hereditary cellular/blood trait, refusal to submit to genetic test.
- Applies when viewed as composite of skill, effort, and responsibility.
- Treble damages (3x) if jury finds violation; amended anti-retaliation provision at N.J.S.A. 10:5-12(r).

**Pay data reporting**:

- No state pay-equity audit requirement; federal EEO-1 filing applies if applicable (federal: 100+ employees).

## 5. Leave and Time: Earned Sick Leave, Family Leave, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**Earned Sick and Safe Leave** (N.J.S.A. 34:11D-1 et seq., effective Oct 29, 2018):

- All employers must provide up to 40 hours of job-protected earned leave per benefit year.
- Accrual: 1 hour per 30 hours worked; employers may establish benefit-year timing (calendar year or other).
- Permitted uses: employee's own physical/mental health diagnosis, care, treatment, or recovery; care for family member; domestic violence, sexual assault, stalking; child's school conference; workplace or school closure due to public health emergency; quarantine recommended during public health emergency.
- No carryover cap specified in statute (verify whether unused hours must carry forward or may reset).
- Employers may require certification if employee uses >3 consecutive days for one reason.

**Family Leave Insurance and Temporary Disability** (N.J.S.A. 34:11B, 43:21-25 et seq.):

- State-funded programs (separate from employer sick leave): Family Leave Insurance (FLI) provides paid leave to bond with newborn/adoptive/foster child or care for seriously ill loved one; Temporary Disability Insurance (TDI) covers employee's own serious illness/injury.
- As of July 17, 2026: employers with 15+ employees covered (previously excluded small employers); minimum employment period drops to 3 months; required hours worked drops to 250.
- (verify) Prior threshold: 1,000 hours in base period for FLI/TDI eligibility; coverage now broader for small employers.

**Interaction with pay/role changes**:

- On promotion during earned sick leave, employee generally returns to promoted role; clarify whether wage must match original or promoted rate.
- Earned leave accrual may continue during unpaid family leave (verify integration with NJFLA case law).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention** (N.J.S.A. 34:11D-6, 34:11-4.6):

- Payroll records: employers must retain for 5 years documenting hours worked by employees and earned sick leave taken.
- General employee records: retain 3 years (hours, wage rate, gross/net pay, deductions per 34:11-4.6).
- Personnel files: retain 2 years after termination; disputed items must be accompanied by employee's written statement.
- Medical records: store separately; retain at least 3 years post-termination (ADA/HIPAA compliance).

**Employee access to personnel files — correction (2026-09-03 contradiction closure):**

- N.J.S.A. 34:8B-1 is not a personnel-records statute; Title 34, Chapter 8B is New Jersey's staffing/temporary-help service firm registration chapter and contains no employee file-inspection right. No "Personnel Files Act" with a 7-business-day window exists in New Jersey law.
- **New Jersey has no general private-sector statute giving employees a right to inspect or copy their personnel file.** As with wage records (§ 6, above, which are a statutory right under the Wage Payment Law), personnel files themselves are not covered by a comparable access statute.
- Employers may voluntarily adopt an inspection policy, and a collective bargaining agreement may grant access; absent one of those, an employee's only recourse is discovery in litigation or a public-records request (public-sector employers only).
- Do not model a statutory NJ personnel-file inspection deadline; model it as employer-policy-defined (nullable) rather than a hard-coded 7-day or 12-month rule.

**Record format**:

- No statute mandates electronic vs. paper format; electronic records acceptable if retention and access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy, Breach Notification, Biometric/Monitoring, Social Media

**Data breach notification** (N.J.S.A. 56:8-163, Identity Theft Prevention Act):

- Applies to personal information (name + SSN, credit card, etc.) accessed by unauthorized person.
- Notification to affected individuals in most expedient time possible without unreasonable delay; within 5 business days where consistent with law enforcement needs.
- Report to NJ Division of State Police before individual notifications.
- Applies broadly to employee records if they contain personal information subject to breach.

**Social media and personal accounts** (N.J.S.A. 34:6B-5):

- Employers cannot require employees or prospective employees to disclose personal social media usernames, passwords, or provide access to personal accounts.
- Violations: firing or refusal to hire for non-disclosure prohibited.
- Employers may view publicly available social media content but should avoid discrimination claims (verify case law on monitoring external posts).
- Exception: employers may require disclosure of non-personal (employer-provided) account credentials for work systems.

**Workplace monitoring**:

- No state statute bans email/internet monitoring of work devices or workplace video monitoring; reasonable expectation of privacy doctrine applies.

**Data residency**:

- No NJ statute mandates employee data residency; employers may store/process in-state or out-of-state provided security and privacy laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box** (Opportunity to Compete Act, N.J.S.A. 34:6B-11 et seq., effective March 1, 2015):

- Employers cannot make any inquiry (verbal or written) about applicant's criminal record during initial employment application process (IEAP) unless position-specific exception applies.
- Exceptions: law enforcement, corrections, judiciary, homeland security, emergency management roles; positions requiring criminal background check by law; arrests/convictions may legally preclude position.
- Violation penalties: up to $300 per applicant per violation for repeated offenses.

**Background checks**:

- No state statute restricting private-sector background checks; federal FCRA applies. Employers using third-party consumer reporting agencies must provide FCRA notice, dispute rights, and adverse-action procedures.

**Drug testing**:

- No state-level statute regulating private-sector drug testing; federal law (pre-employment, reasonable suspicion, post-accident) and federal DOT rules apply.
- Cannabis exception: N.J.S.A. 24:6I-52 prohibits adverse action based solely on cannabinoid metabolites from legally permitted cannabis use; employers may test for on-the-job impairment upon reasonable suspicion or observable signs.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- No state mandate for private-sector E-Verify; federal I-9/E-Verify requirements apply to all employers.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice (Millville Dallas Airmotive Plant Job Loss Notification Act, amended April 10, 2023)** (N.J.S.A. 34:21-1 et seq.):

- Applies to all employers with 100+ employees anywhere in the United States.
- Requires 90 days' written notice (30 days more than federal WARN Act) before mass layoff, termination, or plant closure.
- Notice to: all affected employees (including part-time, temporary, contingent), union representatives, state Department of Labor and Workforce Development, local elected officials.
- Mandatory severance: 1 week per year of service for all affected workers (no statutory cap).
- Penalty for inadequate notice: additional 4 weeks severance on top of standard amount.
- Penalties: civil penalty up to $10,000/day; separate from severance obligations.

**Severance**:

- No state-mandated severance except under WARN Act (above). Generally contractual, not mandatory, absent employment agreement.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- Governed by common law: non-competes enforceable if they (1) protect legitimate employer interests (trade secrets, confidential info, customer relationships); (2) impose no undue hardship on employee; (3) not injurious to public.
- Must be reasonable in duration, geography, and scope. Courts typically uphold 6-month to 2-year durations depending on nature of business and role.
- NJ courts apply "blue penciling" (judicial modification of overly broad agreements).
- Pending bills (e.g., A-4995) propose broader restrictions; verify current legislative status.

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- No specific statute; enforced under common law reasonableness standard.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification** (ABC Test, N.J.S.A. 43:21-19(i)(6)):

- Under Unemployment Compensation Law, alleged employer must prove all three prongs of ABC test or worker deemed employee:
  - (A) Individual exercises total control over when and how they perform job.
  - (B) Work is outside scope of employer's usual business, or work performed away from employer's regular worksites.
  - (C) Individual has own separate business, occupation, or trade.
- Failure to meet any prong = employee classification.
- Proposed NJDOL regulations (April 2025) indicate stricter interpretation forthcoming.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine. If employee works across state lines, each state's laws apply to work performed in that state.
- Platform should flag multi-state workers and ensure compliance with all applicable state rules.

**Remote workers**:

- No NJ statute specifically addressing remote-work classification. Standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If employee works in NJ (even remotely) for any employer, NJ law applies to that work.
- NJ LAD (10:5-1 et seq.) applies to discrimination by NJ employers against employees who reside and work remotely outside NJ.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling: Promotion + Base-Pay Change)

**Mandatory obligations and field restrictions**:

1. **Pay Transparency Notice** (§ 34:6B-23): Before creating or posting a position, require input of wage range (min/max) and benefits description; block job posting if these fields empty; timestamp disclosure. On internal promotion posting, ensure reasonable-notice requirement met (10+ employees threshold).

2. **Pay Rate Change Notice** (§ 34:11-4.1): On any pay decrease, generate automated notice in advance of effective date with new rate, frequency, and effective date; permit pay increases without advance notice (system default).

3. **Salary History Inquiry Block** (§ 34:6B-20): Disable any form field attempting to request prior salary/wages from external candidates; log any such queries as compliance risk.

4. **Equal Pay Audit Flag** (Diane B. Allen § 10:5-12): On promotion + base-pay change, system should flag if new rate differs significantly from peer rates for "substantially similar work" (same county/employer); require manager justification or HR/legal approval to override.

5. **Wage Theft Act Compliance** (§ 34:11-4.1): Document all pay rate changes and provide written notice in advance; failure to provide notice triggers retaliation and wage-theft penalties (200% liquidated damages, attorney fees, 6-year statute of limitations). Track all notices for audit trail.

6. **Earned Leave Accrual and Termination** (§ 34:11D-1): Track accrued sick leave (1 hour per 30 hours worked, 40-hour max per year). On termination, automatic payout of accrued balance unless written policy limits compensation exists and was provided at hire. On promotion within same employer, leave balance carries forward.

7. **Final Pay Timing** (§ 34:11-4.3): Schedule final paycheck by next regular payday following termination; include all wages due.

8. **WARN Act Trigger** (§ 34:21-1 as amended): If planned layoff affects 100+ employees nationwide or 25+ employees in single event and employer has 100+ employees total, auto-flag and require 90-day notice scheduling (vs. federal WARN's 60 days). Mandatory severance: 1 week per year of service, plus 4 additional weeks if notice inadequate.

9. **Ban-the-box Compliance** (§ 34:6B-11): On initial application, do _not_ ask about criminal record; if position requires criminal record inquiry by law, defer until after first interview. Log interview date and track timing for audit compliance.

10. **Multi-State Worker Flag** (§ 43:21-19 ABC test, 10:5-1 LAD): If employee works across state lines, flag and ensure compliance with all applicable state rules. For remote workers physically in NJ, apply full NJ law regardless of employer location.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether earned sick leave accrual continues during unpaid family leave.
- Confirm whether employee returning from family leave to same or promoted role must receive same salary as at departure or promoted salary.
- Verify exact calculation of "substantially similar work" for equal-pay comparisons (composite of skill/effort/responsibility—geographic limits?).
- Clarify whether WARN Act severance obligation applies to employees working outside NJ for NJ employers.
- Confirm status of implied employment contracts in recent NJ case law and factual triggers.
- Verify legislative status of A-4995 (proposed non-compete ban) and any pending narrowing bills.
