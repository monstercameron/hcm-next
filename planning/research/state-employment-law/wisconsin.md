# Wisconsin Employment Law Research

**State:** Wisconsin | **Researched:** 2026-09-03 | **Status:** DRAFTED

## 1. Summary for HCM Next

- **At-will employment with public policy exceptions**: Wisconsin presumes all employment is at-will; exceptions protect employees discharged for asserting legal rights, refusing illegal acts, jury duty, voting, whistleblowing, workers' compensation claims, or military service.
- **Minimum wage $7.25; no local wage ordinances**: Wisconsin adopts the federal minimum wage ($7.25; tipped $2.33, or $2.13 for opportunity employees). Wis. Stat. § 104.001(2) has preempted all local minimum-wage ordinances since 2005, so no Milwaukee wage floor exists.
- **Final pay timing: regular payday or 24 hours on business separation**: Employers must pay all wages on next regularly scheduled payday, or within 24 hours if employment ends due to merger, liquidation, relocation, or business closure (§ 109.03).
- **No paid sick leave at state level; Milwaukee ordinance preempted in 2011**: Wisconsin law prohibits local paid-leave ordinances; Milwaukee's 2008 ordinance voided by 2011 state preemption (§ 103.10(1m)).
- **Family/Medical Leave Act (FMLA) at state level**: Employers with 50+ employees must provide 6 weeks childbirth/adoption, 2 weeks family care, 2 weeks own serious health condition; 52-week tenure + 1,000 hours required (§ 103.10).
- **Non-compete enforceability under reasonableness test**: Governed by § 103.465; courts enforce if duration, geography, and scope are reasonable; blue-pencil doctrine applies (Runzheimer, 2015 WI 45).
- **Wisconsin Fair Employment Act (WFEA) covers all employers**: Prohibits discrimination by sex, sexual orientation, arrest/conviction record (with exceptions), military service, disability, and other classes (§ 111.31–111.395).
- **Personnel file access twice per calendar year within 7 working days**: Employees may inspect records (§ 103.13); corrections/disputes recorded in file.
- **Mass Layoff Notice (Wisconsin WARN) for 50+ employers**: 60 days' written notice required for mass layoff (25%+ workforce or 25+ employees, whichever greater) or business closure (25+ employees) (§ 109.07).
- **No state pay transparency law or salary-history ban**: Wisconsin has no statewide requirement for salary-range posting; AB 748 (2018) preempts local salary-history bans (§ 111.31(1)).

## 2. Employment Relationship: At-Will Status and Exceptions

Wisconsin presumes all employment is at-will under common law; either party may terminate without cause or notice (except where contract or collective bargaining agreement provides otherwise).

**Exceptions to at-will status** (public policy exceptions):

- Discharge for asserting legal rights (jury duty, voting, whistleblowing per § 15.361 et seq.).
- Discharge in retaliation for workers' compensation claim or unemployment claim filing.
- Discharge for refusing to violate law.
- Discharge for military service or reemployment rights after discharge (§ 321.64).

**Required written notices at hire and on change**:

- No Wisconsin statute mandates written notice of employment terms at hire (federal law does not require this).
- No statutory notice period for pay changes; however, best practice is written notice of material changes (hours, rate, role).

## 3. Wages: Minimum Wage, Overtime, Pay Frequency, Final Pay, Deductions

**Minimum wage**:

- Wisconsin minimum wage is $7.25 per hour (tracks federal minimum, Wis. Stat. § 104.035(1)(a)). No automatic inflation adjustment in state statute.
- **No local minimum-wage ordinances**: Wis. Stat. § 104.001(2) (2005) bars municipalities from enacting minimum-wage ordinances and voided existing ones; no Milwaukee wage floor applies.
- **Tipped employees**: $2.33/hour (non-opportunity) or $2.13/hour (opportunity); employer must make up difference if tips plus tipped wage fall below $7.25.
- **Opportunity employees** (age <20, first 90 days of employment): $5.90/hour permitted.

**Overtime**:

- Employees must be paid time-and-one-half regular rate for all hours over 40 per week (DWD 274.03).
- Overtime calculated on weekly basis only (no daily or 7th-day overtime mandates).
- Exemptions include: administrative, executive, professional employees (with salary threshold ~$700–$750/month); outside salespersons; agricultural; motor-carrier employees; taxi drivers; certain amusement/seasonal businesses (DWD 274.04(1)(b)).

**Pay frequency**:

- Employers must pay wages **at least monthly** (Wis. Stat. § 109.03(1)). Wages earned through a date not more than 31 days prior to payment must be paid by the payment date. Logging and farm labor employees: at least quarterly.

**Final pay on termination**:

- **Employee quit or discharge**: All wages due must be paid by whichever comes first: (a) the next regularly scheduled payday, or (b) the payment frequency deadline (§ 109.03(2)).
- **Business separation** (merger, liquidation, relocation, cease operations): Employer must pay all unpaid wages within **24 hours of separation** (§ 109.03(4)).

**Permitted deductions**:

- Deduction for faulty workmanship, loss, theft, or damage is permitted ONLY if: (a) employee writes written authorization _after_ the incident and _before_ deduction; OR (b) employer and employee representative mutually determine negligence/carelessness/willful conduct; OR (c) court finds employee liable (§ 103.455).
- Each deduction requires separate written consent; cannot include deduction already signed for another purpose (e.g., timesheet signature).
- Violations: employer liable for **twice the deduction amount** in civil action.
- Legally required deductions (taxes, garnishments, child support) and authorized plan deductions permitted without separate consent.

**Pay statement requirements**:

- Employers must provide itemized pay statement showing hours worked, rate of pay, and amount and reason for each deduction (DWD 272.11(1)).
- Records (payroll, name, address, occupation, hours, wages, deductions) must be retained for **at least 3 years** (DWD 272.11(1)).

## 4. Pay Transparency and Equity

**Salary range disclosure**:

- **No Wisconsin state law** requires salary ranges in job postings. Federal law does not mandate this either.
- Proposed bills (considered but not enacted) would require salary/wage ranges in postings for employers with 15+ employees and for both new-hire and promotion roles.

**Salary history ban**:

- **No state ban on salary-history inquiries**: Wisconsin AB 748 (2018) explicitly preempts local governments from restricting employer inquiries into prospective employee salary history (§ 111.31(1)).
- Employers may request prior salary information during hiring.

**Pay data reporting**:

- No Wisconsin state pay-gap or pay-equity reporting requirement.
- Federal EEO-1 reporting applies if employer meets coverage threshold (100+ employees and federal contractor).

**Equal pay statute and protected classes**:

- Wisconsin Fair Employment Act (WFEA) (§ 111.31–111.395) prohibits discrimination "because of sex" and other classes; no explicit "equal pay for equal work" statute separate from sex discrimination.
- **Protected classes**: race, color, disability, marital status, sex, sexual orientation (defined as preference for heterosexuality, homosexuality, or bisexuality, or history/identification thereof; § 111.36), national origin, ancestry, age, creed, military service, arrest record, conviction record (with exceptions), use or nonuse of lawful products (with exceptions; § 111.35), genetic testing (verify specific statute).

## 5. Leave and Time: Paid Sick Leave, Family Leave, FMLA, Interaction with Pay/Role Changes

**No paid sick leave requirement** (state level):

- Wisconsin has no statewide paid sick leave mandate. Milwaukee's 2008 ordinance (72 hours paid for 10+ employees, 40 hours for <10) was preempted by state law in 2011 (§ 103.10(1m)).

**Wisconsin Family and Medical Leave Act** (§ 103.10):

- **Employer coverage**: 50+ employees in Wisconsin.
- **Employee eligibility**: Must have worked for employer 52+ consecutive weeks and 1,000+ hours in past 52 weeks.
- **Entitlements**: Up to 6 weeks for childbirth/adoption (must begin within 16 weeks); up to 2 weeks for family member (child, spouse, parent, domestic partner) serious health condition; up to 2 weeks for employee's own serious health condition (per calendar year).
- **Interaction with pay/role changes**: Returning employees have job restoration rights; pay reductions must not be retaliatory for leave use and should be documented if driven by separate policy change.
- **Paid leave substitution**: Employers may require substitution of accrued paid leave for unpaid leave (verify: may require substitution, or optional).

**Interaction with Federal FMLA**:

- Federal FMLA applies if covered (50+ employees within 75-mile radius; 12+ months tenure; 1,250+ hours prior 12 months).

## 6. Records and Access: Retention, Employee Access, Format

**Payroll and personnel record retention**:

- **Payroll records**: Must be kept for **3 years** including name, address, birth date, occupation, pay rate, hours, wages, itemized deductions, fringe benefits (DWD 272.11(1)).
- **Personnel records**: Under Wis. Stat. § 103.13, any record maintained by employer that identifies employee and affects employment decisions (e.g., qualifications, discipline, compensation) is a personnel record. No maximum retention period specified.

**Employee access to personnel files**:

- Employees may inspect records **up to 2 times per calendar year** unless collective bargaining agreement permits more (§ 103.13).
- Access must be provided within **7 working days** of written request.
- Employees may obtain copies; employers may charge reasonable fee for reproduction costs.
- Inspection at worksite during regular business hours, or at employer's alternative reasonable time/place if inconvenient.

**Disputed information**:

- If employee disputes information in file, employer and employee may agree to removal or correction (§ 103.13). If no agreement, employee may submit written statement of position, which must be maintained in file and accompany any third-party disclosure.

**Record format**:

- No Wisconsin statute specifies electronic vs. paper format; electronic records permitted if retention and access requirements met.

## 7. Privacy and Data: Employee Data Privacy Law, Biometric/Monitoring, Breach Notification, Data Residency

**Privacy framework**:

- No comprehensive Wisconsin employee data privacy law analogous to CCPA/GDPR. General privacy torts and confidentiality law apply.

**Breach notification**:

- Wis. Stat. § 134.98 requires any entity owning/licensing personal information to notify individuals **within 45 days** of learning of unauthorized acquisition of personal information not encrypted/redacted/altered in manner rendering it unreadable.
- **Personal information**: Last name + first name/initial linked to SSN, financial account number, driver's license number, passport number, biometric identifier, or similar sensitive data (if not publicly available).
- **Exemption**: No notification required if acquisition does not create material risk of identity theft/fraud.
- Applies to Wisconsin employers with employee records containing PII; no private right of action stated in statute.

**Electronic monitoring and workplace surveillance**:

- No comprehensive Wisconsin statute regulating workplace monitoring.
- **Internet Privacy Protection Act** (Wis. Stat. § 37.271–278): Employers cannot require employees to grant access to personal internet accounts (email, social media, passwords) or discharge/discipline for refusal. Exceptions: employer-provided accounts, publicly posted content, legal process, or employer's reasonable belief of unlawful activity.

**Data residency and processing**:

- No Wisconsin law mandates data residency for employee records; in-state or out-of-state storage permitted so long as data security/privacy laws met.

## 8. Hiring and Background: Ban-the-Box, Background Checks, Drug Testing, E-Verify

**Ban-the-box** (criminal history inquiry):

- Wisconsin law does not impose a statewide ban-the-box requirement for private employers (no statewide mandate; federal law does not require this). **Verify**: local ordinances in Madison/Dane County may apply.

**Background checks**:

- No Wisconsin statute restricts private-sector background checks; federal FCRA applies if third-party consumer reporting agency used.
- Wis. Stat. § 111.335 prohibits employers from requesting arrest records or "undisputed pending charges" except where employment depends on bondability or bond required by law/regulation. Employers cannot request/maintain records of misdemeanor arrests without conviction or detention without conviction.
- Wisconsin Clean Slate law (2020, implementation began April 2023) automatically seals eligible records; employers cannot use sealed records in hiring decisions.

**Drug testing**:

- No Wisconsin state statute regulating private-sector pre-employment or post-accident drug testing. Employers have wide flexibility to test for illegal substances.
- **Medical marijuana**: Employees with medical marijuana cardholders have no statutory protection against termination for THC-positive test in Wisconsin.

**E-Verify mandate**:

- **No state mandate** for private-sector E-Verify use; federal I-9 verification required for all employers.
- **Proposed legislation (2025)**: Bills introduced requiring E-Verify for state contractors (projects >$50K) and certain subcontractors on state construction contracts; not yet enacted statewide.

## 9. Separation: Mini-WARN, Severance, Notice Requirements, Non-Compete/Non-Solicit Enforceability

**Mass layoff notice and severance**:

- **Wisconsin Business Closing and Mass Layoff Law** (Wis. Stat. § 109.07): Applies to employers with 50+ employees in Wisconsin. Notice required 60 days before mass layoff (25% workforce or 25+ employees affected, whichever greater) or business closing (25+ employees affected). No severance mandate; notice only.
- **Federal WARN Act** (60 days' notice for 100+ employees, 50+ affected) supersedes Wisconsin law for covered employers.

**Non-compete enforceability**:

- Governed by Wis. Stat. § 103.465: "Any covenant by an assistant, servant or agent not to compete...is lawful and enforceable only if the restrictions imposed are reasonably necessary for the protection of the employer or principal."
- **Reasonableness test** (Runzheimer Intl., Ltd. v. Friedlen, 2015 WI 45): Courts evaluate (1) legitimate business interest (customer relationships, trade secrets, confidential info); (2) duration (6 months–2 years generally reasonable, longer requires justification); (3) territory (must be limited to areas employer actually conducts business); (4) scope of activity (must be narrowly defined); (5) public interest.
- **Remedy for unreasonableness**: "Any covenant imposing an unreasonable restraint is illegal, void and unenforceable even as to any part...that would be a reasonable restraint." Wisconsin courts may modify (blue-pencil) unreasonable covenants to enforce reasonable portions; no automatic unenforceability.
- **Consideration**: Forbearance from terminating at-will employee is valid consideration; signing after hire does not void covenant (Runzheimer).

**Non-solicitation enforceability**:

- Enforced under § 103.465 reasonableness standard; typically 1 year duration, limited geography, narrow scope.

## 10. Classification and Multi-State: Contractor Tests, Remote Workers, Reciprocity

**Independent contractor classification**:

- Wisconsin applies **ABC test** via unemployment/workers' comp determination: all three factors must be met:
  - (A) Freedom from direction/control in performance and fact.
  - (B) Service outside employer's usual course of business or outside all locations.
  - (C) Customarily engaged in independently established trade/occupation of same nature.
- Misclassification enforced by Wisconsin Department of Workforce Development and Attorney General.

**Remote workers**:

- No Wisconsin statute specifically addressing remote-work classification or benefits. Standard employment law applies regardless of work location.
- If Wisconsin employer or work performed in Wisconsin, Wisconsin law applies.

**Multi-state employment**:

- No reciprocity doctrine; each state's law applies to work performed in that state. HCM platform should flag multi-state workers for separate state compliance analysis.

## 11. Implications for P1A/P1B (HCM Platform Modeling)

**Required typed obligations and timing constraints**:

1. **Minimum wage compliance**: Default non-exempt classification; validate hourly rate ($7.25 statewide floor; no local ordinances under § 104.001(2)).

2. **Overtime accrual and payment**: Flag hours >40/week for non-exempt employees; calculate 1.5x regular rate weekly, not daily.

3. **Final pay timing on termination**: For discharge, trigger payment by next regular payday. For business separation (merger, liquidation, relocation, cease operations), trigger 24-hour payment window; document reason for separation type.

4. **Family/Medical Leave (§ 103.10)**: Gate by employer size (50+), employee tenure (52+ weeks), and hours (1,000+ prior 12 months). Track 6-week birth/adoption entitlement (must begin within 16 weeks), 2-week family care, 2-week own health per calendar year. Allow substitution of accrued paid leave (verify: required or optional substitution).

5. **Non-compete/non-solicit storage**: Store execution date, duration, geography, scope. Flag any non-compete >2 years or nationwide scope for legal review. Require reasonableness certification before enforcing.

6. **Deductions for faulty work**: Block manual wage deduction unless: (a) written authorization _after_ incident dated and signed, OR (b) joint negligence determination documented, OR (c) court judgment referenced. Flag violation attempts; calculate double-penalty liability.

7. **Personnel file access tracking**: Implement 2-per-calendar-year inspection limit; track request dates and employee responses. Store dispute statements separately.

8. **Pay statement itemization**: Ensure pay stubs show hours, rate, and deduction reasons (not coded without explanation).

9. **Mass Layoff Notice (§ 109.07)**: For employers 50+, flag mass layoff (25% workforce or 25+ employees) or business closure (25+ employees) and enforce 60-day notice requirement; validate recipient list (affected employees, unions, municipality).

10. **Wisconsin FMLA return from leave**: Ensure job restoration rights on return; document any role/pay change as separate business decision unrelated to leave.

**Field restrictions and validations**:

- **Minimum wage field**: Enforce the $7.25 statewide minimum; no local overrides (§ 104.001(2)).
- **Overtime exemption**: Default non-exempt; exempt categories require documentation of duties + salary threshold verification.
- **Paid sick leave**: Block or disable (no state mandate; Milwaukee ordinance preempted).
- **Final pay calculation**: Auto-calculate accrued unpaid leave/wages on termination; trigger 24-hour payment if business separation detected.
- **Non-compete field**: Require reasonableness metadata (duration, geography, scope) and legal certification before storage.

**Open questions for legal review**:

- Verify whether paid FMLA leave can be required to be taken as paid leave when accrued leave exists; what is the interaction if employee runs out of paid leave during FMLA leave.
- Verify whether accrued FMLA leave (if paid) must be paid out on termination or can be forfeited.
- Clarify whether implied employment contract doctrine applies in Wisconsin and triggers any notice/severance obligations.
- Confirm whether non-competes signed after hire (post-employment) can be modified or voided absent additional consideration.
- Verify whether blue-pencil modification in Wisconsin allows downward adjustment of duration or only geographic/scope narrowing.
