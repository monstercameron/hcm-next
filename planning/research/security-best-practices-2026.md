# Security best practices for hcm-next, 2026 research

Read date for web sources: 2026-09-05. This is a research baseline for converting security, privacy, financial-control, and public-sector requirements into checkable engineering controls. A cited standard is not automatically a legal obligation for every tenant. Applicability depends on the tenant, data, contract, deployment boundary, payment flow, and jurisdiction. The tables use primary sources where available. Recommendations that synthesize several sources are labeled as engineering controls and should be assigned an owner, test, evidence location, and review date.

The repository documents describe hcm-next as a Go HR transaction-control plane spanning HCM, payroll, finance, identity, and access. Its security model is already more granular than a department-only model: tenant, organization, record, field, purpose, and context are separate authorization dimensions. This survey therefore treats authorization evidence, policy and legal-rule provenance, data placement, controlled financial writes, and ledger verification as first-class security surfaces.

## 1. Enterprise applications in general

Enterprise applications should make identity, authorization, data handling, software provenance, auditability, and recovery properties testable at the system boundary. NIST CSF 2.0 supplies a risk-outcome taxonomy rather than a prescriptive implementation, while NIST SP 800-53 Rev. 5, ISO/IEC 27001:2022, CIS Controls v8.1, SOC 2 Trust Services Criteria, and OWASP ASVS provide complementary control and assurance vocabularies. NIST SP 800-63-4 is the current digital identity guideline and supersedes SP 800-63-3. ### Implications for hcm-next

The repository has a strong authorization and evidence foundation. `internal/trust/authz` models tenant, organization, record, population, relationship, field, purpose, and context decisions, consumes bitemporal projections, fails closed on missing or stale projections, and returns rule IDs, policy versions, input digests, and durable evidence IDs. `internal/trust/session` already specifies server-side lifecycle, refresh rotation, replay detection, idle and absolute limits, revocation, and durable transition evidence. `internal/trust/stepup` binds sensitive operations to a short-lived, single-use proof. `planning/specs/legal-rule-packs-and-state-configuration.md` separates rule author, reviewer, publisher, and customer-defined configuration, and requires signed receipts. `tools/policy/provenance` and `tools/policy/sbom` provide the intended provenance and CycloneDX direction. The ledger checkpoint and evidence packages provide signed, tenant-scoped, independently verifiable evidence, and migrations use forced RLS with transaction-local tenant context.

The gaps are integration and production-hardening gaps rather than absence of concepts. The existing trust documentation describes OAuth, OIDC, SAML, workload identity, and mTLS as pluggable future verifiers, while the session store is currently in-memory and the provenance and SBOM tools have documented operational limitations. The following candidate todos use the repository's RED and GREEN convention. They are proposals only and do not modify `planning/todos.md`.

1. `SEC-ENT-001` Federation verifier and tenant issuer registry. **RED:** trust has a generic verifier seam, but no production OIDC/SAML issuer registry, key rotation behavior, nonce, audience, or organization-to-tenant mapping contract. **GREEN:** registered providers validate issuer, signature, audience, nonce, time, and tenant mapping; negative tests and durable evidence cover every rejection.
2. `SEC-ENT-002` Phishing-resistant assurance and recovery. **RED:** step-up binds operations, but passkey enrollment, authenticator lifecycle, recovery, and NIST IAL/AAL/FAL mapping are not represented as durable policy inputs. **GREEN:** a policy table maps credential and proofing evidence to assurance; high-risk payroll, bank, export, repair, and break-glass operations reject insufficient or recovered assurance without fresh proof.
3. `SEC-ENT-003` Replicated session and revocation store. **RED:** session documentation specifies the lifecycle but identifies in-memory storage and durable replication as deferred. **GREEN:** the production store is replicated, fail-closed on ambiguity, supports refresh-family replay revocation, and exports transition evidence and recovery tests.
4. `SEC-ENT-004` JIT and break-glass lease control. **RED:** the plan names JIT and break-glass as baseline expectations, but there is no end-to-end lease, dual approval, expiry, reason, notification, and post-use review contract. **GREEN:** privileged access is time-bound, purpose-bound, tenant-bound, automatically revoked, independently alerted, and reviewed from ledger evidence.
5. `SEC-ENT-005` KMS/HSM and customer-key adapter. **RED:** the plan recognizes externally held key material, but key custody, envelope encryption, rotation, BYOK availability, and signing-key revocation are not connected to runtime storage and ledger checkpoints. **GREEN:** each protected field or artifact identifies a key version and purpose; KMS/HSM operations, rotation, restore, revocation, and customer-key failure are tested.
6. `SEC-ENT-006` Secret and certificate lifecycle gate. **RED:** no repository-level evidence contract proves that credentials, certificates, and signing keys are absent from source and rotated before expiry. **GREEN:** CI scans, inventory, owner, expiry, rotation test, and emergency revocation evidence are required for release.
7. `SEC-ENT-007` Release provenance verification. **RED:** provenance is designed, but the SBOM generator currently encounters the malformed `agenthub` module path and provenance still needs a production signing and verification gate. **GREEN:** pinned toolchain and dependencies produce a signed provenance and SBOM whose digests, builder identity, source reference, and reproducibility result are verified before deployment.
8. `SEC-ENT-008` Threat-model and application-security evidence. **RED:** no common artifact links threat model, ASVS requirements, SAST, DAST, SCA, fuzzing, mutation testing, and accepted risk to a release. **GREEN:** high-risk packages and policy evaluators cannot release without the required test evidence and time-bounded exceptions.
9. `SEC-ENT-009` Immutable security telemetry retention. **RED:** ledger evidence is verifiable, but security telemetry, alert configuration, clock uncertainty, retention, legal hold, and external anchoring are not one operational contract. **GREEN:** security events and business-control events have tamper evidence, retention class, hold state, alert linkage, and an offline verification path.
10. `SEC-ENT-010` Classification, tokenization, and egress policy. **RED:** tenant and field authorization exist, but classification, lower-environment tokenization, DLP, export watermarking, and controlled egress are not consistently represented. **GREEN:** every sensitive field has handling rules and every export records purpose, scope, reviewer or step-up, destination, redaction, and policy receipt.
11. `SEC-ENT-011` Cross-path tenant isolation tests. **RED:** migrations provide forced RLS and fail-closed tenant settings, but cache, queues, jobs, object storage, logs, backups, and exports need explicit isolation tests. **GREEN:** a reusable test suite proves no cross-tenant read, write, cache hit, job execution, evidence part, backup restore, or log disclosure.

## 2. Financial applications and payment-touching systems

Payroll is a financial-control system even when the product does not directly handle card data. Scope must be determined by actual data and payment paths. PCI DSS applies to entities that store, process, or transmit cardholder or sensitive authentication data, or can affect the cardholder-data environment. ACH obligations combine Nacha rules, bank and third-party risk management, and fraud controls. SOX audit evidence concerns internal control over financial reporting, including relevant application and IT general controls, while GLBA and tax safeguards protect customer or federal tax information. Sources: [PCI DSS scope](https://www.pcisecuritystandards.org/standards/pci-dss/) (read 2026-09-05), [Nacha data-security rule](https://www.nacha.org/rules/supplementing-data-security-requirements) (read 2026-09-05), [FFIEC authentication guidance](https://www.ffiec.gov/news/press-releases/2021/pr-08-11) (read 2026-09-05), [PCAOB AS 2201](https://pcaobus.org/oversight/standards/auditing-standards/details/AS2201) (read 2026-09-05), [FTC Safeguards Rule](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/safeguards-rule) (read 2026-09-05), and [IRS Publication 1075](https://www.irs.gov/privacy-disclosure/safeguards-program) (read 2026-09-05).

### Implications for hcm-next

The repository's proposal, approval, controlled-write, reconciliation, and ledger model is suitable for financial controls. The legal-rule specification already requires immutable rule-pack releases, detached signatures, distinct author, reviewer, and publisher capabilities, tenant-scoped customer-defined releases, and signed receipts containing context, jurisdiction, obligations, preemptions, contradictions, and composition trace. The ledger packages already carry tenant identity, window, chain prefix, signed epochs, manifest digests, and offline verification. These are strong foundations for payroll calculations and auditor evidence.

The missing controls are payment-specific and arithmetic-specific. The repository must not infer that an append-only ledger alone satisfies PCI, Nacha, SOX, GLBA, IRS, or bank requirements. It needs explicit scope, data classification, account-validation, dual-control, fraud, reconciliation, filing, and evidence contracts.

1. `SEC-FIN-001` Payment-data scope and isolation. **RED:** the repository does not yet record whether a tenant or integration stores, processes, transmits, or can affect cardholder data, ACH account data, FTI, or other payment-sensitive data. **GREEN:** a tenant and integration profile produces a versioned PCI, Nacha, GLBA, and FTI applicability decision, data-flow map, CDE boundary, and segmentation test.
2. `SEC-FIN-002` Bank-detail dual control and independent confirmation. **RED:** generic step-up exists, but bank-detail changes are not a distinct operation with two-person approval, independently sourced out-of-band confirmation, cooling-off, and velocity policy. **GREEN:** a changed destination cannot release payment until all proofs, approvals, risk checks, and delays are satisfied and evidenced.
3. `SEC-FIN-003` ACH account validation and unreadable storage. **RED:** no domain contract binds first-use or changed-account validation to payment release or proves unreadable at-rest storage at the applicable Nacha threshold. **GREEN:** account validation method and result are required inputs; raw account numbers are never present in logs, evidence, lower environments, or unapproved exports.
4. `SEC-FIN-004` Payroll exact-decimal calculation contract. **RED:** no documented repository invariant covers scale, rounding, allocation, currency, fractions of cents, and reproducibility for payroll and tax calculations. **GREEN:** calculations use exact decimal or minor units, emit versioned receipts, and pass property, boundary, and mutation tests.
5. `SEC-FIN-005` Reconciliation and auditor evidence package. **RED:** ledger evidence is generic and does not yet require payroll-register, bank-file, tax-liability, filing-acknowledgment, and general-ledger tie-outs. **GREEN:** each run has immutable source and target totals, idempotency keys, variance decision, correction chain, and an exportable auditor package.
6. `SEC-FIN-006` Tax and FTI boundary. **RED:** state configuration and legal packs exist, but Publication 1075 cryptographic, remote-access, disclosure, and retention requirements are not a runtime classification and policy boundary. **GREEN:** FTI-aware tenants and state agencies get enforced storage, key, access, transfer, logging, and disclosure controls with a safeguard evidence report.
7. `SEC-FIN-007` Financial breach and notification decisioning. **RED:** no shared evidence model records affected data, discovery time, jurisdiction, consumer count, regulator notice, and tenant-contract notice decisions. **GREEN:** incident workflows produce a reproducible notification matrix and preserve supporting evidence before containment changes data.

### Implications for hcm-next

The repository already has the pieces needed to make government overlays composable. `organization-scope-and-authz.md` keeps organization scope separate from tenant identity, requires current and proposed bitemporal scope inputs, rejects missing or stale projections, and makes purpose, legal, risk, channel, and environment inputs visible in the decision. `legal-rule-packs-and-state-configuration.md` supports jurisdiction sets, effective windows, signed releases, tenant-specific configuration, and publication evidence. The plan calls out regional storage and isolation, workload identity, controlled egress, signed builds, JIT, telemetry, restore verification, and tenant limits. The ledger checkpoint and evidence design can support SSP, POA&M, authorization, records, and exchange evidence if those artifacts are linked to a system and contract boundary.

The main gap is that government requirements are not merely global flags. The service needs an applicability and evidence profile per tenant, integration, data class, jurisdiction, contract, and effective period. It also needs a current-source update process because FedRAMP, CMS, Texas, state privacy, tax, reporting, and identity requirements move on different schedules.

1. `SEC-GOV-001` Government authorization profile and evidence catalog. **RED:** the repository has control concepts and ledger evidence but no tenant-level profile for FedRAMP, GovRAMP, TX-RAMP, FISMA, CJIS, FTI, CUI, MARS-E, contract clauses, inherited controls, and POA&M. **GREEN:** each government tenant and integration has an immutable applicability profile, boundary, control inheritance, evidence pointers, assessor status, and review date.
2. `SEC-GOV-002` FedRAMP 20x transition tracker. **RED:** the product plan cannot distinguish Rev. 5 authorization, 20x class, agency authorization, and transition deadline. **GREEN:** release and procurement artifacts state the applicable FedRAMP path, class, package version, monitoring evidence, and customer-specific authorization responsibility.
3. `SEC-GOV-003` Government data-class boundary. **RED:** FTI, CJI, CUI, ACA health data, employee PII, and payment data are not yet a single classified-data policy input with key, location, export, personnel, and retention effects. **GREEN:** classification changes storage, encryption, access, disclosure, support, logging, backup, and deletion behavior and is included in every policy receipt.
4. `SEC-GOV-004` CMMC and NIST version contract. **RED:** current NIST 800-171 Rev. 3 and codified CMMC references to Rev. 2 can be confused in a contract or questionnaire. **GREEN:** the contract profile records the exact clause, CMMC level, NIST version, assessment method, POA&M rule, and evidence set, and blocks unsupported compliance claims.
5. `SEC-GOV-005` MARS-E and CMS control mapping. **RED:** public MARS-E v2.2, CMS ARS 5.1, SSPP, ISA, and CMS-specific control inheritance are not represented as distinct releases. **GREEN:** a CMS/ACA tenant pins the applicable release, control catalog, data-use agreement, interconnection, SSPP, and assessment evidence.
6. `SEC-GOV-006` Government identity assurance. **RED:** trust has an authentication seam but no IAL/AAL/FAL policy, Login.gov or state-IdP profile, or assurance-to-role mapping. **GREEN:** identity assurance is recorded, reauthentication is enforced for sensitive work, and external deprovisioning or claim changes revoke access and produce evidence.
7. `SEC-GOV-007` Records, holds, and privacy deletion. **RED:** ledger retention and legal evidence do not yet distinguish public-records schedules, tax retention, privacy deletion, customer contract, and legal hold. **GREEN:** each record class has schedule authority, cutoff, hold, disclosure, export, and disposition evidence, with deletion blocked while a hold or required schedule applies.
8. `SEC-GOV-008` Workforce exchange adapters. **RED:** legal rules and state configuration do not guarantee versioned UI wage, new-hire, state withholding, and local filing schemas, deadlines, acknowledgments, correction chains, or encrypted delivery. **GREEN:** every exchange has a jurisdiction profile, effective date, schema validation, payload digest, transport policy, acknowledgment, correction, and reconciliation evidence.
9. `SEC-GOV-009` Public procurement and accessibility package. **RED:** there is no generated, versioned response pack for local security questionnaires, Section 508, subprocessors, data location, RTO/RPO, incident notice, and audit evidence. **GREEN:** a tenant-scoped pack answers each questionnaire item with current evidence, inheritance, owner, date, and accessible artifact.
10. `SEC-GOV-010` State privacy law release gate. **RED:** state configuration is oriented to legal rules, but there is no effective-date roster and employee-data treatment for California, Colorado, Virginia, Texas, and other enacted comprehensive state laws. **GREEN:** a signed jurisdiction release drives rights, exemptions, sensitive data, processor duties, opt-out or consent signals, retention, and request evidence, with primary-source review before effective date.

## Confidence and staleness

Confidence is high for the cited NIST, IETF, OWASP, PCI SSC, IRS, FTC, Nacha, FBI, CMS, FedRAMP, GovRAMP, Texas DIR, Access Board, NARA, DOL, and Login.gov statements that were confirmed from the linked primary or issuing-body pages on 2026-09-05. OAuth 2.1 remains a draft on the read date, so RFC 9700 and the deployed OIDC and OAuth profiles should be the normative implementation references until an RFC is published. FedRAMP 20x dates and classes are moving program information and must be rechecked before a contract commitment. CMS publicly exposes MARS-E v2.2 materials but separately identifies current CMS ARS 5.1; the applicable contract and CMS authorization package controls. NIST SP 800-171 Rev. 3 is current NIST guidance, while the codified CMMC model page still references Rev. 2, so contract clauses and implementing rules must control. ISO pages currently list ISO/IEC 27017:2026 and ISO/IEC 27018:2025; confirm access to the licensed standard and customer-required edition before citing a certification scope. The 2026 count and landscape of comprehensive state privacy laws was not confirmed from one authoritative primary source in this pass, so the document intentionally uses a state-by-state effective-date matrix and treats the multistate overview as secondary orientation only. State workforce, unemployment, withholding, public-records, and local procurement requirements remain state, agency, and contract specific.
